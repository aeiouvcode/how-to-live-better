# 21. Going abroad, travel, and safety overseas

This chapter is about money and personal freedom, and a few entries bear on personal safety. Once you're abroad, the law that governs you is the host country's, not China's. There is a clear line to what Chinese embassies and consulates can do for you, and this chapter draws that line first.

### 1. Check the foreign ministry's safety advisories once before departure; anywhere marked "do not travel for now" - don't go
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Open the China Consular Service website and read through it - a few minutes.
- TL;DR: The foreign ministry grades overseas safety risk in four tiers, the highest called "do not travel for now." Since September 15, 2026, avoiding high-risk countries and regions is written into administrative regulation. When you apply for documents and pass exit inspection, staff will also remind you, and where necessary talk you out of going. Read that page once before departure - a few minutes' work.
- Benefit: The foreign ministry publishes overseas safety advisories by risk level. From low to high, roughly four tiers: "pay attention to safety," "step up safety precautions," "travel with caution," "do not travel for now." The new rules effective September 15, 2026 also write your side's obligation into administrative regulation. The original text: "Chinese citizens shall pay attention to overseas safety advisories and safety risk warnings for tourist destinations, and avoid traveling to or staying in high-risk countries or regions." The new rules also require the immigration authorities (the departments that issue entry-exit documents and run border inspection) to remind people heading for high-risk regions when they apply for documents and pass exit inspection. The original text of that clause: "for Chinese citizens preparing to travel to countries or regions at the highest risk level or with a sudden high incidence of cases seriously endangering personal safety, they shall, where necessary, be dissuaded from going"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Provisions on exit and entry administration (State Council Order No. 841). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm> (Article 2); State Council (2023). Regulation on consular protection and assistance (State Council Order No. 763). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm> (Articles 19, 21); Consular Department of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Safety advisories. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/>
- Notes: The advisories change with the situation; this book does not copy the list - check the page yourself before traveling. As of September 7, 2026, these "do not travel for now" advisories were still posted in that section. Eswatini (2026-08-25), Palestine (2023-10-10), Sudan (2023-04-17). Also Afghanistan (2023-02-17), Syria (2023-01-04). Plus Peru (2022-12-29), Somalia (2022-11-24). There are also dedicated advisories for northern Myanmar and certain provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Travel agencies have a duty to inform too: before joining a tour, you can demand a written statement of the destination's risks

### 2. Save 12308 and the local embassy or consulate's consular-protection number in your phone, and copy them onto paper in your wallet - don't wait until something happens
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Save two numbers and make one paper copy - a few minutes.
- TL;DR: When something goes wrong, there is one number to call for help. Inside China, dial 12308. Abroad, dial +86-10-12308. Since it opened, this hotline has taken over 3 million calls. In the same period, the foreign ministry and missions abroad handled over 500,000 consular-protection cases.
- Benefit: The foreign ministry runs a 24-hour global consular protection and emergency hotline. Inside China, dial 12308. Abroad, dial +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308. From its opening in 2014 to now, the hotline has answered over 3 million calls in total. In the same period, the foreign ministry and missions abroad handled over 500,000 consular-protection cases of all kinds
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Chinese embassy and consulates in Myanmar (2023). Consular notice on the current security situation in northern Myanmar. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>; State Council Information Office (2023). Regular policy briefing on consular protection and assistance. <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2023zccfh/11/index.htm>
- Notes: The local embassy or consulate also has its own consular-protection number, usually faster than the central line. Before departure, look it up by country on the China Consular Service website and save it alongside the central number. If your phone is lost or dead, the saved numbers do you no good - so copy them by hand into your wallet, or write them down for family at home

### 3. Know what consular protection can and cannot do: it can visit you, it cannot get you out, and the costs are still yours
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes to read this line clearly.
- TL;DR: If you're detained abroad, what the embassy or consulate can do is: verify the situation with the locals, demand lawful and fair handling, visit you, and sit in on the trial. It cannot change your verdict, and it cannot walk you out. Food, lodging, transport and medical costs advanced by others are still yours to repay in the end. So have money and insurance ready before going abroad - don't make "the state will cover it if something happens" your plan.
- Benefit: When you're detained, the embassy or consulate can do these things. Verify the situation with the host country's authorities and demand lawful and fair handling. The regulation's original text: "in accordance with the laws of the host state and the international treaties concluded or jointly acceded to by China and the host state, visit them or maintain contact with them, learn their needs, and request that the host state's authorities accord the Chinese citizen humanitarian and fair treatment." In plain terms, under local law and the treaties between the two countries, they visit you, stay in contact, ask what you need, and demand that the locals treat you to humanitarian and fair standards. At trial the embassy or consulate may attend and may demand that your litigation rights be protected. If you're injured or caught in a disaster, it can press for emergency assistance locally and help your family handle the aftermath. But the money side is written just as clearly: "Chinese citizens, legal persons and unincorporated organizations that receive materials and services such as food, lodging, transport and medical care from third parties in the course of consular protection and assistance shall pay the costs that they themselves should bear." In other words, the food, lodging, transport and medical care someone else advances - what's yours to pay is still yours to pay
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2023). Regulation on consular protection and assistance (State Council Order No. 763). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm> (Articles 9, 10, 14, 26)
- Notes: The embassy or consulate won't pay your fines, won't cover your medical bills, won't buy your plane ticket, and can't change the conclusion of a foreign court. What it can give you is information on local lawyers, translators, medical and funeral services, and it can help you contact family and friends and find relief channels. The practical use of knowing where this line sits: get your own money and insurance ready before going abroad, and don't make "the state will cover it" your plan

### 4. Buy insurance that includes overseas medical care and medical evacuation - not just flight-delay cover
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: A short-term overseas travel policy, tens to hundreds of yuan.
- TL;DR: Medical care abroad is paid out of your own pocket. Domestic medical insurance basically doesn't reimburse it, and consulates don't advance it. What can truly break a family isn't the clinic bill - it's hospitalization after a serious injury, and flying the person home, which takes an air ambulance or a medical escort. When buying travel insurance, look at two limits: overseas medical expenses, and emergency medical evacuation and repatriation.
- Benefit: Medical care abroad comes out of your own pocket. Domestic medical insurance basically doesn't reimburse overseas. Consular protection doesn't advance this money either (see entry 3: what consular protection can and cannot do; the basis is Article 26 of the Regulation). What can truly break a family isn't the clinic bill but hospitalization after serious injury and medical evacuation. Transferring a person from a foreign hospital back home takes an air ambulance or a medical escort - among the most expensive of all out-of-pocket items. When buying insurance, check two limits first. One is the overseas medical-expense limit. The other is the emergency medical evacuation and repatriation limit - the most the policy will pay for transferring hospitals and flying you home after an incident
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Council (2023). Regulation on consular protection and assistance (State Council Order No. 763). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm> (Article 26); author's experience, no direct literature
- Notes: TODO (unverified: typical cost ranges for overseas medical care and medical evacuation; needs figures from official or industry statistics). Confirm three things before buying. First, whether the country you're going to is inside the coverage. Second, whether high-risk activities like diving, skiing and motorcycling are excluded. Third, whether claims are settled directly between the insurer and the hospital, or you pay first and get reimbursed back home. Visa-free doesn't mean risk-free - wherever you go, buy it all the same

### 5. Treat every "high-paying job overseas" offer as a scam; people tricked into telecom fraud abroad can be barred from leaving the country again after they return
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is turning down what looks like a great job with your own hands.
- TL;DR: An absurdly high monthly salary, no questions about education or experience, no proper contract, a meeting point in a border city, and someone who "keeps your passport for you." Take that job, and neither you nor your passport is in your own hands. If your exit documents are faked, border inspection will stop you and refuse passage. If you took part in crime overseas, after returning home you can be barred from leaving the country for 6 months to 3 years.
- Benefit: The official characterization of these cases is blunt. In recent years there have been people "deceived into going abroad, or illegally exiting under fabricated pretexts, to engage in unlawful activities such as cross-border gambling and telecom-network fraud, seriously endangering the lives and property of the people." In other words, people tricked into going, or sneaking out on an invented reason, to run gambling and telecom fraud over there. The new rules tighten both ends against this. The exit end: "the stated purpose of an application for exit, entry, stay or residence shall be true and lawful." Submit fake materials or lie, and the immigration authority has the power not to issue documents and not to let you leave. The return end: "a Chinese citizen who engages in illegal or criminal activities overseas, endangering national security and interests, may... be barred from leaving the country for 6 months to 3 years from the date of return." Administrative detention for fraudulently obtaining documents or illegal exit and entry likewise brings a 6-month-to-3-year exit bar
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Provisions on exit and entry administration (State Council Order No. 841). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm> (Articles 3, 4); Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Public Security, National Immigration Administration (2026). Press Q&A on the "State Council Provisions on Exit and Entry Administration." <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7077270.htm>
- Notes: The identifying features are almost fixed: pay far above the going rate for the role, no education or experience required, no proper contract, a meeting point in a border city, and someone arranging the visa and holding your passport. Northern Myanmar already has a dedicated consular advisory. Go for real, and your personal freedom and your passport are both out of your hands - "leave whenever I want" does not hold

### 6. Cash withdrawals abroad are capped at 100,000 yuan per year - counted across every card in your name
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before leaving, estimate how much cash the trip needs and put the rest on cards. The hard part is changing the habit: if you can tap a card, don't withdraw cash.
- TL;DR: The overseas cash-withdrawal limit is not per card - it's all bank cards in your name (supplementary cards included) combined, 100,000 yuan per calendar year. Hit the cap, and you cannot withdraw cash abroad for the rest of that year and the next. What's suspended is only cash withdrawal; card spending is unaffected and doesn't eat into your annual $50,000 foreign-exchange quota.
- Benefit: The basis is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's "Notice on regulating large overseas cash withdrawals by bank card" (Huifa [2017] No. 29, effective January 1, 2018). Article 1: "for individuals holding domestic bank cards withdrawing cash overseas, the bank cards in the person's own name (including supplementary cards) may not exceed the equivalent of 100,000 yuan in total per calendar year. Anyone exceeding the annual quota will be suspended from overseas cash withdrawals with domestic bank cards for the current year and the following year." Paragraph 2 of the same Article: "individuals may not evade or assist in evading the administration of overseas cash withdrawals by borrowing others' bank cards or lending their own." Article 2: the foreign-exchange authority sends the suspension list to card-issuing banks daily, and each bank must suspend the listed people from overseas withdrawals with its cards no later than 17:00 Beijing time that day. Article 3: people on the suspension list may take valid ID to a branch of the foreign-exchange authority to check their own overseas withdrawal details. Article 4: the per-card daily limit for foreign-currency cards was adjusted from the equivalent of $1,000 to the equivalent of 10,000 yuan; yuan cards stay at no more than the equivalent of 10,000 yuan per card per day. Article 6: card-issuing banks, domestic yuan-card clearing organizations and individuals violating this Notice are penalized under the relevant provisions of the "Regulation of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration." The authority's press Q&A also makes several points clear. The Notice does not affect overseas card spending by individuals. Food, lodging, transport and shopping for overseas tourism, business and study - current-account transactions all - can be paid by bank card. This spending also doesn't consume the individual annual $50,000 facilitated foreign-exchange quota. The annual quota is computed by the authority aggregating data from all card-issuing institutions; because real-time control would slow transaction response, "the foreign-exchange authority uses delayed-control measures" - it doesn't intercept on the spot. Separately, statistics show that in 2016, 81% of overseas cash withdrawals with domestic bank cards were below 30,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (2017). Notice on regulating large overseas cash withdrawals by bank card (Huifa [2017] No. 29, Articles 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7; effective January 1, 2018). <https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2017/1230/21873.html>; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (2017). Press Q&A by the responsible official on regulating large overseas cash withdrawals by bank card. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2017-12/31/content_5251958.htm>; China UnionPay International. Overseas ATM withdrawals. <https://m.unionpayintl.com/wap/cn/serviceCenter/cardUsingInstructions/805.shtml>
- Notes: The quota runs by calendar year, resetting on January 1. What's suspended is only overseas cash withdrawal; card spending is unaffected. If you genuinely need a large amount of cash abroad, first purchase foreign exchange under the "Measures for the administration of individual foreign exchange," then carry the foreign currency cash out. Withdrawals themselves have two more traps, spelled out on UnionPay International's "overseas ATM withdrawals" page. One: at some ATMs in some countries, dual-brand cards cannot select the UnionPay channel. Two: some overseas ATMs display an extra surcharge on screen, collected by the local acquirer and "unrelated to UnionPay." The same page also advises that "since credit-card cash advances incur overdraft interest, cardholders are advised to use UnionPay debit cards for overseas withdrawals." What to do if a card is lost, swallowed or fraudulently charged is in entry 5 of chapter 14 (if your card is fraudulently charged, freeze it first, then report to police)
### 7. Photograph your passport, visa and ID card and store copies in the cloud; if lost, report to police first, then get a replacement travel document
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. A few photos before departure.
- TL;DR: If your passport is lost, the embassy or consulate can issue you a travel document to get home - provided you can prove who you are. With photos of the data page and visa pages plus your entry-exit records in hand, replacement goes much faster. You also have to report to the local police first and get the receipt - exit checks and insurance claims both honor that piece of paper. Phones often get lost together with passports, so don't keep the photos only on your phone.
- Benefit: If your passport is lost, the mission abroad can issue you a travel document for returning home, but you must first prove your identity. With clear photos of the passport data page, the visa pages and your entry-exit records in hand, the speed of replacement differs enormously. At the same time, you must report to the local police first and obtain the report receipt. Many countries' exit checks and insurance claims require this document. The entry point to the embassy or consulate is the two consular-protection numbers saved under entry 2
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Council (2023). Regulation on consular protection and assistance (State Council Order No. 763). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm> (Article 16); author's experience, no direct literature
- Notes: Store the photos in two different places - one copy in cloud storage, one sent to a family member. Also print a paper copy and keep it apart from the original. Photograph your bank cards and prescriptions for regular medication too. Phones and passports usually get lost together; kept only on the phone, the photos are as good as not taken

### 8. Before driving abroad, confirm whether the destination recognizes a Chinese license; the "international driver's licenses" sold online are mostly worthless paper
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Check the destination's rules once before departure.
- TL;DR: The "international driver's license" arranged online has no standing in mainland China. That document is a translation mutually recognized among parties to the 1949 Geneva convention, and mainland China is not among its 103 parties. So whoever arranges one for you in China, it has no legal effect. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the destination's own rules - if you haven't confirmed it, don't drive.
- Benefit: The so-called "international driving permit" (IDP) is a translation mutually recognized among parties to the 1949 Convention on Road Traffic. That convention was concluded in Geneva on September 19, 1949 and entered into force on March 26, 1952. It currently has 103 parties - the countries and regions that signed it. Mainland China is not on that list. So China does not issue this kind of international permit. Any agency "arranging an international driver's license" in China produces no legal effect. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the destination country or region's own rules: some accept a Chinese license with a notarized translation, some require converting to a local license, some don't recognize it at all
- Evidence: A
- Sources: United Nations Treaty Collection. Convention on Road Traffic, Geneva, 19 September 1949 - status of treaties. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsV.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-1&chapter=11&clang=_en>
- Notes: In most countries, driving without a license is a criminal offense or carries a heavy fine. And if something happens, the insurance will very likely refuse to pay - that is the real cost. Two ways to check: find the destination country's "notice for Chinese citizens" on the China Consular Service website, or get a written confirmation from the rental company directly. Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan and some countries have separate arrangements of their own

### 9. Before using an agency for a visa, study abroad or immigration, ask whether it's registered with the immigration authority
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Ask the one extra question about registration, and keep the paperwork.
- TL;DR: Since September 15, 2026, agencies handling visas, study abroad and immigration must register with the immigration authority. Phrases like "guaranteed pass," "visa guaranteed," "we'll make the materials for you" are themselves on the expressly banned list. Fill in a form wrong yourself, and at worst this application is refused. Use fake materials and get found out, and the stain stays in your entry-exit record.
- Benefit: In 2018 the entry license for private exit-entry agencies was abolished - opening such an agency no longer required prior approval. The number of agencies grew fast after that, and problems appeared: "unclear totals of agencies, some agencies handling business in violation of laws and regulations, harming the lawful rights and interests of exit-entry persons" - officially, even the count of agencies is unknown. Since September 15, 2026, these agencies are under registration-based administration. An agency must file with the local immigration authority within 15 days of establishment. Those already operating must complete filing within 90 days of the rules taking effect. The expressly banned conduct includes: first, "publishing false information, or soliciting clients through exaggerated or misleading publicity"; second, "providing or assisting in providing false materials, assisting others in improperly obtaining visas, stay and residence permits, passports and other exit-entry documents or procedures"; third, "leaking, selling or illegally providing... personal privacy or personal information"; fourth, "organizing or assisting others in cross-border illegal or criminal activities." Foreign enterprises and organizations may not provide this kind of agency service inside China
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Provisions on exit and entry administration (State Council Order No. 841). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm> (Articles 7, 8, 10, 12, 13)
- Notes: An agency with "guaranteed pass," "visa guaranteed," "we'll make the materials for you" on its lips is doing things that are themselves expressly banned. Once discovered, the stain lands on your visa record. A mistake in a form you filled out yourself means at worst this one refusal. Being found to have used fake materials carries far heavier consequences. Policy consultation and information lookup not done for profit don't count as agency services

### 10. Don't issue invitation letters for strangers, and don't let anyone use your name for entry-exit procedures
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is turning down a favor face to face.
- TL;DR: Issue an invitation letter or application materials for someone with untrue content, and the one fined is the issuer. An individual is fined 5,000 to 10,000 yuan, with any illegal gains confiscated as well. An organization is fined 10,000 to 50,000 yuan, and the people who handled it are separately fined 5,000 to 10,000 yuan. One favor, and the money comes out of your own pocket.
- Benefit: Whoever issues an invitation letter is responsible for whether its content is true. The original text: "where an individual issues a false invitation letter or other application materials for another person's application for exit, entry, stay or residence, the immigration authority shall impose a fine of 5,000 to 10,000 yuan; any illegal gains shall be confiscated." An organization issuing false materials is fined 10,000 to 50,000 yuan. At the same time, the directly responsible supervisors and other directly responsible personnel - the leaders in charge of this work and the people who handled it - are separately fined 5,000 to 10,000 yuan
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Provisions on exit and entry administration (State Council Order No. 841). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm> (Articles 3, 11)
- Notes: This is the same kind of risk as "don't carry things for strangers" in chapter 8: your signature, your seal, your guarantee - so the consequence lands on you. If public officials or military personnel try to use an agency to improperly obtain foreign nationality or overseas residency, the agency must not only refuse - it must report it to the supervisory authority

### 11. Before leaving the country, send your itinerary, lodging and companions to one family member, and agree on a contact rhythm
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One message before departure.
- TL;DR: If someone goes missing abroad, the family can ask the missions overseas for help. They will tell the family how to report to the local police and what other help channels exist. Once a case is filed locally, the embassy or consulate follows up and presses for action. Whether this chain can start at all depends on someone back home knowing where you should be and when you should check in. Flight number, address, companions' phone numbers, the agreed contact rhythm - send them to one person and that's enough.
- Benefit: When a Chinese citizen's whereabouts are unknown abroad, relatives may turn to the diplomatic missions for help. The regulation's original text: "the diplomatic missions abroad shall provide the local ways to report to police and other information on obtaining assistance." Once local police file a case, the embassy or consulate presses them to handle it promptly. Whether this whole chain can start turning depends on someone back home knowing where you were supposed to be and when you were supposed to make contact
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Council (2023). Regulation on consular protection and assistance (State Council Order No. 763). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm> (Article 13); author's experience, no direct literature
- Notes: Four things written down are enough: flight number, address of where you're staying, companions' names and phone numbers, the agreed contact rhythm. Heading somewhere with poor signal or poor security, spell out the sentence "if there's no word for this long, call the police" too. This entry costs almost nothing, yet every rescue procedure above needs it to begin
