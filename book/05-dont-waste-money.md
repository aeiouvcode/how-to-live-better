# 5. Don't waste money

This chapter keeps the money account. It looks at three things: how much you can save, how much the annual return differs, and what a sum of money earns back on long-run average. None of this is converted into lifespan or time. Every entry touching investment explains only how to do the math, using only verified historical data. None of it constitutes investment advice.

### 1. Turn off every auto-renewal - renew manually when things expire
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Going through the auto-debit lists in Alipay, WeChat, Apple and Android takes 10-20 minutes once. Afterward, each renewal takes one extra tap from you.
- TL;DR: What you save is the subscriptions you stopped using long ago that still debit you monthly. One pass through the auto-debit lists of Alipay, WeChat, Apple and Android takes under 20 minutes. By regulation, merchants must clearly remind you before charging and must give you an easy-to-find cancel-anytime option.
- Benefit: The money saved equals the sum of subscriptions you no longer use but are still being charged for. It varies per person - no uniform number. Regulators require merchants to remind you prominently before a renewal charge, and to offer a "convenient cancel-anytime" option. Canceling costs nothing extra (China, in force since 2021)
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Administration for Market Regulation (2021, amended 2025). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Transactions (Order No. 37, Article 18). <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Notes: For services you genuinely use often, annual billing is usually cheaper than monthly - keeping auto-renewal there actually pays. This entry targets the other kind: the ones you forgot about that keep charging you.

### 2. Do the annual individual income tax reconciliation each March to June, and fill in the special additional deductions you qualify for
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Done in the "Individual Income Tax" app in ten-odd minutes.
- TL;DR: These kinds of people usually get money back: those who worked only part of the year, changed jobs mid-year, or never filled in deductions like children's education, mortgage, rent or supporting elderly parents. Ten-odd minutes on your phone. If your annual comprehensive income is under 120 thousand yuan, or the tax you'd owe is under 400 yuan, you can skip it.
- Benefit: Wages, labor remuneration, author remuneration and royalties get totaled into one annual account. Overpayment comes back to you; underpayment must be made up. Two situations let you skip it: annual comprehensive income not over 120 thousand yuan, or tax due not over 400 yuan. Those two thresholds come from a 2023 State Taxation Administration announcement, valid through December 31, 2027. Conversely, these kinds of people usually get refunds: those with income in only part of the year, job changes mid-year, or unfilled deductions for children's education, mortgage interest, rent, supporting elderly parents
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Taxation Administration (2025). Administrative Measures for the Annual Reconciliation of Comprehensive Individual Income Tax (Article 6) and its interpretation. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm> ; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2023). Announcement on extending the policy on annual reconciliation of comprehensive individual income tax. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Notes: People who owe tax must file too - not filing is illegal. There are seven special additional deductions in total: children's education, continuing education, serious-illness medical costs, home-loan interest, housing rent, supporting elderly parents, and care for children under 3. Mortgage interest and rent are mutually exclusive - you can claim only one

### 3. The housing provident fund isn't only for buying a home: rent, renovation and property fees can all be drawn - the new regulation governs from September 20, 2026
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to file the withdrawal.
- TL;DR: The provident fund isn't untouchable until you buy a home. The new regulation lists nine withdrawal situations - paying rent, renovating your own home, and paying property fees all count. Once you apply, the fund center must answer yes or no within 3 days.
- Benefit: The amended regulation lists nine situations allowing withdrawal, including paying rent, renovating your owner-occupied home, and paying its property fees. The housing provident fund management center must decide to approve or reject within 3 days of accepting your application
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Decision of the State Council on Amending the Regulation on the Administration of Housing Provident Funds (State Council Order No. 844, adopted at the 93rd executive meeting of the State Council on July 31, 2026, "effective September 20, 2026"): amended Article 24 - "An employee may withdraw the balance in their housing provident fund account under any of the following circumstances: (1) paying rent; (2) purchasing, building, rebuilding or substantially repairing an owner-occupied home; (3) repaying principal and interest on a home loan; (4) renovating an owner-occupied home; (5) paying property fees for an owner-occupied home; (6) retiring; (7) having completely lost the ability to work and terminated the employment relationship; (8) settling abroad; (9) other housing-consumption circumstances approved by the State Council."; Article 25 - "The housing provident fund management center shall decide whether to approve the withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application"; Article 18 - contribution rates "shall not be lower than 5% of the employee's average monthly wage in the previous year". <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>
- Notes: The money in the account is your own wages plus the part your employer matches. Left sitting, it earns only a very low interest rate. The regulation also states: when an employee dies or is declared dead, heirs and legatees may withdraw the account balance. What materials to submit and exactly how to file are set by each local fund center - check your local center's service guide before going.

### 4. Recompute your phone and broadband plans once a year, downgrade what you don't use, and complain if the carrier won't switch you
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Checking a bill takes ten-odd minutes. The hard part is standing your ground when customer service refuses the downgrade.
- TL;DR: The old plan you're on often costs tens of yuan more than what's sold now. Tens a month is hundreds a year thrown away. By regulation, without legitimate grounds a carrier cannot refuse your number-portability switch, stall it, or set special plans just for switchers. If it stonewalls, complain.
- Benefit: The MIIT regulation states that carriers may not "without legitimate grounds refuse, obstruct or delay providing number-portability service to users", nor "adopt interception, restriction or other technical means that degrade communication service quality for number-porting users", nor "set special pricing plans or marketing schemes for number-porting users". An old plan often costs tens of yuan more than currently sold plans. Never switching means throwing away hundreds a year
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2019). Administrative Provisions on Number Portability Services (MIIT Xinguan [2019] No. 242). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Notes: First check your actual data and call usage over the past six months in the carrier's app, then pick a plan to match. If customer service says "downgrades need an in-store visit" or "you can't change mid-contract", demand the basis in writing. If they can't produce it, call 12300 to complain

### 5. Don't buy lottery tickets
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Not buying is saving.
- TL;DR: By regulation, lotto-type number lotteries pay out at least 50% of sales as prizes; Double Color Ball pays 51%. Meaning every 1 yuan you put in returns 0.51 yuan on long-run average - a steady 49% loss. In 2024 China sold 623.486 billion yuan of lottery tickets, of which 161.031 billion went straight to the public-welfare fund, outside the prize pool.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Finance sets the minimum prize share of sales: 50% for lotto and number lotteries, 65% for scratch cards, 73% for sports betting. Double Color Ball allocates 51% of sales to prizes - of every 100 yuan sold, only 51 goes to prizes. Converted to you: every 1 yuan in returns 0.51 yuan on long-run average, a 49% long-run loss. In 2024, national lottery sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion entered the public-welfare fund and never returned to ticket buyers (China, 2024)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance (2015). Notice on regulating and strengthening the management of lottery fund composition ratios (Caizong [2015] No. 94). <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm> ; Ministry of Finance (2025). MOF Announcement No. 8 of 2025 (2024 lottery public-welfare fund collection and distribution). <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm> ; China Welfare Lottery Double Color Ball Game Rules (Article 14). <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Notes: The long-run average loss is fixed by the system's design - it's not a matter of luck. If you must buy, treat it as 2-yuan entertainment, never as financial management.

### 6. Walk straight away from "high yield", "principal guaranteed", "sure profit"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Just pause when you hear those words.
- TL;DR: The regulator's own phrasing: yields over 6% deserve a question mark, over 8% is dangerous, over 10% means be ready to lose all your principal. Formal wealth management is no longer allowed to promise principal or returns. Money lost to illegal fundraising is, by regulation, borne by you - nobody bails you out afterward.
- Benefit: The former chairman of the banking and insurance regulator said it at the 2018 Lujiazui Forum, verbatim: "Yields over 6% deserve a question mark, over 8% are very dangerous, and over 10% you should be prepared to lose all your principal." The new asset-management rules require financial institutions to "not promise principal protection or guaranteed returns" and "when redemption difficulties arise, financial institutions may not advance funds to cover redemption in any form". In other words, formal wealth management no longer has any such thing as principal protection. Losses from participating in illegal fundraising are "borne by the fundraising participants themselves". After something blows up, nobody covers you (China, 2018, 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Guo Shuqing's speech at the 10th Lujiazui Forum (June 2018), reposted by the Tianjin Local Financial Regulatory Bureau. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html> ; People's Bank of China et al. (2018). Guiding Opinions on Regulating Financial Institutions' Asset Management Business (Yinfa [2018] No. 106, Articles 2, 6). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5323101.htm> ; State Council (2021). Regulation on the Prevention and Handling of Illegal Fundraising (State Council Order No. 737, Article 25). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>
- Notes: The 6%, 8% and 10% lines are the regulator's rules of thumb, not statutory text. ### 7. Never use credit-card minimum payment, and don't take installments or consumer loans for spending
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. You need enough cash set aside to pay in full before the statement date. The hard part is resisting installments that spread a big purchase thin.
- TL;DR: Minimum payment is not a courtesy - it's a loan accruing interest daily, about 18% a year. Use it and your interest-free period dies on the spot, with interest charged on the unpaid principal from day one. When reading rates, trust only the annualized number. The central bank already requires card issuers to display the annualized rate prominently - showing only a "daily rate" is banned.
- Benefit: Before 2020, credit-card overdraft rates had a floor and ceiling: the ceiling was "0.05% per day" and the floor was 0.7 times that. 0.05% daily annualizes to 18.25% - simple interest, meaning interest doesn't compound into principal. From 2021, pricing moved to negotiation between issuer and cardholder. Once you use minimum payment, the unpaid principal accrues interest in full every day, and the interest-free period is void at the same time. The central bank requires issuers to display the annualized rate prominently and forbids "showing only the daily rate or daily repayment amount". Look at that annual number before deciding (China, 2020 notice)
- Evidence: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China (2020). Notice on advancing market-oriented reform of credit-card overdraft interest rates (Yinfa [2020] No. 327). <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Notes: 18.25% is just "0.05% × 365" multiplied out, without compounding - truly rolled monthly it runs higher. Some banks discount; go by the annualized rate printed on your own statement. When cash genuinely won't stretch, prefer a cheaper formal borrowing channel over letting minimum-payment interest roll.

### 8. No tipping streamers, no game top-ups, no impulse orders
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Three moves taking minutes: turn off password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, and unbind payment methods from platforms. The hard part is not tapping in the moment.
- TL;DR: In 2024 China's game revenue was 325.783 billion yuan across 674 million users - about 480 yuan per person. Money spent is basically unrecoverable. The law supports exactly one kind of refund: what a minor spends without parental consent. Adults get no such provision. The most effort-saving fix is preparing ahead: kill password-free payment, set limits, unbind payment methods.
- Benefit: This is a large outlay. In 2024, China's game market took 325.783 billion yuan in actual sales revenue with 674 million users - about 480 yuan per person (dividing the first two numbers). Money spent is basically unrecoverable, with one exception: when a person with limited civil capacity (a minor) tips or tops up without guardian consent, and the guardian demands a refund, "the people's court shall support it". Adults' own tipping and top-ups have no such rule. Separately, minors may play online games only on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and statutory holidays, 20:00-21:00, one hour a day (China, 2020, 2021)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: National Press and Publication Administration (2024). China's game market revenue tops 325.7 billion yuan this year, a record (2024 China Game Industry Report by the Game Publishing Committee). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/ywdt/202412/t20241216_877445.html> ; Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding Opinions (II) on Properly Adjudicating Civil Cases Related to COVID-19 According to Law (Fafa [2020] No. 17, Article 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/230181.html> ; National Press and Publication Administration (2021). Notice on further strictly managing and effectively preventing minors' addiction to online games (Guoxinchufa [2021] No. 14). <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/tzgs/202108/t20210830_666285.html>
- Notes: Graded B because nobody has directly measured "how much not tipping saves" - only industry-wide totals exist. Killing password-free payment and setting limits are practice-based moves, grade C on their own. The verbatim text of Civil Code Articles 19 and 145 is quoted in entry 9 (kids topping up and tipping on phones). Not investment advice.

### 9. If your kid tops up games or tips streamers on a phone: big spending by kids eight or older, not ratified by a parent, can be clawed back
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. It takes time collecting evidence and negotiating with the platform - platforms usually refuse first, refund later. If talks fail, a lawsuit is months minimum, and lawyer fees come out of your pocket. So prepare the material first; going through the platform and 12315 is cheaper. The hard part: the kid won't talk, and you have to ask patiently.
- TL;DR: Money spent by a child eight or older, when the amount clearly doesn't match their age and understanding and the parent neither agreed in advance nor ratifies it afterward - that transaction is invalid and a refund can be demanded. Children under eight can't validly spend on their own at all. A parent who never takes a position is treated by law as refusing to ratify.
- Benefit: Minors eight and older are persons with limited civil capacity - they can independently perform only acts matching their age and understanding. Civil legal acts beyond that take effect only with the legal representative's consent or ratification - the parent nodding beforehand or owning it afterward. Children under eight have no civil capacity and cannot act on their own; their legal representative acts for them. If the legal representative makes no indication, it is deemed a refusal to ratify - a parent staying silent counts as not owning the transaction
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code. Full text reposted by the Supreme People's Procuratorate. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> Article 19: "A minor aged eight or older is a person with limited capacity for civil conduct; civil legal acts are performed by their legal representative as agent, or with the legal representative's consent or ratification; however, they may independently perform civil legal acts that purely benefit them or that suit their age and intelligence." Article 20: "A minor under the age of eight is a person without capacity for civil conduct; civil legal acts are performed by their legal representative as agent." Article 145: "Civil legal acts performed by a person with limited capacity for civil conduct that purely benefit them or suit their age, intelligence and mental health are valid; other civil legal acts they perform become valid upon the consent or ratification of their legal representative." "If the legal representative makes no indication, it is deemed a refusal to ratify."
- Notes: The crux is evidence: you must prove the child, not you, operated the phone. Useful material: top-ups clustered in school hours or the parent's work hours, device usage records, the child's chat and game records, and the child's own account. "Suited to age and intelligence" has no uniform money line - a few dozen yuan for a game skin is not tens of thousands in tipping. Preventing it beforehand is far less effort: cap password-free payments, turn on teen mode, and don't tell the kid your payment password. For your own impulse tipping and top-ups, see this chapter's entry "no tipping streamers, no game top-ups, no impulse orders".

### 10. Teach your kid one sentence: anyone who tells you to operate a parent's phone or read out a verification code is a scammer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Walk your kid through the six scheme types below, and agree on one line: "if you get scammed, tell me first - I won't yell at you." Meanwhile cap password-free payments, turn on teen mode, and keep payment passwords from the kid. The hard part is holding your temper if it actually happens.
- TL;DR: These six scam types all work differently, but the last step is always the same move: getting the kid to transfer money from a parent's phone, or to read out a verification code. So one sentence is enough: whoever tells you to touch a parent's phone or report a code is a scammer. Plus one more: "if you get scammed, tell me first - I won't yell at you." Only if the kid dares to speak up the same day can the money possibly be recovered.
- Benefit: The Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center, together with the Quanzhou Education Bureau, dissected six scam types aimed at teenagers. Their tricks differ, but the last step is the same move: getting the kid to transfer from a parent's phone. Type one: cheap private deals for game accounts and gear. Little Wang privately transferred 260 yuan; the other side said "minors transferring violates rules, your account will be frozen"; he clicked a "verification link" on his dad's phone and over 30 thousand yuan left the account. Type two: "lifting the anti-addiction limit". The other side threatened "if you don't transfer to cancel, your parent goes to jail"; the kid transferred 7,000 yuan from his mom's phone. The original states the anti-addiction system is officially set, with "absolutely no 'private unlock' channel". Type three: tipping refunds. Scammers pose as "official customer service", claim minors' tips can be refunded, and require "gifting a designated livestream room from a parent's Alipay to process the refund" - 73 thousand yuan left the mom's account in gifts, then the scammer blocked them. The original says "anyone who adds you and asks you to 'gift for a refund' is 100% a scam". Type four: impersonating classmates or teachers. Scammers hijack accounts and send voice messages naming names to borrow money. At back-to-school season they slip into class groups posing as teachers, posting "pay 500 yuan in fees", with the real teacher's avatar and name, plus shills saying "paid". Type five: celebrity fandom. Scammers pull kids into a "star fan group - claim an 888 yuan red packet". First the kid transfers 48.88 yuan "for verification". Then comes the scare: "a minor participating violates rules, the platform will freeze 80 thousand, your parent goes to jail". Total take: 16 thousand yuan. The original says "real police and lawyers never contact minors online for investigations, much less ask them to operate a parent's phone". Type six: low-priced goods on secondhand platforms that lead people off-platform for private transfers. The same material lists three red lines kids themselves must not cross. One: never lend, sell or rent your bank card or phone card - a card used to move criminal proceeds can constitute the crime of aiding information-network criminal activity, and a record affects graduate-school and civil-service applications. Two: "phone port" part-time work is illegal. Three: overseas "high-paying recruitment" - people can end up controlled the moment they land abroad (nationwide, October 2025)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center (2025). First lesson of the school year: anti-fraud is a required course for teenagers - avoid these 6 traps! <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202510/t20251028_7026836.htm> (reposted by the Fujian Provincial Public Security Department)
- Notes: Graded B because the official material offers scam-script dissections and individual cases, with no incidence rates or prevention-effect statistics. Keep this distinct from entry 9 (kids topping up and tipping on phones). Money a kid spends topping up or tipping themselves can be reclaimed under the Civil Code. Money scammed away cannot - that money can only be frozen by reporting to police per entry 2 of chapter 8, and whether it comes back depends on whether it's still in the account. The scammers' core script for scaring kids is "you broke the rules, your parent goes to jail". Telling your kid in advance that this sentence itself marks a scam works better than interrogating afterward. And "tell me first, I won't yell" has to be real: kids hiding scams out of fear of scolding is the shared reason losses snowballed in these cases. For the adult version of impersonation, including AI face-swapping, see entries 3 and 4 of chapter 8 (anti-fraud hard rules; hang up first when money transfers come up).

### 11. Decline extended warranties on electronics
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The price is: if something breaks after the warranty, you pay for the repair yourself - a low-probability event.
- TL;DR: Extended warranties typically sell for 10% to 50% of the product's price. The profit margin on selling them is a dozen-plus times that of ordinary goods. America's Best Buy made over half its profit from extended warranties back in 2003, while they were only 3-4% of revenue. That spread is the extra money you pay.
- Benefit: Extended warranties are typically priced at 10% to 50% of the product's original price. Industry estimates cited in the research put the average gross margin at 50% to 60% - of every 100 yuan sold, 50 to 60 is profit. That's about 18 times the margin on ordinary goods. In 2003, extended warranties were only 3-4% of Best Buy's revenue but contributed over 50% of its profit (US, 2000s retail data)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611-623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Notes: The margin figure is an industry estimate the paper cites from elsewhere (Business Week 2004), not something it measured. This entry fits ordinary people who can absorb a one-off repair bill. People whose livelihood depends on one device with no backup must do their own math. Phone screen-breakage insurance and the like also need separate math: estimate how often you drop your phone first, then check whether the premium pays.

### 12. Prefer generics that passed consistency evaluation and drugs selected in centralized procurement
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. When the doctor prescribes or you buy medicine, ask one extra question: "is there a centrally procured, generic-name version?"
- TL;DR: The second batch of centralized procurement cut prices 53% on average, up to 93% at most. By April 2026, 11 batches had run, covering 490 drugs. Asking that one question when getting prescriptions means the same ingredients for a lot less money.
- Benefit: National centralized drug procurement, second batch: average price cut 53%, maximum 93%. As of April 2026, 11 batches had run, covering 490 drugs. Drugs passing or deemed to pass consistency evaluation reached 1,695 - about two-thirds of commonly used clinical chemical drugs (China, official data 2020-2026)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Healthcare Security Administration (2020). Q&A on the second batch of national centralized drug procurement and use. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2020/1/17/art_38_2264.html> ; National Healthcare Security Administration (2026). State Council policy briefing: improving the full-cycle, full-channel drug price formation mechanism. <http://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/15/art_14_20221.html> ; General Office of the State Council (2016). Opinions on conducting consistency evaluation of generic drug quality and efficacy (Guobanfa [2016] No. 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2016-03/05/content_5049364.htm> ; China Food and Drug Administration (2016). Notice on issuing three technical guidelines including the selection and determination of reference preparations for ordinary oral solid dosage forms (No. 61 of 2016), Annexes 1 and 3, reposted by the Hainan Provincial Medical Products Administration. <https://amr.hainan.gov.cn/himpa/ztzl/fzy/201607/t20160720_1591587.html> ; National Healthcare Security Administration (2024). Strict quality supervision of procured drugs: two "full coverages" of company inspections and variety sampling. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2024/12/30/art_52_15287.html> ; National Healthcare Security Administration (2025). Officials from healthcare security and drug regulators interviewed by People's Daily Health Client on procured-drug issues. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2025/2/9/art_14_15647.html>
- Notes: Disputed: people often say "consistency evaluation compares ingredients, not efficacy". Half right. The evaluation gives the drug to healthy volunteers, draws blood to trace the concentration curve in the bloodstream, and compares it with the originator drug's curve - passing means landing within 80% to 125%. Drugs where a small dose difference causes trouble face stricter requirements. Excipients - the fillers and coatings beyond the active ingredient - may differ from the originator. The regulation requires only that the active ingredient, dosage form, route and therapeutic effect match. So it indeed is not "give it to patients for a while and see whether they improve". Patient outcomes are answered by a different line of research. The NHSA organized hospitals to do real-world studies: by December 2024 they covered 63 procured drugs, nearly a hundred top-tier hospitals, and over 300 thousand patients, with results showing no difference from originators. Metformin, for example: the share reaching the HbA1c target was 83.4% generic versus 78.9% originator; fasting-glucose target rates went the other way, 83.8% generic versus 87.6% originator - each side ahead on one measure. In January 2025, doctors publicly claimed procured drugs "don't lower blood pressure, anesthetics don't sedate, laxatives don't move". Healthcare security and drug regulators checked 7 Shanghai hospitals and concluded the claims mostly came from hearsay and personal impressions - the named laxative wasn't even in hospital use at the time. The same review did admit real problem drugs had been caught: of the 1,600-plus products selected across the first 9 batches, 9 had their qualification canceled over quality risks - 6 imported (3 of them originators) and 3 domestic. Two things you can act on. One, if you want to keep using an originator drug, you usually still can: centralized procurement covers only 60-80% of hospital purchasing, the rest is the hospital's own choice, originators aren't banned - the cost is paying more yourself. Two, after a chronic-disease medication switch, don't judge by feel - track blood pressure, blood sugar and HbA1c per entry 3 of chapter 16 (keep every measurement in one notebook). If things genuinely worsen, see the doctor and ask them to file an adverse drug reaction report - don't stop the medication on your own.

### 13. Set up family mutual-aid binding once in the healthcare insurance app, and your employee-insurance personal account can pay for your spouse, parents and children's care and medicine
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One family mutual-aid binding in your insured region's healthcare app, or at the handling agency.
- TL;DR: One binding, and the money in your employee medical insurance personal account can pay for your spouse, parents and kids' care and medicine. Without it, that money only ever works for you and just sits there.
- Benefit: The money in the personal account can pay the out-of-pocket share of medical costs for you, your spouse, parents and children at designated medical institutions. It can also pay the out-of-pocket share for drugs, medical devices and medical consumables at designated retail pharmacies. The document also proposes exploring the use of personal accounts to pay family members' individual premiums for urban-rural resident medical insurance
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2021). Guiding Opinions on Establishing and Improving the Outpatient Mutual-Aid Security Mechanism for Employee Basic Medical Insurance (Guobanfa [2021] No. 14), item (5): "The personal account is mainly used to pay the in-scope out-of-pocket costs incurred by the insured at designated medical institutions or designated retail pharmacies. ### 15. Don't trade stocks frequently
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is wanting to act on every piece of news and holding back.
- TL;DR: In data from 66 thousand American accounts, the most frequent traders averaged 11.4% a year while the whole market returned 17.9%. The 6-plus-point gap was eaten almost entirely by the transaction costs of buying and selling. Before those costs, the two groups earned about the same. Every move of the hand costs money.
- Benefit: The study looked at 66,465 household accounts at a US discount broker, 1991 to 1996. The most frequent traders earned 11.4% annualized - 11.4% a year on average. The whole market over the same period returned 17.9%, and the average household 16.4%. The average household's annual turnover was 75% - they swapped out three-quarters of their holdings in a year. The frequent and infrequent traders' gross returns - what they earned before costs - differed little. So the gap comes mainly from transaction costs paid while trading (US, 1991-1996)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Barber BM, Odean T (2000). Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth: The Common Stock Investment Performance of Individual Investors. The Journal of Finance 55(2):773-806. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226>
- Notes: This data is 1990s America. A-shares have a different commission and stamp-tax structure, but the logic - the higher the turnover, the lower the take-home return - is the same. Not investment advice.

### 16. Don't invest borrowed money, don't use leverage, don't buy what you don't understand
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->

### 17. Use broad index funds instead of actively managed funds for your long-term core position (the money you hold untouched)

### 18. Among funds of the same kind, pick the one with the lowest fees

### 19. Don't stake your money on one stock, one platform, or one apartment
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is accepting one thing: you won't happen to pick the single biggest winner.
- TL;DR: As long as several things don't all rise and fall in perfect lockstep, splitting money across them keeps the average return the same while the account's swings shrink. In reverse: everything on one stock, one platform or one apartment means when it blows up, everything you have blows up.
- Benefit: As long as several assets don't move in perfect unison, splitting money across them keeps expected return unchanged while reducing volatility - the average you earn stays the same, the up-and-down amplitude shrinks. That's the basic conclusion of Markowitz's 1952 portfolio selection, in use for decades since. Conversely, with everything on one stock or one platform, its variance is all of your variance - however it swings, you swing. When it blows up there is no hedge, nothing else to make it back
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77-91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Notes: Graded B because that classic paper is pure mathematical derivation - it computes no real number for "how much less diversification loses you". Diversification only shrinks swings; it doesn't guarantee against losses. Buying a broad index fund (entry 17 of this chapter) is itself the most effort-free way to diversify. Not investment advice.

### 24. Don't stockpile for "strikethrough prices" and big sales

### 37. Sell losing stocks by rules set in advance; don't average down to dig yourself out
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is admitting the position is already a loss, then selling it.
- TL;DR: Human instinct is to sell winners and ride losers. In seven years of records from 10,000 US accounts, winning stocks were over 50% more likely to be sold than losing ones. - Benefit: The study looked at every trade in 10,000 accounts at a US discount brokerage from 1987 to 1993. The proportion of gains realized (PGR) was 0.148. In other words, measured by the day, a winning stock was over 50% more likely to be sold than a losing one. The same accounts also added to losers more eagerly: the repurchase rate for losing stocks was 0.135, against 0.094 for winners, t = 19. The study also tested the idea that "the ones I keep will bounce back." The result ran the other way: the winners that were sold beat the losers that were kept by an average of 3.4 percentage points in excess returns over the following year. Excess return means the part above the market index. The author also worked an average-case example: selling $1,000 of a loser instead of $1,000 of a winner returned about 4.4% more over the next year. That 4.4% includes the tax saved by realizing the loss earlier, computed under the US tax code (United States, 1987 to 1993)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Odean T (1998). Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses? The Journal of Finance 53(5):1775-1798. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072>
- Notes: This entry governs "whether to sell"; entry 15 (don't trade stocks frequently) governs "keep your hands still" - the two don't conflict. Set rules in advance and follow them, and you actually trade less. Averaging down is not wrong arithmetic: buy more and your average cost really is lower. What's wrong is treating it as a way back to even: its real effect is to increase the money you have riding on that one stock - see entry 19 (don't bet everything on a single stock). The tax part follows the US tax code and doesn't transfer to China; the rest still holds. This is US account data from the 1980s-90s; A-share trading costs and tax rules differ. This is not investment advice.

### 38. Trade less during surges and crashes; don't scale up at the hottest moment
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is not piling in while everyone around you is making money.
- TL;DR: The Shanghai exchange ran the numbers on 40 million accounts through the 2014-2015 boom and bust. The 85% of households with the smallest accounts lost 250 billion yuan to frequent trading compared with simply holding on - about 30% of what their stocks were worth at the start. In the calm two and a half years before, that gap was only one to three percent.
- Benefit: The study used daily account-level data from the Shanghai Stock Exchange covering all roughly 40 million accounts. The sample period ran from July 2014 to December 2015, 18 months in which the Shanghai Composite first rose more than 150%. It peaked at 5166.35 on June 12, 2015, then fell 40% by the end of December. Household accounts were split into four tiers by starting market value. The smallest tier held 85% of household accounts; the largest held 0.5%. The two tiers started with similar total stock values, 880 billion and 808 billion yuan respectively. The result: the smallest 85% lost 250 billion yuan to active trading, while the largest 0.5% gained 254 billion yuan. "Lost" here is measured against holding without trading - doing nothing through the same market. Each figure equals roughly 30% of its group's starting stock value, 28% for the small accounts and 31% for the large ones. For comparison, the market was relatively calm in the two and a half years before June 2014: over any 18-month window in that period, the same gap was only 1% to 3% of starting value. During the boom, household accounts turned over their holdings about once every three weeks, nearly 18 times a year (Shanghai, China, 2014 to 2015; comparison period 2012 to 2014)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: An L, Lou D, Shi D (2022). Wealth redistribution in bubbles and crashes. Journal of Monetary Economics 126:134-153. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2022.01.001> ; the same authors' public working-paper version (October 2021, the figures in this entry were checked against it verbatim). <https://personal.lse.ac.uk/loud/AnLouShi.pdf>
- Notes: This entry does not say "don't buy stocks." It says that within the same market move, the more actively the small-account end trades, the more money flows to the other side of their trades - and "just holding on" is the study's baseline. The winners weren't institutions either, but the individuals with the largest accounts: about 90% of Shanghai exchange volume in this period came from retail accounts. The claim circulating online that "90% of retail investors lose money" has no verifiable official statistic behind it, so this entry doesn't use it. Turnover is worth comparing: households in this boom changed their holdings nearly 18 times a year, while the US households in entry 15 (don't trade stocks frequently) turned over only three-quarters of their portfolio in a year. This is not investment advice.
