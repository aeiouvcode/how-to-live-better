# 10. Is Dating and Marriage Worth It?

This chapter splits "is marriage worth it" into separate accounts, computed one by one: money, time, health, legal risk, and relationship quality. Each account uses only numbers with a checkable source - official statistics or meta-analyses (pooling multiple studies' data and recomputing). No moral judgments here, and no conclusion drawn for you. The health accounts run on mortality risk, the time accounts on minutes per day, the legal accounts on what the statutes write. These three computations don't convert into each other. The account-splitting method, the fill-in checklist and the common mistakes are in [docs/is-marriage-worth-it.md](../docs/is-marriage-worth-it.md).

### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on one: whether two people click can't be predicted before they meet
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Spend the time meeting more people, not chewing over the same one. The hard part is not fixating.
- TL;DR: In two speed-dating studies, people filled in 100-plus mate-preference questionnaires before meeting. The questionnaires could compute who generally likes people easily and who is generally popular - but not whether these two specific people would spark. Clicking is known only after meeting. So meeting more people beats betting everything on one.
- Benefit: In two speed-dating studies, participants filled in 100-plus self-report scales (questionnaires where you rate yourself) related to mate choice before meeting. The researchers used a random-forest model (a pattern-finding algorithm). It could compute "how easily a person generally likes others" - the questionnaires explained 4%-18% of the variance, variance being the differences between people, of which the questionnaires explain only a small part. And "how popular a person generally is" - 7%-27%. But "whether these two specific people spark" couldn't be computed - no combination of pre-meeting traits and preferences did it. The same researchers' earlier speed-dating experiment: participants reported their ideal-partner preferences beforehand - saying whether they valued looks or economic prospects. That preference didn't predict their interest in any specific person at the event.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478-1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580> ; Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245-264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: These two studies say "it can't be computed before meeting", not "no effort helps". Since clicking is known only after meeting, spend the time meeting more people - actually meeting each - rather than betting everything on one. The subjects were European and American college students. China's dating environment differs - discount when applying to yourself.

### 2. Stop after a clear rejection - continuing to pester is a public-security violation, not "sincerity"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free and no time. The hard part is holding back: no more messages, no more waiting around for them.
- TL;DR: The other person has clearly said no, and you keep messaging and waiting for them - from January 2026 that's a public-security violation: up to 5 days' detention or up to 1,000 yuan; graver, 5 to 10 days. Police can also order you not to contact them - violating that brings another 5 to 10 days.
- Benefit: The Public Security Administration Punishments Law effective January 1, 2026 lists pestering and stalking as their own item: "repeatedly sending obscene, insulting or threatening messages, or disturbing another's normal life by pestering, entangling, stalking or other means". It brings up to five days' detention or up to 1,000 yuan; graver circumstances, five to ten days plus a possible 1,000 yuan. One more layer: with approval from the police chief, the actor can be ordered not to contact the victim for a set period. Violating the no-contact order brings another five to ten days.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2025 revision). Public Security Administration Punishments Law (Article 50(1)(5), (2)). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (reposted by the SPP)
- Notes: "Pursuing to the end will move them" has no research support; detention and the no-contact order are written in black and white. The record left behind also affects civil-service and public-institution exams, political vetting, and qualifications for some professions. The line between pursuing someone and harassing them isn't drawn by what you think - it's drawn by whether they have clearly said no.

### 3. Judge interest by behavior, not "signals": who initiates, who arranges, who follows up
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free and little time. The hard part is changing the habit: stop parsing expressions and tones, start recording facts.
- TL;DR: 52 pairs of strangers chatted ten-odd minutes, then judged whether the other had been flirting. "The other wasn't flirting" was judged fairly accurately; "the other was flirting" couldn't be seen. Switching to 261 people judging videos gave the same conclusion. So don't guess expressions and tones - watch behavior: who initiates, who arranges the next meeting, whether they follow up when you don't push.
- Benefit: Two studies measured how accurately people detect flirting. The first had 52 pairs of unacquainted opposite-sex strangers talk 10 to 12 minutes, then each reported whether they themselves had flirted and whether they thought the other had. Result: "interactions where no flirting happened were judged more accurately; interactions where flirting happened were judged worse". The second used 26 one-minute videos judged by 261 observers - same conclusion. People can fairly reliably see "the other isn't flirting" but can barely see "the other is flirting".
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939-958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972> ; Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70-77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727> (the disputing side)
- Notes: Disputed. Other research holds that men don't overestimate women's sexual intent - women underreport their own intent on questionnaires. Who judges more accurately remains unsettled. But both camps agree on one thing: inferring the other's mind from subtle cues like expression and tone has very low accuracy. Visible behavior is far more reliable: whether they initiate, whether they arrange the next meeting, whether they follow up when you don't push.

### 4. Whether the relationship is good shows mainly in your own feelings inside it, not the other person's credentials
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free and little time. The hard part is changing the frame: put the mate checklist away and watch the getting-along instead.
- TL;DR: In an analysis of 43 paired longitudinal datasets from 29 labs, what best explains relationship quality is all your own feelings - like the commitment and appreciation you sense, and how much conflict there is. Self-reported measures predict two to four times better than partner-reported ones. But whether the relationship gets better or worse from here, no questionnaire can basically compute.
- Benefit: A machine-learning analysis of 43 longitudinal paired datasets from 29 labs - longitudinal meaning following the same couples over time. The strongest predictors of relationship quality all came from the person's feelings about the relationship: sensed commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, sensed partner satisfaction, and conflict. The strongest individual-level ones were life satisfaction, negative emotion, depression, avoidant attachment and anxious attachment. The relationship measures explained up to 45% of the variance at the study's start - 45% of the differences in relationship quality between couples - and up to 18% by its end. And the predictive power of "self-reported measures" was two to four times that of "partner-reported measures". Beyond the self-reported relationship feelings, adding individual differences and the partner's reports added no predictive power at all.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061-19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: The same study also found that whether the relationship gets better or worse next - its change during the study - basically can't be computed from any set of self-report measures. So the usable part of this entry: to judge whether the relationship right now is good, asking your own feelings beats checking the other side's credential list. But don't count on predicting where it goes. This says the same thing as this chapter's "emotional value - don't just ask whether it's there" entry; this one names the specific measures.

### 5. To get closer, take turns answering a set of questions that go from shallow to deep - don't learn scripts
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. It takes 45 minutes.
- TL;DR: Pair two strangers and have them take turns answering three sets of progressively deeper questions - 36 in all. After 45 minutes, the pair's closeness is clearly higher than a control group that made small talk for the same length of time. What works is taking turns telling truths, layer by layer deeper - not scripts. What it measured is only short-term closeness, not whether a long-term relationship follows.
- Benefit: Pairing strangers and having them take turns answering three progressively deeper sets of questions, 36 total - from "whom would you most want to invite to dinner" to "when did you last cry in front of another person". Closeness after 45 minutes was significantly higher than in the same-length small-talk control - and higher than that study's college students' average rating of their closest real-life relationship.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363-377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: One experiment only, college students, measuring short-term closeness - not whether a long-term relationship develops. What's usable is the mechanism: closeness comes from two people taking turns telling their real situation, layer by layer deeper - not scripts, routines, or one-sided performance. So the effective move in conversation is "I share something real, and ask something equally deep", taking turns.

### 6. Long-distance is no worse than same-city - the real test is the first three months after moving together
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=yes benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Travel money, plus the time on the road back and forth. You meet less often than same-city couples. The hard part is enduring not seeing each other for long stretches.
- TL;DR: Long-distance is actually more stable while apart, because meeting rarely makes it easy to imagine the other as better than reality. The real test comes after moving to the same city. In the tracking data, about half of long-distance couples made it to same-city; the other half ended while apart. Of the reunited half, a third broke up within 3 months.
- Benefit: Two studies give a consistent picture. Long-distance relationships were more stable than same-city ones while apart. The reason: long-distance relationships showed clearly more idealization - measured as idealization distortion, romantic love, relationship reminiscence and perceived agreement. And the less they met, the rosier the picture. But that stability holds only while the two remain apart. Once they turn same-city, former long-distance couples break up more easily. The study tracking the long-distance-to-same-city transition: about half of long-distance couples reached moving together; the other half ended while apart. Of the reunited half, a third broke up within 3 months of reunion. The longer the gaps between meetings and the stronger the idealization while apart, the less stable after reunion.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901-919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472> ; Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37-54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578> ; Dargie E et al. (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181-202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367> (the long-distance study in the notes)
- Notes: The subjects were American college students in dating relationships, no marriages, from some years back - don't apply the absolute proportions directly. Another study surveyed 717 long-distance and 425 same-city people: the quality difference between the two kinds of relationships was very small. Whether a relationship is good depends on the two people and the relationship itself, not the distance. The usable conclusion: don't use "long-distance always fails" as a reason against it. What to prepare for in advance is the few months after reunion - less autonomy space, more mutual knowledge, and more conflict and jealousy.

### 7. Read the registration data before listening to elders: what the marriage and divorce rates each measure
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Reading the marriage-registration section of the civil affairs annual bulletin takes minutes.
- TL;DR: In 2024, 6.106 million couples registered marriages nationwide - down 20% from the year before - and 3.513 million divorces were processed. The internet's "over half of marriages end in divorce" comes from dividing those two numbers: about 57.5%. But they aren't the same people - it can't be read as "half of marriages end". The bulletin's marriage rate of 4.3 per thousand and divorce rate of 2.5 per thousand use the whole population as denominator, not married people.
- Benefit: In 2024, 6.106 million couples lawfully registered marriages nationwide, down 20.5% from the previous year - marriage rate 4.3 per thousand. The same year, 3.513 million divorces were processed: 2.622 million registered at civil affairs departments and 891 thousand by court judgment or mediation - divorce rate 2.5 per thousand. The bulletin notes: marriage (divorce) rate = that year's marriages (divorces) / that year's average total population x 1000. These are "crude rates" - the denominator is the whole population, not married people.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs (2025). 2024 statistical bulletin on civil affairs development (section 5(2)1 marriage registration services, note 5), web and PDF versions. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html> and <https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>
- Notes: The internet's common "divorce rate over 50%" divides that year's divorces by that year's marriages: 351.3 / 610.6 ≈ 57.5%. That's called the "divorce-to-marriage ratio". The two numbers being divided aren't the same people, and the bulletin carries no such indicator - so it can't be read as "half of marriages end". Computing "the probability a marriage eventually ends in divorce" requires following the same cohort of married people over time - this chapter found no such official data.

### 8. Count the health benefits in, but discount them as observational data
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free and no time. Just remember one thing: these numbers say mortality is lower among married people - two things appearing together, not marriage making you live longer.
- TL;DR: Married people's probability of dying in the same period is about 12% lower than unmarried people's; never-married people's is about 24% higher than married people's. But these are two things appearing together, not marriage doing the work. People who are healthier, higher-earning and more social to begin with both marry more easily and live longer. The numbers come mostly from Europe and America - magnitude reference only for China.
- Benefit: One meta-analysis of the elderly - pooling and recomputing multiple studies - covered 53 comparison groups and over 250 thousand people. All-cause mortality for married versus unmarried: RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.85-0.91 (RR is the risk ratio, CI the plausible range) - about 12% lower probability of dying in the same period. In the same analysis: widowed 1.11, divorced or separated 1.16, never-married 1.11 - about 11%, 16% and 11% higher respectively. No sex difference. The most conservative estimate was RR 0.94 - only about 6% lower. Another meta-analysis compared never-married versus married across 95 papers and over 500 million people: HR 1.24 (1.19-1.30) - about 24% higher probability of dying in the same period, with the gap shrinking as age rises. A sex-split meta-analysis of 21 studies and 7.89 million people: in both sexes, being unmarried appeared together with total, cancer and cardiovascular mortality. The gaps in cardiovascular and total mortality were larger on the male side. Never-married men versus never-married women: 31% more stroke death, 9% more all-cause death.
- Evidence: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Soc Sci Med 64:77-94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031> ; Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. Am J Epidemiol 174(4):379-389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111> ; Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Glob Health Res Policy 5:4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: The dispute: married people were selected to begin with - healthier, higher-earning, more social people both marry more easily and live longer. All three meta-analyses used observational data from existing populations - records followed, no randomized groups. The authors themselves wrote of publication bias or methodological limits. Roelfs also found that the more rigorous the study, the smaller the computed gap. The numbers are mostly from European and American populations - magnitude reference only for China.

### 9. The time account runs on "unpaid labor" - settle the division of work before getting the certificate
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Before marriage, spend an hour or two talking housework, elder and child care, and whose career yields into an explicit division - then follow it. The hard part is laying it all on the table instead of letting it slide.
- TL;DR: In the national time-use survey, unpaid work like housework and family care averaged 1 hour 52 minutes a day for men and 3 hours 29 for women. The survey doesn't split by marital status, so there is no official figure for "how much more housework marriage adds" - the sex gap can only serve as an upper-bound reference. The division can be negotiated into a different shape before the certificate.
- Benefit: The 2024 national time-use survey. Unpaid labor means work without pay - housework, accompanying and caring for family. People who did such work that day averaged 2 hours 45 minutes: men 1 hour 52, women 3 hours 29. The share of people who did such work that day: men 67.5%, women 83.9%. Residents' housework averaged 1 hour 17 minutes a day; counting only those who did it, 1 hour 59 - 28 minutes less than 2018. Accompanying and caring for family averaged 30 minutes; counting only those who did it, 1 hour 46. The 2018 figures: housework - men 45 minutes, women 2 hours 6; accompanying and caring for children - men 17 minutes, women 53.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics (2024). Third national time-use survey bulletin (No. 2, No. 3). <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html> and <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html> ; National Bureau of Statistics (2019). 2018 national time-use survey bulletin. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: Both surveys grouped only by sex, urban-rural and age - not by marital status. So "how much more housework marriage adds" has no official figure; the sex gap serves only as an upper-bound reference. The numbers are national averages - the two of you can absolutely negotiate a different division. That is exactly the value of the talk.

### 10. For the money account, read the law's default rules first, then decide whether to agree in writing
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Read the property articles of the Civil Code's marriage-and-family book. If needed, the two of you sign a written property agreement - the law requires only written form, no notarization. The hard part is opening the money topic in advance.
- TL;DR: Without a written agreement, the law defaults to joint ownership of post-marriage wages and bonuses, business and investment gains, and inherited or gifted property. Premarital property, and property a will or gift contract states goes to one side only, stays personal. The two of you can agree in writing to keep things separate - signed, it binds. The side that did more child-raising and elder care can demand compensation at divorce.
- Benefit: The default rules without a separate agreement: post-marriage wages, bonuses and labor remuneration; production, business and investment gains; intellectual-property gains; inherited and gifted property - all jointly owned by the couple (Article 1062). Premarital property, personal-injury compensation, property a will or gift contract expressly assigns to one side, and one side's personal daily-use articles are personal property (Article 1063). The two sides can agree in writing that premarital and post-marriage property belongs to each separately, jointly, or partly each - the agreement legally binds both (Article 1065). The side bearing more duties in raising children, caring for elders or assisting the other's work has the right to demand compensation at divorce (Article 1088).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the PRC (effective January 1, 2021, Articles 1062, 1063, 1065, 1088). National Laws and Regulations Database. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf> ; full text reposted in the SPC Gazette. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>
- Notes: Betrothal gifts, weddings and marital housing have no official statistics - no numbers written here. How much the Article 1088 compensation is: negotiated by the two first, set by the court if that fails - no fixed formula. A written property agreement serves both sides: the higher earner can use it, and so can the side doing more unpaid housework and care.

### 11. When parents fund the home purchase, write down whether it's a loan or a gift on the day of the transfer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. On the day of the transfer, write one page stating whether the money is a gift to whom or a loan to whom, signed by the contributing parents and the child. If it says loan, have both spouses sign. The transfer note says the same sentence.
- TL;DR: Parents funding a home purchase before marriage defaults to a gift to their own child alone. Money contributed after marriage without an agreement is handled as joint property. From February 2025: where one side's parents funded it fully during marriage and the gift contract didn't state it goes only to their own child, at divorce the court can award the home to the funders' child, then decide whether and how much to compensate the other side. To treat it as a loan, write it clearly that day with both sides signing.
- Benefit: Parents contributing to the couple's home purchase before marriage is recognized as a gift to their own child personally - unless they expressly state it's a gift to both. Contributions after marriage follow the agreement; without one, or with an unclear one, the joint-property principle applies. One more rule from February 1, 2025: where one side's parents funded it fully during marriage and the gift contract didn't state it goes only to their own child, at divorce division the court may award the home to the funders' child. After awarding, it weighs several circumstances to decide whether to compensate the other side and how much: the life together and children born, fault in the divorce, contributions to the family, and the home's market price at divorce. Where one side's parents contributed partly, or both sides' parents contributed, the award follows the sources and proportions of funding, with reasonable compensation (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court (2020). Interpretation (I) on applying the Civil Code's marriage and family book (Fa Shi [2020] No. 22, Article 29). <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html> ; Supreme People's Court (2025). Interpretation (II) on applying the Civil Code's marriage and family book (Fa Shi [2025] No. 1, Article 8). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452771.html> ; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 1062(1)(4)). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: This entry serves both sides. Contributing parents no longer need to count on an IOU produced at divorce to protect their contribution. After Interpretation (II) took effect, whose money it was is itself the basis for dividing the home - the name on the deed no longer decides ownership. The receiving side also no longer waits until divorce to learn what the money counts as. To treat it as a loan, really treat it as one: both sides agree it's a loan, agree how it's repaid, write it on the transfer, and keep repayment records where needed. Missing any piece means explaining it in court.

### 12. A large debt your spouse borrowed without your signature or later ratification doesn't automatically become yours
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Don't sign "co-borrower" or "guarantor" on your spouse's IOUs and guarantees. And don't write things like "we will repay this money" in chats - it can be read as your after-the-fact ratification of the debt. The hard part is not engaging when being dunned.
- TL;DR: Money your spouse borrowed behind your back, clearly beyond the household's daily needs, without your signature or after-the-fact ratification, doesn't automatically become your debt. The creditor must prove the money went into your life together or a business together. But money like a home purchase, if it genuinely became the home you live in or own together, is often provable to that layer.
- Benefit: These kinds of borrowed money count as joint marital debt: signed jointly by both spouses; ratified afterward by one side - acknowledging the debt later so both stand behind it; or borrowed by one side in their own name during the marriage for the household's daily needs. Money borrowed by one side in their own name, clearly beyond daily household needs, is not joint marital debt - except where the creditor can prove the money went into the couple's life together, production or business together, or was acknowledged by both. Home-purchase money clearly exceeds daily needs, so the burden of proof sits with the claiming creditor - they must produce the evidence themselves. But in reverse, if the money genuinely became the home the couple lives in or owns together, the creditor can often prove the "used in the couple's life together" layer. So what genuinely works is the step in entry 11 of this chapter (write down loan or gift when parents contribute) (nationwide, from 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 1064). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court (2020, second amendment). Rules on applying law in private-lending cases (Articles 15, 16). <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/94b6623974526df7d2430a3c73f050.html> (SPC Gazette)
- Notes: When dividing property at divorce and the other side's parents suddenly produce a loan agreement bearing only the spouse's signature, look in two steps. Step one: did the loan genuinely happen. If you say no money ever changed hands and can make the reasoning hold, the court weighs several things together - the amount, how it was delivered, both sides' finances, how the parties usually transacted, whether assets moved accordingly, what witnesses say. If the other side produces only a transfer record, they still bear proving the loan stands. Step two: once the loan stands, whether it's joint debt. The general rules for IOUs and guarantees: entry 18 of chapter 8.

### 13. Registering a marriage takes an ID card plus one signed declaration - no household book, no fee, certificate issued on the spot
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Both people appear in person. The whole thing usually takes tens of minutes.
- TL;DR: From May 2025, registering takes two things: your ID card, plus a signed declaration stating you have no spouse and no direct-blood or three-generation collateral-blood relation to the other party. No household book, and no trip to either side's registration place. Registration charges nothing; if you qualify, the certificate is issued on the spot.
- Benefit: The new Marriage Registration Regulation effective May 10, 2025 changed what mainland residents submit to marry. The text says only two things: "(1) the person's resident identity card; (2) the person's signed declaration of having no spouse and no direct-blood or three-generation collateral-blood relationship with the other party". The household book is no longer required. The registering organ is "the civil affairs department of the county people's government, or the township people's government designated by the province, autonomous region or province-level city on convenience principles". The old rule's limit of "going to the place of one party's permanent household registration" is removed. The regulation also writes "marriage registration organs handling marriage registration may not charge fees", and "for parties meeting the marriage conditions, registration shall be done on the spot and the marriage certificate issued".
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2025). Marriage Registration Regulation (State Council Order No. 804, Articles 2, 6, 8, 10). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017752.htm>
- Notes: Registration is refused in only four cases: under the legal marriage age, not fully voluntary on both sides, one or both already having a spouse, or the two being direct blood relatives or collateral within three generations. What may charge is only peripheral services like photos and photocopies. Registration itself charges nothing - refuse and complain if you meet a "document fee" or "expedite fee". The 30-day cooling-off period for divorce registration: this chapter's exit-cost entry.

### 14. Do a premarital health check before marrying - a major disease must be truthfully disclosed to the other side before registration
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free or reduced for people of marriageable age in most regions; a few hundred yuan if paid yourself. It takes half a day.
- TL;DR: Having a major disease and not truthfully disclosing it before registration gives the other side the right to ask a court to annul the marriage - filed within one year of learning of it. The premarital check itself hasn't been a prerequisite for the certificate since 2003 - voluntary. Cost depends on local policy; half a day.

### 15. For emotional value, don't just ask "is it there" - look at relationship quality
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Judge relationship quality across a few conflicts and a few months of getting along before marriage - don't just check the credential list. The hard part is not judging by the list.
- TL;DR: A pooling of 126 studies and over 72 thousand people shows higher marital quality goes with better health. But the association is weak - about the magnitude of diet's effect on health. The usable part: judge whether the relationship is good across a few conflicts and a few months of getting along, not by checking a credential list. Whether "a bad marriage is worse than not marrying" has no directly comparing data.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 126 studies and over 72 thousand people shows higher marital quality goes with better health. Effect sizes r run 0.07 to 0.21 - r measures how two things move together, 0 being no relation and 1 perfect synchrony. Mortality risk r = 0.11; cardiovascular response during conflict r = -0.13. That magnitude is comparable to the health effect of health behaviors like diet. Studies explicitly testing sex differences basically found none. A cardiovascular review notes marital dissatisfaction and marital quality significantly affect cardiovascular risk.
- Evidence: B (disputed)
- Sources: Robles TF, et al. (2014). Marital quality and health: a meta-analytic review. Psychol Bull 140(1):140-187. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031859> ; Dhindsa DS, et al. (2020). Marital status and outcomes in patients with cardiovascular disease. Trends Cardiovasc Med 30(4):215-220. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcm.2019.05.012>
- Notes: The dispute: the association itself is weak. Some results carry publication bias. And most studies surveyed at one point in time - which causes which can't be seen. Robles' original lists these limits itself. This chapter found no meta-analysis directly comparing mortality between "low-quality marriage" and "unmarried", so "a bad marriage is worse than not marrying" is currently an inference, not a numerical conclusion.

### 16. Compute the exit cost: agreed divorce has a 30-day cooling-off period, litigated divorce has statutory conditions
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before marriage, spend some time reading: if you ever need to exit, how long it takes and through what procedure. The hard part is thinking about parting before marrying.
- TL;DR: Agreed divorce isn't done on signing. For 30 days after filing, either side can withdraw. After that, both must collect the divorce certificate in person within 30 days - not collecting counts as withdrawal. If the other side disagrees, the only road is suing. The court mediates first; only with circumstances like domestic violence, bigamy, or two years of separation, where mediation fails, does it grant the divorce.
- Benefit: Agreed divorce requires a signed written divorce agreement and a personal application at the marriage registration organ (Article 1076). For 30 days from the organ receiving the application, either side may withdraw. Within 30 days after that period ends, both must collect the divorce certificate in person - not collecting counts as withdrawal (Article 1077). When one side disagrees, the road is litigation, and the court mediates first. With bigamy or cohabitation with another, domestic violence or abuse or abandonment, gambling or drug use uncurbed after repeated admonition, or two years of separation over incompatibility - where mediation fails, divorce shall be granted. After a judgment against divorce followed by another full year of separation, a new suit shall be granted (Article 1079). In 2024, 3.513 million couples divorced nationwide, 891 thousand of them by court judgment or mediation.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the PRC (Articles 1076, 1077, 1079). <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html> ; Ministry of Civil Affairs (2020). Notice on implementing the marriage-registration provisions of the Civil Code (Min Fa [2020] No. 116). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-12/04/content_5567010.htm> ; Ministry of Civil Affairs (2025). 2024 statistical bulletin on civil affairs development. Same listing as entry 7 (what the marriage and divorce rates each measure).
- Notes: The cooling-off period applies only to agreed divorce. Litigated divorce has no cooling-off, but a longer cycle. Litigation and lawyer fees differ by place - no numbers written here. A high exit cost is not a reason not to marry - but it should be known before marrying.

### 17. Keep a separate account for "marrying for the elders" - don't merge it with your own
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Write two columns on paper. One: which gains in this marriage are yours - computable from this chapter's earlier entries. The other: which gains are the elders' - peace of mind, face, the pressure stopping. The hard part is keeping the elders' column separate, not folding it into yours.
- TL;DR: The elders' share of the gains - peace of mind, face, the end of nagging - is real. But it doesn't automatically turn into your health benefit or relationship quality. And the time account, the money account and the exit cost are all borne by you alone. Two columns on paper - which gains are yours, which are theirs - and whether the account pays shows at a glance.
- Benefit: The elders' gains are real. But they don't automatically convert into your health benefit (entry 8) or relationship quality (entry 15). And the time account (entry 9), the money account (entries 10 to 12) and the exit cost (entry 16) are all borne by the person in the marriage. Once the accounts are kept separately, whether "checking-a-box marriage" is good value shows at a glance - nobody needs to judge it for you.
- Evidence: C
