# 9. Legal Red Lines Ordinary People Easily Cross

This chapter counts two accounts: your personal freedom, and your money. The things below, ordinary people often assume are "no big deal" - they can actually bring detention, fines, and at the heavy end a prison sentence. Each entry gives the specific numbers in the statutes and cases published through official channels. The Public Security Administration Punishments Law cited here is the 2025 revision, effective January 1, 2026 - its article numbers differ from the old law. The Criminal Law is quoted from the consolidated text reposted by the Beijing Traffic Management Bureau - the version "amended by Criminal Law Amendment (XI)" - with the corresponding amendment noted in the text. Entry order: from "easiest to step on by accident" plus "heaviest consequences", high to low. This chapter is China-specific: the statutes, hotlines and agencies named are China's.

### 1. Don't forward unverified disaster, epidemic, or police-incident news in group chats; no edited images, no AI-fabricated scene photos
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before forwarding, take one look at whether it's an official notice. When unsure, don't forward - and don't edit the image or add "allegedly". The hard part is resisting the urge to be the first one with the news.
- TL;DR: For disaster, epidemic or police-incident news, forwarding while knowing it's fake is punished as heavily as inventing it yourself. Even if you didn't invent it: 5 to 10 days' detention plus a 1,000 yuan fine. Seriously disturbing social order: up to 3 years; serious consequences, 3 to 7. Edited images and AI-fabricated scene photos count as inventing it yourself.
- Benefit: Deliberately spreading rumors, falsely reporting dangers, epidemics, disasters or police incidents, and disturbing public order: 5 to 10 days' detention, plus a possible fine up to 1,000 yuan. The lighter tier: up to 5 days' detention or up to 1,000 yuan. Fabricating false dangers, epidemics, disasters or police incidents and putting them online: up to 3 years. Knowingly spreading them anyway is the same tier - the premise being serious disturbance of social order, in the statute's words. Causing serious consequences: 3 to 7 years (nationwide)

### 2. Don't post or forward images, memes or comments insulting martyrs and heroes, and don't joke with the national flag or anthem
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Content touching martyrs and heroes, the national flag, emblem or anthem: no commenting, no editing, no forwarding. The hard part is not chiming in when the group starts joking.
- TL;DR: Joking with martyrs, the flag or the anthem: at the light end, 5 to 10 days' detention or a 1,000-to-3,000 yuan fine; heavier, 10 to 15 days. Constituting a crime: up to 3 years. One person posted 2 messages defaming Yuan Longping in a 499-member village WeChat group - sentenced to 6 months of public surveillance: not jailed, but movement restricted.
- Benefit: Infringing the name, image, reputation or honor of heroes and martyrs by insults or fabricated facts, harming the public interest, is punished at this tier: 5 to 10 days' detention or a 1,000-to-3,000 yuan fine. Blaspheming or denying martyrs' deeds and spirit, or making and spreading speech, images, audio or video glorifying wars of aggression, disturbing public order - the same tier. Graver circumstances: 10 to 15 days, plus a possible fine up to 5,000 yuan. At "serious circumstances" it becomes a crime - up to 3 years. Publicly insulting the national flag or emblem, or altering the anthem's words or tune or singing it in a distorted or derogatory way, where serious, also up to 3 years. Official cases: Xiao posted 2 messages defaming and insulting Yuan Longping in a 499-member village WeChat group - 6 months of public surveillance (a sentence restricting movement without imprisonment). Qiu, with 2.5-million-plus Weibo followers, posted 2 items distorting the deeds of border-defense soldiers - 8 months, plus a public apology in national media (nationwide)

### 3. What you see on overseas sites, close when done - no screenshots, no forwarding, no carrying into group chats, no chiming in in comment sections
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Close the page and the matter ends. No archiving, no forwarding, no carrying onto domestic platforms. Don't use accounts linkable to your identity to post political content on overseas platforms long-term, and don't accept fees or funding from overseas organizations or individuals. The hard part is not screenshotting or sharing when something glaring appears.
- TL;DR: These statutes punish spreading outward, not viewing. One category is an exception: books and audio-video advocating terrorism or extremism - knowingly keeping them in your hands, where circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years for possession alone. For everything else, your forwarding, carrying into a group, or chiming in in comments all count as dissemination during case handling. Reaching the tier of inciting subversion or inciting separatism: up to 5 years; ringleaders, 5 years or more.
- Benefit: Using rumors, defamation or other means to incite others to subvert state power or overthrow the socialist system: up to 5 years. Ringleaders (the statute's term) or those whose crimes are major: 5 years or more, fixed-term up to 15. Inciting the splitting of the country or the undermining of national unity: the same terms. Colluding with overseas institutions, organizations or individuals to do either: punished heavily. Where institutions, organizations or individuals inside or outside China fund others to do them, the directly responsible people get up to 5 years, or 5 or more where serious. Stealing, spying out, buying or unlawfully providing state secrets or intelligence for overseas institutions, organizations or people: 5 to 10 years; especially serious, 10 years to life; relatively minor, up to 5. Crimes endangering national security all carry an additional deprivation of political rights for 1 to 5 years - voting rights and eligibility for public office both go for those years. Confiscation of property may also be imposed - personal property taken into state hands. The administrative side has rules too. No individual or organization may use the network to endanger national security, honor or interests; nor to incite subversion of state power or overthrow of the socialist system; nor to incite splitting the country or undermining national unity. Publishing or transmitting such information is punished under relevant laws and administrative regulations (nationwide). One more category is complete without any dissemination: knowingly and unlawfully holding items advocating terrorism or extremism - books, audio-video materials or other articles - where circumstances are serious, is enough for conviction: up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention (short-term custody) or public surveillance (unconfined but movement-restricted), plus a fine or a fine alone. The basis is Criminal Law Article 120f, added by Amendment IX.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Articles 45, 55, 56, 103, 105, 106, 107, 111, 113 - these articles unmodified by amendments). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; NPC Standing Committee (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 amendment, effective January 1, 2026, Article 13(2), Article 71(1)). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; NPC Standing Committee (2015). Full text reposted by the National Public Complaints and Proposals Administration. <https://www.gjxfj.gov.cn/gjxfj/fgwj/flfg/webinfo/2017/03/1601761496614423.htm>
- Notes: The statutes quoted in this entry punish inciting others, spreading outward, and providing to others - not "viewing". But that sentence covers only ordinary content. Terrorism and extremism materials are the statute's written exception - possession alone can convict (Article 120f). For that category, "don't archive" is not cautious advice but a line not to touch. When you see it, it ends there: no screenshots, no forwarding, no commenting, no pulling others to forward. Forwarding and chiming in in comments both count as dissemination in case handling - "I only forwarded it" doesn't exempt. Content from overseas platforms can also aggravate punishment. When unsure whether something is real, still less should you forward it - same as entry 1 of this chapter (don't forward the unverified). How circumvention tools themselves are punished: entry 11 of chapter 11. Do the math: forwarding this kind of content buys nothing real; the price is a prison term plus deprivation of political rights plus confiscation of property. Publicly findable similar cases appear only in overseas media and documents published by families - for instance a blogger who long posted political commentary on overseas platforms was sentenced to 7 years in 2023. This book obtained no judgment verifiable through official channels, so this entry follows the statutes alone, with no case attached.

### 4. Adult videos are your own affair in private - don't post them to groups, don't sell "resources", don't run groups
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. No forwarding, no stashing in group files, and no being the owner or admin of such groups.
- TL;DR: Watching yourself isn't in the punishment provisions. Posting to a group once counts as disseminating to the whole group: 10 to 15 days' detention plus up to 5,000 yuan; serious circumstances, up to 2 years. Selling "resources" for money: up to 3 years; serious, 3 to 10; heaviest, 10 years to life. Running a dissemination group of 30 or more members: the founder, the admins and the main posters are all treated as criminals.
- Benefit: Disseminating obscene information online: 10 to 15 days' detention plus a possible fine up to 5,000 yuan. The lighter tier: up to 5 days or 1,000 to 3,000 yuan. Content involving minors: punished heavily. Disseminating obscene materials at "serious circumstances": up to 2 years. Selling or disseminating for profit: up to 3 years plus a fine; serious circumstances, 3 to 10; especially serious, 10 years to life. Founding a group mainly used to disseminate obscene electronic information with 30 or more members is handled as a crime, as is causing serious consequences. Founders, admins and main posters are all convicted of disseminating obscene materials (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2025). Public Security Administration Punishments Law (2025 revision, Article 80). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> ; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (amended by Amendment XI, Articles 363, 364). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2010). Interpretation (II) on specific application of law in criminal cases of producing, copying, publishing, selling or disseminating obscene electronic information via the internet, mobile terminals or voice services (Article 3). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201002/t20100204_25001.shtml>
- Notes: Both laws punish "dissemination" - private viewing isn't in the provisions. But posting to a group once counts as disseminating to the whole group. What quantities make "serious circumstances" is set by judicial interpretation; this chapter hasn't verified the specific numbers.

### 5. A "side job" that has you receiving, withdrawing or transferring money through your own card - don't do it at any fee
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Give up this kind of "few hundred a day, bring your card to the bank" work.
- TL;DR: These side jobs earn a few hundred yuan; the price is a criminal record. One person took his ID and 1 bank card to a designated spot and cooperated in transfers and withdrawals, earning 500-some yuan. The procuratorate didn't prosecute him; police still detained him 10 days and fined him 1,000 yuan. Another handed his own and others' bank cards and SIM cards to a ring for receiving and withdrawing money: 2 years 3 months, plus a 22,000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Knowing money and goods come from crime and still helping transfer, buy, sell or otherwise conceal them: up to 3 years, plus a fine or a fine alone. Official cases: Wang, on instructions, took his ID and 1 bank card to a designated spot and cooperated in transfers and withdrawals, earning 500-some yuan. The procuratorate didn't prosecute; police, under the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, detained him 10 days and fined him 1,000 yuan. Man handed his own and others' bank cards and SIM cards to a ring for receiving, transferring and withdrawing money: 2 years 3 months plus a 22,000 yuan fine (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (amended by Amendment XI, Article 312). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023). Typical cases of procuratorates punishing telecom and online fraud and related crimes (case 9). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202311/t20231130_635188.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Typical cases of punishing the crime of concealing or disguising criminal proceeds and their gains (case 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202508/t20250825_704514.shtml>
- Notes: The consequences of lending your bank card or helping "run points": entry 8 of chapter 8 (lending bank cards). This entry covers doing the withdrawals and transfers yourself. "I didn't know where the money came from" isn't necessarily accepted during case handling. Absurdly high pay, face-scan cooperation demanded, transactions abnormal by any measure - these suffice to find you actually knew. The lightest outcome is still 10 days' detention plus a criminal record.

### 6. If someone recruits you to "package materials" for loans and pays you a cut of the loan amount - none of it
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Refuse outright. Don't lend your ID card or credit record, don't sign loan contracts you haven't read, and don't go to the bank to sign in person for someone else.
- TL;DR: Helping "package materials" to get loans and taking a cut is handled as fraud. A 126-person ring packaged jobless people into "premium customers" and defrauded over 80 banks of more than 30 million yuan. The front-men who signed took a 30% cut of the loan; of the 80 sentenced so far, 76 got 1 year 4 months to 6 years 6 months. Whose name the loan is in, whose the debt is.
- Benefit: Using deception to obtain loans, bill acceptance, letters of credit or guarantees from banks or other financial institutions, causing heavy losses to the institution: up to 3 years, plus a fine or a fine alone. Especially heavy losses or other especially serious circumstances: 3 to 7 years plus a fine. The police filing threshold is direct economic loss of 500,000 yuan or more. Having never intended to repay and using fabricated fund-introduction projects, fake economic contracts, fake certification documents or fake property certificates to defraud loans is the crime of loan fraud: 50,000 yuan is enough to file - up to 5 years plus a 20,000-to-200,000-yuan fine; huge amounts or other serious circumstances, 5 to 10 years; especially huge, 10 years to life. Forging, altering or trading official documents, certificates or seals of state organs: up to 3 years; serious circumstances, 3 to 10. The official case: a 126-person ring packaged jobless people into "premium customers" across 16 provinces and cities, defrauding over 30 million yuan at 80-plus bank branches. The fronting "clean-record" borrowers took a 30% cut of the loan amount. The procuratorate pursued 105 of them; of the 80 sentenced at first instance by 2026, 76 got 1 year 4 months to 6 years 6 months for loan fraud, plus fines. Another 4, for withdrawing money for the ring, were sentenced for loan fraud and money laundering combined - the two crimes' terms computed together - 2 years 4 months to 7 years 6 months (nationwide). The crime of fraudulently obtaining loans in this entry follows the Criminal Law Amendment (XI) text from March 2021.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2020). Criminal Law Amendment (XI) (Article 11, amending Criminal Law Article 175a). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202012/t20201227_503700.shtml> ; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Articles 88, 193, 280). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate and Ministry of Public Security (2022). Rules on filing and prosecution standards for criminal cases under police jurisdiction (II) (Articles 22, 45). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202204/t20220429_555906.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Money-laundering hands behind the loan-fraud assembly line (Procuratorial Daily). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202604/t20260428_726494.shtml> ; NPC Standing Committee (2012). Exit and Entry Administration Law (Article 12). <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html> (National Immigration Administration) ; NPC Standing Committee (2023 amendment). Civil Procedure Law (Article 266). <https://amr.guizhou.gov.cn/zwgk/xxgkml/jcxxgk/zcfg/fl/202401/t20240129_83645867.html> (reposted by Guizhou AMR)
- Notes: "I meant to repay at the time" was not accepted in that case. The procuratorate found these people had no jobs and no fixed assets, couldn't possibly repay, and had spent their cuts - and on that basis found they never intended to repay. Withdrawing money for the ring can add a money-laundering charge. Whose name the loan is in and whose signature is on the contract - that person is the borrower. Even short of a crime, the debt and the credit record are yours. Taking the money and fleeing abroad doesn't end it either: hiding after a case is filed, it can be pursued however many years pass. Those who haven't finished serving a sentence may not leave the country. Those currently treated as criminal suspects or defendants may not either. With a civil case unresolved, the court can bar your exit; a judgment debtor who won't perform can also be exit-restricted. Withdrawing and transferring for others: entry 5 of this chapter (a "side job" using your own card). How to discover a loan taken out in your name: entry 9 of chapter 8.

### 7. A found bank card is not for withdrawing; a found phone or wallet goes back to its owner or to the police - not into your pocket
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, a matter of minutes. Call the owner, or take the thing to the police station.
- TL;DR: Keeping found things for yourself, refusing to hand them over when the amount is "relatively large": up to 2 years; huge amounts, 2 to 5. Using a found bank card at an ATM is heavier - it counts as fraudulently using another's credit card: a relatively large amount brings up to 5 years plus a 20,000-to-200,000-yuan fine. Withdrawing is the kind police file directly - the owner doesn't have to sue.
- Benefit: Something another person lost must be returned - promptly notify them to collect it, or hand it to police or other departments. Keeping what another left behind for yourself, refusing to hand it over when the amount is "relatively large": the crime of embezzlement, up to 2 years; huge amounts, 2 to 5. Using a found credit card at an ATM is "fraudulently using another's credit card". At criminal level it's handled as credit-card fraud: a relatively large amount, up to 5 years plus a 20,000-to-200,000-yuan fine; a huge amount or other serious circumstances, 5 to 10 years plus 50,000 to 500,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 314). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> ; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (amended by Amendment XI, Articles 196, 270). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2008). Reply on how to characterize using a found credit card at an ATM. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/flfg/sfjs/201208/t20120830_47871.shtml>
- Notes: Embezzlement is "handled only upon complaint" - the owner must sue in court themselves; police don't act on their own. But withdrawing with a found card is different: police file directly, no owner complaint needed. What amount counts as "relatively large" is set by judicial interpretation; this chapter hasn't verified it. After handing the item to the relevant department, the owner pays the safekeeping costs when collecting it (Civil Code Article 317).

### 8. Claim discounts and coupons under your real identity - no bulk-registering alt accounts with virtual numbers, no exploiting loopholes to claim repeatedly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Give up the work in "deal-task groups". If you spot a platform loophole, don't touch it. The hard part is not treating a discount as income that can be replicated in bulk.
- TL;DR: Using your real identity, one account, one coupon - outside the punishment scope. Bulk-registering fake users to claim more, placing and accepting your own orders - handled as fraud: 3,000 yuan defrauded can bring up to 3 years; from 30,000 yuan, 3 to 10. A delivery rider used 0.1-yuan virtual numbers to farm coupons and cash out over 90,000 yuan; the 12 people in his case got from 3 years 6 months down to 3 months' detention.
- Benefit: Defrauding others or the public of property in a relatively large amount: up to 3 years, plus a fine or a fine alone. A huge amount or other serious circumstances: 3 to 10 years plus a fine. An especially huge amount or other especially serious circumstances: 10 years to life, plus a fine or confiscation of property. The three tiers - relatively large, huge, especially huge - are set by each province within these ranges: 3,000 to 10,000 yuan or more; 30,000 to 100,000 or more; 500,000 or more. Teaching the method to others or selling "tutorials": the crime of teaching criminal methods, up to 5 years; serious circumstances, 5 to 10; especially serious, 10 years to life. Buying and selling phone numbers and verification codes upstream and downstream: the crime of infringing citizens' personal information, up to 3 years; especially serious, 3 to 7. Bulk accounts, automatic network-address switching systems, and code-receiving platforms that bulk-receive SMS verification are expressly banned by Article 14 of the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law. Illegally trading, renting or lending phone cards and internet accounts, and helping others pass real-name verification, are under Article 31. Violating Article 14: all gains confiscated, plus a fine of 1 to 10 times the gains; with no gains or gains under 50,000 yuan, a fine up to 500,000 yuan, plus up to 15 days' detention for serious circumstances. Violating Article 31: the same 1-to-10-times fine, or up to 200,000 yuan with no gains or gains under 20,000 yuan, plus up to 15 days' detention for serious circumstances. Participating in telecom or online fraud short of a criminal sentence: 10 to 15 days' detention, gains confiscated, plus a 1-to-10-times fine; with no gains or gains under 10,000 yuan, a fine up to 100,000 yuan. Official case one: delivery rider Liu bought virtual phone numbers at 0.1 yuan each and registered new platform users. He claimed the 29-minus-15 new-user coupons to place orders, then resold the goods to small shops to cash out. He also used virtual numbers to invite each other for 2-to-20-yuan referral bonuses. He and rider Tan defrauded 90,000-plus and 70,000-plus yuan respectively. Shanghai Yangpu procuratorate prosecuted 12 people for fraud; the court sentenced from 3 years 6 months to 3 months' detention. Official case two: Wang exploited the time gap between online-shop refunds and the clawback of virtual lottery coins: 1,156 refunds, 224 figures taken, 249,000-plus yuan, cashed out on a secondhand platform. The court sentenced him for fraud to 3 years suspended for 4, plus a 30,000 yuan fine; during trial he returned the proceeds and prepaid fines totaling 330,000 yuan. Official case three: a ride-hailing platform's new-user coupons and driver subsidies were defrauded through fake "drive yourself, accept your own order" runs, spanning 19 provinces and 70-plus cities, over 2 million yuan. Chaoyang Longcheng procuratorate in Liaoning prosecuted 163 people, all convicted. Ringleaders Huang and Cai got 3 years 6 months each, combining fraud, infringing citizens' personal information, and teaching criminal methods. Another 34 minor accomplices were not prosecuted by the procuratorate but still sent to police for administrative punishment (nationwide)
- Evidence: A

Criminal Law (amended by Amendment XI, Articles 253a, 266, 295). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> (reposted by Beijing Traffic Management Bureau) ; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011). Joining an activity under your real identity, one account one coupon, the coupon spent on yourself - that's a discount the platform gives you. Selling cards and accounts: entry 8 of chapter 8 (lending bank cards) and entry 16 of this chapter (don't lend your ID).

### 9. Throw nothing off balconies or out windows - cigarette butts, garbage bags and bottles all count
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free and no time. Cigarette butts, garbage bags and bottles go to the bin inside - never out the window or off the balcony.
- TL;DR: Throwing things downstairs, even hitting nobody: up to 5 days' detention and 1,000 yuan; serious circumstances, 10 to 15 days. Hitting someone changes the charge: one person, drunk on the 21st floor, threw empty bottles and glasses onto the playground below, seriously injuring a 13-year-old student - 10 years. Another threw 8 bricks from the 32nd floor and killed 1 person - death sentence approved in 2024.
- Benefit: Throwing objects from a building or other height, where there's a danger of hurting people, damaging property or endangering public safety: up to 5 days' detention or up to 1,000 yuan; serious circumstances, 10 to 15 days plus a possible 1,000 yuan. ### 21. Don't stage accidents or inflate losses to defraud claims - that's insurance fraud, and the people who testify, repair or appraise for you are counted with you
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free and no time. What this entry does is just not doing it.
- TL;DR: Fabricating accidents or inflating losses to defraud claims is the crime of insurance fraud. A relatively large amount: up to 5 years plus a 10,000-to-100,000-yuan fine. "Getting help" doesn't exempt either: people who testify, appraise or assess property for you and deliberately produce false certificates are convicted with you as accomplices. In the Xuancheng, Anhui case, Wang and 7 others got 4 years to 2 years, each fined 10,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Criminal Law Article 198 lists five circumstances: (1) the policyholder deliberately fabricating the insured subject - inventing an insured object that doesn't exist; (2) the policyholder, insured or beneficiary fabricating a false cause for an insured event that occurred, or exaggerating the extent of the loss; (3) fabricating an insured event that never happened; (4) the policyholder or insured deliberately causing an insured event of property loss; (5) the policyholder or beneficiary deliberately causing the insured's death, disability or illness. A relatively large amount: up to 5 years' imprisonment or detention plus a 10,000-to-100,000-yuan fine. A huge amount or other serious circumstances: 5 to 10 years plus 20,000 to 200,000 yuan. An especially huge amount or other especially serious circumstances: 10 years or more plus 20,000 to 200,000 yuan or confiscation of property. The article's last paragraph covers the helpers: appraisers, certifiers and property assessors of insured events who deliberately produce false certification documents, creating conditions for others' insurance fraud, are handled as accomplices in insurance fraud - convicted together with the fraudster. Insurance Law Article 27: claiming an insured event that never happened lets the insurer terminate the contract without refunding premiums. Using forged or altered certification materials, or fabricating a false cause or exaggerating the loss: the insurer doesn't pay the inflated part, and what's already paid must be returned or compensated. The case: in the Xuancheng, Anhui Zhang case, Wang and 7 others were sentenced for insurance fraud to 4 years down to 2 years, each fined 10,000 yuan. In a false-litigation case it uncovered, the insurer had assessed the loss at 20,000 yuan; the party used a forged vehicle-transfer agreement to gain standing and sued, and the court awarded 70,000-plus yuan. That effective civil judgment was revoked on retrial on September 26, 2019. The Measures for Countering Insurance Fraud require risk-information verification at both the underwriting and claims ends, and exploration of a multi-line industry anti-fraud information platform and anti-fraud intelligence center. These platforms centrally screen suspicious fraud data; leads go to industry associations, anti-fraud organizations and insurers for verification, with suspected crimes promptly reported to police. The financial regulator and its local offices are to build a sound administrative-enforcement-to-criminal-justice linkage with police, procuratorates and courts (nationwide, issued July 31, 2024)

### 22. Don't sell your own organs, and don't broker donors either: the seller gets 20-some thousand while the same kidney resells for 200,000 - the money is confiscated plus a fine of 10 to 20 times the transaction
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free and no time. What this entry does is just not doing it.
- TL;DR: The "match online, cash on the spot" road has no lawful version. Living organs may go only to a spouse, a direct blood relative, or a collateral relative within three generations. The money math doesn't pay either: in the Beijing case, kidney sellers got 20,000 to 25,000 yuan while the same kidney sold to patients for 180,000 to 220,000. The proceeds are confiscated, plus a fine of 10 to 20 times the transaction. What the body pays after losing a kidney: entry 35 of chapter 1 (the remaining kidney works for two).
- Benefit: The Regulation on Human Organ Donation and Transplantation, Article 5: "no organization or individual may buy or sell human organs in any form, or engage in activities related to buying or selling human organs". Article 10: "no organization or individual may obtain a living organ from a citizen under 18 for transplantation". Article 11: "recipients of living organs are limited to the living donor's spouse, direct blood relatives, or collateral blood relatives within three generations". Article 37 sets the punishment for trading organs: trading human organs or engaging in related activities is investigated by the health department of the local government at county level or above. First the unlawful gains are confiscated - everything the transaction brought in. Then a fine of 10 to 20 times the transaction amount. Participating medical personnel have their practice certificates revoked and are banned for life from medical and health services. Where a crime is constituted, criminal responsibility follows (nationwide, effective May 1, 2024, with the former Human Organ Transplantation Regulation repealed). Criminal Law Article 234a: "whoever organizes others to sell human organs shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than five years, and shall also be fined; where the circumstances are serious, the sentence shall be fixed-term imprisonment of not less than five years, and a fine or confiscation of property shall also be imposed". Three more circumstances are convicted and punished under Articles 234 and 232: removing a person's organ without their consent; removing the organ of a person under 18; coercing or deceiving another into donating an organ - handled as intentional injury or intentional homicide. Case one: the Zheng Wei et al. case of 15 people, finally adjudicated by the Beijing First Intermediate Court on August 20, 2014, involved 51 kidneys and 10.34 million-plus yuan. The sellers (donors) "received in return only payments of 20,000 to 25,000 yuan". The price from waiting patients (recipients) was "formerly 180,000 or 190,000 yuan per kidney, later 210,000 or 220,000". The extraction site was a four-story building rented at 7,500 yuan a month; the ring's head nurse testified it was "filthy, without even resuscitation equipment". Organizer Zheng Wei got 12 years for organizing the sale of human organs; the other 14 got 3.5 to 9 years. Case two: the SPP-published "teenager sells kidney for an iPhone" case. Seventeen-year-old Wang had his right kidney removed in Chenzhou on the night of April 28, 2011, at a site provided by a contracted-out department in a hospital without transplant qualifications. Discharged on May 2 with 22,000 yuan; the recipient side paid the broker 150,000 yuan plus 10,000 US dollars, and the ringleader profited 56,000-plus. After surgery Wang's kidney function was impaired - "his injury constituted serious injury, third-degree disability".
