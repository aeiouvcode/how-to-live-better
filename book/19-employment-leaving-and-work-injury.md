# 19. Staying employed, quitting, and work injuries

This chapter is about money. How the money is counted, how the papers are signed, how the deadlines run. The first three entries cover money you should be getting while still on the job. The middle six cover leaving. The last seven cover occupational disease and work injury. Work-injury money is an order of magnitude bigger than layoff money, and the deadlines are harder. The damage from an occupational disease can never be bought back.

### 1. Overtime pay comes in three tiers - 1.5x, 2x, 3x; if it isn't paid, complain to labor inspection, and overdue payment adds another 50% to 100%
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Make a habit of saving your clock-in records, shift schedules and overtime approval slips, and screenshot the messages where your boss assigns work in the group chat. The hard part is remembering to save them every month.
- TL;DR: Overtime on a normal workday pays 1.5 times your wage. Working a rest day with no compensatory time off pays 2 times. Working a statutory holiday like New Year's Day, Spring Festival, Labor Day or National Day pays 3 times, and comp time cannot be substituted. If the company won't pay, complain to labor inspection. If it still won't pay after being ordered to by a deadline, the employer owes an extra half to one times the amount owed.
- Benefit: The legal normal working time is no more than 8 hours a day and no more than 44 hours a week on average. Ordinary overtime generally may not exceed 1 hour a day. With special reasons for more, it may not exceed 3 hours a day and 36 hours in a month. Overtime pay has three tiers. For extending your working hours on a normal day, no less than 150% of your wage. For scheduling you on a rest day with no compensatory time off possible, no less than 200%. For scheduling you on a statutory holiday, no less than 300%. For the statutory-holiday tier, the statute offers no "swap it for comp time" option. An employer that schedules overtime and won't pay for it is ordered by the labor authorities to pay by a deadline. Past that deadline, it is ordered to pay the worker an additional penalty of 50% to 100% of the amount due (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Labor Law (2018 second revision, Articles 36, 41, 43, 44). <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_d9aa750028b14b99a776cb93726a360d.html> (published by the State Administration for Market Regulation); Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law (Article 85, item 3). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: A rest day and a statutory holiday are not the same thing. Statutory holidays are only the few days fixed by law - New Year's Day, Spring Festival, Labor Day, National Day and the like. Long weekends stitched together by shifting workdays count as rest days and pay 200%. "Company policy says overtime needs approval" and "I stayed of my own accord" do not mean the company can skip paying, but they do make your evidence harder to produce - which is why you keep the assignment and approval records. A company that wants to use the comprehensive-hours or flexible-hours systems must get labor-authority approval first; it cannot decide on its own. The arbitration limitation period for disputes is in entry 19 of chapter 8.

### 2. Annual leave is 5, 10 or 15 days by cumulative years of service; untaken days convert to 300% of daily wage
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Add up the years you've worked at every employer; your social-insurance contribution record is the proof. The hard part is being able to refuse to sign when the company hands you a "voluntary waiver of annual leave" form.
- TL;DR: With cumulative service across all employers of 1 to under 10 years, you get 5 days of annual leave per year. At 10 years, 10 days. At 20 years, 15 days. Job changes still count toward the total. If the employer genuinely can't schedule the leave and you agree not to take it, those days pay 3 times your daily wage. Sign the "voluntary waiver" form, and you're left with just your normal wage.
- Benefit: After 12 consecutive months of work you are entitled to annual leave. The number of days is set by **cumulative** working time - every year at every employer added together. 1 to under 10 years: 5 days. 10 to under 20 years: 10 days. 20 years: 15 days. Statutory holidays and rest days don't count toward annual leave. If the employer truly cannot arrange the leave because of work needs and obtains your consent to skip it, the untaken days are paid "at 300% of the employee's daily wage income." That 300% already includes the normal pay for a worked day, so the extra money in hand is 200%. Daily wage = your monthly wage / 21.75. The monthly wage is the average over the 12 months before the payment, excluding overtime pay. If you joined during the year, the days are prorated by the remaining calendar days at that employer. When you leave, they are prorated by how much of the year you worked and settled; leave already overtaken is not clawed back. An employer that neither schedules the leave nor pays is ordered to correct by a deadline. Past the deadline, it owes an additional penalty equal to the leave pay itself (nationwide, effective January 1, 2008)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2007). Regulation on paid annual leave for employees (State Council Order No. 514, Articles 2, 3, 4, 5, 7). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2008/content_859865.htm>; Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2008). Implementation measures for paid annual leave for enterprise employees (MOHRSS Order No. 1, Articles 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 12, 15). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2009/content_1265995.htm>
- Notes: Cumulative working time adds up across employers - it is not "how long at this company." If you've changed jobs, don't count by your current employer alone. **Only when the employee, for personal reasons and in writing, declines the leave may the employer pay just the normal wage** - so don't sign the "voluntary waiver of annual leave" form. If you take 20 or more days of personal leave in a year and the employer doesn't dock your pay, you get no annual leave that year. The same applies to sick leave beyond the matching number of months: 2 months for under 10 years of service, 3 months for 10 to 20 years, 4 months for over 20. Home-visit leave, marriage and bereavement leave, maternity leave and the paid suspension period for work injury do not count against annual leave.

### 3. Probation has a legal cap, pay cannot fall below 80%, and it can only be agreed once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before signing, take a few minutes to check the contract term against the probation length.
- TL;DR: For a contract of up to 1 year, probation is at most 1 month. For 1 to 3 years, at most 2 months. For 3 years or more, at most 6 months. The same company may only put you on probation once; making you permanent and then starting a new probation after a transfer is illegal. Probation pay cannot be lower than 80% of the lowest tier for the same position or of the contract wage, and not below the local minimum wage.
- Benefit: For a contract of 3 months to under 1 year, probation may not exceed 1 month. For 1 to under 3 years, 2 months. For 3 years or more, and for open-ended contracts with no end date, 6 months. A contract for the completion of a specific task, or one lasting under 3 months in total, may not include probation at all. "The same employer and the same employee may agree on only one probation period" - one company gets one try per person. If the contract specifies only a probation period, that probation is void and the period is the term of the labor contract. Probation pay "shall not be lower than 80% of the lowest wage tier for the same position at the employer or of the wage agreed in the labor contract, and shall not be lower than the minimum wage standard where the employer is located." If the agreed probation exceeds the legal length and the person actually worked it, the employer pays an additional penalty for the excess time at your post-probation monthly wage. To terminate during probation, the employer may only use the specific circumstances listed in the law, and must explain the reason to the employee (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law (Articles 19, 20, 21, 83). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: The three most common violations. One: a 1-year contract with a 3-month probation. Two: a second probation after a transfer following confirmation. Three: "no social insurance during probation" - the social-insurance obligation starts on the first day of work and has nothing to do with probation; the employer-side duties are in entry 16 of chapter 12. Being dismissed during probation does not mean getting nothing; it depends on whether the reason given is one the law allows. Illegal termination is counted as 2N under entry 6.

### 4. When you're laid off, settle N first: one month's wage per full year, half a month for under six months
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One pass with a calculator, ten minutes.
- TL;DR: The severance a company owes when it lays you off is counted like this: one month's wage per full year worked, six months to under a year counts as a year, under six months pays half a month. The monthly wage here is your average over the 12 months before you leave, with bonuses, allowances and subsidies all counted in. If your wage exceeds 3 times the local average monthly wage of the previous year, it is capped at 3 times, and at most 12 years of service count.
- Benefit: The economic compensation fixed by the Labor Contract Law - the money the employer owes when it lets you go - is what everyone calls N. The formula: "one month's wage for each full year... six months to under one year counts as one year; for under six months, the employee is paid half a month's wage." If the monthly wage exceeds three times the local average monthly wage of employees in the previous year, the count is capped at three times, and the years of service counted are capped at twelve
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law of the People's Republic of China (effective January 1, 2008) (Article 47). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: The monthly wage means the average over the twelve months before the contract is terminated. Bonuses, allowances and subsidies all count - not just base pay. This is the number companies most often lowball

### 5. If the company fires you without 30 days' notice, it owes an extra month's wage
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: If the company lets you go without 30 days' written notice, it owes one extra month's wage on top of N - the N+1 everyone talks about. In negotiations, list that month separately; don't let the company fold it into N.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law gives an employer terminating under this article two options. One is "notifying the employee in writing thirty days in advance." The other is "paying the employee an additional month's wage, after which the labor contract may be terminated." Wanting you gone immediately means paying the extra month. That extra month is commonly called payment in lieu of notice, a separate line on top of N
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law of the People's Republic of China (effective January 1, 2008) (Article 40). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: So the usual result of a lawful layoff is N+1. In negotiations, count N and the 1 separately; don't let the company fold the extra month into N

### 6. For illegal termination, the penalty is twice the severance standard
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Arbitration takes a few months. The hard part is staying with the process for those months and not giving up halfway.
- TL;DR: If the termination is illegal, the penalty is double the severance standard - the 2N everyone talks about. Whether a termination is illegal depends on the specific circumstances. Ask legal aid or a lawyer first, then decide whether to negotiate with the company or go to arbitration.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law provides that an employer terminating or ending a labor contract in violation of the law "shall pay the employee compensation at twice the economic compensation standard in Article 47 of this Law." In other words, work out the severance by the usual formula, then double it
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law of the People's Republic of China (effective January 1, 2008) (Article 87). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: This is the 2N everyone talks about. What counts as illegal termination depends on the circumstances. Consult legal aid or a lawyer first, then decide whether to negotiate or arbitrate; the paths are in chapter 7

### 7. Don't sign "voluntary resignation for personal reasons" - one signature and the N is gone
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is withstanding the pressure to sign on the spot.
- TL;DR: Sign an acknowledgment of "voluntary resignation for personal reasons," and the severance and unemployment insurance usually go down together. Resigning on your own initiative is not among the circumstances that carry compensation, except where the company is at fault - unpaid wages, missing social insurance and the like. You don't have to sign that day; sleep on it.
- Benefit: The Labor Contract Law limits the circumstances that carry economic compensation to the ones it lists. An employee leaving of their own accord is not among them, unless it is over a statutory fault by the company such as unpaid wages or missing social insurance. Sign "voluntary resignation," and the severance and unemployment insurance usually go down together
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labor Contract Law of the People's Republic of China (effective January 1, 2008) (Articles 38, 46). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>
- Notes: The company's usual line is "sign first so we can run the process; the compensation comes separately." If it's real, write it into the agreement on the spot; if it isn't written, don't sign. Not signing that day has no legal consequence at all - sleep on it

### 8. Before leaving, save your pay slips, attendance records, labor contract, social-insurance records and chat history
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Half an hour to save the materials. The hard part is remembering before you go.
- TL;DR: Once the computer and accounts are handed back, the pay slips, attendance records, contract, social-insurance records and chat history are gone for good. In arbitration, how much you were paid and whether you worked overtime still comes down to the materials in your own hands. What you save is your own employment-relationship material; don't take the company's source code or client lists with you.
- Benefit: Once the computer and accounts are handed back, these materials are gone for good. The burden of proof in arbitration does tilt somewhat toward the employer's side, but facts like how much you were paid and whether you worked overtime still depend on the evidence in your own hands
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience, no direct literature; the rights-protection paths are in chapter 7
- Notes: What you save is your own employment-relationship material. Don't walk off with the company's source code, client lists or technical documents - that's a different matter, see chapter 11

### 9. The moment you're out, do two things: register as unemployed to claim unemployment insurance, and read your non-compete carefully
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: The moment you're out, do two things. First, register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance. Second, read through your non-compete agreement. Unemployment insurance is counted from the day you register - register a month late and you get a month less. Only people who lost their job against their own wishes can claim, which is another reason for entry 7 (don't sign "voluntary resignation for personal reasons").
- Benefit: Unemployment insurance has four gates: contributions paid for at least 1 year, job lost against your own wishes ("employment interrupted not of your own volition"), unemployment registration completed, and still actively looking for work. The years contributed decide whether you can claim for up to 12, 18 or 24 months. For how to claim online, see entry 1 of chapter 7 (if you lose your job, claim unemployment insurance online first). A non-compete lasts at most 2 years. During those 2 years the company must pay you monthly compensation. If the contract doesn't say how much, it is 30% of your average wage over the 12 months before leaving, and not below the local minimum wage. Only if the company, for its own reasons, fails to pay for 3 straight months may you ask to be released from the agreement - see entry 12 of chapter 11 (signed a non-compete and the company won't pay). The non-compete side has real deadlines: until it expires, the agreement you signed still has to be honored
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience, no direct literature; the figures quoted come from State Council (1999). Regulation on unemployment insurance (State Council Order No. 258, Articles 14, 16, 17: "unemployment insurance benefits are calculated from the date unemployment registration is completed"). <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=517> and Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012). Labor Contract Law (2012 revision, Articles 23, 24). <https://yjglj.fushun.gov.cn/008/20260622/d846d3c2-df3d-4c4c-af16-898ede43e1ee.html> (reposted by the Fushun Municipal Emergency Management Bureau)
- Notes: The C grade is because "go register the day you leave" as an action has no direct literature behind it; the figures in the Sources field are the statutes themselves. Unemployment money doesn't expire - it is counted from the day you register, so every day earlier is a day more of money. Claim materials and the online entry point are in chapter 7. The non-compete compensation standard and release conditions are in chapter 11. Signing "voluntary resignation for personal reasons" costs you not only the N but the unemployment insurance too - see entry 7 (don't sign "voluntary resignation for personal reasons")

### 10. Before taking a job with dust, noise or chemicals, check whether the hazards are written into the contract; the three occupational health exams are arranged and paid for by the employer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. When signing the contract, read the "occupational disease hazard disclosure" section line by line. Keep a copy of every exam report for yourself. The hard part is asking face to face and walking away with the documents in hand.
- TL;DR: When the contract is signed, the employer must truthfully write into it what occupational disease hazards the position has, how they are protected against, and what the benefits are - no concealing, no deceiving. If it conceals them, you may refuse to do the work, and the employer cannot fire you for it. The three occupational health exams - before taking the post, during employment, and when leaving - are arranged and paid for by the employer. Without the exit exam, the employer cannot terminate or end your contract.
- Benefit: The Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases requires four things to be made clear when a labor contract is signed: what occupational disease hazards the work may produce, what consequences they may leave, how they are protected against, and what the benefits are. The statutory text: "truthfully inform the employee and state this in the labor contract; concealment or deception is not allowed." If, after the contract is signed, you are transferred to hazardous work the contract never mentioned, the employer must again inform you truthfully and negotiate a change to the contract terms. Where the employer fails to do this, "the employee has the right to refuse work involving occupational disease hazards, and the employer shall not terminate the labor contract on that account." There are three exams: before taking the post, during employment, and when leaving. "The cost of occupational health examinations is borne by the employer" - the money comes from the employer, and the results must be given to you in writing. Someone without the pre-post exam may not be assigned to work involving occupational disease hazards. Someone medically unsuited to such work (with an occupational contraindication) may not be assigned to those tasks either. If an exam finds health damage related to the job, the person must be moved from the original post and properly placed. **"An employee who has not undergone the pre-departure occupational health examination may not have their labor contract terminated or ended."** When you leave, you "have the right to request a copy of your occupational health surveillance file, which the employer shall provide truthfully and free of charge, stamped on the copy." In other words, the employer must make you a faithful copy of that file for free and stamp it (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases (2018 revision, Articles 33, 35, 36). <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/201711/t20171104_779851.html> (reposted by the Beijing Municipal People's Government)
- Notes: Three questions at the interview tell you roughly whether the place is legitimate: does this position have occupational disease hazards, are they written into the contract, and who pays for the pre-post exam. If they can't answer or say "just start and we'll see," weigh the risk yourself. The exit exam is the most easily overlooked protection of all: without it, the employer cannot terminate or end your contract. That exam is also key material for a later occupational-disease diagnosis, and so is the file copy - get both before you go. An occupational disease itself is handled as a work injury; the benefits start at entry 12.

### 11. Damage from dust, noise and chemical poisons is irreversible: the employer must provide protective equipment, and you may refuse work without protection
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is wearing the particulate respirator, earplugs and goggles day after day, and refusing unprotected work - at the price of coworkers calling you "fussy."
- TL;DR: Pneumoconiosis, noise-induced deafness and chemical eye burns are all in the statutory catalog of occupational diseases. Once lungs and hearing are gone, no treatment can bring them back. You have the right to know what hazards the position carries and to demand protective facilities and personal protective equipment from the employer. Work without protective measures, you may refuse to do. If the employer cuts your pay or fires you for raising these demands, those actions are void.
- Benefit: Since August 1, 2025, the statutory occupational diseases number 135 kinds in 12 major classes, including 4 open-ended clauses. First on the list is occupational pneumoconiosis: silicosis, coal workers' pneumoconiosis, graphite pneumoconiosis, carbon black pneumoconiosis, asbestosis, talc pneumoconiosis; also cement pneumoconiosis, mica pneumoconiosis, potters' pneumoconiosis, aluminum pneumoconiosis, welders' pneumoconiosis and founders' pneumoconiosis; plus one open-ended clause. The occupational ear-nose-throat and oral diseases include noise-induced deafness and blast deafness. The occupational eye diseases include chemical eye burns and electric ophthalmia. All of these injuries are irreversible - what is gone is gone, and no treatment can repair lungs or hearing. The law grants four rights you can invoke on the spot. First, know what occupational disease hazards the workplace has, what consequences they cause, and what protective measures should be taken. Second, demand that the employer provide compliant protective facilities and personal protective equipment and improve working conditions. Third, criticize, report and accuse practices that violate the laws and regulations on occupational disease prevention or endanger life and health. Fourth, "refuse to follow orders that violate regulations or to be compelled to perform work without occupational disease protective measures" - non-compliant orders and unprotected work, you may refuse. And "where an employer reduces an employee's wages, benefits or other treatment, or terminates or ends their labor contract, because the employee lawfully exercised their legitimate rights, such actions are void." In other words, cutting your pay, cutting your benefits or firing you for using these rights doesn't count (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Health Commission, Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, National Disease Control and Prevention Administration, All-China Federation of Trade Unions (2024). Classification and catalog of occupational diseases (effective August 1, 2025). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202412/content_6992843.htm>; for the new catalog's count of 135 kinds in 12 classes, see National Health Commission (2024). New catalog adjusts occupational diseases to 135 kinds in 12 classes. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202412/content_6992838.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases (Article 39). <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/201711/t20171104_779851.html> (reposted by the Beijing Municipal People's Government)
- Notes: Get the mask right: only a particulate respirator stops dust; ordinary gauze masks and disposable surgical masks don't. If the employer on a noise job won't issue earplugs, buy your own - a few yuan a pair; hearing doesn't grow back. "Work two years, save enough, then quit" doesn't hold for pneumoconiosis. Silicosis can onset years after you leave the dusty work, and it keeps getting worse. That's why the exit exam matters so much (see entry 10: the three occupational health exams are arranged and paid for by the employer). The new catalog also adds two classes. One is occupational musculoskeletal disease, containing carpal tunnel syndrome, limited to manufacturing workers who use their wrists repetitively or forcefully for long periods. The other is occupational mental and behavioral disorders, containing post-traumatic stress disorder, limited to police officers, medical personnel, fire and rescue and other emergency responders involved in handling sudden incidents. For what to do when chemicals splash on you, see entry 21 of chapter 13.

### 12. Injured at work or hit on the commute: the first thing is a work-injury determination - if the employer won't file, file it yourself
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - one trip to the social-insurance administration. Bring three things: the work-injury determination application form, proof of the employment relationship, and the medical diagnosis.
- TL;DR: An injury at work counts as a work injury. Being hit on the commute, when the main responsibility isn't yours, also counts. But without the work-injury determination, the medical bills, the wages paid during recovery, and the disability allowance all fall through. The employer should file within 30 days. If it doesn't, you, a close relative or the union can file directly with the social-insurance administration within one year.
- Benefit: Without the work-injury determination, the medical bills, the wages paid during recovery (the paid suspension period), and the disability allowance all fall through. If the employer fails to file within 30 days, the work-injury benefit costs for that period come out of the employer's own pocket
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2010 revision). Regulation on work-related injury insurance (State Council Order No. 586). Article 14 lists seven circumstances that "shall be determined as work injury," including "(6) being injured in a traffic accident for which the person is not mainly responsible, or in an urban rail transit, passenger ferry or train accident, on the way to or from work"; Article 17: "the employer shall, within 30 days of the injury or of the diagnosis or certification of an occupational disease, file a work-injury determination application with the social-insurance administration of the coordinating region" and "if the employer fails to file as prescribed, the injured employee or a close relative or a trade union may, within 1 year of the injury or of the diagnosis or certification of an occupational disease, file the application directly with the social-insurance administration of the coordinating region where the employer is located" and "if the employer fails to file within the time limit in the first paragraph of this Article, the work-injury benefit and related costs under this Regulation arising during that period are borne by the employer"; Article 19: "where the employee or a close relative considers it a work injury and the employer does not, the burden of proof rests on the employer"; Article 20: "a work-injury determination decision shall be made within 60 days of accepting the application." <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>
- Notes: One year is a hard deadline - miss it and there is no applying. The burden of proof is on the employer, not you: if the employer says it isn't a work injury, the employer has to prove it. But keep your own materials too: photos from the day, coworkers' contact details, treatment records, attendance. Article 16 of the Regulation lists three circumstances that don't count: intentional crime, drunkenness or drug use, self-harm or suicide. Article 15 separately defines "deemed work injury": sudden death from illness during working hours at the work post, or death within 48 hours despite rescue.

### 13. Don't believe "make it to your desk and it counts": when sudden illness hits, call 120 first - don't rush to clock in
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: Dragging yourself toward the office during a sudden illness wins you no extra money. Deemed work injury requires the onset during working hours and at the work post; falling ill on the road before reaching the post never counted. And cases where rescue lasts beyond 48 hours don't count either. The tens of minutes lost trying to satisfy the conditions are paid for with your own survival odds.
- Benefit: This clause requires a sudden illness "during working hours and at the work post" causing death, or death within 48 hours despite rescue. Delaying treatment to satisfy these two conditions trades your chance of surviving for a payout you may never receive
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Regulation on work-related injury insurance, Article 15, paragraph 1, item (1): "sudden death from illness during working hours and at the work post, or death within 48 hours despite rescue" is deemed work injury; Article 14, item (6) counts only "a traffic accident for which the person is not mainly responsible, or an urban rail transit, passenger ferry or train accident" on the commute - sudden illness on the way is not among them. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>
- Notes: Two conditions must both be met: during working hours, and at the work post. Falling ill on the road before reaching the post doesn't qualify. Falling ill at the post and then being sent home or to hospital, dying within 48 hours despite rescue, may still qualify. What decides it is where you were at onset, not where you die. Short-video platforms tell this rule as "hold on until you reach the company and there's money" - exactly backwards: what actually changes the ending is whether the tens of minutes after onset are spent getting to a hospital. And 48 hours is a hard line: rescue lasting beyond 48 hours doesn't count.

### 14. If the employer didn't pay your work-injury insurance, the benefits are the same - paid in full by the employer at the same standard
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. If the employer won't accept it, you go through arbitration or even a lawsuit - a few months at minimum. The hard part is staying with it for those months.
- TL;DR: If the employer didn't pay your work-injury insurance, the benefits are the same. They simply switch to being paid in full by the employer, at the same items and standards, not a cent less. Not enrolling brings extra punishment on the employer: ordered to pay in, a late fee of 0.05% per day, and past the deadline a fine of 1 to 3 times the arrears. "The company didn't buy work-injury insurance so there's nothing to pay you" is a lie.
- Benefit: The Regulation states it plainly: if an employer that should have enrolled in work-injury insurance did not, and an employee suffers a work injury, the employer pays the money at the items and standards the Regulation prescribes. Which payments there are and how much each is - exactly the same as with insurance, just paid out of the employer's own pocket
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Regulation on work-related injury insurance, Article 62, paragraph 2: "where an employee of an employer that should have participated in work-related injury insurance under this Regulation but did not suffers a work injury, the employer shall pay the costs at the work-injury insurance benefit items and standards prescribed by this Regulation." ### 15. Once the injury stabilizes, get a work-capacity assessment - the disability grade converts directly into money

### 16. For a work-related death, keep the three payments apart: funeral subsidy, dependent relatives' pension, and the lump-sum work-death allowance

- Cost: Free - one set of procedures.
- TL;DR: A work-related death is three payments; don't negotiate over just one. The funeral subsidy is 6 months of the local average monthly wage of employees in the previous year. The dependent relatives' pension is paid monthly from the deceased's own wage: 40% per month for a spouse, 30% per person per month for other relatives. The lump-sum work-death allowance is 20 times the previous year's national per-capita disposable income of urban residents - one national figure.
- Benefit: The money for a work-related death comes in three payments.
