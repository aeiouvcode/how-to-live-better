# 11. Red lines programmers and tech people trip over

This chapter's lens is personal freedom and money - what these things buy you is "don't get locked up" and "don't pay damages". People who write code and do technical work often treat the things below as "just technical problems". In fact they map to specific criminal offenses, or a specific sum owed in a civil suit. Each entry gives the numbers in the statute and cases published on the Supreme People's Procuratorate and Supreme People's Court sites. Criminal-law citations use the consolidated text reposted by the Beijing traffic-management bureau - the version "as amended by Criminal Law Amendment (XI)". Entries run from "easiest for a tech person to assume is fine x heaviest consequence", high to low. Entry 1 is the general one: how to judge before acting, and what to do first if something happens.

### 1. Before acting, ask three questions - whose interests it harms, how able the other side is to pursue it, how much evidence I'm leaving; if pursued, find a criminal lawyer at once
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before acting, swap "can it technically be done" for another question: will the vendor, platform or company file a police report. The hard part is resisting the thought "nobody cares about something this small" - server logs, payment records and group-chat records are pulled as a matter of course in a case
- TL;DR: Before acting, ask first: will the vendor, platform or company file a report. Server logs, payment records and chat records can all be pulled in a case. If you're criminally detained, whether to arrest you is decided within at most 37 days. You can retain a lawyer from the first interrogation on - those 37 days are when a criminal lawyer helps most.
- Benefit: Any organization or individual that discovers criminal facts has the right to report. Where the case-handling organ finds criminal facts, it shall open a case. After criminal detention, police must request arrest approval - a formal arrest application to the procuratorate - within 3 days, extendable by 1 to 4 days in special circumstances, and to 30 days for things like roving serial offenses (continuous offending across places). The procuratorate then decides within 7 days. Total: arrest decided within at most 37 days. From the first interrogation, or from compulsory measures being taken (detention, arrest and the like), you may retain a lawyer. While the case is still being investigated by police, only a lawyer may be retained. When a lawyer requests a meeting, the detention center arranges it within 48 hours at the latest. Where control or short-term custody - sentences lighter than prison - may apply, or only a supplementary punishment without a principal one, release on bail pending trial is possible: post a bond or provide a guarantor and wait at home. Bail lasts at most 12 months (nationwide)
- Evidence: C
- Sources: National People's Congress (2018). Criminal Procedure Law (2018 amendment, Articles 34, 39, 67, 79, 91, 110, 112). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>
- Notes: Those 37 days run from detention to the procuratorate's arrest decision - not a cap on custody. After arrest approval there is the investigative custody period, counted onward. The 37 days are also when a capable lawyer helps most: meeting you, submitting a "should not approve arrest" opinion to the procuratorate, applying for bail. Waiting until prosecution to find a lawyer is late. Every other entry in this chapter says the same thing: whether the other side has a reason to file a report.

### 2. Don't make or sell game cheats and assistants - including "screen-read-only, never touches memory" AI assistants
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The price is giving up this kind of gig and "little tool" income. The hard part: the money comes fast, and it's hard to see who would come after you
- TL;DR: Selling to 20 person-times, or earning 5,000 yuan, reaches "serious circumstances" - up to 3 years. Especially serious: 3 to 7 years. One person made wallhack and aimbot cheats, sold 80,000+ yuan in 3 months and profited 30,000+, got 1 year suspended for 1 year plus a 5,000-yuan fine. People who took a game server program, ran a private server and took 20 million+ in top-ups got up to 4 years, with fines up to 10 million yuan.
- Benefit: Providing programs or tools specially used to intrude into or illegally control computer information systems - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine (sentenced and fined together, or fined alone); especially serious, 3 to 7 years plus a fine. The "serious" threshold: providing tools to 20 or more person-times, or unlawful gains of 5,000 yuan or more - unlawful gains meaning the money earned from this. Deliberately bypassing or breaking a rights-holder's technical protection measures can also constitute the crime of copyright infringement - up to 3 years, or 3 to 10 in serious cases. Official cases: Wang and others made and sold cheats using game-memory reads and writes for wallhack and aimbot. 80,000+ yuan sold in 3 months, 30,000+ profited - 1 year suspended 1 year, 5,000-yuan fine. Suspended 1 year means sentenced to 1 year but not jailed: no new offense in that year, no prison. Xiang and others sold the "TZ" series cheats - 200,000+ yuan in sales; Xiang got 3 years suspended 3 years, 20,000-yuan fine. Liu and others obtained a game server program, changed parameters and ran a private server. They took 20 million+ yuan in top-ups - 4 years to 2 years 6 months, fines of 10 million to 1.3 million yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Articles 285(3), 217(6)). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; SPC and SPP (2011). Interpretation on applying law in criminal cases endangering computer information system security (Article 3). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> (reposted by Shenzhen Public Security Bureau) ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Shanghai Jing'an: severely punishing the cheat-making gray-and-black industry to protect a clean cyberspace. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202608/t20260825_735480.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2021). 2020 typical procuratorial IP-protection cases (case 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202104/t20210425_516525.shtml>
- Notes: Published cheat cases all involve reading and writing game memory or bypassing anti-cheat. No official case yet for a "screenshot-only, simulated-keypress-only" assistant. But the ticket-grabbing case in entry 3 shows that never touching memory - only sending requests - can still be convicted as illegally obtaining data. Whether it ends up a crime, and under which charge, is decided by the case-handling organ from the materials the vendor submits with its report - not by this author. Twenty person-times reaches "serious" - a very small volume.

### 3. Don't write or sell ticket-grabbing, flash-sale, order-faking or promo-gaming scripts - even "just automating button clicks"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free, and no time
- TL;DR: A script that doesn't modify memory or crack anything - merely sends requests faster than a person - is still convicted as illegally obtaining computer information system data. The threshold is earning 5,000 yuan or causing the other side 10,000 yuan of loss. One person batch-grabbed tickets with bought 12306 accounts - 200+ tickets grabbed for clients, 20,000+ yuan earned - got 1 year 6 months suspended 1 year 6 months, 5,000-yuan fine. Someone who sold ticket-grabbing software in an online shop was separately found to have committed unfair competition and paid 20,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Intruding into a computer information system, or using other technical means to obtain its data - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 7 years. The "serious" threshold: unlawful gains of 5,000 yuan or more, or losses caused of 10,000 yuan or more. Official case: one person used a plugin to batch-import bought 12306 accounts and submit ticket requests intensively across threads. He grabbed 200+ train tickets for clients and profited 20,000+ yuan. The court convicted him of illegally obtaining computer information system data: 1 year 6 months suspended 1 year 6 months, 5,000-yuan fine. Zheng sold ticket-grabbing software targeting a ticketing app in an online shop - found to constitute unfair competition, 20,000 yuan in damages (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Article 285(2)). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; SPC and SPP (2011). Interpretation on applying law in criminal cases endangering computer information system security (Article 1). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Shanghai Jing'an: case of batch ticket-grabbing for profit using plugin software. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202607/t20260707_731426.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court (2025). 2024 typical people's-court IP cases (case 7, the ticket-grabbing software unfair-competition case). <https://enipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-4205.html>
- Notes: The script in the ticket case didn't modify memory or crack anything - merely "faster than a person" - and was still convicted under Criminal Law Article 285. Both paid grabbing and selling scripts count as profiting. No official case found for one-time personal use.

### 4. Crawl only public pages needing no login; don't bypass anti-crawling, don't touch personal information, don't sell what you crawl
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. A crawler is a program that automatically flips through web pages and pulls their data. Before crawling, glance at the robots file and the API authorization notes - robots is the file a site puts in its root directory saying which pages may not be scraped. Data that needs a login, a token (a temporary access credential) or a cracked signature - don't crawl. The hard part: this data is technically one grab away, and resisting takes self-discipline
- TL;DR: Data requiring a login, a token or a cracked signature means bypassing protection - up to 3 years, 3 to 7 in serious cases. Crawled personal information is worse: 50 records of whereabouts, communications, credit or property data count as serious circumstances; 500 of accommodation, health or transaction data; 5,000 of other kinds; or 5,000 yuan earned.
- Benefit: Bypassing protection to take a system's data is sentenced like entry 3 (grabbing, flash-sale and faking scripts) - up to 3 years, or 3 to 7. Illegally obtaining, selling or providing citizens' personal information reaches "serious circumstances" at any of these lines: 50+ records of tracks, communication content, credit or property information; 500+ of accommodation, communication records, health or transaction information; 5,000+ of other personal information; or 5,000+ yuan of unlawful gains. Serious: up to 3 years plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 7. Official cases: software developer Li disguised phone-album theft as a "face-score test" app, took 1,751 facial photos and also resold social-engineering-database material (a social-engineering database pools personal information leaked from various places) - 3 years suspended 3 years. A network tech company used crawlers to take a food-delivery platform's shop and order data, causing 40,000+ yuan of loss - prosecuted for illegally obtaining computer information system data (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Articles 285(2), 253a). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; SPC and SPP (2017). Interpretation on applying law in criminal cases of infringing citizens' personal information (Articles 3, 4, 5). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201705/t20170509_190088.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Typical procuratorial cases punishing personal-information crimes (case 2). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202212/t20221207_594915.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Procuratorates punishing crimes against enterprise data security, serving enterprise innovation (case 1). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: The 2017 judicial interpretation provides that "giving legally collected information to others without the person's consent" also counts as "providing". Exporting the user table to an outside partner from inside a company falls under the same sentence. Five thousand records is one small table.

### 5. Don't take development gigs for gambling, scam or porn apps; don't build payment channels or unban accounts for "clients"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The price is giving up outsourcing that quotes high and can't say what it needs. The hard part: the money offered is often far above a normal gig
- TL;DR: These high-priced, vague-spec gigs, once taken, are counted as complicity in the gambling gang - they follow the crime of opening a casino: up to 5 years, 5 to 10 in serious cases. Not the "just helping out, capped at 3 years" tier. In the esports-betting app case, the 28 people writing code in Hainan got 3 years 6 months to 1 year for opening a casino. Someone who unbanned QQ accounts for a scam gang got 1 year 6 months plus a 20,000-yuan fine.
- Benefit: Knowing others are using the network to commit crimes and still providing technical support, payment settlement or similar help - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine. Where it also constitutes another crime, the heavier one applies. Opening a casino: up to 5 years plus a fine; serious, 5 to 10. Official cases: the Zhang Sheng group developed gambling games then "packaged and sold the sites", charging setup and reporting fees - nearly 7 billion yuan involved; Zhang Sheng got 9 years 9 months, 50-million-yuan fine; Wang Qiang and 78 others responsible for outsourced development got 6 months to 9 years 9 months. Chen Dong and others developed and ran an esports-betting app; the tech team wrote code in Hainan - 28 people got 3 years 6 months to 1 year for opening a casino, plus fines. Zhang opened a studio unbanning QQ accounts blocked for scam gangs - 1 year 6 months, 20,000-yuan fine (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Articles 287a, 303(2)). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023). Building and selling gambling websites, nearly 7 billion yuan involved. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202307/t20230711_621248.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Shanghai Minhang: a new kind of online casino case. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202208/t20220805_569952.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Typical cases punishing aiding information-network crime and related offenses (case 1). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202507/t20250728_702377.shtml>
- Notes: "Knowing" doesn't need the client to say the purpose out loud. A quote clearly above market, features of top-up plus withdrawal plus odds, a client who won't say the use - all are used in a case to infer you knew. Developers of a gambling app are handled as accomplices in opening a casino, not the aiding-information-network-crime tier capped at 3 years ("aiding-information-network crime" is short for the crime of helping information-network criminal activity).

### 6. When leaving a job, hand over all account access - don't drop databases, leave backdoors or change passwords to lock systems, even if the company owes you money
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. If the company owes you wages, recover them through labor arbitration - don't act yourself (labor arbitration: chapter 7). The hard part is not touching the system while angry
- TL;DR: The account and password being company-issued doesn't mean you may still use them after leaving - logging in counts as illegal control, up to 3 years. Deleting data so the system can't run: up to 5 years, over 5 if the consequences are especially serious. A dismissed senior engineer logged into the company server with his old admin account, deleted data and logs, caused 120,000+ yuan of loss - 3 years suspended 5 years, 30,000-yuan fine.
- Benefit: Illegally controlling a computer information system - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 7. Deleting or modifying system functions or data so the system can't run normally - serious consequences bring up to 5 years; especially serious, over 5. Official cases: senior IT engineer Lv, after dismissal, logged into the company's shared server with his old admin account, changed the password, deleted data and logs - 120,000+ yuan of loss; 3 years suspended 5 years, 30,000-yuan fine. Software-company employee Bai deliberately wrote wrong code into an upgrade program, leaving a client's system unloggable - 40,000+ yuan of loss; prosecuted for sabotaging a computer information system (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Articles 285(2), 286). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2024). Typical cases of "punishing cybercrime, aiding comprehensive cyberspace governance" (case 6). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202402/t20240223_644505.shtml> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Procuratorates punishing crimes against enterprise data security, serving enterprise innovation (case 7). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>

### 7. When leaving, don't take source code, client lists or technical documents; don't upload them to a personal cloud drive; don't reuse them at the next employer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before leaving, delete company material from your personal computer and cloud drives. The hard part: some files you wrote line by line, and can't bear to
- TL;DR: Code you wrote yourself still belongs to the company - taking it is taking company property. Client lists likewise count as trade secrets. One departing employee took a security-inspection system's source code and core algorithms to a new company: he got 5 years and a 500,000-yuan fine, and the new company was fined 4 million. Someone used a system flaw to download 33,000+ design files - 1 year 10 months, 100,000-yuan fine. Beyond sentencing, the company can sue separately in civil court.
- Benefit: Breaching a confidentiality duty by disclosing or using trade secrets - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 10 plus a fine. The Anti-Unfair-Competition Law also lists two things as infringement: using "electronic intrusion" - technical intrusion into a system - to obtain trade secrets; and abetting or helping others breach confidentiality duties. Official cases: Sun Mouming and other departing employees took a security-inspection system's source code and core algorithms and used them in the new company's products - Sun got 5 years, 500,000-yuan fine; the new company was fined 4 million. Tian used a system flaw to download 33,000+ design files - 1 year 10 months, 100,000-yuan fine. Engineer Yang stored an app module's source code on a personal computer and in the cloud, then made a similar app after job-hopping - prosecuted for infringing trade secrets (nationwide)

### 8. Don't run your own programs on other people's computers, servers or cameras; don't mine on company machines

Below the crime line, detention up to 5 days plus a possible 50,000-to-500,000-yuan fine. Heavier for tech people is the attached consequence: anyone criminally punished is barred for life from key posts in cybersecurity management and network operation.
- Benefit: Illegally controlling a computer information system - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years, plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 7. Illegal control of 20 or more systems counts as "serious". Below the crime line, police confiscate unlawful gains, detain up to 5 days, and may fine 50,000 to 500,000 yuan; in heavier circumstances, 5 to 15 days detention and a possible 100,000-to-1,000,000-yuan fine. Anyone given an administrative punishment is barred 5 years, and anyone criminally punished barred for life, from key posts in cybersecurity management and network operation. Official cases: Li bought IP addresses of network cameras and used software to control 105 cameras to spy on private life - 3 years suspended 5 years, 20,000-yuan fine. ### 9. No written authorization, no testing of others' systems - "good intentions" and "reporting afterward" are not defenses
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before joining a vendor's SRC or crowdsourced test, get a document stating the authorized scope, target list and testable time window. An SRC is a vendor's own security response center, taking vulnerability reports from outside. Stop once you've verified the vulnerability exists. The hard part is giving up "just a quick try while I'm here"
- TL;DR: Whether it's a crime turns on authorization and how much you took - not on what you meant to do. Five hundred sets of ordinary account credentials, or 5,000 yuan earned, or 10,000 yuan of loss caused, reaches up to 3 years. Exporting a user table on the side to prove a vulnerability often crosses the 500 line in one go. Using credentials you legitimately hold beyond their authorized scope is itself intrusion.
- Benefit: This covers computer information systems outside state affairs, defense and cutting-edge science and technology - ordinary company and personal systems. Intruding into such a system, or using other technical means to obtain data it stores, processes or transmits - serious circumstances bring up to 3 years' imprisonment or custody, plus or solely a fine; especially serious, 3 to 7 plus a fine. "Serious" has several lines: 10+ sets of identity-authentication information (accounts, passwords and the like) for online financial services like payment settlement, securities or futures trading; 500+ sets of other identity-authentication information; illegal control of 20+ systems; unlawful gains of 5,000+ yuan or economic losses of 10,000+ yuan caused. Five times any of those numbers is "especially serious". Official case: Guiding Case No. 36 established the sentence "using an account and password to log into a computer information system beyond the authorized scope is intrusion into a computer information system" - meaning credentials you legitimately hold, used beyond their authorization, is itself intrusion. In that case, three people used accounts, passwords and tokens from their work to log into the company's internal system remotely, downloaded data outside their work scope and sold it online - 37,000 yuan of unlawful gains; they got 4 years, 3 years 9 months and 4 years respectively, 40,000-yuan fines each (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law (as amended by Amendment (XI), Articles 285(1), 285(2)). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> ; SPC and SPP (2011). Interpretation on applying law in criminal cases endangering computer information system security (Article 1). <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> (reposted by Shenzhen Public Security Bureau) ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2017). Ninth batch of guiding cases (Guiding Case No. 36: Wei Menglong, Gong Xu, Xue Dongdong illegally obtaining computer information system data). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/jczdal/201710/t20171017_202593.shtml>
- Notes: Guiding Case No. 36 was itself a case of selling data for profit. It's cited only for the sentence "login beyond the authorized scope counts as intrusion" - not to say good-faith testing is sentenced the same. What you meant to do and whether you reported afterward don't cancel the charge; they can only affect how heavy the sentence is or whether to prosecute. A vendor thanking you first and reporting you later are not contradictory - thanks are not authorization. At writing time, no verbatim-checkable good-faith-testing case was found on the SPC or SPP sites, so this entry is written from the statute and punishment standards only. If you test, go through the vendor's public SRC or a written-commission assessment, with scope, targets and time window all in the document. Prove the vulnerability with minimal evidence; don't pull data in bulk. Even below the crime line, administrative punishment and the employment ban apply - fine amounts and ban years as in entry 8 (don't run your own programs on others' machines). What to do after finding a vulnerability: entry 10 (report vulnerabilities through the regulated channel).

### 10. Report vulnerabilities through the regulated channel; before the fix, publish no details, release no exploit tools, hand nothing overseas
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Report the vulnerability to the product provider, or to an official platform. Before the vendor fixes it, publish no details and no verification code
- TL;DR: The Rules on Managing Network Product Security Vulnerabilities cover not just companies - individuals are inside too. Publishing a vulnerability has five hard lines, the first two being: not before the vendor has a fix, and no vulnerability details for systems in use. Also no programs or tools specially made to exploit it - and when publishing, say how to fix it at the same time. One easily missed line: an undisclosed vulnerability may not be given to overseas organizations or individuals other than the product provider.
- Benefit: The rules cover three kinds of subjects: network product providers and network operators in China, and organizations or individuals engaged in vulnerability discovery, collection and publication. No organization or individual may use vulnerabilities to endanger cybersecurity, or illegally collect, sell or publish vulnerability information. Publishing vulnerability information to the public has five rules. First, not before the product provider offers a fix. Second, no details of vulnerabilities in networks, information systems and equipment in use by network operators. Third, no deliberately exaggerating the harm and risk, and no using vulnerability information for malicious hype or fraud. Fourth, no publishing or providing programs and tools specially used to exploit vulnerabilities for harming cybersecurity. Fifth, publish fix or mitigation measures at the same time. Separately, undisclosed vulnerability information may not be provided to overseas organizations or individuals other than the product provider. The rules encourage reporting to four official platforms: the MIIT cybersecurity threat and vulnerability information sharing platform; the national network and information security information notification center vulnerability platform; the national computer network emergency response technical coordination center (CNCERT) vulnerability platform; and the China Information Security Evaluation Center vulnerability database. Collecting or publishing vulnerability information in violation is handled by MIIT and the Ministry of Public Security under their respective duties; where it falls under the Cybersecurity Law, that law's penalties apply. Publishing system-vulnerability and other cybersecurity information to the public must follow state rules; violators are ordered to correct and warned, with a possible 10,000-to-100,000-yuan fine. Refusing to correct, or serious circumstances: 100,000 to 1 million yuan, plus possible suspension of related business, shutdown for rectification, closure of the website or app, or revocation of the related license or business license. Directly responsible supervisors and other directly responsible persons: 10,000 to 100,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: MIIT, CAC and Ministry of Public Security (2021). ### 11. Don't sell circumvention tools or VPN accounts, and don't set up such nodes for people

- Cost: Free, and no time

### 12. Signed a non-compete? If the company stops paying monthly compensation after you leave, demand it in writing - after 3 unpaid months you can terminate; people whose posts never touched trade secrets can ask to have the clause declared ineffective
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. When leaving, keep the non-compete original and evidence that the company isn't paying monthly
- TL;DR: A non-compete bars you from working for competitors for a period after leaving. This entry protects you. A non-compete runs at most 2 years, and the company must pay compensation monthly. With no agreed amount, it's 30% of your average wage over the 12 months before leaving, and not below the local minimum wage. If the company's own fault leaves 3 months unpaid, you can request termination. From 2025, someone whose post never knew or touched trade secrets can ask to have the clause declared ineffective.
- Benefit: A non-compete may bind only senior management, senior technical staff and others with confidentiality duties. The term runs at most 2 years. The company must pay economic compensation monthly through the term. With no agreed amount: 30% of average wages over the 12 months before leaving, not below the local minimum wage. Where the company's own fault leaves 3 months unpaid, the worker may request termination of the non-compete. Where the company terminates on its own initiative, it pays 3 extra months of compensation. From 2025, a worker whose post never knew or touched trade secrets may ask to have the clause declared ineffective; where the agreed scope exceeds the secrets they actually knew, the excess is void. Official case: a pharmaceutical company sued its former CTO Zheng for 7.1 million yuan in liquidated damages. The court found his new employer's products weren't substitutes for the old company's, that the non-compete scope should be limited to the secrets he actually knew, and dismissed the claim in full (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2012). Labor Contract Law (2012 amendment, Articles 23, 24). <https://yjglj.fushun.gov.cn/008/20260622/d846d3c2-df3d-4c4c-af16-898ede43e1ee.html> (reposted by Fushun Emergency Management Bureau) ; Supreme People's Court (2020). In reverse: if the agreement is valid, the company pays monthly, and you still join a competitor, you owe the agreed liquidated damages - and may be required to serve out the rest of the non-compete term. ### 13. Code written on work time with company resources belongs to the company - do your own open-source projects on your own time and equipment, and don't mix in company code
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Read the IP clause of your labor contract carefully when joining. The hard part: personal projects and company work often live on one computer - keep them apart
- TL;DR: A work-for-hire is something made doing your job. Software is explicitly listed by law in the "copyright belongs to the employer" tier. Where the work mainly used the company's equipment, intranet or business data and the company bears responsibility, the code's copyright is the company's. Same where the contract so agrees. You keep only attribution. An invention made with company resources has its patent with the company too. Something "written after work" that used a company computer or company business data can likewise be found to have mainly used the employer's material and technical conditions.
- Benefit: A work created to complete the employer's work tasks is a work-for-hire - something made doing your job. Generally the copyright belongs to the author, with the employer having priority use within its business scope, and the author unable to license a third party the same use for 2 years. But two cases differ: engineering designs, software and the like created mainly using the employer's material and technical conditions, with the employer bearing responsibility; and works the contract assigns to the employer. In both, the author keeps only attribution. Material and technical conditions means the company's equipment, intranet and business data. An invention made performing the employer's tasks, or mainly using its material and technical conditions, is a service invention - the right to apply for the patent, and the granted patent, belong to the employer (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2020). Copyright Law (2020 amendment, Article 18). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html> ; NPC Standing Committee (2020). Patent Law (2020 amendment, Article 6). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html>
- Notes: Software is written directly into the "copyright belongs to the employer" class, so a programmer almost never lands in the "author owns it, employer has 2-year priority" tier. Something "written after work" that used a company computer, intranet or business data can likewise count as made mainly with the employer's equipment and materials. To keep a personal project clean: your own machine, no company repos, no overlap with company business.

### 14. Using GPL and other open-source code in a product: open-source as the license requires or switch licenses - don't treat it as "nobody cares"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before using someone's open-source code, look at its license - the use conditions the author wrote. The GPL family requires products using it to open their source too, which conflicts with selling closed-source; if it conflicts, switch. The hard part is dropping a library you're already used to
- TL;DR: Copyright infringement is compensated by the rights-holder's actual loss or what you earned. Where neither can be computed, refer to what properly buying the license would cost. Deliberate infringement in serious circumstances can bring 1 to 5 times that. In one case, the defendant hired the plaintiff's ex-employees to develop gateway software with substantially similar code. The defendant argued the plaintiff's software was GPLv2-based and should have been open anyway; the Supreme Court held those were two independent questions and still ordered the infringement stopped with 500,000 yuan in damages.
- Benefit: Copyright infringement is compensated by the rights-holder's actual loss, or by the infringer's earnings. Where neither can be computed, refer to what properly buying that license would cost. Deliberate infringement in serious circumstances can bring 1 to 5 times compensation. Harming the public interest can also bring confiscation of unlawful gains plus a fine. Official case: (2021) SPC IP Civil Final No. 51. The defendant hired the plaintiff's former employees; the gateway software developed was substantially similar to the plaintiff's. The defendant argued the plaintiff's software was based on GPLv2 and should have been open anyway. The Supreme Court held "whether the developer itself breached the GPLv2 and whether it holds the software copyright are two independent questions", and ordered the infringement stopped with 500,000 yuan in damages (nationwide)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2020). Copyright Law (2020 amendment, Articles 53, 54). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html> ; SPC Intellectual Property Court (2022). ### 15. An app collects only the personal information its business needs; under-14s require guardian consent; write the privacy policy clearly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before launch, cross-check the app's permission list against its privacy policy and drop every permission not needed. The hard part: the product side always wants to keep extra data "that might be useful later"
- TL;DR: Collect only what the business genuinely needs; under-14s require guardian consent. Violations start with correction orders, warnings and confiscation of unlawful gains, and the app can be ordered to suspend or terminate service. Refusing to correct: up to 1 million yuan, with 10,000 to 100,000 for directly responsible persons. Serious circumstances: up to 50 million yuan or 5% of last year's revenue, with 100,000 to 1 million for responsible persons. The statute's "directly responsible persons" can include the specific technical and product people who did the work.
- Benefit: Processing personal information requires a clear and reasonable purpose, direct relevance to that purpose, and the way with the least impact on personal rights. Processing requires the individual's consent or a statutory ground. Under-14s require parental or guardian consent. Violations bring correction orders, warnings and confiscation of unlawful gains, and the offending app is ordered to suspend or terminate service. Refusing to correct: up to 1 million yuan, with 10,000 to 100,000 for directly responsible supervisors and other directly responsible persons. ### 16. Before a website or app launches, do the ICP filing; under graded protection, keep logs for at least 6 months
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Filing is free - through a cloud vendor's process it takes a few weeks. Graded-protection assessment is charged by level. "Graded protection" (dengbao) is the state's set of security-management actions required of websites and systems, tiered by importance; a small site generally doesn't need the assessment
- TL;DR: Filing costs nothing - a few weeks through a cloud vendor. A domestic server without filing simply won't resolve its domain. Operating unfiled brings a correction order; refusing means an order to close the site. No filing number shown on the homepage: 5,000 to 50,000 yuan. Filed as non-commercial but actually charging: handled as unlicensed operation - confiscation of unlawful gains plus 100,000 to 1 million yuan. Keep logs a full 6 months; not keeping them brings a correction order and warning, and refusing to correct or causing consequences brings 10,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Commercial internet information services require a license; non-commercial ones require filing. Without the license or filing, the service may not operate. Unfiled: the telecom authority orders correction; refusing brings an order to close the site. Operating without the license: confiscation of unlawful gains plus a fine of 3 to 5 times those gains, or 100,000 to 1 million yuan. A homepage not showing its filing number: 5,000 to 50,000 yuan. A network operator must, under the graded-protection system, set security management rules, defend against attacks, keep network logs at least 6 months, and classify, back up and encrypt data. Not doing so brings a correction order and warning. Refusing to correct or causing consequences: 10,000 to 100,000 yuan, with 5,000 to 50,000 for responsible supervisors (nationwide)
- Evidence: A

- Notes: A domestic server without filing simply won't resolve its domain.
