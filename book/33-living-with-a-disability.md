# 33. Living with a disability

This chapter is about the days after a disability has already happened. Preventing the disabling event itself is in chapters 1 and 13 and is not repeated here.

Four lenses, counted separately, never converted against each other. The emergency and complication entries count by mortality. The subsidy, benefit and lawsuit entries count by money. The schooling and paperwork entries count by time. The psychiatric hospitalization, capacity and guardianship entries count by personal freedom.

The World Health Organization's fact sheet gives three numbers. About 1.3 billion people worldwide - 16% of the world's population, one in six - live with significant disability. Some people with disabilities die up to 20 years earlier than people without. People with disabilities carry double the risk of depression, asthma, diabetes, stroke, obesity or poor oral health. A large part of these gaps is not caused by the disability itself, but by being unable to see a doctor, attend school, find work or leave the house. So half of this chapter's entries are the body, and half are paperwork and rights.

Five things already written elsewhere get a pointer, not a repeat. How to get the disability certificate and how the seven categories and grades one to four are assessed: entry 11 of chapter 24 (disability certificate). When a disability assessment should be done: entry 10 of chapter 24 (disability assessment). How to claim the two disability subsidies: entry 8 of chapter 7 (two subsidies). Work-injury labor-capacity assessment and how disability grades convert to money: entry 15 of chapter 19 (labor-capacity assessment). How to apply for long-term care insurance for severe loss of function: entry 7 of chapter 17 (long-term care insurance) - that entry sits in the "elders at home" chapter, but the benefit has no age threshold and younger people can claim it too.

Most entries in this chapter benefit you and your family - the top two tiers. Where other people are affected, the notes say so separately.

### 1. If someone with a spinal cord injury gets a sudden severe headache and sweating, sit them upright, loosen their clothing, and call 120 at the same time
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Three moves, done in tens of seconds.
- TL;DR: In a person with a spinal cord injury, part of the body's alarm wiring is cut. A full bladder or tight clothing may feel like nothing to them, yet their blood pressure can spike hard. The signal is a sudden severe headache and sweating. When it comes: sit the person upright, loosen tight clothing, check for a held-back bladder - and call 120 at the same time.
- Benefit: This attack is called autonomic dysreflexia. A systematic review searched Medline, CINAHL, EMBASE and PsycINFO, including 31 studies, 6 of them randomized controlled trials. The review states that non-drug management during an attack has three steps: place the patient upright, loosen tight clothing, remove the triggering stimulus. These three steps rest on clinical consensus and physiological data - level 5 evidence. Antihypertensives are used only when blood pressure keeps rising: prazosin has level 1 evidence, nifedipine and prostaglandin E2 level 2. The review also states that prevention strategies target the two common trigger classes - the genitourinary system and surgery - mostly supported only by level 4 and 5 evidence
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Krassioukov A, Warburton DE, Teasell R, Eng JJ; Spinal Cord Injury Rehabilitation Evidence Research Team (2009). A systematic review of the management of autonomic dysreflexia after spinal cord injury. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 90(4):682-695. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2008.10.017>
- Notes: Grade B because the three on-the-spot moves have no randomized trial behind them - only consensus and physiological reasoning. The review itself says most of these measures are supported only by uncontrolled studies. The medication part is the doctor's business; don't give antihypertensives at home on your own. Lying the person flat raises the pressure further - hence sitting upright. Family members and caregivers should know these three steps too. Don't tough it out as an ordinary headache.

### 2. The first ten years after a disabling injury are a high-risk period for suicide - not something a "cheer up" settles
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call, or one clinic registration.
- TL;DR: In the first years after becoming disabled, it is common for the person or the family to collapse emotionally - not a willpower problem. Long-term follow-up at two UK spinal-injury centers found this group's suicide rate five times the general population's. Over ninety percent of the suicides happened in the first ten years after injury. When the injury itself came from a suicide attempt, the risk is higher still.
- Benefit: The study followed 2,304 traumatic spinal-cord-injury patients newly admitted from 1991 to 2010 who survived the first year and were discharged with neurological deficits, through the end of 2014. Of them, 63 (2.7%) had been injured in a suicide attempt. By follow-up's end, 533 people had died, 4.2% of them by suicide - 91% of the suicides in the first ten years after injury. The age-standardized suicide mortality was 62.5 per 100,000 per year (95% CI 36.4-88.6, the credible range) - five times the 2014 general-population suicide rate in England and Wales (12.2 per 100,000). For those injured in a suicide attempt versus other causes, the odds ratio for death was 4.32 (P<0.001) and for suicide 9.46 (P<0.001)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Savic G, DeVivo MJ, Frankel HL, Jamous MA, Soni BM, Charlifue S (2018). Suicide and traumatic spinal cord injury - a cohort study. Spinal Cord, 56(1):2-6. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sc.2017.98>
- Notes: This study looked only at spinal cord injury; stretching it to blindness, amputation or burns is extrapolation. What it establishes is the time window: the risk doesn't end at discharge - it needs watching for ten years. For someone to talk with, call 12356 - entry 11 of chapter 29 (12356). Don't stockpile sleeping pills or pesticides at home - entry 25 of chapter 1 (suicidal thoughts). Grief still stuck in the same place after six months: register at a psychiatric or clinical-psychology department - entry 8 of chapter 29 (grief past six months).

### 3. Psychiatric hospitalization is voluntary in principle; only two situations allow it without the person's consent
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Memorize the two situations so you can say them when needed.
- TL;DR: Neither a family's objection nor an employer's can keep a person locked in a psychiatric hospital long-term. The law recognizes only two situations: the person has already harmed themselves or is in danger of doing so; the person has already endangered others or is in danger of doing so. The first requires the guardian's consent for admission - without it the hospital may not admit. Anyone disputing the diagnosis may demand a second diagnosis within three days, and after that may commission an appraisal on their own.
- Benefit: Article 30 of the Mental Health Law states "hospitalization for mental disorders follows the principle of voluntariness." Only when the diagnostic conclusion and condition assessment show the patient has a severe mental disorder, plus one of the following, shall hospitalization be carried out. One: "has already committed acts of self-harm, or is in danger of self-harm." Two: "has already committed acts endangering others' safety, or is in danger of endangering others' safety." Article 31: in the first situation, the medical institution carries out hospitalization only with the guardian's consent; without it, it may not. Article 32: in the second situation, if the person or the guardian objects, they may demand a second diagnosis - filed within three days of receiving the diagnostic conclusion - made by two practicing psychiatrists other than the initial diagnosticians; still objecting to the second conclusion, they may independently commission a psychiatric medical appraisal by a lawfully qualified appraisal institution. Article 35: if the second diagnosis or the appraisal report shows the person cannot be determined to have a severe mental disorder, or does not need hospitalization, the medical institution may not hospitalize them. Article 78: deliberately sending a person without a mental disorder into a medical institution as a mental patient brings liability for compensation under law (nationwide, effective May 1, 2013, amended 2018)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mental Health Law of the People's Republic of China (amended 2018, Articles 30, 31, 32, 35, 78). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7448a066d>
- Notes: The other side must also be written clearly, or this entry reads as "nobody can be sent for care." Article 28 provides that where a suspected mental-disorder patient has already harmed themselves or endangered others' safety, or is in danger of doing so, their close relatives, employer and the local police shall immediately take measures to stop it and send them to a medical institution for a psychiatric diagnosis. A medical institution receiving such a delivery may not refuse to diagnose. So "sent in for diagnosis" and "kept for hospitalization" are two gates - the first low, the second high. Article 75 lists medical institutions' liabilities: unlawful restraint or isolation, or diagnosing a person without a mental disorder as having one, brings sanctions up to revocation of the practice certificate. What to do when someone near you has urges to hurt people is in chapter 8.

### 4. The person caring for a disabled family member long-term needs their own body watched
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Hand off part of the care work and get yourself a checkup.
- TL;DR: Caregivers collapsing is not weakness. A US study followed over eight hundred elderly people for four years. Those caring for a disabled spouse while reporting mental strain had about sixty percent higher death risk than non-caregivers. Those who cared but reported no strain showed no such rise. So what needs watching is not the caring itself - it's the long-term tension.
- Benefit: The study was a prospective population cohort, 1993 to 1998, average follow-up 4.5 years, in four US communities. 392 caregivers and 427 non-caregivers, aged 66 to 96, all living with a spouse. Over four years, 103 people (12.6%) died. After adjusting for sociodemographic factors, existing disease and subclinical cardiovascular disease, those who cared and reported mental or emotional strain had a death risk 63% above the non-caregiving controls (RR 1.63, 95% CI 1.00-2.65, the credible range). Those who cared without reporting strain (RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.61-1.90) and those with a disabled spouse who did not themselves care (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.73-2.58) showed no adjusted rise in mortality
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Schulz R, Beach SR (1999). Caregiving as a risk factor for mortality: the Caregiver Health Effects Study. JAMA, 282(23):2215-2219. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.282.23.2215>
- Notes: Caveat: this study's credible-range lower bound sits right on 1.00 - the possibility of "no difference" isn't fully excluded. The population was US elderly living with a spouse, caring for a disabled elderly spouse; stretching it to people caring for a young disabled family member is extrapolation. What it establishes is the direction: the strained part of the group carries higher risk. Hand off whatever care work can be handed off - ask the local disabled persons' federation and civil affairs about residential and day-care services. Don't stop your own chronic-disease medication - entry 1 of chapter 16 (take medication as prescribed, in full). This entry's beneficiary is you.

### 5. Long-term wheelchair users: switch to a pressure-relief cushion, and look at the sit bones and tailbone once a day
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A pressure-relief cushion, a few hundred to over a thousand yuan. One daily look, one minute.
- TL;DR: An ordinary foam cushion pressed long enough breaks the skin. Once broken, it heals slowly and infects easily. In a nursing-home trial, the group on pressure-relief cushions had markedly fewer pressure ulcers at the sit bones. The wheelchair itself must also be fitted to the person's dimensions - a generic model off the shelf won't do.
- Benefit: This randomized clinical trial ran in 12 nursing homes, enrolling 232 residents aged 65+ who sat in a wheelchair over 6 hours daily with a Braden score under 18, from 2004 to 2008. Everyone first got a properly fitted wheelchair, then was randomized into two groups. One used skin-protection cushions (air, viscous fluid plus foam, or gel plus foam); the other used 7.6 cm segmented foam cushions. Followed 6 months or until a pressure ulcer occurred. Pressure ulcers at the ischial tuberosities: 8 cases (6.7%) in the segmented-foam group, 1 case (0.9%) in the skin-protection group, P=0.04. Combining ischial and sacrococcygeal ulcers: 21 cases (17.6%) segmented foam, 12 (10.6%) skin-protection, P=0.14 - no difference shown
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Brienza D, Kelsey S, Karg P, et al. (2010). A randomized clinical trial on preventing pressure ulcers with wheelchair seat cushions. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 58(12):2308-2314. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2010.03168.x>
- Notes: Two reasons for grade B. One, the sample was only 232, the primary endpoint's P of 0.04 barely crossed the line, and the secondary endpoint showed no difference. Two, the population was elderly nursing-home residents; stretching it to young wheelchair users is extrapolation. Everyone in the trial first got a fitted wheelchair, so "fitted" and "cushion" worked together - don't just swap the cushion. Pressure-ulcer protection for the long-term bedridden - air mattresses and scheduled turning - is in entry 8 of chapter 17 (pressure ulcers). How to apply for the basic assistive-device subsidy: entry 7 of this chapter (ask the county disabled persons' federation for everything).

### 6. Don't buy therapies or devices that claim to "cure paralysis, blindness or deafness"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - it saves money. The hard part is family members holding onto hope and refusing to be talked out of it.
- TL;DR: The Advertising Law says it dead-flat. Ads for medical services, drugs and medical devices may not guarantee efficacy, may not state cure or effectiveness rates, and may not use people giving testimonials. So "stand up in three courses of treatment" and "this many already recovered" are illegal ads on their face - no need to study the principle they claim. Something that isn't a medical device may not even mention treating disease.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Advertising Law provides that ads for medical services, drugs and medical devices may not contain: "(1) assertions or guarantees of efficacy or safety; (2) statements of cure or effectiveness rates; (3) comparisons with the efficacy or safety of other drugs or medical devices, or with other medical institutions; (4) recommendations or endorsements by advertising spokespersons." Article 17 provides that outside medical, drug and medical-device ads, "all other ads are prohibited from involving disease-treatment functions, and may not use medical terminology or wording liable to confuse the promoted product with drugs or medical devices." Article 4 provides that ads may not contain false or misleading content and may not deceive or mislead consumers, with the advertiser responsible for the truth of the ad's content (nationwide, amended 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China (amended 2021, Articles 4, 16, 17). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817ab231eb017abd6bd860052d>
- Notes: There is exactly one way to judge: see whether it dares to speak in absolutes. The rehab department of a proper hospital won't promise you'll stand after a few courses. Report such marketing to 12315 or the local market regulator. The supplement sales pitch runs the same playbook - entry 10 of chapter 6 (supplements). Already bought and want your money back: take the online-shopping and prepaid-consumption route - entry 29 of chapter 5 (online shopping follows platform rules). This entry's beneficiaries are you and your family.

### 7. Once the disability certificate is in hand, go to the county disabled persons' federation and ask about everything you can apply for in one visit
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to the county disabled persons' federation, one morning of asking. More than one department will need visits afterward.
- TL;DR: The disability certificate itself pays nothing - it's a key. Behind it hang several independent benefits, each of which must be applied for separately; none comes to your door on its own. On your first visit to the federation, work down the list item by item - it saves round trips.
- Benefit: At least six benefits connect to the certificate, each with its own source. One, the living subsidy for disabled people in difficulty and the care subsidy for the severely disabled - entry 8 of chapter 7 (two subsidies). Two, rehabilitation aid for disabled children - entry 8 of this chapter (rehabilitation aid). Three, the basic assistive-device subsidy, resting on Article 26 of the Regulation on disability prevention and rehabilitation. Four, the home accessibility-modification subsidy - entry 9 of this chapter (ramps, grab bars and bathroom modifications). Five, quota employment and employment services - entry 10 of this chapter (state your certificate proactively). Six, individual income-tax reduction - entry 11 of this chapter (tax reduction). Article 26 of the Regulation on disability prevention and rehabilitation also states that the state provides basic rehabilitation services to urban and rural poor disabled people and the severely disabled through key rehabilitation projects, "subsidizing basic assistive-device provision under relevant state provisions." Article 20 provides that the relevant departments of people's governments at or above county level and the disabled persons' federations shall use community resources to set up rehabilitation venues or purchase services, carrying out community rehabilitation work: rehabilitation guidance, daily-living-skills training, rehabilitation nursing, assistive-device provision, information consultation and referral (nationwide, effective July 1, 2017)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2017). Regulation on disability prevention and rehabilitation (Articles 20, 26). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Notes: The certificate application itself is in entry 11 of chapter 24 (disability certificate). Subsidy standards and detailed rules are mostly set at provincial level - the same item can differ several-fold between two provinces. When asking, write down each item's name, then look up the original documents by name on the local federation and government websites at home - don't rely only on the counter's spoken word. Don't expect to get everything in one visit; go back and ask again a year later - policies change.

### 8. A child under 7 with a disability or autism: apply for rehabilitation aid at the county disabled persons' federation
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One application at the county federation, then pick one from the designated institutions.
- TL;DR: The state built a rehabilitation-aid system for disabled children and children with autism aged 0 to 6, covering surgery, assistive devices and rehabilitation training. Aid goes to families in difficulty first; localities whose finances allow may relax the age and family-income conditions. This stretch is the window where rehabilitation works best - don't drag it until school age. Choose institutions from the federation's designated list.
- Benefit: The State Council's Opinion on establishing a rehabilitation-aid system for disabled children states the aid targets: eligible disabled children aged 0-6 with visual, hearing, speech, physical or intellectual disabilities and children with autism. It includes disabled children in urban and rural subsistence-allowance families and registered poor-household families, disabled children raised by child-welfare institutions, disabled orphans, disabled children in extreme-poverty support, and disabled children of other families in financial difficulty - the specific determination rules for "other families in financial difficulty" are made by local people's governments at or above county level. The document also states "localities with the means may expand the age range of rehabilitation aid for disabled children, and may relax the family-income conditions for aid recipients." Funding standards are determined by category - local finances, number of recipients, disability type - by local people's governments at or above county level, with a dynamic adjustment mechanism. The higher-level basis is Article 26 of the Regulation on disability prevention and rehabilitation: the state establishes a rehabilitation-aid system for disabled children, "gradually achieving free surgery, assistive-device provision and rehabilitation training services for disabled children aged 0-6 with visual, hearing, speech, physical and intellectual disabilities and children with autism" (nationwide, from 2018)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2018). Opinion on establishing a rehabilitation-aid system for disabled children (Guofa [2018] No. 20). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-07/10/content_5305296.htm>; State Council (2017). Regulation on disability prevention and rehabilitation (Article 26). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Notes: Aid standards are set by each province - first search "implementation measures for rehabilitation aid for disabled children" on the local federation's website. The designated-institution list is also held by the federation. The document requires the federation to strengthen entry-and-exit supervision of designated rehabilitation institutions with the relevant departments, building regular inspection and comprehensive assessment mechanisms plus a blacklist system. An institution not on the list means paying everything yourself, and trouble afterward is hard to pursue. This entry's beneficiary is your child - the second tier. An institution promising "guaranteed cure" is handled per entry 6 of this chapter (curing paralysis).

### 9. Ramps, grab bars and bathroom modifications at home: you can apply for a subsidy from the government at or above county level
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Applying is free. The modification itself is mostly subsidized, with part possibly out of pocket. Expect trips to the federation and the subdistrict.
- TL;DR: The Accessibility Environment Construction Law writes it in one clause: governments at or above county level shall support and guide home accessibility modifications, and shall give appropriate subsidies to eligible families with disabled members or elderly people. These projects are usually led by the federation with annual quotas - register early. How much and which items are set locally.
- Benefit: Article 19 of the Accessibility Environment Construction Law: "people's governments at or above county level shall support and guide home accessibility modifications. Families with disabled members or elderly people who meet the conditions shall be given appropriate subsidies." Article 18 provides that for existing residences, residential areas, public buildings, public places, transport facilities and urban-rural roads that don't meet accessibility engineering standards, people's governments at or above county level shall make targeted accessibility-modification plans according to actual conditions and organize their implementation. Article 62 provides that any organization or individual has the right to offer opinions and suggestions to the competent government departments on strengthening and improving accessibility-environment construction, and to complain or report violations of this law; the competent department receiving a complaint "shall handle it promptly and give a reply." Article 63 provides that for violations of this law harming the public interest, the people's procuratorate may issue a procuratorial suggestion or bring a public-interest lawsuit (nationwide, effective September 1, 2023)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Accessibility Environment Construction Law of the People's Republic of China (Articles 18, 19, 62, 63). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>
- Notes: Grade B because the law writes only "shall give appropriate subsidies" - how much and who qualifies are entirely left to localities, with no unified national figure. First search "home accessibility modification for families of severely disabled people in difficulty" on the local federation's website. Quotas are mostly allocated by year - queue early. Adding elevators to the residential compound and modifying stairwells is a different process, run through the housing-construction department. For substandard accessibility in public places, complain to the competent department under Article 62 - it owes you a reply.

### 10. When job hunting, state your certificate proactively - hiring you offsets a real sum for the employer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One extra sentence in the interview. The hard part is opening your mouth.
- TL;DR: An employer's share of disabled employees must not fall below 1.5% of its total staff, with the specific ratio set by the province. Falling short means paying the disabled-employment security fund on the shortfall, computed per head at the employer's average annual wage last year. So hiring you isn't charity - it's a real saving. State your certificate plainly in the interview; the other side can do this math itself.
- Benefit: Article 8 of the Regulation on the employment of persons with disabilities: "the proportion of disabled persons employed by an employer shall not be lower than 1.5% of its total in-service staff. The specific proportion is prescribed by the people's governments of provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities according to local conditions." Article 9: falling short of the provincial ratio means paying the disabled-employment security fund. Article 8 of the Measures for the collection and use of the disabled-employment security fund gives the formula: "annual fund payable = (number of the employer's in-service staff last year × the disabled-employment ratio prescribed by the provincial people's government - number of disabled persons actually employed last year) × the employer's average annual wage of in-service staff last year." Article 16: within 3 years of business registration, small and micro enterprises with 20 or fewer staff (20 included) that haven't met the ratio are exempt from the fund. Article 36 of the Law on the protection of persons with disabilities provides that the state gives tax preferences under law to employers meeting or exceeding the prescribed proportion, or employing disabled persons in concentration (nationwide, the employment regulation effective May 1, 2007)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2007). Regulation on the employment of persons with disabilities (Articles 8, 9). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6646.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Administration of Taxation and China Disabled Persons' Federation (2015). Measures for the collection and use of the disabled-employment security fund (Caishui [2015] No. 72, Articles 6, 8, 16). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2015-09/15/content_5650063.htm>; Law on the protection of persons with disabilities of the People's Republic of China (Article 36). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Notes: For small and micro enterprises within three years of registration and at or under 20 staff, this argument doesn't hold - don't count on it. The fund's reduction policies have kept being adjusted in recent years; follow the finance ministry's current-year announcement. Also, quota employment demands real work. Drawing a salary in name only so the employer can use you to offset the fund is a fraudulent taking - you get pulled in too. The employment-difficulty determination and social-insurance subsidy side is in entry 12 of chapter 7 (people with employment difficulties). Vocational-training subsidies are in entry 13 of chapter 7 (vocational-training subsidies). This entry's beneficiary is you.

### 11. Disabled people's individual income tax can be reduced - call the provincial tax bureau to ask how much
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Tick one item at filing, or submit one set of filing materials.
- TL;DR: The Individual Income Tax Law states that disabled people's income may be taxed at a reduced rate. But how much and for how long - the law hands to provincial governments, so every province differs. Some set an annual reduction cap, some reduce by a proportion. The way: call 12366 to ask the local tax bureau, or find the reduction item in the individual income-tax app.
- Benefit: Article 5 of the Individual Income Tax Law: "in any of the following situations, individual income tax may be reduced, with the specific extent and period prescribed by the people's governments of provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities and filed with the standing committees of the people's congresses at the same level: (1) income of persons with disabilities, solitary elderly persons and families of martyrs; (2) heavy losses suffered in natural disasters." Article 36 of the Law on the protection of persons with disabilities additionally provides that the state gives tax preferences under law to disabled persons in individual business, and exempts them from administrative and institutional fees (nationwide, the Individual Income Tax Law effective January 1, 2019)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Individual Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (amended 2018, Article 5). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf724bd0609>; Law on the protection of persons with disabilities of the People's Republic of China (Article 36). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Notes: The benefit grades "small" because the law itself gives no number - most provinces' reductions run a few hundred to a few thousand yuan a year. Not much money, but it's done once and lasts - worth the trip. What your province specifically reduces: only the local tax bureau's announcement counts; comparison tables circulating online are perennially out of date. Running your own small shop: administrative and institutional fees are exempted under Article 36 of the protection law - mention it proactively when getting the license.

### 12. Guide dogs may be brought into public places and public transport, and blind people ride city buses free
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Being stopped means opening your mouth and citing the law, possibly standing your ground a while.
- TL;DR: The law states that management units of public places, transport facilities and public-transport operators shall facilitate disabled persons bringing guide dogs, hearing dogs and assistance dogs. Also: blind people with a valid certificate ride city buses, trams, subways and ferries free, and disabled people may carry necessary assistive devices free. Being stopped: say these two clauses out loud; if that fails, complain.
- Benefit: Article 46 of the Accessibility Environment Construction Law: "management units of public places, transport facilities and public-transport operators shall provide convenience for disabled persons bringing guide dogs, hearing dogs, assistance dogs and other service dogs." Article 35 provides that government-service convenience hotlines and emergency call systems - police, fire, traffic accidents, medical emergencies - shall gradually gain accessible functions such as voice, large print, Braille and one-touch calling. Article 62 provides that any organization or individual has the right to complain and report violations of this law, and the competent department shall handle it promptly and give a reply. Article 50 of the Law on the protection of persons with disabilities: "blind persons with a valid certificate ride public buses, trams, subways, ferries and other public transport within the city free of charge. Mail of reading matter for the blind is delivered free." The same article provides that people's governments at or above county level shall give disabled persons convenience and preference in public transport, and that disabled persons may carry necessary personal assistive devices free (nationwide, the accessibility law effective September 1, 2023)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Accessibility Environment Construction Law of the People's Republic of China (Articles 35, 46, 62). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>; Law on the protection of persons with disabilities of the People's Republic of China (Article 50). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Notes: The law says "provide convenience" with no penalty attached - so the other side may not yield at once in a standoff. A screenshot of the provision saved on your phone beats reciting article numbers. If it can't be settled on the spot, complain under Article 62 - the competent department must reply. Rail and civil aviation have their own separate rules; call their customer service before traveling. What counts as the "valid certificate" for free rides isn't recognized identically everywhere - ask the local federation which kind it recognizes.

### 13. Disabled candidates taking the national college entrance exam can apply for reasonable accommodations - Braille papers get 50% extra time
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. One written application within the province's time window, plus an on-site confirmation.
- TL;DR: The college entrance exam has an accommodation scheme for disabled candidates, from a document by the Ministry of Education and the China Disabled Persons' Federation. Braille papers, large-print papers, waiving the foreign-language listening test, extended time, a separate exam room, bringing hearing aids and wheelchairs in - all inside. None of it comes automatically: a formal written application must be filed by the provincial exam authority's time and method, with a second-generation-or-later disability certificate. Objecting to the outcome, you may apply to the provincial education authority for review.
- Benefit: Article 5 of the Provisions on the administration of persons with disabilities taking the national unified college entrance examination lists twelve reasonable accommodations. They include providing visually impaired candidates with current Braille papers, large-print papers or ordinary papers; waiving the foreign-language listening test for hearing-impaired candidates; allowing visually impaired candidates to bring Braille pens, Braille slates, storage-free Braille typewriters, storage-free electronic visual aids, white canes and optical magnifiers; allowing hearing-impaired candidates to bring hearing aids and cochlear implants; allowing candidates with impaired mobility to use wheelchairs and walkers, with special desks and chairs brought in for special needs. The extension amounts are written in Article 5, item 6: "visually impaired candidates using Braille papers receive 50% extra time on top of the subject's prescribed total; visually impaired candidates using large-print or ordinary papers, and candidates with particular writing difficulty such as those with upper limbs unable to write normally due to cerebral palsy or other diseases, or without upper limbs, receive 30% extra time." There are also priority entry to the test site and room, a separate standardized exam room, and guide assistants and sign-language interpreters. Article 7 sets the application procedure: the candidate files a formal written application by the provincial exam authority's time, place and method, providing their second-generation-or-later disability certificate; an expert group confirms on site and assesses comprehensively, and the provincial exam authority issues a Notice. Article 8 provides that a candidate objecting to the Notice may apply in writing to the provincial education authority for review. Article 9 provides that when the foreign-language listening test is waived, the foreign-language score is computed as "written-test score × total foreign-language value / total written-part value." Article 18 provides that disabled persons taking other national education examinations may apply for reasonable accommodations with reference to these provisions (nationwide, issued and effective April 7, 2017)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Education and China Disabled Persons' Federation (2017). Provisions on the administration of persons with disabilities taking the national unified college entrance examination (Jiaoxue [2017] No. 4, Articles 5, 7, 8, 9, 18). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-04/12/content_5650060.htm>
- Notes: The application time is set by each province, written into the year's exam-registration rules - miss it and it's gone. So confirm the time on the provincial exam institute's website in the fall of the final school year. Article 18 opens an extension: graduate admissions, the adult college exam and other national education examinations can also be applied for by reference. The rule that schools may not refuse admission at the schooling stage is in entry 14 of this chapter (schools may not refuse). This entry's beneficiary is you or your child.

### 14. A school may not refuse a disabled child who applies; for those who can't reach school, the education bureau arranges teaching at home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One application, possibly one assessment. When the school stalls, go up to the education bureau.
- TL;DR: The regulation states plainly: preschool institutions and schools of all kinds and levels may not refuse admission to a disabled person who applies and meets the conditions. The same for ordinary vocational schools. School-age disabled children who need dedicated nursing and can't reach school receive compulsory education through home-visit teaching or distance education arranged by the county education authority - with enrollment status managed. How enrollment and transfer are arranged is decided after assessment by an expert committee on education of persons with disabilities commissioned by the county education bureau - not by the school itself.
- Benefit: Article 7 of the Regulation on the education of persons with disabilities: "preschool institutions, schools of all kinds and levels and other educational institutions shall implement education for persons with disabilities under this regulation and relevant state laws and regulations; they may not refuse admission to persons with disabilities who apply and meet the conditions prescribed by laws and regulations." Article 29: "ordinary vocational schools may not refuse admission to persons with disabilities meeting the state-prescribed admission standards, and ordinary vocational-training institutions shall actively admit persons with disabilities." The regulation also provides that school-age disabled children and adolescents needing dedicated nursing and unable to attend school receive compulsory education through home-visit teaching or distance education arranged by the county people's government's education department, with enrollment status managed. Article 20 provides that the county education department shall, together with the health, civil-affairs departments and the disabled persons' federation, establish an expert committee on education of persons with disabilities composed of experts in education, psychology, rehabilitation and social work, which assesses physical condition, ability to receive education and ability to adapt to school life, and makes enrollment and transfer recommendations; the assessment results are the child's privacy, and the units and staff involved bear a duty of confidentiality. Article 23 provides that disabled students learning in regular classes at ordinary schools may follow the ordinary compulsory-education curriculum plan, standards and textbooks, but with appropriate flexibility in learning requirements. Article 52 provides that disabled persons taking national education examinations may apply for necessary support conditions and reasonable accommodations (nationwide, effective May 1, 2017)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2017). Regulation on the education of persons with disabilities (Articles 7, 20, 23, 29, 52). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/23/content_5170264.htm>
- Notes: A school saying "we don't have the conditions here" out loud doesn't count. Demand a written reply, and take that written reply to the county education bureau. The assessment is done by the expert committee the education bureau commissions. Article 23's phrase "learning requirements may have appropriate flexibility" is useful - cite it when a school uses grades as a reason to push the child out. How to keep evidence when a child is bullied at school, and which procedure the school must follow: entry 3 of chapter 30 (school bullying). Exam accommodations for the college entrance exam: entry 13 of this chapter (reasonable accommodations). This entry's beneficiary is your child.

### 15. Missing a right leg or both legs, you can still get a driver's license - the class is called C5
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Tuition about the same as an ordinary license - a few thousand yuan in most places. The car must be converted to hand controls. A physical exam every three years.
- TL;DR: The public security ministry's rules have a dedicated vehicle class: "small automatic passenger car dedicated for persons with disabilities," code C5. People missing a right lower limb or both lower limbs, or having lost their motor function, who can sit upright unaided and whose upper limbs qualify may apply. Those missing one palm have a route too. Monocular vision impairment and hearing impairment each have their corresponding classes. The physical exam must be done at a dedicated medical institution recognized by the provincial health authority.
- Benefit: Annex 1 of the Provisions on applying for and using motor-vehicle driver's licenses writes the physical conditions in detail. Item 8: "a person with a missing right lower limb or missing both lower limbs, or lost motor function thereof, who can sit upright unaided, and whose upper limbs meet item 5 of this paragraph, may apply for a driver's license for the small automatic passenger car dedicated for persons with disabilities. A person missing one palm, with the other hand's thumb intact and two other fingers intact, normal upper-limb and finger motor function, and lower limbs meeting item 6 of this paragraph, may apply for a driver's license for the small automatic passenger car dedicated for persons with disabilities." Item 6: a person with only the left lower limb missing or its motor function lost, with a normal right lower limb, may apply for the small automatic-car class. Vision: "monocular vision impairment, with the better eye's uncorrected or corrected visual acuity reaching 5.0 or above on the logarithmic chart, and a horizontal visual field of 150 degrees," may apply for small cars, small automatic cars, low-speed trucks, three-wheeled vehicles and the dedicated small automatic class. Hearing: a person with hearing impairment who, wearing a hearing device, can tell the direction of sound sources, may apply for small-car and small-automatic classes - wearing the device while driving. Article 85 provides that the physical-condition certificate submitted for the dedicated small automatic class must be issued by a dedicated medical institution recognized by the provincial health authority. Holders of this class undergo a physical exam every three years, submitting the physical-condition certificate within 30 days after the scoring period ends. Article 78 provides that when driving, the dedicated disabled-person motor-vehicle sign must be displayed on the vehicle as required (nationwide, Ministry of Public Security Order No. 172 as amended, effective January 1, 2025)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Public Security Order No. 172. Provisions on applying for and using motor-vehicle driver's licenses (Articles 78, 85, Annex 1). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11866/202502/content_7004031.html>
- Notes: A much lower-threshold route is the motorized wheelchair for disabled persons. Article 119 of the Road Traffic Safety Law classes it as a non-motor vehicle, so no license is needed; Article 58 caps its speed in the non-motor-vehicle lane at 15 km/h. A C5 car needs hand-operated throttle and brakes - the conversion must be done by a qualified manufacturer, followed by a change registration at the vehicle office. Failing to submit the physical-condition certificate within one year after three scoring periods end gets the license canceled.

### 16. Do rehabilitation at a proper institution with a rehabilitation-medicine department, at the intensity the therapist sets - harder is not better
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Inpatient rehabilitation runs a few hundred yuan a day, with the out-of-pocket part varying by place. Time is counted in months. Daily practice, very boring.
- TL;DR: Taking the person home to lie in bed and wait for natural recovery is the most common mistake of this stretch. Organized multidisciplinary team care leaves more people alive a year later and still living in their own homes. But don't swing to the opposite extreme and add intensity yourself. In a two-thousand-person trial, earlier, more frequent and heavier activity within 24 hours of a stroke produced worse outcomes three months later. How much to practice and when - follow the rehab department and the therapist.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review included 29 trials and 5,902 participants, comparing organized inpatient stroke-unit care against other care. At final follow-up (median 1 year), the odds ratio for poor outcomes (death, disability or needing institutional care) was 0.77 (95% CI 0.69-0.87, the credible range); for death, 0.76 (95% CI 0.66-0.88); for death or dependency, 0.75 (95% CI 0.66-0.85) - moderate-quality evidence. In absolute numbers: per 100 people receiving stroke-unit care, 2 more survive, 6 more live at home, 6 more live independently. On the other side, the AVERT trial randomized 2,104 patients across 56 acute stroke units in 5 countries, comparing "usual care" against "usual care plus very early intensive mobilization." At three months, good outcomes (modified Rankin scale 0-2): 480 people (46%) in the very-early group, 525 (50%) in the usual-care group - adjusted odds ratio 0.73 (95% CI 0.59-0.90, P=0.004). Deaths: 88 versus 72 - no difference shown (odds ratio 1.34, 95% CI 0.93-1.93, P=0.113)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Langhorne P, Ramachandra S; Stroke Unit Trialists' Collaboration (2020). Organised inpatient (stroke unit) care for stroke: network meta-analysis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 4:CD000197. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000197.pub4>; AVERT Trial Collaboration group (2015). Efficacy and safety of very early mobilisation within 24 h of stroke onset (AVERT): a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 386(9988):46-55. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)60690-0>
- Notes: Caveat: both bodies of evidence come from stroke; stretching them to spinal cord injury, amputation or burns is extrapolation. What AVERT establishes is that "earlier and harder" doesn't hold - not that "early activity is useless." In the trial, the great majority in both groups got out of bed within 24 hours; what differed was frequency and total amount. When choosing an institution, check it has a rehabilitation-medicine department with dedicated physical and occupational therapists - don't read only the word "rehab" on the sign. How referrals work between grass-roots and tertiary hospitals and how deductibles count: entry 1 of chapter 24 (stepped referral). The community-rehabilitation and assistive-device side: entry 7 of this chapter (ask the county disabled persons' federation for everything).

### 17. When hearing declines, get a hearing aid - the reason is hearing now, not preventing dementia
<!-- tags: money=high time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Hearing aids run from a few thousand to tens of thousands of yuan, mostly out of pocket. They need fitting and several rounds of adjustment.
- TL;DR: The claim "hearing aids prevent dementia" hasn't held up so far. A trial of nearly a thousand people over three years showed no difference overall. A difference appeared only in the subgroup already at higher risk of cognitive decline. So the reason to get a hearing aid is hearing right now - talking with people, hearing the car when crossing the road - not the brain decades from now.
- Benefit: The ACHIEVE trial ran in 4 US communities, enrolling 977 people aged 70 to 84 with untreated hearing loss and no significant cognitive impairment. Randomized into two groups: one received a hearing intervention (hearing counseling and hearing-aid fitting), the other a health-education control, followed every 6 months. The primary endpoint was the 3-year change in global cognition score. In the primary analysis pooling both recruitment sources, the two groups showed no significant difference: hearing intervention -0.200 (95% CI -0.256 to -0.144), control -0.202 (95% CI -0.258 to -0.145), difference 0.002 (95% CI -0.077 to 0.081), P=0.96. A prespecified sensitivity analysis showed the intervention effect differed between the two recruitment sources (interaction P=0.010). Participants from the long-term cardiovascular-health observation study were older, carried more risk factors for cognitive decline, and scored lower at baseline. The researchers' conclusion: the hearing intervention may reduce 3-year cognitive change in populations at higher risk of cognitive decline, but not in lower-risk ones. The trial reported no serious adverse events attributed to the study
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lin FR, Pike JR, Albert MS, et al.; ACHIEVE Collaborative Research Group (2023). Hearing intervention versus health education control to reduce cognitive decline in older adults with hearing loss in the USA (ACHIEVE): a multicentre, randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 402(10404):786-797. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01406-X>
- Notes: Caveat: the "hearing aids cut cognitive decline by nearly half" line common online comes from a subgroup of this trial, not its primary result - the primary result was no difference between the groups. This entry sits here to separate "get a hearing aid" from "prevent dementia": the former should be done; the latter shouldn't be the reason. Hearing-impaired exam candidates may have the foreign-language listening test waived - entry 13 of this chapter (reasonable accommodations). People with hearing impairment should wear a hearing device when driving - entry 15 of this chapter (C5).

### 18. Declaring an adult "unable to manage their own affairs" takes a court application - the family doesn't get to decide
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: The court application carries a filing fee, usually tens to a hundred yuan. The appraisal fee is separate, usually a few thousand, advanced by the applicant. Months to complete.
- TL;DR: Whether an adult is a "person without civil capacity" or a "person with limited civil capacity" can only be determined by a court. Family, employer, residents' committee - none gets to decide on its own. An interested party or a relevant organization applies to the court. It also runs the other way: if intelligence or mental health recovers, an application can have capacity restored. This procedure is both a restriction and a protection.
- Benefit: Article 21 of the Civil Code: "an adult unable to recognize their own conduct is a person without civil capacity; civil juristic acts are performed on their behalf by their statutory agent." Article 22: "an adult unable to fully recognize their own conduct is a person with limited civil capacity; civil juristic acts are performed by their statutory agent on their behalf, or with their statutory agent's consent or ratification - except that they may independently perform civil juristic acts purely beneficial to them, or suited to their intelligence and mental-health condition." Article 24: "for an adult unable to recognize, or unable to fully recognize, their own conduct, an interested party or a relevant organization may apply to the people's court to have the adult determined as a person without civil capacity or with limited civil capacity." The same article provides that after the determination, on application by the person, an interested party or a relevant organization, the people's court may, according to the recovery of their intelligence and mental health, determine them restored to limited or full civil capacity. Relevant organizations include: residents' committees, villagers' committees, schools, medical institutions, women's federations, disabled persons' federations, lawfully established senior-citizen organizations, civil-affairs departments and others. Article 23 provides that the guardian of a person without or with limited civil capacity is their statutory agent (nationwide, effective January 1, 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (Articles 21, 22, 23, 24). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: The process costs must be written clearly. The application goes through the court's special procedure - faster than an ordinary civil case, but still months. A hospital diagnostic certificate must be submitted, and the court usually commissions a forensic appraisal, whose fee - usually a few thousand yuan - is advanced by the applicant. Signing for someone or disposing of their property without this procedure can later be ruled invalid - a sold apartment may have to be handed back. One more thing to remember: limited civil capacity doesn't mean incapable of everything. Acts suited to their intelligence and mental condition, and acts purely beneficial, they can do themselves - don't take everything over. How the guardian is set: entry 19 of this chapter (guardian).

### 19. An adult's guardian follows a statutory order; if the ward injures someone, the guardian pays
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Disputes are settled at the residents' committee, villagers' committee, civil-affairs department or court.
- TL;DR: After an adult loses civil capacity, the guardian follows this order: spouse; parents and children; other close relatives; last, any other individual or organization willing to serve. If a fight breaks out over who serves, the residents' committee, villagers' committee or civil-affairs department of the ward's residence designates one - and the designation can be challenged in court. Running the other way: while still lucid, a person can designate their future guardian in writing. If the ward injures someone, the guardian pays.
- Benefit: Article 28 of the Civil Code: for an adult without or with limited civil capacity, the following persons with guardianship capacity serve as guardian in order: "(1) spouse; (2) parents, children; (3) other close relatives; (4) any other individual or organization willing to serve as guardian, subject to consent by the residents' committee, villagers' committee or civil-affairs department of the ward's residence." Article 31: disputes over the guardian's determination are designated by the residents' committee, villagers' committee or civil-affairs department of the ward's residence; objecting to the designation, one may apply to the people's court for designation - or apply directly to the court. The designation "shall respect the ward's true wishes and follow the principle of the ward's best interests." Where rights and interests sit unprotected before the designation, the residents' committee, villagers' committee, an organization designated by law or the civil-affairs department serves as temporary guardian. Article 33: "an adult with full civil capacity may consult in advance with their close relatives or other individuals or organizations willing to serve as guardian, and determine their guardian in written form." Article 1188: "where a person without or with limited civil capacity causes harm to another, the guardian bears tort liability. Where the guardian has fulfilled their guardianship duties, their tort liability may be mitigated." If the ward has property, the compensation is paid from it, with any shortfall paid by the guardian. Article 1189: where the guardian entrusts the guardianship duties to another person, the guardian still bears the tort liability; the entrusted person bears corresponding liability if at fault (nationwide, effective January 1, 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (Articles 28, 31, 33, 1188, 1189). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: Article 33 is intentional guardianship. Choosing the person in writing while lucid saves most of the later disputes - how to write it is in entry 1 of chapter 17 (intentional guardianship). Be careful with the compensation part: leaving the person in a care institution or with relatives doesn't move the liability - it stays with the guardian, and the entrusted party answers only for its own fault. So buying guardian-liability insurance for the ward makes sense. Article 78 of the Mental Health Law also provides that a guardian who abandons the patient or fails their guardianship duties, causing harm, bears compensation liability under law.

### 20. Refused hiring, confirmation or full pay because of a disability: you can sue - but count the process costs first
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=yes benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Attorney's fees of a few thousand to tens of thousands of yuan, out of your own pocket. First-instance ordinary procedure: 6 months and up, extendable. Evidence is yours to keep.
- TL;DR: The law prohibits discriminating against disabled people in recruitment, confirmation, promotion, professional-title evaluation, pay and social insurance. You can complain to the competent authority, or sue in court directly. But count first: winning doesn't mean the other side covers your attorney's fees. Attorney's fees are not among the litigation costs the law lists, and the "loser pays" clause doesn't cover them. So complain first, apply for legal aid first - then consider litigation.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Law on the protection of persons with disabilities: "discrimination based on disability is prohibited. Insulting and infringing upon persons with disabilities is prohibited." Article 38: "in recruitment, confirmation, promotion, professional-title evaluation, labor remuneration, welfare benefits, rest and leave, and social insurance of staff, there shall be no discrimination against persons with disabilities." The same article provides that the employer of disabled staff shall provide suitable working conditions and labor protection according to their characteristics, and modify workplaces, work equipment and living facilities as actually needed. Article 64: "violating this law by discriminating against persons with disabilities in recruitment and the like shall be ordered corrected by the competent department; the disabled worker may bring a lawsuit in the people's court under law." Article 4 of the Regulation on the employment of persons with disabilities: "discrimination against persons with disabilities in employment is prohibited." Article 13: employers may not discriminate against disabled staff in promotion, advancement, professional-title evaluation, remuneration, social insurance and welfare benefits. Article 42 of the Legal Aid Law provides that an applicant with materials proving they belong to a specific group - "minors, elderly people, persons with disabilities and others without a fixed source of livelihood" - is exempt from the financial-difficulty check. Article 45 provides that legal-aid institutions providing services to elderly people and persons with disabilities shall provide accessible facilities, equipment and services as actually needed (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Law on the protection of persons with disabilities of the People's Republic of China (Articles 3, 38, 64). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>; State Council (2007). Regulation on the employment of persons with disabilities (Articles 4, 13). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6646.htm>; Legal Aid Law of the People's Republic of China (Articles 42, 45). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817b6472a3017b6577820f0061>

So take the two low-cost routes first: complain to the human-resources and social-security department or the disabled persons' federation, and have the competent department order correction; at the same time, apply for legal aid - Article 42 exempts disabled people from the financial-difficulty check. Evidence is the hardest gate. When rejected in an interview, the other side rarely leaves a written reason - record audio and keep chat records where you can.
