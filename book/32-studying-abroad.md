# 32. Studying abroad: status, working, insurance, and recognition back home

This chapter counts two ledgers: money and personal freedom. It doesn't argue whether to go abroad, and doesn't compare schools. It writes only the four things that cost you if unknown: what counts as unbroken visa status, where the cap on work hours sits, which insurance and fees are mandatory, and whether the diploma will be recognized back home.

It covers four countries - the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia - with the figures listed country by country. Sources are the official pages of each country's immigration authority and China's Ministry of Education. **Every country's study-abroad policy changes often, even more often than Chinese regulations. All figures in this chapter are current as of September 2026. Before departure and before every visa renewal, re-check the links in the sources field yourself.**

Finding the embassy or consulate when something happens abroad, what 12308 can do, and how to buy overseas medical and evacuation insurance are in chapter 21. The study-versus-work ledger is in chapter 23. A hijacked account is in chapter 14, telecom fraud in chapter 8. None of that is repeated here.

### 1. Before paying tuition, check whether the school is on the Service Center's list of certifiable institutions
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Type the school's name once into the Service Center's "certifiable institutions query" - a few minutes
- TL;DR: Back home, using this diploma for the civil-service exam, state-owned enterprises, household registration or professional titles requires passing one certification first. The certification is done by the Chinese Service Center for Scholarly Exchange under the Ministry of Education. If the school isn't on the certifiable list, the tuition and the time are wasted - the diploma is unusable in China. Checking the list costs nothing. Check it, then pay the tuition.
- Benefit: The Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange has a certifiable-institutions query. You can search by country or by school name. It checks whether the degree this school issues falls within the certification scope. Separately, the Ministry of Education's foreign-related education supervision site publishes supervision information on Chinese-foreign cooperative education and offshore programs
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange. Certifiable institutions query. <http://yxcx.cscse.edu.cn/>; Ministry of Education foreign-related education supervision information network. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/>
- Notes: Check it yourself online, and go by what you find. An agent saying "Ministry of Education recognized" out loud doesn't count. The list changes. On the list at enrollment doesn't mean on it at graduation - re-check once a year during your studies. Chinese-foreign cooperative programs have their own certification-registration system. Those who enrolled after 2008 can look up their registration number on the foreign-related supervision site with their name and ID number.

### 2. The US changed its rules on September 15, 2026: F-1 student visas are no longer "valid until you finish" - four years maximum, and 30 days to leave after finishing
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Copy two dates into your calendar. One is the program end date on the I-20 - the I-20 is the study-qualification certificate your school issues. One is the admission period on the I-94 - the arrival/departure record immigration gives you at entry. Set a reminder 90 days ahead of each
- TL;DR: F-1 visas used to last however long you studied. Now they come with a fixed period of at most four years, and extending takes a separate application. Once you finish - or stop early - you have only 30 days to prepare to leave or convert to another lawful status. Past the date, you are unlawfully present.
- Benefit: The section of US federal immigration regulations governing foreign students is 8 CFR 214.2(f). Under it, an F-1 student who completes their program, and an F-1 student with approved practical training, has 30 additional days to prepare for departure or to change status lawfully. Those 30 days run from the program end date - or possibly from the four-year maximum admission period, or from the end date of the OPT/STEM OPT work authorization. OPT and STEM OPT are post-graduation practical-training work authorizations under student status. Ending the program or training early: within 30 days of the end date you must depart or find another lawful status. This fixed-period rule was published by the Department of Homeland Security on July 17, 2026 and took effect September 15, 2026
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 214.2(f) (current version). <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2>; DHS (2026). Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors (published 2026-07-17, effective 2026-09-15). <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant>
- Notes: This rule just took effect September 15, 2026. Everything circulating online and in agents' hands is old - like "60-day grace period" and "duration of status, valid until graduation." To extend, you file a separate application under paragraph (f)(7) before the period expires - nothing renews automatically. J visas changed in the same rule, with details different from F-1. Check the original text for your own visa category.

### 3. Work-hour limits are a hard red line written into the rules of all four countries - one hour over is a visa-condition violation
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Record your own hours week by week. The hard part is that employers will say "a few extra hours, nobody checks" - that line can't be trusted
- TL;DR: All four countries cap how much a student visa lets you work. Over the cap is a visa-condition violation: the visa can be canceled, you can be removed, and later applications get questioned. The caps: the US, 20 hours a week; the UK, 20 hours a week at degree level and 10 below; Canada, 24 hours a week; Australia, 48 hours per fortnight. Two jobs count together.
- Benefit: US F-1 students: on-campus work during the term may not exceed 20 hours a week. Approved off-campus part-time work is likewise 20 hours a week. Full-time is allowed during vacations. Basis: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(9). UK student visas: degree-level and above, 20 hours a week during term. Below degree level, 10 hours a week. Part-time courses: no work at all. Also no self-employment - you may not work for yourself - and no professional sport or entertainment work. Basis: the student part of the UK Immigration Rules, Appendix Student ST26.1, ST26.5. Canada: eligible full-time students may work off campus at most 24 hours a week, per IRPR section 186(v). Australia: student visas, at most 48 hours per fortnight while the course is in session. Research master's, doctoral students and their family members are exempt from this limit
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(9). <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2>; UK Home Office. Immigration Rules Appendix Student (ST26.1, ST26.5). <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-student>; IRCC. Work off campus as an international student. <https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html>; Australian Government Department of Home Affairs. Student visa (subclass 500). <https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500>
- Notes: In the US, off-campus work must be approved first. While the I-765 work-authorization application is pending, you may not start. Canada's older permits print 20 hours a week, but as long as you still qualify you may actually work 24 - the current rule governs. The UK's no-self-employment clause reads broadly: freelance gigs, opening an online shop, freelancing all count.

### 4. The root of your status is "enrolled full-time": during suspension, withdrawal or a transfer gap, no work at all
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Before stopping classes, suspending or changing schools, ask the school's international-students office what your status state is
- TL;DR: Whether you may work depends on whether you are currently in full-time study. The moment you suspend, withdraw, are expelled, or are between schools without attending classes, the work eligibility is gone on the spot. Keep working then, and it's illegal work. Many people think an unexpired visa makes it fine - in fact the status broke long ago.
- Benefit: Canada states plainly: no off-campus work during an approved leave. No off-campus work while between schools and not studying. Work may resume only after studies resume. In the US, on-campus work eligibility goes only to F-1 students whose SEVIS status is Active. SEVIS is the US student-status records system; Active means validly enrolled. These students must also maintain a full course load. The UK's work permission is granted by course type; part-time courses may not work
- Evidence: A
- Sources: IRCC. Work off campus as an international student. <https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/work-off-campus.html>; U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Study in the States. Working in the United States. <https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/work/working-in-the-united-states>; UK Home Office. Immigration Rules Appendix Student (ST26.1). <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-student>
- Notes: Under course pressure and thinking of dropping classes? Ask first: at what point do you stop counting as full-time. That breaks before the work question does. Expelled, or your program terminated: the status usually breaks that same day. Don't wait for the school's formal letter before acting.

### 5. United States: report your new address within 10 days of moving
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. One online change-of-address submission, ten minutes
- TL;DR: After moving in the US, the law requires notifying immigration within 10 days. Not reporting is no small thing. One, it's itself a violation. Two, the letters immigration mails you will go astray.
- Benefit: US federal immigration regulation 8 CFR 265.1 provides that aliens required by law to register must report within 10 days of an address change. Report to USCIS - US Citizenship and Immigration Services - by the method it requires, reporting both the fact of the change and the new address
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 8 CFR 265.1. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-265/section-265.1>; USCIS. AR-11, Alien's Change of Address Card. <https://www.uscis.gov/ar-11>
- Notes: Reporting to immigration and reporting to the school are two different things. The SEVIS record at the school - your student-status file - must be updated separately. The other three countries likewise require notifying immigration or the school of an address change. Deadlines and methods differ by country; follow the immigration authority's page of the country you're in.

### 6. Before departure and during your studies, glance at the Ministry of Education's study-abroad alerts
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Once before departure, then once each semester
- TL;DR: The Ministry of Education issues alerts for specific countries or regions, saying recent public order or policy there is unfavorable to Chinese students. This is an officially published risk notice, checkable in black and white - more reliable than an agent's word. A glance costs nothing.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Education publishes study-abroad alerts through the foreign-related education supervision information network. In 2025 it issued 4. No. 1 flagged that a higher-education act passed by a US state contained China-negative clauses (2025-04-09). Nos. 2 and 3 twice flagged frequent cases targeting Chinese citizens in the Philippines and a poor study environment (2025-07-18, 2025-08-30). No. 4 flagged Japan's public-order situation and study environment as poor, advising caution in planning study in Japan (2025-11-16)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Education foreign-related education supervision information network. Alert information. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/>; Ministry of Education's 2025 No. 4 study-abroad alert. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/n2/2/2/2060.shtml>; No. 1. <https://jsj.moe.gov.cn/n2/2/2/2001.shtml>
- Notes: An alert is not a ban, and doesn't say you can't go. Its use is giving you an official basis for weighing that place's risk. The alert list changes with the situation; this section won't track it long-term - go by the current page on the site. Personal safety abroad and consular protection are in chapter 21.

### 7. Australia: OSHC must cover the whole stay with no gap - landing without proof of insurance can mean refused entry
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Several thousand to over ten thousand yuan. Bought in one go for the visa's duration - as many years as you study
- TL;DR: Australia writes student medical insurance into the visa conditions - it's not optional. The insurance must cover the entire stay. When renewing and switching policies, no gap in between. Arriving without proof of insurance can mean refused entry on the spot. Family members coming to accompany you each need their own policy.
- Benefit: Australia's Department of Home Affairs requires student-visa applicants and their family members to buy Overseas Student Health Cover - OSHC - for the whole stay in Australia, unbroken. The insurance must come from an Australian-recognized insurer. Exemption cases where buying isn't required are excepted. No gap is allowed between the new insurance and the previous visa's insurance. Arriving without proof of coverage can mean refused entry. Entering earlier than the course start date: the policy start date should be the actual day of arrival in Australia
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Australian Government Department of Home Affairs. Student visa (subclass 500). <https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500>
- Notes: If the school arranges it, ask clearly for the insurer's name, the policy start and end dates and the policy number - the visa application asks for these. Buying short makes renewals a problem; better to buy past graduation with a little margin.

### 8. United Kingdom: on top of the visa fee there's a per-year health surcharge - 776 pounds a year for students, paid in full at application
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Visa fee 558 pounds. Health surcharge 776 pounds per year. Both paid in one go at application
- TL;DR: The UK costs more than the visa fee. You also prepay a health surcharge by visa year, and only then can you use the National Health Service. A two-year visa means 1,552 pounds in one payment. Together with the visa fee, this sum is due in full before departure - don't leave it out of the budget.
- Benefit: The UK student visa fee is 558 pounds. Applying from outside the UK, extending or switching inside - same figure. On top of that is the health surcharge, which must be paid to use the National Health Service, prepaid in one go by visa year at application. The student and their family members pay 776 pounds per year - a 2-year visa, for example, is 1,552 pounds. Other applicant categories pay 1,035 pounds per year. Visas over 6 months but under 1 year are charged as a full year
- Evidence: A
- Sources: UK Government. Student visa. <https://www.gov.uk/student-visa>; UK Government. Pay for UK healthcare as part of your immigration application. <https://www.gov.uk/healthcare-immigration-application>
- Notes: Amounts and exchange rates both change - compute by the current figures on the two pages in the sources. Studying a degree course at 18 or older, you can generally stay up to 5 years. Below degree level, 2 years. Estimate the surcharge by those years.

### 9. Leave time for certification before coming home: Service Center certification takes 10 to 20 working days
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Apply online, pay online. Preparing materials plus review, counted in working days - about a month end to end
- TL;DR: Using a foreign degree back home requires degree certification first. The review alone takes 10 to 20 working days. In hiring season, or if materials get sent back, even longer. Fall recruiting, civil-service registration and household registration all have deadlines - don't start the process when you already need it.
- Benefit: Certification of foreign (overseas) degrees is done in the online service hall of the Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange. The flow has four steps: register and complete real-name verification, submit the application and materials online, pay online, wait for evaluation and review. The published certification time limit is 10 to 20 working days. Working days exclude weekends and holidays; this stretch is the Service Center's review time
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange. Online service hall - degree certification. <http://zwfw.cscse.edu.cn/>
- Notes: The materials to submit: the diploma, passport or travel permit, residence card or visa endorsement, ID photo, authorization statement. Entry-exit records during the time abroad are pulled by the system itself - so don't lose the visa pages from your time abroad. The Service Center's site also posts announcements saying which certificates have been voided and which schools' applications are suspended. Check for your school before starting.

### 10. The list of schools under "enhanced review" grows every year - the buy-a-diploma route is already dead
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before signing up, search the school's name once in the Service Center's notices
- TL;DR: The Service Center names batches of foreign institutions for enhanced certification review, and suspends certification applications from individual schools. The ones named are usually the kind advertised as "no language requirement, no time abroad, a master's in a few months." One search before signing up is far less trouble than an appeal afterward.
- Benefit: The Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange keeps issuing announcements enhancing certification review for certain foreign institutions' degrees - an extra round of checking on those schools' diplomas. The latest is No. 9 (2025-10-28). The Center has also posted announcements suspending certification applications from individual overseas institutions, and warnings that people are committing fraud in the name of foreign-degree certification
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange. Announcement (9) on enhanced certification review for certain foreign institutions' degrees (2025-10-28). <https://www.cscse.edu.cn/cscse/sy/tzgg/2025102809225023345/index.html>; Chinese (Ministry of Education) Service Center for Scholarly Exchange. <https://www.cscse.edu.cn/>
- Notes: "Enhanced review" doesn't mean certification necessarily fails, but you'll be asked for more materials and it takes longer. Any agent promising "guaranteed certification" or "expedited through internal channels" is a scam - the Service Center has published a fraud warning specifically. Cross-border ghostwriting of papers or having someone take your online classes: when caught, beyond the degree being voided, your visa record can be affected.
