# 17. When there are elderly at home

Lens: money and personal freedom. With elderly at home, most trouble isn't too little money - it's not having said the words and signed the papers in advance. The last entry covers pressure ulcers in the long-term bedridden, counted on mortality and not compared with this chapter's other entries.

### 1. While the elder is still lucid, designate their future guardian in writing
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: A few hundred yuan at a notary office. One conversation settles it. The hard part is opening it - saying to their face the day they can no longer manage themselves
- TL;DR: While the elder's mind is still clear, spend a few hundred yuan at a notary office on a written document naming their future guardian. Without it, once they can't decide for themselves, who signs for them and who manages their money gets designated by a court - and family disagreement drags it out longer.
- Benefit: The Civil Code provides that a clear-minded adult who can decide for themselves (in law, "an adult with full civil capacity") may agree in advance with someone on a future guardian. The counterparty can be a close relative or another willing person or organization. The agreement must "designate the guardian in written form". When they later can't decide, or can decide only partly ("losing or partly losing civil capacity"), the named person takes over. Without this document, who signs for them and manages their money goes through court designation
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Article 33). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: This document is called voluntary guardianship designation - the person you pick yourself in advance, ranked ahead of the law's default guardianship order. Elders living alone, children in other cities, complicated family relations - all the more reason to do it early. Have it notarized at a notary office.

### 2. Make the will - remember the later will overrides the earlier, and a notarized will no longer outranks
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: A handwritten will is free. Notarization runs hundreds to over a thousand yuan. The hard part is opening it - talking with family about after death
- TL;DR: The law recognizes six will forms, but a wrong form voids the whole thing - the most common failure. A self-handwritten will must be written by the person's own hand start to finish, signed, dated year-month-day. Several wills conflicting: the last one stands. Since 2021, the notarized one no longer automatically ranks first.
- Benefit: The Civil Code recognizes six will forms: self-handwritten, written by another on request, printed, audio-video recorded, oral, and notarized. Each has its own mandatory procedural requirements ("formal requirements" in law). Several wills conflicting: the last one stands. Since 2021, the notarized one no longer automatically ranks first
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Articles 1134 to 1142). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: A self-handwritten will must be written by the person's own hand start to finish, signed, dated year-month-day. A printed will needs two or more witnesses; the testator and witnesses sign every page, dated year-month-day. Heirs, beneficiaries named in the will, and their close relatives may not be witnesses. A wrong form voids the whole thing - the most common failure.

### 3. Keep the elder's money in a separate account, with a two-person-confirmation rule for large spending
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One family conversation to set a threshold. The hard part is how to open it without the elder feeling guarded against
- TL;DR: Supplement, collectible and wealth-product scams on elders follow one script: months spent getting close, then a one-time harvest. Agree on a rule in advance: spending above a set amount gets mentioned to a named person first. That pushes the paying moment back - past the most impulsive stretch.
- Benefit: Supplement, collectible and wealth-product scams targeting elders all build trust over months, then harvest at once. Agreeing in advance that "spending above a set amount gets mentioned to a named person first" pushes the paying decision back - past the most impulsive stretch
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience; no direct literature. General anti-fraud rules: chapters 8 and 13
- Notes: Don't frame it as "in case you get scammed". Frame it as "the household keeps one ledger" - far easier for the elder to accept. And don't hand all the elder's money to the children for safekeeping - that raises another class of disputes.

### 4. Give the elder a ready-made shield phrase they can always say
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One conversation, a few minutes
- TL;DR: Selling and scamming run on on-the-spot pressure - making refusal embarrassing in the moment. Agree on one line in advance: "I need to go home and discuss it with my family". Always sayable, it gives them a way out on the spot - far more useful than reasoning with them afterward.
- Benefit: Selling and scamming both run on on-the-spot pressure. An agreed "I need to go home and discuss it with my family" gives the elder a way out. More effective than reasoning afterward
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience; no direct literature
- Notes: Use together with entry 3 (the elder's money in a separate account). Do the fall-proofing home fixes along the way - see chapter 1. The line isn't for guarding against the elder - it's a way out so they needn't push back hard in front of a room. Cornered by sellers, the hard part was never judging true from false - it's saying no in front of a room of people. Agreeing on the line in advance takes that pass for them.

### 5. Any "investment retirement" that asks the elder to pay first is off-limits: cards, beds, retirement apartments, travel-based retirement and senior products are all the same illegal fundraising
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Read the list of names below to the elder once - minutes. Thereafter, refuse on sight: no lectures, no tours, no phone number left. The hard part: an elder who's already been a few times and grown familiar is hard to talk back
- TL;DR: Any retirement scheme that asks the elder to pay first and promises returns is the same scam: cards, prepaid elder-care fees, buying beds, buying retirement apartments, travel-based retirement, seminar-sold senior products. By regulation the money can't be recovered - losses are borne by the participant. In officially announced cases, one gang took 136 million yuan in the name of reserving retirement-apartment beds, hurting 2,100+ people. The only stoppable step is the paying one.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Civil Affairs lists five forms - recognizing the name beats recognizing the person. First, borrowing an elder-care institution's name: no real facility, a temporarily rented venue, claiming a cooperation agreement with an institution to borrow its credibility. Second, in the name of "elder-care services": selling "VIP cards", "membership cards", "prepaid cards", having people prepay "elder-care fees", promising services clearly beyond its own bed capacity. Third, in the name of investing in "elder-care projects": common names include buying into a wellness-retirement base and selling fictitious retirement apartments - also long-term bed rentals and "time bank" mutual-aid retirement - flying the flags of principal-returning sales, sell-and-lease-back, agreed buybacks. Fourth, in the name of selling "senior products": goods buybacks, consignment sales, purchase rebates, free checkups, gift giveaways, seminar marketing, wellness lectures, expert free clinics. Fifth, in the name of "travel-based retirement": cheap or free tours, stored-value rebates, points-based retirement. That the money can't be recovered is written into the system: Article 25 of the regulation on preventing and handling illegal fundraising provides "losses from participating in illegal fundraising are borne by the participants themselves" - losses are yours to swallow. Scale, from officially announced cases: Dai Mouping and others illegally raised 136 million yuan in the name of reserving retirement-apartment beds, hurting 2,100+ people - Dai got 15 years for fundraising fraud plus a 400,000-yuan fine. Cao Mouming raised 13.207 billion yuan fraudulently in the name of investing in the elder-care industry, involving 110,000+ people - life imprisonment, lifetime deprivation of political rights, full confiscation of personal property. Supplement seminar-sales go through ordinary fraud: Ni Mourong used free banquets, gifts and chauffeured cars to pull elders into lectures, where instructors fabricated anti-cancer effects through "false personas, false cases and inflated haggling". Elders were induced to buy "dihydroquercetin" at tens of times cost - 620,000+ yuan sold; Ni got 10 years 3 months plus a 120,000-yuan fine (nationwide; the regulation effective May 1, 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs (2023). Risk notice on illegal fundraising in elder-care services. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202306/content_6886556.htm> (published on the Chinese government site) ; Ministry of Civil Affairs and CBIRC (2021). Risk notice on illegal fundraising in the elder-care field. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html> ; State Council (2021). Regulation on preventing and handling illegal fundraising (State Council Order No. 737, Articles 2, 25, 30). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586632.htm> ; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022). Typical procuratorial cases cracking down on elder-targeting fraud. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202206/t20220617_560010.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court (2025). Typical cases punishing livelihood-field fraud (case 5, the Ni Mourong fraud case). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/482861.html>
- Notes: Judge not by "this place looks legitimate" but by "did they ask you to pay first, did they promise returns". A retirement institution sells services - the moment the pitch mentions investment, rebates or principal return, it's out. An elder pulled into lectures, tours and free clinics breaks no law and is hard to stop. What can be stopped is the paying step - pair with entry 3's two-person rule. Money already paid: call 110 or 96110 at once to stop payment per entry 2 of chapter 8, and report to the local lead department for handling illegal fundraising. House-for-pension is another path - entry 6 (reverse-mortgage pension insurance).

### 6. Outside insurers' reverse-mortgage pension insurance, touch no "house-for-pension" scheme - never mortgage the home to buy wealth products
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The home's title certificate goes to no one showing up in the name of "house-for-pension". Mortgage, guarantee and loan agreements get shown to the children before signing. The hard part: door-to-door sellers are often warm and persistent, and the elder is embarrassed to refuse
- TL;DR: The legitimate house-for-pension has exactly one form: insurers' reverse-mortgage pension insurance - still in pilot, with few participants. The door-to-door kind has nothing to do with the state pilot. It talks the elder into mortgaging the home and buying its recommended wealth products with the borrowed money - essentially paying old money with new. Regulators say some didn't even know their home was mortgaged - the home gone, the loan remaining.
- Benefit: The CBIRC's consumer-protection bureau lays out real and fake. The legitimate house-for-pension has one form: "reverse-mortgage pension insurance for the elderly". An elder holding full lawful title mortgages the property to an insurer. They keep living in it, using it, collecting its rent. Selling or disposing requires the mortgagee's (the insurer's) consent. The elder draws pension per the agreed terms until death. The business remains in pilot with few participants. The lawbreakers' "house-for-pension" is "completely unrelated" to the state's piloted reverse-mortgage insurance - "a means of borrowing state policy to build momentum for illegal fundraising". The operation: inducing the elder to mortgage the property, then buying its recommended wealth products with the borrowed money - "usually a Ponzi scheme paying old with new". The consequence, verbatim: "some participants didn't even know their property was mortgaged - they lost the home in the end, and still carry the loan". The notice also gives a yield line, verbatim: "a wealth product promising over 6% return warrants a question mark; over 8% is dangerous; over 10%, prepare to lose the whole principal - 'guaranteed principal with high returns' is financial fraud". The same notice demands "treat contract signing with care - never sign a blank contract" (nationwide, June 2021)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: CBIRC Consumer Rights Protection Bureau (2021). Risk notice on "investment retirement" and "house-for-pension" financial fraud (2021 No. 3). <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html> (reposted by Hunan Local Financial Regulation Bureau) ; Ministry of Civil Affairs and CBIRC (2021). Risk notice on illegal fundraising in the elder-care field (fourth form: illegal fundraising in the name of "house-for-pension"). <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>
- Notes: B because these claims come from regulators' risk notices and experience judgments, not research data. The 6%, 8% and 10% lines are reference lines the regulator gives consumers, not legal standards. The CBIRC has since merged into the National Financial Regulatory Administration; the original page no longer opens, so a provincial financial-regulator repost is cited. The crux isn't "the wealth product lost money" - it's that after the home is mortgaged, the elder still owes a loan, a loss far beyond the principal. General rules for signatures and blank contracts: entry 17 of chapter 8. Stopping payment after being scammed: entry 2 of chapter 8.

### 7. An elder long-term bedridden or severely disabled at home: apply for long-term care insurance at the insured-place medical-insurance department - it's not only for the elderly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: One application at the insured-place medical-insurance department, plus a disability-grade assessment. Pass the first assessment and the fund covers the assessment fee - nothing out of pocket
- TL;DR: Long-term care insurance means the medical-insurance fund helps pay for hired care. With severe disability lasting over half a year, apply at the insured-place medical-insurance department. Eligible care fees need no up-front threshold: about 70% reimbursed under the employee scheme, about 50% under the urban-rural-resident scheme. Bed and meal fees aren't covered. It's often taken as an elder policy, but the documents say "insured persons" - no age threshold; a severely disabled young person applies just the same.
- Benefit: Eligible long-term care fees have no deductible - no paying a threshold yourself before reimbursement starts. Under the employee scheme, the fund pays about 70%. Under the non-employed urban-rural-resident scheme, about 50%. The most payable per year (annual payment cap): no more than 50% of the local pooling region's prior-year per-capita disposable income of urban and rural residents
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NHSA and seven other departments (2026). Notice issuing the implementation plan for accelerating the long-term care insurance system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202603/content_7063915.htm>: beneficiaries are "insured persons who pay contributions as prescribed and whose disability persists long-term (generally over 6 months), assessed and recognized on application", with "severe disability covered at the system's initial stage"; "no deductible for benefits. For eligible long-term care fees, the fund pays about 50% under the non-employed urban-rural-resident scheme and about 70% under the employee scheme; retirees enjoy employee-scheme treatment"; "the annual fund payment cap per insured person does not exceed 50% of the pooling region's prior-year per-capita disposable income of urban and rural residents"; "a fixed waiting period for benefits is in principle set at 6 months"; also see the CPC Central Committee General Office and State Council General Office's Opinions on accelerating the long-term care insurance system. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12666/202604/content_7065119.html>
- Notes: The disability must "persist long-term (generally over 6 months)" - a few weeks in hospital doesn't qualify. Benefits carry a further 6-month waiting period. Bed and meal fees aren't care and aren't paid. Medical costs that medical insurance should pay aren't paid by long-term care insurance either. Anyone already drawing work-injury living-care fees doesn't double-dip. This money and the care subsidy in the two disability subsidies are two procedures and two sums - no conflict: entry 8 of chapter 7 (the two subsidies). Care at home and in the community gets a reimbursement tilt - moving into an institution isn't mandatory. The system rolls out in batches by region, and assessment standards differ. First call 12393 or the insured-place medical-insurance agency to ask whether it's launched locally and how to book an assessment. A failed assessment isn't the end - reapply after the disability deepens.

### 8. With someone long-term bedridden at home, treat pressure ulcers as the number-one enemy: a powered alternating-pressure mattress, scheduled turning, and a daily look at bony prominences
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A home powered alternating-pressure mattress starts at a few hundred yuan. One turn takes minutes. The hard part: turning every two to three hours, nights included - one person can't carry it alone
- TL;DR: A pressure ulcer isn't just an extra skin wound. In records of 19,520 hip-fracture surgeries, patients arriving with a pressure ulcer were about 20% more likely to die within 30 days. Three doable things all have evidence: switching to a powered air mattress cut new ulcers about 60% versus an ordinary hospital mattress; genuinely executing turning, about 70% better than usual care; and not leaving anyone prone for long. Check the sacrum, heels, hips, shoulders and back daily - a red spot that doesn't fade under pressure gets a nurse or doctor the same day.
- Benefit: 19,520 hip-fracture surgery patients (US NSQIP 2016-2019): those with a pressure ulcer before surgery died more within 30 days. The association held after accounting for other factors (OR 1.2, 21% higher odds, P=0.004). They also had more deep-vein thrombosis (OR 1.59), pneumonia (OR 1.39), unplanned readmission (OR 1.43), and 0.4 days longer stays on average. 324 hospitalized elders (average 86) followed a median 18 months: pressure ulcers independently predicted long-term death (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059-2.126, P=0.020) - "independently" meaning it predicts on its own after other factors are removed. 65 randomized trials pooled (network meta-analysis), comparing powered active air surfaces with standard hospital mattresses: powered active air surfaces - the powered-air-mattress class - had lower ulcer incidence (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.29-0.63, moderate certainty). Powered hybrid air surfaces: RR 0.22 (0.07-0.66). A Cochrane review of 11 trials and 4,462 people (2026 update): turning every 2 hours versus every 4 - the difference can't be called (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79-1.39, very low certainty); other interval comparisons equally uncertain. In a trial of 1,226 ICU patients, wearable sensors gave real-time reminders with visual alerts, genuinely executing 2-hourly turns: ulcer incidence below usual care (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10-0.75, moderate certainty). In a 116-person trial, the prone (lying face-down) group had more ulcers than the supine (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31-8.98)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Porter SB, Pla R, Chow JH, et al. (2022). Preoperative Pressure Ulcers, Mortality, and Complications in Older Hip Fracture Surgery Patients. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews, 6(11). <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117> ; Ottaviani S, Rondanina E, Longo E, et al. (2026). Impact of pressure ulcers and frailty on long-term mortality: a prospective cohort study of hospitalized older adults. European Geriatric Medicine, 17(2), 665-672. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8> ; Shi C, Dumville JC, Cullum N (2018). Support surfaces for pressure ulcer prevention: A network meta-analysis. PLoS ONE, 13(2), e0192707. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707> ; Latimer SL, Chaboyer WP, Probst S, et al. (2026). Repositioning for pressure injury prevention in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD009958. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4>
- Notes: B for three reasons. First, the two mortality studies saw only "people with ulcers fare worse later" - not "preventing ulcers lowers mortality". Second, the turning-interval trials' conclusions are very shaky. Third, the mattress evidence is harder, but those studies compared hospital-bed surfaces - extending to home mattresses is an extrapolation. Checking sacrum, heels, hips, shoulders and back daily for "red that doesn't fade under pressure" is routine care practice without dedicated literature. This entry counts on mortality and isn't compared with this chapter's money accounts. What long-term care insurance covers: entry 7 of this chapter. One leg suddenly swollen - handle as deep-vein thrombosis: entry 11 of chapter 13. Long-term bed rest after a fall or serious injury: entry 34 of chapter 1.
