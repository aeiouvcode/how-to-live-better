# 20. Caring for a newborn

Lens: infant mortality and money. This chapter keeps only two kinds of things: one kind decides whether the child survives, the other decides how much money you spend. Both kinds keep only items where the difference is large enough and the evidence is hard. The parenting details - how to soothe, how to raise - are not written in this book. What to do before the birth and on the day you leave the hospital is in chapter 27.

### 1. Put the baby to sleep on their back, on a firm flat surface, in your room but not your bed, with nothing soft in the bed
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Swapping to a firm mattress costs a few hundred yuan. The hard part is holding your ground against the elders' opinions and keeping at it
- TL;DR: Put the baby down to sleep face up, on a firm flat surface. Sleep in the same room as the baby, but not in the same bed. No pillows, blankets, bumpers, stuffed toys or positioning pillows in the bed. Inclined cradles and baby seats are for sitting, not for sleeping in. In the United States alone, about 3,500 babies a year die in their sleep.
- Benefit: About 3,500 infants a year in the United States die of sleep-related causes. That includes sudden infant death syndrome, deaths of unknown cause, and accidental suffocation in bed. The American Academy of Pediatrics' safe sleep environment is "supine position; a firm, non-inclined sleep surface; room-sharing without bed-sharing; avoiding soft bedding and overheating." Several other things also lower the risk: breastfeeding, keeping the baby away from nicotine and alcohol, vaccinating on schedule, and using a pacifier
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Moon RY, Carlin RF, Hand I, Task Force on Sudden Infant Death Syndrome and the Committee on Fetus and Newborn (2022). Sleep-Related Infant Deaths: Updated 2022 Recommendations for Reducing Infant Deaths in the Sleep Environment. Pediatrics, 150(1), e2022057990. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2022-057990>
- Notes: "Nothing soft in the bed" means no pillows, blankets, bumpers, stuffed toys or positioning pillows at all. The nursing pillow that looks so safe doesn't go in either. Inclined cradles and baby seats are not sleeping places. This is the entry in this chapter with the biggest difference at stake - and the one where you most need to withstand pressure from the elders

### 2. The first hepatitis B shot goes in within 24 hours of birth
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hepatitis B vaccine is an immunization-program vaccine, free by default. It's given at the hospital on the day of birth - no extra trip needed
- TL;DR: The first hepatitis B shot goes in within 24 hours of birth. Of people infected with hepatitis B in infancy and early childhood, about 95% progress to chronic hepatitis. People infected as adults mostly recover on their own. So the earlier this shot, the more it's worth. If the mother's hepatitis B surface antigen is positive, the baby also gets a hepatitis B immunoglobulin shot at the same time.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization's wording: "all infants should receive the hepatitis B vaccine as soon as possible after birth (within 24 hours)." The reason: "infection in infancy and early childhood progresses to chronic hepatitis in about 95% of cases." Most people infected as adults recover on their own - they get well without treatment
- Evidence: A
- Sources: World Health Organization. Hepatitis B fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hepatitis-b>
- Notes: If the mother's hepatitis B surface antigen is positive, the baby also gets a hepatitis B immunoglobulin shot right after birth. Check this during prenatal care; don't wait until after the birth to ask. The screening the mother should have for herself is in chapter 1

### 3. Complete the national immunization schedule - free all the way, and a missed dose only means making up that dose
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The law states plainly that "vaccination units may not charge any fee for administering immunization-program vaccines." It does take a few trips to the vaccination clinic by age in months. Keep the vaccination certificate safe; don't lose it
- TL;DR: The vaccines in the national immunization program are free all the way through - the vaccination unit cannot charge a cent. Nursery and school enrollment both check the vaccination certificate, and any missed shot will definitely be dug up then and made up. Missing one only means making up the doses not yet given, not starting the whole course over. Switching manufacturers mid-course for the same vaccine also works. Eczema, hives, allergic rhinitis, asthma - as long as the condition is stable - and newborn jaundice likewise are not reasons to skip a shot.
- Benefit: At nursery and school enrollment, the institution checks the vaccination certificate. Finding a child who hasn't received the immunization-program vaccines as required, it reports to the vaccination unit and helps press the guardians to complete the shots. So a missed shot is never "deal with it later" - it will definitely be dug up at enrollment. The 2026 edition of the schedule is now in effect. The human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine has been added to the national immunization program. The starting age for the DTaP vaccine moved from 3 months to 2 months, 5 doses in all - one each at 2 months, 4 months, 6 months, 18 months and 6 years. Eligible children in Tibet, Qinghai, Xinjiang and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps also receive the Japanese encephalitis vaccine routinely from March 2026. Several vaccines can be given the same day: all current national-program vaccines may be administered simultaneously under the schedule or the catch-up principles. Two or more injected vaccines go in different sites, and "it is strictly forbidden to draw two or more vaccines into the same syringe for one injection." For shots not given the same day, two or more injected live attenuated vaccines must be at least 28 days apart. Intervals involving inactivated vaccines, oral live attenuated vaccines and other vaccines are unrestricted. The catch-up principle for anyone under 18 is to catch up as early as possible - "only the unfinished doses need be made up; there is no need to restart the full course" - and the same vaccine from a different manufacturer continues the course (nationwide, 2026 edition of the schedule)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2019). Vaccine Administration Law (Articles 48, 49). <http://ynswsjkw.yn.gov.cn/html/2019/faguiguizhangxin_0731/6509.html> (reposted by the Yunnan Provincial Health Commission); National Disease Control and Prevention Administration, National Health Commission (2026). Q&A interpreting the "National Immunization Program Childhood Vaccination Schedule and Instructions (2026 Edition)." <https://www.ndcpa.gov.cn/jbkzzx/c100013/common/content/content_2073002930292035584.html>
- Notes: The most common misunderstanding is "the child's constitution is weak, hold off for now." The official position: a so-called "allergic constitution," family members with allergic disease, or the child's own past food or drug allergies are none of them reasons to skip. Atopic dermatitis (that is, eczema), hives, allergic rhinitis and bronchial asthma are all fine to vaccinate with as long as the condition is stable. Taking antihistamines or using inhaled steroids is fine too. Physiological jaundice and breast-milk jaundice in newborns are fine as well. There are only two true reasons not to vaccinate: a past acute severe allergic reaction to the same vaccine, or a severe allergy to a known ingredient in it. Children with immune deficiency, and children in chemotherapy or radiotherapy or on immunosuppressants, are assessed one by one - explain the situation at the vaccination clinic and let them decide. The first hepatitis B shot is in entry 2. Self-paid non-program vaccines carry a separate vaccine fee and service fee - unrelated to the free shots this entry is about.

### 4. For the first 6 months, breast milk only - not even water; from 6 months, add complementary food and keep breastfeeding
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=yes benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: No money - it even saves the formula money. What it spends is time: feeding takes a real slice of every day. The hard part is keeping it up without a break for the first 6 months
- TL;DR: Start feeding within the first hour after birth. For the first 6 months, breast milk only - not even water. From 6 months, start adding complementary food while continuing to breastfeed to age 2 or beyond. The complementary food should include an iron-rich animal food every day. If breast milk isn't enough or feeding isn't possible, use formula, without blaming yourself. What the baby is fed has far less effect on survival than the back-sleeping entry.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization recommends "initiating breastfeeding within the first hour of life." For the first 6 months, "exclusive breastfeeding - no other food or liquid, not even water." Then "from 6 months of age, begin adding safe and adequate complementary foods while continuing to breastfeed up to 2 years of age or beyond." The National Health Commission's service guide likewise advocates exclusive breastfeeding for ages 0 to 6 months. That guide also requires that from 6 months on, "each day's complementary food must include an iron-rich animal food"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: World Health Organization. Breastfeeding. <https://www.who.int/health-topics/breastfeeding>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2024). Service guide for assessing infant and toddler nutrition and feeding (trial) (Guoweiban Youyou Letter [2024] No. 452). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002872.htm>
- Notes: If breast milk isn't enough, or the body doesn't allow feeding, use formula - no guilt needed. The gap between breast milk and formula is far smaller than the safe-sleep entry. Complementary-food frequency: once or twice a day at 6 to 8 months, two or three times a day at 9 to 12 months. Each day's food should cover at least four of the seven categories. Whether to avoid peanut when the child has severe eczema or egg allergy is in entry 12 of this chapter (don't avoid peanut).

### 5. Mix formula with water above 70 °C, let it cool before feeding, and throw out what's left
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A few extra minutes each time letting the milk cool. The hard part is waiting for the water to drop to temperature while the baby cries with hunger
- TL;DR: Formula powder is not sterile - it can carry a bacterium called Cronobacter sakazakii. Once a newborn is infected, the reported fatality rate is forty to eighty percent. When mixing, pour the hot water into the bottle first, wait until it drops to about 73.8 °C, then add the powder. The milk is then still above 70 °C, which cuts this bacterium to below one hundred-thousandth of its starting level. Let the mixed milk cool before feeding, and throw out what's left.
- Benefit: Formula powder is not sterile and can carry Cronobacter sakazakii. The reported fatality rate after newborn infection is 40% to 80%. Experimental research shows: pour the hot water into the bottle first, wait until the water drops to about 73.8 °C, then add the powder - in the vast majority of cases the milk is then still above 70 °C. This reduces the bacterium by more than 5 log levels, that is, to below one hundred-thousandth of the starting level
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Beary MA, Daly SE, Baker J, Snyder AB (2025). Assessing Hot Water Reconstitution Instructions and Labeling of Powdered Infant Formula to Ensure Cronobacter spp. Reduction. Journal of Food Protection, 88(9), 100571. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfp.2025.100571>; Sima WG, Legesse T, Girma S, et al. (2025). Emerging microbial risks: Cronobacter sakazakii in powdered infant formula for infants under six months of age in Ethiopia. BMC Microbiology, 26, 307. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12866-025-04380-y>; World Health Organization & FAO (2007). Safe preparation, storage and handling of powdered infant formula: guidelines. <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241595414>
- Notes: The 70 °C threshold comes from public-health guidance. Beary et al.'s own words: "public health guidance for caregivers of high-risk infants recommends reconstituting powdered infant formula with water heated to at least 70 °C (158 ℉) to inactivate microorganisms." Preemies, low-birth-weight babies and babies under 2 months are at the highest risk. Don't mix with water that sat in a thermos all day. And don't leave mixed milk at room temperature waiting for the next feed

### 6. No honey before age 1
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free
- TL;DR: Before the child turns 1, don't feed honey. Honey water, honey mixed into complementary food, pastries containing honey - all count. Honey can carry the bacteria that cause botulism. The gut flora of an adult can hold it down; an infant's can't. Homemade pickles and fermented foods work the same way.
- Benefit: The US CDC's wording: "do not give honey to children younger than 1 year of age," because "honey can contain the bacteria that cause botulism." An adult's gut flora can hold down the spores - the dormant state of this bacterium; an infant's cannot
- Evidence: A
- Sources: US CDC. Preventing Botulism. <https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/prevention/index.html>
- Notes: What can't be fed includes honey water, complementary food mixed with honey, and pastries containing honey. By the same logic, don't feed homemade pickles and fermented foods to an infant either
### 7. The vitamin K shot at birth is a must
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: 0 to tens of yuan. It's usually already included in the cost of the hospital delivery - nothing extra to pay
- TL;DR: Newborns store very little vitamin K in their bodies. Without a supplement they can bleed, and the late-onset kind is often bleeding inside the brain. Where this shot isn't given universally, that bleeding occurs in 478 of every 100,000 newborns. Getting the shot is estimated to cut the rate by over seventy-eight percent. Proper hospitals in China give it routinely. For a home birth or a birth at an unregulated facility, ask whether it was given.
- Benefit: Newborns carry very little vitamin K. Without supplementation, vitamin K deficiency bleeding can occur, and the late-onset kind often presents as intracranial bleeding - bleeding inside the brain. The Belgian consensus recommendation is "a single intramuscular injection of 1 or 2 mg of vitamin K at birth." In regions without systematic prophylaxis, the incidence of vitamin K deficiency bleeding is 478 per 100,000 - a figure from 42,000 newborns in Tashkent. The authors estimate that prophylaxis lowers the incidence by "more than 78.5%"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Fiesack S, Smits A, Rayyan M, et al. (2021). Nutrients, 13(11), 4109. <https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13114109>; Tursunov D, Yoshida Y, Yrysov K, et al. (2018). Nagoya Journal of Medical Science, 80(1), 11. <https://doi.org/10.18999/nagjms.80.1.11>
- Notes: At a proper hospital in China, this shot is routine - nothing for you to manage. For a home birth or a birth at an unregulated facility, confirm it yourself. Oral dosing works less well than the shot; the shot here means into the muscle

### 8. A baby under 3 months with a temperature of 38 °C goes straight to the hospital - no watching at home
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration plus tests, a few hundred yuan
- TL;DR: Under 3 months old, a temperature of 38 °C means straight to the hospital. Don't watch at home, and don't give a fever reducer first and wait and see. At this age even a serious bacterial infection can show up as nothing but fever. So "he seems in decent spirits" is no reason to wait at home. A baby under 8 days old isn't even covered by the guideline - all the more reason to go immediately.
- Benefit: The American Academy of Pediatrics has a dedicated guideline for full-term infants 8 to 60 days old who appear well. It defines fever as "a temperature ≥38.0 °C." It splits babies into three bands - 8 to 21 days, 22 to 28 days, and 29 to 60 days - with a management path for each. Babies under 8 days old fall outside even this guideline's scope, meaning that stage calls for immediate medical care all the more
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Pantell RH, Roberts KB, Adams WG, et al. (2021). Evaluation and Management of Well-Appearing Febrile Infants 8 to 60 Days Old. Pediatrics, 148(2), e2021052228. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2021-052228>
- Notes: At this age, a serious bacterial infection can show up as nothing but fever. So looking "in decent spirits" can't be the reason to watch at home. Don't give a fever reducer on your own first and wait and see either

### 9. Never shake a baby, no matter how tired or angry you are
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is stopping your own hands when you're exhausted or furious
- TL;DR: A baby's head is a large share of their body, and the neck muscles are weak. Shaking hard bleeds the brain and the eyes - it can kill, or leave a lifetime of disability. The gentle rocking of soothing a baby to sleep doesn't count; the danger is the violent shaking you can't control. When you're about to boil over, put the baby back in the crib and walk away for a few minutes. Letting them cry a while is far safer than shaking them in your arms.
- Benefit: A baby's head is proportionally large and the neck muscles weak. Violent shaking causes intracranial bleeding and retinal bleeding - bleeding in the brain and in the eyes. That can kill, or leave lifelong disability. The gentle rocking of soothing to sleep is a different thing. The danger is uncontrolled violent shaking
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience and general consensus; no directly citable primary literature was obtained this round
- Notes: If you really reach the edge of losing control, put the baby back in the crib first and walk away for a few minutes. Letting them cry a while is far safer than holding and shaking them. This entry matters most for someone caring for a baby alone and chronically short on sleep. For handling your own emotions, see chapter 3

### 10. Diapers: don't look at the brand - look at fit, how often you change, and whether it's been flagged in spot checks
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Two to three hundred yuan a month up to over a thousand, depending on the tier you use
- TL;DR: No official body has ever ranked "which brand is best." The only things you can check are the market regulator's product-quality spot-check bulletins and regulators' investigations of specific incidents. Expensive doesn't mean safe; imported doesn't mean compliant. Buy a small pack first and try a week - check for diaper rash and elastic marks, then stock up if it fits.
- Benefit: No official body has published a "which brand is best" ranking, so this book makes no brand recommendations. Only two kinds of information can be verified. One is the State Administration for Market Regulation's product-quality spot-check bulletins. The other is regulators' investigations of specific incidents. After the "formamide in baby diapers" issue drew attention in June 2026, the market regulator led a joint investigation team to look into it
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Chinese government website (2026-06-22). Market regulator leads joint investigation team to examine the "formamide in baby diapers" issue. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202606/content_7072931.htm> (the page shows only a headline, with no body text displayed, so this entry cites only the fact of the investigation, not any conclusion)
- Notes: TODO (unverified: the outcome of that investigation and the official text of the regulator's formal bulletin). How to actually buy: expensive doesn't mean safe, imported doesn't mean compliant. Buy a small pack first and try a week - check for diaper rash and elastic marks, then stock up if it fits. Diaper rash comes mostly from not changing often enough and poor breathability; the brand matters far less than you'd think. How to look up spot-check bulletins is in chapter 5

### 11. For big items, think in the order "borrow, buy used, buy new" - don't kit everything out at once
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: No extra spending; what you save is usually in the thousands of yuan. The hard part is resisting the urge to kit everything out at once
- TL;DR: Strollers, cribs, swaddles and toys all get used for a very short time, and the secondhand market has plenty of supply and few buyers. Ask to borrow first; if you can't borrow, buy used; buy new only as a last resort - usually a saving in the thousands of yuan. A car seat and a mattress are the two things not to buy used: the structural damage to a seat after a crash is invisible to the eye. Confinement centers and early-education classes are big optional expenses - give them the 24-hour cooling-off period first.
- Benefit: Strollers, cribs, swaddles and toys all have very short use periods, and supply far exceeds demand in the secondhand market. The two things that truly shouldn't be skimped on or bought used are the car seat and the mattress. The structural damage to a car seat after a crash can't be seen with the naked eye
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience, no direct literature; the evidence on car seats is in chapter 1, impulse spending in chapter 5
- Notes: Confinement centers, early-education classes and baby-swimming venues - this book neither recommends nor dismisses them. But each is a big optional expense, so the 24-hour cooling-off period from chapter 5 applies

### 12. If the baby has severe eczema or egg allergy, don't avoid peanut - add it early under a doctor's guidance, but never serve it whole
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A jar of peanut butter costs tens of yuan. A few servings a week, done in passing. Before adding it, take the baby to a doctor for an assessment.
- TL;DR: A randomized trial of 640 high-risk infants. The group that ate peanut regularly starting between 4 and 11 months had a peanut-allergy rate of 1.9% at age 5; the group that avoided it completely had 13.7%. Among children whose skin test was already weakly positive at enrollment, it was 10.6% versus 35.3%. Avoiding made allergy more likely. But never serve whole peanuts - they can block the airway.
- Benefit: The British LEAP randomized trial. 640 infants enrolled, selected for severe eczema, egg allergy, or both. They were 4 to 11 months old at enrollment. Randomized into two groups - one ate peanut products regularly, one avoided them completely - and peanut-allergy rates were measured at 60 months. Among the 530 with a negative peanut skin test at enrollment, 13.7% of the avoidance group were allergic versus 1.9% of the consumption group (P<0.001). Among the 98 whose skin test was already weakly positive at enrollment, 35.3% versus 10.6% (P=0.004). Serious adverse events did not differ between the groups.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Du Toit G, Roberts G, Sayre PH, et al. (2015). Randomized trial of peanut consumption in infants at risk for peanut allergy. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(9), 803-813. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1414850>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2020). Core information for health education on infant and toddler feeding. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-08/01/content_5531915.htm>; Perkin MR et al. (2016). Randomized Trial of Introduction of Allergenic Foods in Breast-Fed Infants. New England Journal of Medicine, 374(18), 1733-1743. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1514210> (the EAT trial in general infants, from the Notes)
- Notes: **The only forms allowed are peanut butter thinned down or peanut powder mixed into food - never whole peanuts.** The health commission's core feeding information states it plainly: "whole peanuts, nuts, jelly and similar foods can easily be inhaled into the windpipe and cause choking; infants and toddlers should avoid them." What to do if someone chokes is in entry 26 of chapter 13 (someone choking and unable to speak). This entry applies only to high-risk children - those with severe eczema or an existing egg allergy. **See a doctor for an assessment before adding it; don't experiment at home**: the LEAP trial skin-tested every child before enrollment, and children with a reaction larger than 4 mm were excluded from the trial rather than fed peanut. The evidence for children in general is much weaker. In another trial of 1,162 ordinary breastfed infants, analyzed by assigned group, the early-introduction group had 5.6% food allergy versus 7.1% in the standard group - not a significant difference (P=0.32); significance appeared only among the children who actually complied (2.4% versus 7.3%). That way of counting tends to inflate the effect, and the authors themselves said the by-group analysis failed to prove efficacy - but it did show early introduction is safe. China's official feeding documents currently say nothing about whether allergenic foods should be introduced early or avoided, so this entry follows the international trial evidence. Mind the timing too: LEAP started at 4 months, while China starts complementary food at a full 6 months - see entry 4 of this chapter (breast milk only for the first 6 months). For a high-risk child, when to start and how - follow the doctor.
