# 23. Which skills are worth learning

This chapter counts money and time, with entry 2 additionally counting mortality. The first six entries answer one question: should you keep studying. Entry 1 first says which stretch of options the law has crossed out. Entry 2 counts the relationship between schooling and lifespan. Entry 3 looks at where the country's education levels actually sit. Entry 4 covers the aid available when you can't afford school. Entry 5 covers the paths left when you don't get into academic high school. Entry 6 turns "study or work" into a problem you can solve, with a method for doing the math yourself. The next seven answer a second question: once you've decided to learn something, how to choose. Chinese wage statistics by occupation are hard to verify item by item from open official channels, so only a few things with findable sources are written here: roughly how much an extra year of schooling adds to wages (also called the return to education), how to verify whether a certificate is legitimate, where training subsidies come from, and which dimension to look at when choosing a skill least likely to be replaced. This book gives no figure for what any specific trade earns per month. The last five entries answer a third question: once you've settled on what to learn, how to learn it in the least time. Those five count time, not money, and the two are not converted.

### 1. Under 16, "going to work" is not an option: an employer who hires you is fined 5,000 yuan a month, and anyone willing to hire you is running illegal labor
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Take one minute to work out how old you are.
- TL;DR: Finishing middle school usually leaves you at 15 or 16 - right on this line. Under 16, an employer hiring you is breaking the law, fined 5,000 yuan per month per child laborer. So whoever is willing to take you is an employer that knows it's illegal and does it anyway. If something really happens, medical costs and compensation can only be chased from that employer - if it stalls or vanishes, nobody stands behind it.
- Benefit: Article 11 of the Compulsory Education Law: "for every child who has reached the age of six, the parents or other legal guardians shall send the child to school to receive and complete compulsory education." Article 14: "employers are prohibited from hiring school-age children and adolescents who should be receiving compulsory education." Article 58: parents who, without legitimate reason, fail to send school-age children to school as this Law requires are given "criticism and education, and ordered to correct within a time limit" by the local township government or the county education department. Article 2 of the Provisions on Prohibiting the Use of Child Labor: state organs, social organizations, enterprises, public institutions, private non-enterprise units and individual businesses "may not employ minors under the age of 16." Article 6: a fine "of 5,000 yuan per month for each child laborer employed," rising to 10,000 yuan per person per month for failure to correct by the deadline. Article 7: anyone who introduces a child laborer to employment is fined "5,000 yuan per person introduced." Article 10: when a child laborer falls ill or is injured, the employer "shall be responsible for sending them to a medical institution for treatment and shall bear all medical and living expenses during treatment," with a lump-sum compensation for disability or death. Article 13 leaves one exception: art and sports organizations may, with the consent of the parents or other guardians, employ professional art workers and athletes under 16 (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Compulsory Education Law (2018 second revision, Articles 11, 14, 58). <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html> (published by the Ministry of Education); State Council (2002). Provisions on prohibiting the use of child labor (State Council Order No. 364, Articles 2, 6, 7, 10, 13). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiary is yourself. Working after turning 16 is legal. But the jobs willing to take a 16-year-old with no credentials are exactly the kind in entry 10 of this chapter (choose automation-resistant skills): fixed processes, the easiest for machines to take over. The overtime pay, annual leave and work-injury benefits in chapter 19 all presume an employment relationship and evidence. Illegal labor has neither - so that money can't be had

### 2. Put "is education useful" into the mortality ledger too: each additional year of education lowers adult mortality risk by about 1.9%
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: No money. What it spends is the time and effort of those extra years of schooling; the tuition part is in entry 4 of this chapter (secondary vocational tuition and grants). The hard part is sticking it out without giving up midway.
- TL;DR: Pool 603 studies from 59 countries, and each additional year of schooling lowers the risk of death in adulthood by about 1.9% on average. The effect is clearest at ages 18 to 49 - about 2.9% per year. Over 70, only 0.8%. In other words, the difference between finishing high school and stopping after middle school is not just wages.
- Benefit: The researchers gathered the prior studies and pooled them (a systematic review and meta-analysis). The search covered January 1980 to May 2023, with 603 studies finally included - 59 countries, 70 locations, 10,355 observations. Education and adult mortality (death from all causes combined) show a dose-response relationship: the more schooling, the bigger the drop. Each additional year of education lowers mortality risk by 1.9% on average (95% uncertainty interval 1.8-2.0 - the true value roughly falls within this span). For people 18 to 49, 2.9% per year (2.8-3.0). For people over 70, 0.8% (0.6-1.0). No difference was seen between men and women. Split by national development level (the socio-demographic index), no difference was seen either
- Evidence: A (disputed)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Notes: Disputed. The authors themselves report publication bias (p<0.0001), meaning studies with good-looking results get published more easily. The individual studies' results also spread far apart. Everything included is follow-up observation, not experiments assigning people to groups. Years of schooling tangle together with family background, region and occupation; how much of this 1.9% comes from schooling itself and how much from "people able to keep studying start off better off" - this kind of study cannot separate. So it strongly shows "more schooling and longer life point the same way," but it does not equal "if you go study, you extend your life by 1.9%." The beneficiary is yourself

### 3. Before judging "are degrees devalued," look at the country's education structure: only 15,467 in every 100,000 people have a college education
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One look at the numbers below.
- TL;DR: The 2020 census found that per 100,000 people nationwide, only 15,467 have a junior-college education or higher - about 15%. Middle-school graduates number 34,507, the largest band. People over 15 average just 9.91 years of schooling - barely past middle school. So a middle-school education in China isn't "good enough" - it's the most ordinary band there is.
- Benefit: The seventh national census was taken as of November 1, 2020. Of the national population, 218,360,767 had a college education (junior college and above). High school (including secondary vocational): 213,005,258. Middle school: 487,163,489. Primary school: 349,658,828. Compared with the sixth census in 2010, the number with a college education per 100,000 people rose from 8,930 to 15,467. High school rose from 14,032 to 15,088. Middle school fell from 38,788 to 34,507. Primary school fell from 26,779 to 24,767. Average years of schooling for the population 15 and over rose from 9.08 years to 9.91 years. The illiteracy rate fell from 4.08% to 2.67%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics, Leading Group Office of the State Council for the Seventh National Population Census (2021). Communiqué of the seventh national population census (No. 6) - educational attainment of the population. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>
- Notes: This entry answers only "how scarce are degrees in the population" - not "does studying pay off for a specific person." The latter is worked out by the method in entry 6 of this chapter (how to calculate study versus work). Note too: the census counts the entire population, old and young included. The share of young people with a college education is far higher than this figure, so comparing yourself against your peers with it overstates your edge. In the other direction, using it to refute "education is useless" holds - because the denominator is exactly the whole workforce competing with you for jobs. The beneficiary is yourself

### 4. "Can't afford school" - run the policy numbers first: secondary vocational tuition is mostly waived, the grant is 2,300 yuan, and student loans go up to 20,000 a year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before the term starts, ask once at the school's student-aid office and submit the paperwork once.
- TL;DR: At public secondary vocational schools, tuition is already waived for rural students, urban students in agriculture-related majors, and students from families in financial difficulty. The grant for secondary vocational and academic high school students is 2,300 yuan a year from spring 2025. At university, the undergraduate grant is 3,700 yuan a year, and student loans go up to 20,000 a year. Not having the money can mean delaying or taking a different path - it rarely adds up to "must drop out and work right now."
- Benefit: Tuition-free secondary vocational education has been policy since the fall term of 2012. The original text: "tuition is waived for all rural (including county-town) students, urban students in agriculture-related majors, and students from families in financial difficulty among first-, second- and third-year students with full-time formal enrollment at public secondary vocational schools (except students in performance-related art majors)." Student aid policy was adjusted in 2024. The national undergraduate grant rose from 3,300 yuan per student per year to 3,700 yuan. The national endeavor scholarship rose from 5,000 yuan to 6,000 yuan. Both took effect from the fall term of 2024 (the endeavor scholarship from 2024). The national grants for secondary vocational students and academic high school students both rose from 2,000 yuan per student per year to 2,300 yuan, from the spring term of 2025. National student loans were also adjusted, from the fall term of 2024: for undergraduates, "the loan amount each person may apply for per year rises from no more than 16,000 yuan to no more than 20,000 yuan"; for graduate students, "from no more than 20,000 yuan to no more than 25,000 yuan." The interest rate is the loan prime rate (LPR) for the same term and tier minus 70 basis points - another 0.7 percentage points lower (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, National Development and Reform Commission, Ministry of Education, Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2012). Opinion on expanding the tuition-free policy for secondary vocational education and further improving the national grant system (Caijiao [2012] No. 376). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>; Officials of the relevant departments of the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Education and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2024). Press Q&A on adjusting student aid policy. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>; Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Education, People's Bank of China, National Financial Regulatory Administration (2024). Notice on adjusting and improving national student loan policy (Caijiao [2024] No. 188). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. Exactly whom the tuition waiver covers and how many tiers the grant has are set by each province. Private schools are subsidized at the standard of the local public equivalent, and the difference still comes out of your pocket - so check against the local documents before enrolling. A student loan is a loan, not a grant - it's repaid after graduation. But it splits "I can't produce tuition right now" from "do these years pay off" into two separate questions, so don't treat a lack of money as the conclusion. Chapter 7 covers training subsidies and temporary relief during unemployment - if the household income really has stopped, take that line first

### 5. Not getting into academic high school doesn't end the road: secondary vocational has through-enrollment and separate exams, and skill jobs can even lower their education requirements
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Before filling in your school preferences, check your province's vocational-education admissions platform once.
- TL;DR: The 2022 Vocational Education Law states plainly that vocational and general education are of equal importance. Secondary vocational schools can run through-enrollment with higher vocational colleges, and higher vocational colleges must set aside admission quotas or run separate exams specifically for secondary vocational graduates. Employers may not raise barriers against you for graduating from a vocational school. Falling short of academic high school means switching paths, not stopping at a middle-school education.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Vocational Education Law (effective May 1, 2022): "vocational education is a type of education of equal importance to general education." Article 37: secondary vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, run through-enrollment and through-training with higher vocational school education in relevant majors." Higher vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, admit students through assessment combining cultural aptitude and vocational skills." Education departments at the provincial level and above must build unified vocational-education admissions platforms, publishing schools, majors and admissions in aggregate and providing application services. Article 53: "students of vocational schools enjoy equal opportunities with students of general schools at the same level in further education, employment and career development." Higher vocational schools and general higher-education institutions running vocational education "shall set a corresponding proportion of their admission plans, or use separate examinations, specifically for graduates of vocational schools." "Employers shall not set application, recruitment or hiring conditions that obstruct the equal employment and fair competition of vocational school graduates." For public-institution hiring, positions with vocational skill-level requirements "may appropriately lower their education requirements" (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Vocational Education Law (2022 revision, Articles 3, 37, 53). <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html> (published by the Ministry of Education)
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. These are statutory channels, not guaranteed admission. Which majors have through-training, what proportion is set aside for separate admission, and which subjects the vocational-education entrance exam covers are all set by each province. Before applying, you must check your province's vocational-education admissions platform and admission plans for the current year - don't extrapolate from other provinces' experience. When choosing a secondary vocational major, run it through the dimensions in entry 10 of this chapter (choose automation-resistant skills): majors that require being on-site in non-standard environments are harder to replace. To judge a school, check whether it's in your province's current-year vocational admissions plan. Then verify its credentials with the three steps in entry 8 (check whether the certificate is in the catalog). Any admissions pitch promising "guaranteed job placement" or "guaranteed pass" - walk away

### 6. Turn "study or work" into a problem: three years of early wages against decades of annual income difference
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Sit down and run the numbers once - about half an hour.
- TL;DR: Lay the two paths side by side on paper. Starting work early earns three years of wages from a low-barrier job - a one-time gain. Studying on buys a higher slice of income every year afterward, multiplied by the thirty or forty years remaining. The estimate for urban China: one more year of schooling raises income by an average of 4.0% (1988) to 10.2% (2001). The data is old - treat it as an order of magnitude only.
- Benefit: This is an estimate for urban China, 1988 to 2001. How much one more year of schooling raises income is called the return to education. The original text: "rising sharply from 4.0% per year of schooling in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001." Most of the rise came after 1992. It reflects the widening of the higher-education wage premium - the extra that college graduates earn over those without. This is the Chinese domestic order of magnitude usable when converting "one more year of schooling" into an annual income difference. The global average is in entry 7 of this chapter (the global average return to one more year of education)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>
- Notes: Before calculating, first subtract the tuition waivers and grants from entry 4 of this chapter (secondary vocational tuition and grants), and there's also the account in entry 2 (extra schooling and mortality). The data ends in 2001 and covers only urban areas - it can't be used as today's figure. The same goes for the global average of 9% in entry 7. What this entry gives is the method, not the answer: run it honestly and the conclusion will differ from person to person. High tuition, a major mismatched to available jobs, and long years of study can genuinely come out not worth it. That only means "this specific program isn't worth it" - it does not add up to "education is useless," and the two mustn't be confused. A few more things don't fit into the formula but do exist. One is statutory education thresholds, see entry 5 of this chapter (through-enrollment and separate exams for secondary vocational). Two is resistance to replacement, see entry 10 (choose automation-resistant skills). Three is whether you can switch industries. The beneficiary is yourself

### 7. Remember the baseline first: one more year of education has a global average private return (the part landing in your own income) of about 9% a year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Memorize this number and use it as the ruler for every "learn something" proposal.
- TL;DR: A review covering 139 countries and 1,120 estimates finds: one more year of schooling raises personal income by about 9% a year on average. The return for women has consistently been higher than for men. Note this is the average for "one more year of formal education," not the yield of "signing up for a class." Use it as the ruler: a course asking 30,000 yuan and half a year of you should be asked whether its income effect matches a year of formal education.
- Benefit: A review covering 1950 to 2014, 139 countries and 1,120 estimates finds: "the global average private return to an additional year of education is 9% a year." The private return is the part landing in the person's own income - one more year of schooling raises income by about 9% on average. The private return to higher education is rising. The social return to education (the part landing on society rather than the person) remains high. The average return to education for women is consistently higher than for men
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>; World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>
- Notes: This is the average for "one more year of schooling" - not the yield of "signing up for a class," and not a China-specific figure; treat it as an order-of-magnitude reference. Its use is to give every training proposal a ruler: a course asking 30,000 yuan and half a year should be asked whether it can produce an income change matching a year of formal education. Note too that the global average hides enormous differences between majors and between countries
### 8. Before paying for a certificate, check whether it's in the national vocational qualification catalog or issued by an evaluation body registered with the human-resources ministry
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check, a dozen minutes.
- TL;DR: Some organizations run classes, charge fees and hand out certificates indiscriminately in the name of new occupations and skill grades - some even impersonate authoritative bodies - and the human-resources ministry ran a special cleanup over it. Before signing up, check three things: whether the occupation is in the national vocational qualification catalog, whether the issuing body is an evaluation organization registered with the human-resources department, and whether the certificate number can be looked up on the official platform. What you can't find - the sign-up fee is money thrown away.
- Benefit: The human-resources ministry ran a nationwide cleanup specifically over this. The document names the disorder: "some organizations and units, in the name of new-occupation vocational qualifications and vocational skill grades, arbitrarily hold training, evaluation and certification activities, charging indiscriminately and issuing certificates indiscriminately; some organizations and units make false or exaggerated claims, and even organize training, evaluation and certification in the name of impersonated authoritative bodies." The cleanup measures include joint enforcement with the cyberspace, public-security and market-regulation departments, "severely cracking down on evaluation and certification activities by fake evaluation organizations and by bodies impersonating evaluation organizations registered with the human-resources department." Non-compliant promotion on websites and new media is shut down by law. The document also requires "guiding the public to learn the channels and methods for checking certificates, so they can judge a certificate's legality, compliance and real worth"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2022). Notice on the special cleanup of "knockoff certificates" in technical skills. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>
- Notes: Verify in this order. First check whether the occupation is in the National Vocational Qualification Catalog - if it isn't, it isn't a national vocational qualification. Then check whether the issuing body is an evaluation organization registered with the human-resources department. Finally, go to the official skilled-talent certificate query platform and check whether the certificate number comes up. Anything that fails - don't sign up. Names carrying "international," "advanced," "registered," with promises of "guaranteed pass" or "income from affiliating the certificate" - the highest risk of all

### 9. For training, go through the government subsidy channels first - don't pay for a commercial class out of pocket at the start
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free, or heavily reduced. The training itself takes time. The hard part is resisting the impulse to pay for a class yourself - go read the subsidy catalog first.
- TL;DR: From 2025 to the end of 2027, the state plans to run subsidized training for over 30 million person-times. The money comes from several channels: employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, enterprise employee-education funds and more. The scarcer the trade and the higher the training cost, the higher the subsidy. If you're employed, ask your employer first; everyone else should read the local human-resources bureau's subsidy catalog before deciding what to learn.
- Benefit: The national scale is explicit: from 2025 to the end of 2027, "carry out subsidized training for over 30 million person-times, focusing on high-tech industries and urgently needed sectors, key employment groups and others." The funding sources are combined into one package: employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, industry development funds, east-west collaboration funds, enterprise employee-education funds and more. The subsidy level is set by how urgently scarce the occupation (trade) is and by training cost - it differs by trade. On the enterprise side there is also a requirement to "withhold and use employee-education funds in full as prescribed, ensuring that over 60% goes to the education and training of frontline workers"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council Leading Group for Employment Promotion and Labor Protection (2025). Guiding opinion on launching the large-scale vocational skill upgrading training campaign. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: How exactly to claim, and how much, is set by each province. The entry points are the "vocational training subsidy catalog" on the local human-resources bureau's website and the public training bases. Chapter 7 covered the vocational training subsidy during unemployment (at most 3 times per person) and the social-insurance subsidy. If you're employed, ask about your employer's employee-education funds first. Checking the subsidy catalog before choosing what to learn saves far more than choosing first and hunting for a subsidy afterward

### 10. When choosing a skill, look first at "does it require hands, does it require on-the-spot judgment" - these are the hardest for automation to take over
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Run whatever you're thinking of learning through this dimension before choosing.
- TL;DR: One assessment of 702 US occupations put about 47% of jobs in the high-risk zone. What stops the machine is three things: dexterity of the hands, the ability to come up with new things, and the ability to deal with people. So work that happens on-site, in non-standard environments, demanding judgment in the moment is harder to replace; jobs with fixed processes that mostly process information carry the highest risk. Don't fixate on the 47% figure - look at the three dimensions.
- Benefit: Each of 702 US occupations was scored on "how likely could this work be handed to a computer" (a probability-of-computerisation assessment), and about 47% of employment fell in the high-risk zone. What decides the risk in the model is three hurdles machines currently can't clear, which the authors call "engineering bottlenecks." One is perception and manipulation - finger dexterity, manual dexterity, working in cramped spaces and awkward postures. Two is creative intelligence - the ability to come up with new things. Three is social intelligence - the ability to deal with people. So work in non-standard environments requiring manual skill and on-the-spot judgment has consistently lower replacement probability - not a coincidence of individual occupations. Jobs with fixed processes centered on information processing carry the highest risk
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>
- Notes: Disputed. This study began as an unpublished 2013 working paper, and it scored "can the entire occupation be taken over by machines." Later research re-estimated by breaking jobs into individual tasks and arrived at much lower high-risk shares. It also says nothing about when any of this would happen. The right way to use it is not to look at the 47% figure but at the three places where machines get stuck. That explains why on-site installation, repair and care work resist replacement better than pure paperwork - and why "learning a skill" should prioritize the kind that requires being on-site facing non-standard environments

### 11. Before learning, confirm whether the skill has a grading system - and whether the grades convert into money
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check is enough.
- TL;DR: The state is pushing a "new eight-grade worker" system, requiring enterprises to link post pay to skill grade in both directions and to expand assessment and hiring at the senior-worker level and above. Qualified workers can even have a grade recognized directly, skipping seniority requirements. But this is a policy direction, not a wage figure already cashed out - implementation differs enormously by place and employer. There is only one usable judgment: a skill with a national occupational standard and a grade ladder is one where raises by grade become possible later.
- Benefit: The state is pushing the "new eight-grade worker" vocational skill grade system. The document explicitly requires linking grades to pay; the original words: "strengthen the skill-oriented pay distribution system... guide enterprises in adopting a distribution method that links post pay to both post value and skill grade." Localities must "expand the intensity and frequency of assessment and hiring at the senior-worker level and above." Qualified enterprises may run skill-grade evaluations of their own workers. For workers with outstanding performance, "the conditions of working years, grade and seniority may be broken, and the corresponding vocational skill grade directly recognized"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: State Council Leading Group for Employment Promotion and Labor Protection (2025). Guiding opinion on launching the large-scale vocational skill upgrading training campaign. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>; Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2021). Implementation plan for the "Skilled China Action." <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/06/content_5622619.htm>
- Notes: This is a policy direction, not a wage figure already cashed out - implementation differs enormously by place and employer. The part you can act on: when choosing a skill, look at whether it has a corresponding national occupational standard and grade ladder; only with a ladder does raising pay by grade become a conversation later. A "skill" with no grades and no industry entry threshold leaves you finding work entirely on your own after learning it

### 12. Don't take "the internet says the pay is high" as proof of shortage - check the local shortage-occupation catalog and subsidy catalog
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Look up two local documents.
- TL;DR: The subsidy amounts each place offers are themselves the official pricing of "which trades are short of people," set by urgency of shortage and training cost. How much the same trade's subsidy differs between cities reflects the difference in local shortage. The "trade X pays Y a month" claims online mostly come from listing prices on recruitment platforms or personal accounts - they look only at the winners, and they don't count hours, commutes, occupational disease or off-seasons.
- Benefit: The subsidy amounts are themselves the official pricing of "which trades are short of people." The policy requires "improving the differentiated subsidy mechanism set according to the urgency of shortage of the occupation (trade), training cost and other factors," and each locality uses it to define its own subsidized vocational training project scope. So how much the same trade's subsidy differs between cities directly reflects the local degree of shortage
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Council Leading Group for Employment Promotion and Labor Protection (2025). Guiding opinion on launching the large-scale vocational skill upgrading training campaign. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: TODO (unverified: an official data source for national average wages by occupation and shortage-occupation rankings; the relevant human-resources ministry pages could not be retrieved this round). ### 13. For the same money and time, prefer short-cycle programs that put you straight into a job
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: The short course has tuition, not much. It also takes a stretch of time. The hard part is stopping at the agreed conditions when nothing has come of it halfway through.
- TL;DR: What the policy encourages is "short, fast" training that leads to quick employment, plus micro-majors at vocational colleges. Judge a program by one thing only: after finishing, is there a concrete job you can send a resume to. Something that takes a year or half a year and still needs "further experience accumulation" after graduation costs far more time than its sticker price. Write the exit conditions before you start: if no job by this point, stop.
- Benefit: The policy wording aligns with this: it supports "private training institutions running market-oriented, high-quality vocational skill training programs and 'short, flat, fast' vocational skill training programs that lead to employment in a short time." It also pushes vocational colleges to "focus on the knowledge and skills urgently needed in the job market... opening micro-majors and vocational-ability training courses"
- Evidence: C
- Sources: State Council Leading Group for Employment Promotion and Labor Protection (2025). Guiding opinion on launching the large-scale vocational skill upgrading training campaign. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: The criterion is one thing: after finishing, is there a concrete job you can send a resume to. A program that takes a year or half a year and still needs "further experience accumulation" after graduation costs far more than its sticker price. The exit conditions from chapter 4 apply here too: before starting, write down plainly "if no job by this point, stop"
### 14. After studying, close the book and test yourself - don't go back and reread
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=time -->
- Cost: Free, and total time doesn't increase. Take the time you used to spend rereading and spend it reciting from memory or doing problems with the book closed. The hard part is that self-testing exposes on the spot what you haven't retained - it feels worse than rereading.
- TL;DR: Reading something and then reading it again feels familiar, but it doesn't stick. Closing the book and testing yourself works markedly better. Pool 222 classroom studies with nearly fifty thousand students, and classes with quizzes added improved grades by a middling amount. In a review that ranked ten study methods, self-testing sat in the top tier.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 222 independent studies and 48,478 students: classroom quizzing improves students' academic achievement, with a medium effect size (g = 0.499). The size of the effect depends on several conditions: what study method the control group used, whether the quiz format matched the final exam, whether the materials matched, whether corrective feedback was given, how many times the quiz was repeated, where and when testing happened, how long the intervention lasted, and what experimental design was used. Another review assessing ten common study methods one by one rated "practice testing" and "distributed practice" in the top tier (high utility), on the grounds that they work for learners of different ages and abilities and have been verified across many assessment formats and real educational settings
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Yang C, Luo L, Vadillo MA, Yu R, Shanks DR (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 147(4), 399-435. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309>; Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4-58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: Self-testing isn't for giving yourself a score - it's for forcing the material out of your head. The method: after reading a section, close the book and write out the key points from memory, then flip back and check. Corrective feedback improves the effect, so after checking answers, look carefully at where you went wrong. The "big" benefit grade here is judged by effect size and rating; these two sources measure grades, not hours, and weren't graded against a time-saved threshold. This and entry 15 (spread the same time across days) are the two top-rated methods in the same review - use them together.

### 15. Spread the same time across several days - don't learn it all in one sitting
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=time -->
- Cost: Free, and total time doesn't change. Only the scheduling changes: the same 3 hours split into 1 hour on each of three days. The hard part is starting a few days early instead of dragging it to the last night.
- TL;DR: For the same total time, learning spread across days sticks better than one sitting. 317 experiments have tested this, with a consistent conclusion. There's also a practical rule: the longer you need to remember something, the longer your review intervals should be. Exam tomorrow - review today; exam in a month - go over it every few days.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooling 184 articles, 317 experiments and 839 assessments of distributed-practice effects. It compared two things: learning in one stretch versus spread over sittings (the spacing effect), and short versus long intervals (the lag effect). The conclusion: the review interval and "how far ahead you need to remember" together decide retention at the final test; specifically, the review interval that gives the best retention lengthens as the retention period lengthens. Another review assessing ten common study methods one by one rated distributed practice in the top tier (high utility)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Cepeda NJ, Pashler H, Vul E, Wixted JT, Rohrer D (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354-380. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354>; Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4-58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: This works best stacked with entry 14 (close the book and test yourself): spread the self-testing itself across days. Note that the material in the Cepeda meta-analysis is mostly word-list recall and the like; no universal number of days is given as the best interval for complex content - "the further out the test, the longer the interval" above is the rule the original gives, not a concrete schedule. The problem with cramming the night before isn't that it fails on the spot - it's that it fades fast.

### 16. Don't make highlighting, rereading and summarizing your main study methods
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free - it actually saves time. The hard part is that these are the smoothest to do and give the strongest "I'm studying" feeling.
- TL;DR: What students use most is highlighting and rereading, and the review that ranked ten study methods put both in the lowest tier. Summarizing, the keyword mnemonic and imagery for text are in the lowest tier too. Not that they're completely useless - the conditions under which they work are too narrow. The same time is better spent on self-testing.
- Benefit: A review assessing ten common study methods rated them by "whether the effect generalizes across learning conditions, student characteristics, materials and assessment formats." The top tier: practice testing and distributed practice. The middle tier: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaved practice. The five in the lowest tier: summarizing, highlighting (or underlining), the keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, and rereading. The review states plainly: most students report using rereading and highlighting, but these two do not reliably improve achievement and should be swapped for other methods - such as replacing rereading with practice testing
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4-58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: This entry doesn't ban underlining. Underlining as "marking which spots to self-test on later" is useful; the problem is treating the underlining itself as studying. The review also notes that the lowest tier doesn't mean zero - the conditions under which they work are narrow, and more research is needed. The replacement moves are in entry 14 (close the book and test yourself) and entry 15 (spread the same time across days).

### 17. Mix problem types when you practice - don't do twenty of one kind in a row
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Just switch the workbook from type-by-type order to shuffled order. The hard part is that mixed practice lowers your accuracy in the moment - it feels like regressing.
- TL;DR: Doing a long string of one problem type feels smooth in the moment, but afterward you may not be able to tell which method to use. Shuffling several types together works better long-term, by a middling amount. But it depends on the material: most effective for images and classification, smaller for math problems, and for vocabulary, blocked practice is actually better.
- Benefit: A multilevel meta-analysis of 59 studies, 238 effect sizes and 158 samples. The overall effect of interleaved practice over blocked practice is medium (Hedges' g = 0.42). By material: paintings did best (g = 0.67), with other visual materials also doing well; math tasks showed a smaller effect (g = 0.34); expository texts and taste materials showed no significant overall effect; for vocabulary, blocked practice was actually better (g = −0.39). Regression analysis showed: the more similar the categories are to each other, the less similar items within a category, and the more complex the material, the greater the benefit of interleaving
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Brunmair M, Richter T (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin, 145(11), 1029-1052. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209>
- Notes: The review cited in entry 16 (don't make highlighting your main method) rated interleaved practice in the middle tier, on the grounds that research was then still scarce. Reading the two together, the usage is: content where you must "recognize which kind this is and which method to use" - math problem types, clinical cases, applying statutes - practice mixed; content for rote memory - vocabulary, term definitions - practice blocked.

### 18. Don't pick a study method by "I'm a visual type, he's an auditory type"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. You simply stop picking materials and courses by this classification, and stop paying for style assessments.
- TL;DR: "Everyone has their own learning style, and teaching matched to the style works best" circulates widely. Proving it requires grouping students by style, randomly assigning teaching methods to each group, and testing everyone on the same exam - and almost no study has produced results supporting it. People really do have preferences; that teaching to the preference improves learning has no evidence.
- Benefit: A review commissioned to assess the learning-styles evidence points out: to prove that "teaching to learning styles" works, students must first be grouped by style, each group randomly assigned to different teaching methods, and everyone finally tested on the same exam. The criterion for validity is a specific interaction - the method most effective for one style is not the most effective for another. The review's conclusion: almost no such interaction has been found; while the learning-styles literature is large, very few studies use an adequate experimental design, and several of those produced results directly opposite the "meshing hypothesis." On this basis the authors hold that the current evidence is insufficient to support using learning-style assessments in educational practice
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Pashler H, McDaniel M, Rohrer D, Bjork R (2008). Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105-119. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x>
- Notes: The review also notes that this doesn't mean every version of learning-styles theory has been tested and refuted - many have never been tested. What this entry blocks is two moves: paying for a style assessment, and picking a training course by style. The standards for picking training are in entry 9 (use government subsidy channels first) and entry 13 (prefer short-cycle programs that put you straight into a job).

### 19. For material you need to memorize, quiz yourself in the form you'll actually use it - don't just recite it as-is
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free, and no extra time. Only the drill changes: "recite it as-is once" becomes "quiz yourself in the form you'll actually use it." The hard part is thinking through how you'll use this material later.
- TL;DR: Testing yourself with the book closed doesn't just help the exact words you recited. Pool 192 comparisons with over ten thousand people, and it also works on questions with changed formats, on application problems and on reasoning problems - by a medium-to-small amount. But two cases are almost useless: swapping the question and answer around, and material seen in the book but never tested.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooling over 40 years of research - 67 published and unpublished articles, 122 experiments, 192 transfer effect sizes, 10,382 people in total. Compared with "look again without testing" controls, the learning from practice testing does transfer to other situations, with effect size d=0.40 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.50). Transfer was strongest in these cases: testing with a changed question format, testing application and inference problems, medical-diagnosis-type questions, and testing semantically related cue words. Transfer was weakest for: swapping stimulus and response, material seen during initial study but never tested, and worked-example-type problems. Among the moderators, whether the response format at practice matched the one at test, whether retrieval practice with detailed feedback had been done, and whether the initial test was answered correctly all strongly affected whether positive transfer appeared. The authors also corrected for publication bias in two ways: the moderators' effect sizes were little affected, but the intercept estimate was pushed down markedly - meaning that when none of the conditions above hold, positive transfer often can't be seen
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Pan SC, Rickard TC (2018). Transfer of test-enhanced learning: meta-analytic review and synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710-756. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151>
- Notes: The word "memorize" in everyday speech mixes two things: going over the material again (rereading, highlighting), and actively recalling with the book closed. The first kind sits in the lowest tier in the review that ranked ten study methods - for the ratings themselves, see entry 16 (don't make highlighting, rereading and summarizing your main study methods). The second kind is what this entry is about: actively recalling with the book closed, specifically entry 14 (close the book and test yourself), and done across several days, see entry 15 (spread it across days). The method is to think through how you'll use it first - whether you need to pick it out on an exam or judge it on the spot - then quiz yourself in that form. Quizzing once in the swapped direction is also worth doing, since transfer in the swapped direction was the weakest in this meta-analysis - both directions need practice. Memorizing something and then never testing it in a changed form, never putting it to use - that's the real waste.
