# 26. Building a website or platform: qualifications, registration, and servers

You want to build a website or an app. This chapter answers four things: what kind of business it legally counts as, which license you need, where to put the server, and who answers for what users post on it and the money it takes. The lens is personal freedom and money. The order of the entries: what can land you in prison first, then what needs a license in advance, then what a platform has to do every day, and finally an experience-based entry on choosing a server - that one can give judgment but no numbers. If you're an employed technician, the red lines are in chapter 11. How to register a company and file its taxes is in chapter 12, not repeated here. For a table matching each license to its case and a decision table for choosing a server, see [docs/licenses-for-running-a-platform.md](../docs/which-licenses-a-platform-needs.md).

### 1. A platform should not take buyers' money itself and pass it to sellers - let the money settle directly through a licensed payment institution
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. It takes some time to integrate a licensed payment institution's product. A licensed payment institution is a company holding the central bank's payment license. Using its split-settlement or escrow feature, the buyer's money settles straight to the seller and never enters the platform's own account
- TL;DR: The buyer's money lands in your account first and you forward it to the seller - that is running a payment business. That business needs approval from the People's Bank of China, plus 100 million yuan of real capital. Run it unapproved and it gets shut down, the earnings confiscated, plus a fine - with a separate fine for the boss and the people who handled it. Serious cases bring prison. There is only one safe approach: the money never enters your account.
- Benefit: What the regulation calls payment business is "the transfer of monetary funds according to electronic payment instructions submitted by a payee or payer." In plain terms, moving money for others on their instruction. To run this business, you "shall be approved by the People's Bank of China and obtain a payment business license." Minimum registered capital is 100 million yuan, fully paid in monetary capital - the 100 million must really land in the company account. "Whoever, without lawful approval, establishes a non-bank payment institution or engages or disguisedly engages in payment business shall be banned by the People's Bank of China by law, with illegal gains confiscated." In other words: do it unapproved and it's shut down outright, the earnings taken. With illegal gains of 500,000 yuan or more, an additional fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. With no illegal gains, or under 500,000 yuan, a fine of 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan. The legal representative and the directly responsible people are separately fined 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. The criminal law also counts "illegally engaging in fund payment and settlement business" as illegal business operation: serious cases carry up to 5 years, especially serious cases over 5 years. What counts as "serious" is lined out in the Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate's interpretation: an illegal business volume of 5 million yuan or more, or illegal gains of 100,000 yuan or more (nationwide, since May 2024)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2023). Regulation on the supervision and administration of non-bank payment institutions (State Council Order No. 768, Articles 2, 6, 8, 27, 47). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; People's Bank of China (2024). Implementation rules for the regulation on the supervision and administration of non-bank payment institutions (PBOC Order [2024] No. 4, Article 68). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Article 225, item 3). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate (2019). Interpretation on the application of law in criminal cases of illegally engaging in fund payment and settlement business and illegally trading foreign exchange (Articles 1, 3, 4). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Notes: Designing the money so it never enters your account is the only safe approach. The trade calls this collect-and-forward setup "second clearing," but that term appears in no official document. The implementation rules use "disguisedly engaging in payment business," meaning moving money on users' instructions without approval. The Supreme People's Court and Procuratorate interpretation lists only three situations: fabricating transactions to cash out, using an organizational account to cash out, and using checks to cash out. It does not name platform collect-and-forward. So this entry follows the statutory text and does not say "it definitely constitutes a crime"

### 2. A website that charges users needs a license; a platform matching transactions is another kind of license; a free site only needs registration
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Registration (beian) is free. The value-added telecom business license costs money - it governs "whether a website that charges users may operate." Getting that license first requires a company. Operating within one province, registered capital of at least 1,000,000 yuan. Across provinces, at least 10,000,000 yuan. The review period is 60 days and the license is valid 5 years
- TL;DR: A website that charges users first needs a value-added telecom business license. That license is issued only to companies - an individual cannot get one. A website that doesn't charge only needs registration, which is free. Charging without the license brings confiscation of revenue plus a fine, and serious cases get the site shut. Whether your platform counts as the transaction-matching kind, this entry does not decide for you - the reason is in the notes.
- Benefit: The regulation's original words: "the state implements a licensing system for commercial internet information services, and a registration system for non-commercial internet information services. Without a license or without completing registration, no one may engage in internet information services." In other words, charging needs a license first, free needs registration first, and with neither you can't open the site. What does commercial mean? The regulation says "service activities that provide information or webpage production and the like to internet users for payment via the internet." In short, charging users. The transaction-matching kind is called online data processing and transaction processing business, known in the trade as EDI. The regulation's definition: "business that uses various data and transaction/affair processing application platforms connected to public communication networks or the internet to provide users with online data processing and transaction/affair processing via public communication networks or the internet." It also "includes transaction processing business, electronic data interchange business, and network/electronic equipment data processing business." In plain terms, a website processing data online for users, or matching and handling transactions between buyers and sellers. Operating without the license, or beyond its scope, brings confiscation of illegal gains plus a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains. Beyond-scope operation means having the license but doing things outside what it states. With no illegal gains, or illegal gains under 50,000 yuan, the fine is 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Serious cases are ordered to close the website (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2000). Measures for the administration of internet information services (State Council Order No. 292, second revision December 6, 2024, Articles 3, 4, 7, 19). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; State Council (2024). Decision on amending and repealing certain administrative regulations (State Council Order No. 797, item 18). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; State Council (2000). Telecommunications regulation (2016 second revision, Articles 7, 9, 13, 69). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2017). Measures for the administration of telecom business operating licenses (MIIT Order No. 42, Articles 6, 9, 11, 14). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Notes: Whether what you're building is the EDI kind, match it yourself against the definitions in the "Telecom business classification catalog." No official sentence anywhere states outright that "an e-commerce platform with matching and fund handling must obtain the EDI license." The MIIT's service guide says only to "apply for the telecom business operating license corresponding to the business as defined." So this entry lays out the definitions and does not conclude for you. One more point: this license can't be had by an individual - the applicant must be a lawfully established company

### 3. Livestreaming needs the internet culture business license; the audiovisual-program license is basically out of reach for private companies
<!-- tags: money=high time=high will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: The internet culture business license - colloquially the "net-culture cert" - governs "whether you can earn from network performances like livestreaming and game commentary." It is approved by the provincial culture department, decided within 20 days of acceptance, valid 3 years, and renewed 30 days before expiry. The audiovisual-program license governs "whether you can show programs online," and it requires the applicant to be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity
- TL;DR: To do livestreaming or game commentary, you need the internet culture business license from the provincial culture department. The license's business scope must explicitly include network performance. Decided within 20 days of acceptance, valid 3 years. Videos you have broadcast must be kept by you for a full 60 days. Opening without the license brings a stop order and a warning, plus a fine of up to 30,000 yuan; keep going and you're on the blacklist.
- Benefit: An organization doing network performances must get the license first. The regulation's original words: "it shall, in accordance with the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture, apply to the provincial culture department for the Internet Culture Business License, and the license's business scope shall explicitly include network performance." Playing games for an audience or commentating on them, the regulation says, is "administered with reference to these Measures" - the same set applies. For all network performance video material, the "retention time shall be no less than 60 days" - you keep what you broadcast for a full 60 days yourself. Engaging in commercial internet culture activities without approval brings an order to stop and a warning, plus a fine of up to 30,000 yuan; refuse to stop and you're entered on the culture-market blacklist. Showing programs online is a different license: you "shall obtain the License for the Dissemination of Audiovisual Programs via Information Networks." The applicant must "have legal-person status and be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity" - that is, fully state-funded or state-controlled, which private companies basically cannot meet. Operating without this license brings a warning and an order to correct, plus a possible fine of up to 30,000 yuan. Serious cases are punished under the radio and television administration regulation (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Culture (2016). Measures for the administration of network performance business activities (Wenshifa [2016] No. 33, Articles 2, 4, 6, 13, 20). <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; Ministry of Culture (2011). Interim provisions on the administration of internet culture (Ministry of Culture Order No. 51, revised 2017 by Ministry of Culture Order No. 57, Articles 7, 8, 9, 16, 21, 28). <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; State Administration of Radio, Film and Television, Ministry of Information Industry (2007). Provisions on the administration of internet audiovisual program services (Order No. 56, 2015 revision, Articles 2, 7, 8, 24). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments (2021). Guiding opinion on strengthening the standardized administration of online livestreaming (Guoxinbanfawen [2021] No. 3, item 9). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Notes: Which business pairs with which license is laid out most clearly in the seven-department document. A platform earning from livestreaming needs the net-culture cert plus ICP registration - the ICP registration being the filing procedure with the industry ministry. Showing programs online needs the audiovisual license, or completed registration. Publishing news needs the internet news information service license. One more thing: the version of the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture reposted on the Chinese government website in 2025 is the un-revised 2011 text - cite the current text from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism

### 4. A server kept in mainland China must be registered, and the access provider must itself hold a value-added telecom license
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Registration is free. It's submitted together with the server at the provider, usually taking days to weeks. Checking once whether the provider is qualified takes ten minutes
- TL;DR: With the server kept in mainland China, the site must be registered before it opens. Registration is free, submitted with the server at the provider, generally taking days to weeks. Unregistered sites get an order to correct by a deadline plus a 10,000-yuan fine, and refusal means shutdown. Not displaying the registration number on the homepage is a separate fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan.
- Benefit: The regulation's original words: "without registration, no one may engage in non-commercial internet information services within the People's Republic of China." In other words, even a website that doesn't charge users must register first if it's kept in the mainland. Unregistered: ordered to correct by a deadline, plus a 10,000-yuan fine. Refusal to correct: the website is closed. The provider connecting your server to the internet is regulated too: it "may not provide internet access services to unregistered organizations or individuals engaging in non-commercial internet information services." The provider collecting money for you likewise. The regulation says such providers "may not provide access, fee-collection or other services to organizations or individuals that have not lawfully obtained a business license or completed non-commercial internet information service registration." In other words, a site with neither license nor registration may not be taken on by a proper provider. Furthermore, colocation and cloud hosting are called internet data center business in the regulation, and broadband access is called internet access service business - both require their own licenses, so the provider must hold them itself. Not displaying the registration number on the homepage: ordered to correct, plus a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Information Industry (2005). Measures for the administration of registration of non-commercial internet information services (MII Order No. 33, revised 2024 by MIIT Order No. 68, Articles 5, 13, 18, 22). <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2017). Measures for the administration of telecom business operating licenses (MIIT Order No. 42, Articles 20, 24). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2015). Telecom business classification catalog (2015 edition, B11, B14, B21, B25). <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html>
- Notes: Before talking price, check whether the provider holds the licenses. To check a license, use the MIIT telecom business market comprehensive administration system tsm.miit.gov.cn, searching by the company's full name or license number. To check a website's registration, use beian.miit.gov.cn. A "server room" without the matching license: the day something happens, you won't even know whom to appeal to. Moving the machines offshore does bypass the registration requirement. But with users in the mainland and money in the mainland, not one of the obligations in entries 5 through 10 of this chapter goes away - and a whole layer of data-export cost is added

### 5. Let users sell on your platform, and you must verify and register them, report their information, and keep records for three years
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. You have to build seller qualification verification into the back end and keep a registration file. Tax-related information goes in every quarter, and transaction data is kept three years. The hard part is that it's year-round work, not a one-time procedure
- TL;DR: Let others sell on your platform, and you must first verify each seller's identity, address, contact details and licenses, then register them and build a file. Once the file exists, go back and re-verify at least every six months. Who the sellers are is reported to market regulation; their tax situation goes to the tax authority quarterly. Product and transaction records are kept three years. Miss any one item and you're first ordered to correct; refuse, and the fine is 20,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: The law has a passage of requirements for platforms. The original words: "it shall require operators applying to enter the platform to sell goods or provide services to submit true information on their identity, address, contact details and administrative licenses, conduct verification and registration, establish registration files, and verify and update them regularly." In other words, before a seller enters, they hand over identity, address, contact details and the licenses they should hold. You verify it all, register and file it, and after a stretch go back and re-verify. How long is "regularly"? The departmental rule is more specific: "verify and update at least once every six months." You also report the identity information of on-platform operators to the market-regulation department. The tax side gets reports too; the original words: "in accordance with tax-collection administration laws and administrative regulations, report to the tax department the identity information of on-platform operators and information related to tax payment." In one sentence: who the sellers are goes to market regulation; their identity and tax situation goes to the tax authority. Product and service information and transaction information are kept "no less than three years from the completion of the transaction" - at least three years from the day each deal closes. Failing any of the above: ordered to correct by a deadline. Refusing past the deadline: a fine of 20,000 to 100,000 yuan. Serious cases: ordered to suspend business for rectification, plus a fine of 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. Tax-related information must be reported "within the month following the end of each quarter." Not reporting on time carries the same penalties (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). E-Commerce Law (Articles 10, 27, 28, 31, 80). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; State Administration for Market Regulation (2021). Measures for the supervision and administration of online transactions (SAMR Order No. 37, Articles 8, 24, 25, 31). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; State Council (2025). Provisions on internet platform enterprises reporting tax-related information (State Council Order No. 810, Articles 2, 3, 4, 10). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; State Taxation Administration (2025). Announcement on matters concerning internet platform enterprises reporting tax-related information (STA Announcement No. 15 of 2025). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Notes: The sellers themselves must also register as market entities. The law lists a few cases that don't have to: "individuals selling self-produced agricultural and sideline products or home handicrafts, individuals using their own skills for convenience services that lawfully require no license, and sporadic small transactions." "Sporadic small" has a concrete number: Article 8 of the online-transaction supervision measures sets it at 100,000 yuan in annual transaction volume. The platform side has two more jobs: marking registered and unregistered sellers apart, and not deleting consumers' reviews

### 6. What users post is yours to police: review mechanisms, a report channel, and immediately stopping illegal content and reporting it
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Hiring reviewers costs money - the platform's biggest long-term expense. The report channel and the record of every action taken must leave a trace. The hard part is that someone has to watch it every day; one day off and something can happen
- TL;DR: For what users post, the platform answers when something goes wrong. You must build this system: registration, post review, comment review, daily patrols, and how incidents are handled. Put a report channel somewhere prominent on the page. See illegal information - stop its spread at once, keep the records, report it to the authorities. Skip this, and the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan; serious cases, more.
- Benefit: The regulation says the platform "shall perform its primary responsibility for information content management" - what users post is the platform's own job to police. Specifically it must "improve systems for user registration, account management, information-release review, follow-up comment review, page-section ecology management, real-time patrols, emergency handling, and handling of online rumors and black-industry-chain information." From sign-up, posting and commenting to daily patrols and incident handling, every link needs written rules. It must also "set up a convenient complaint and report entrance in a prominent position" - the report button where users can see and reach it at a glance. On discovering illegal information, it "shall immediately take handling measures by law, preserve the relevant records, and report to the relevant competent departments." The cybersecurity law is more specific. The original words: "on discovering information whose publication or transmission is prohibited by laws or administrative regulations, it shall immediately stop transmitting that information, take handling measures such as removal, prevent the information from spreading, preserve the relevant records, and report to the relevant competent departments." Falling short: ordered to correct, warned, and circulated a notice of criticism, with a possible fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. Refusing to correct, or serious cases: a fine of 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan, plus possible orders to suspend business for rectification, close the website or app, or revocation of the license or business license. The directly responsible people are separately fined 50,000 to 200,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Cyberspace Administration of China (2019). Provisions on the governance of the online information content ecology (CAC Order No. 5, Articles 8, 9, 10, 16, 37). <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 revision, effective January 1, 2026, Articles 49, 69). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; State Council (2000). Measures for the administration of internet information services (Articles 15, 16, 23). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Notes: You don't have to invent the review rules - copy them. The nine categories of content that may not be published are listed plainly in Article 15 of the internet information service measures. Livestreaming adds three more jobs: "establish a livestream content review platform," be able to cut off an ongoing stream at any moment - the statute calls this "immediate blocking" - and keep livestream content and logs for 60 days. Network performance video material likewise: no less than 60 days
### 7. Offering information publishing or instant messaging means you must require users' real identity information
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: You have to integrate a real-name verification channel, which charges per call. Design the product on the "real name in the back end, voluntary up front" model - the back end stores the real name, the page shows a nickname
- TL;DR: If your website lets people post, or lets people chat, you must require their real identity information when they activate the service. If they won't give it, they can't use it. The way it's done: the back end stores the real name, the page shows whatever nickname they choose, and a phone number is enough for verification. Skip real-name and you're first ordered to correct. Refuse, and the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, with possible shutdown.
- Benefit: The law's original words: "when a network operator handles network access or domain-name registration services for users, handles fixed-line or mobile network enrollment, or provides users with services such as information publishing or instant messaging, it shall, when signing an agreement with the user or confirming the provision of services, require the user to provide real identity information. Where the user does not provide real identity information, the network operator may not provide the relevant services to them." Not requiring real names - or letting someone use the service anyway when they haven't given their real identity - brings an order to correct. Refusing to correct, or serious cases: a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, plus possible orders to suspend the relevant business, suspend business for rectification, close the website or app, or revocation of the license or business license. The directly responsible people are separately fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Livestreaming services follow the "real name in the back end, voluntary up front" principle, doing "real-identity authentication based on mobile phone numbers and similar means." Meaning the platform's back end holds the user's real name, the page shows whatever nickname the user chooses, and verification runs on phone numbers and the like. The livestream-commerce side is stricter: "real-identity authentication based on ID-card information, unified social credit codes and the like." The unified social credit code is the number on the business license (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law (2025 revision, effective January 1, 2026, Articles 26, 64; the 2016 text had Articles 24, 61). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2016). Provisions on the administration of internet livestreaming services (Articles 12, 16). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments (2021). Measures for the administration of online livestream marketing (trial) (Guoxinbanfawen [2021] No. 5, Article 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Notes: The article numbers moved. The Cybersecurity Law was revised in 2025, effective January 1, 2026. The real-name clause moved from Article 24 to Article 26, and the penalty clause from Article 61 to Article 64. The text of the provisions didn't change. If the material in your hands still cites the old numbers, update it

### 8. No streaming accounts for anyone under 16, and tipping is handled in tiers by age
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Add one step each at registration and at payment: determine the age first, then confirm the guardian consented
- TL;DR: Anyone under 16 cannot register a streamer account with you. At 16 and over, you must verify their identity and obtain their parents' or other guardian's consent. Tips fall into three tiers by age: under 8 cannot tip at all, 8 to 16 needs guardian consent, 16 and over needs guardian consent or verified proof of income. When a child tips behind the guardian's back and the guardian comes to reclaim it, the court orders it returned.
- Benefit: The law's original words: "online livestreaming service providers shall not provide livestream publisher account registration services to minors under sixteen years of age; when providing livestream publisher account registration services to minors who have reached sixteen, they shall authenticate their identity information and obtain the consent of their parents or other guardians." The regulation also requires "establishing a dynamic verification mechanism for livestream publishers' real identity information" - you must be able to re-verify a streamer's identity at any time. It also requires "reasonably limiting the per-transaction and single-day cumulative spending amounts of minors of different age groups when using its services" - age-based per-transaction and daily spending caps for minors. Tips now fall into three tiers: "tipping services shall not be provided to minors under eight years of age. Providing tipping services to minors aged eight to sixteen requires the consent of their guardian. Providing tipping services to minors over sixteen requires the consent of their guardian or verification of their proof of income." One more clause: where a person with limited civil capacity tips without the guardian's consent and the guardian seeks repayment, the court shall support it - the money can be clawed back. A person with limited civil capacity means a minor who legally cannot yet fully decide for themselves (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Law on the protection of minors (Articles 74, 76). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; State Council (2023). Regulation on the online protection of minors (State Council Order No. 766, Articles 31, 44). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; Secretariat of the Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission (2026). Notice on strengthening the standardized administration of online livestream tipping (item 7). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding opinion (II) on properly trying civil cases involving the COVID-19 pandemic by law (Fafa [2020] No. 17, Article 9). <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>
- Notes: If a refund dispute really arises, resolve it in the direction most favorable to the minor. The wording on this has changed. The 2022 four-department opinion and the broadcast regulator's 2020 notice both said minors may not tip at all. The new April 2026 notice switches to tiers at 8 and 16 - without stating that the earlier two are repealed, and officials have not said how the two connect. So "tipping is banned for all minors" is no longer accurate

### 9. Handle infringement notices promptly; 15 days after forwarding the counter-statement with no follow-up, restore the content
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. You need a fixed process: receive the notice, forward it to the seller, take the seller's defense, publish the outcome. File every step
- TL;DR: Someone sends an infringement notice with preliminary evidence attached, and you must promptly delete, block or delink the item, while forwarding the notice to the seller. Drag your feet, and you and the seller are jointly liable for the additional damage. If the seller files a defense, you forward the defense to the claimant and wait 15 days. No complaint and no lawsuit - put the item back.
- Benefit: The rights holder "has the right to notify the e-commerce platform operator to take necessary measures such as deleting, blocking, disconnecting links, and terminating transactions and services. The notice shall include preliminary evidence of the infringement." The rights holder is the owner of the trademark, copyright or patent. A bare sentence doesn't count - preliminary evidence must come with it. After the platform receives the notice, it "shall promptly take necessary measures and forward the notice to the on-platform operator; where it fails to take necessary measures promptly, it bears joint and several liability with the on-platform operator for the enlarged part of the damage." In other words, drag your feet and you and the seller together pay for the extra damage. After the seller's defense, the platform "shall, if within fifteen days of forwarding the statement to the intellectual-property rights holder it has not received notice that the rights holder has complained or sued, promptly terminate the measures taken." Meaning: forward the seller's defense to the rights holder, wait 15 days; no complaint and no lawsuit - restore what was deleted or taken down. A platform that knows or should know of infringement and doesn't act bears joint and several liability with the infringer - pays together with the seller. The intellectual-property department can also order you to correct by a deadline; past it, a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and serious cases 500,000 to 2,000,000 yuan. In the other direction, "maliciously issuing an erroneous notice and causing loss to the on-platform operator bears doubled compensation liability" (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). E-Commerce Law (Articles 42, 43, 44, 45, 84). <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code (Articles 1195, 1196, 1197). <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: If you're not an e-commerce platform, just an ordinary website, you follow the Civil Code's notice-and-takedown rule. The difference is how long you wait after the counter-statement: the E-Commerce Law gives an explicit 15 days, the Civil Code only says "a reasonable period." Also, publishing the notices you receive, the sellers' defenses, and your handling outcomes is an express requirement of Article 44 of the E-Commerce Law

### 10. Don't casually store user information overseas - exporting it has statutory conditions and headcount thresholds
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. The easiest path is keeping user data inside the country. If you truly must transfer it abroad, use the standard contract or get certified
- TL;DR: Moving mainland users' information onto machines abroad - that's a data export. Keeping the server abroad does not mean the data hasn't been exported. Export requires one of four routes: the security assessment organized by the cyberspace authority, certification by a specialized body, signing the standard contract, or another basis in law. You also have to tell users who the overseas recipient is and what they'll do with the data, and ask the user about this specifically.
- Benefit: Providing personal information abroad - handing mainland users' information to a company abroad or storing it on machines abroad - "shall satisfy" one of four conditions. One, pass the security assessment organized by the cyberspace authority. Two, obtain personal-information protection certification from a specialized body. Three, sign a contract with the overseas recipient using the standard contract drawn up by the cyberspace authority. Four, other conditions provided by laws or administrative regulations. At the same time you must tell the user: the overseas recipient's name, contact details, purpose and method of processing, the categories of personal information, and how they exercise their rights. And you must "obtain the individual's separate consent" - ask the user about this specifically, on its own. The concrete thresholds are tiered by cumulative headcount. An ordinary enterprise is, in the regulation's terms, a data handler other than a critical-information-infrastructure operator. Such enterprises have an exemption line, in the original words: "having cumulatively provided abroad, since January 1 of the current year, the personal information (excluding sensitive personal information) of fewer than 100,000 people." Below this line, the declaration for assessment, the standard contract and the certification are all waived. From 100,000 people up to 1,000,000, the standard contract or certification is required. At 1,000,000 people or more, or sensitive personal information of 10,000 people or more, the security assessment must be declared (nationwide, since March 2024)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law (Articles 38, 39, 40). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2024). Provisions on promoting and regulating cross-border data flows (CAC Order No. 16, Articles 3, 4, 5, 7, 8). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (2022). Measures for security assessment of data exports (CAC Order No. 11, Article 4). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>
- Notes: A server kept abroad does not equal "the data hasn't been exported." The user registers in the mainland, you send their information to machines abroad - that's an export. One category doesn't need any of the three routes (assessment, certification, standard contract): cross-border shopping, cross-border shipping, booking flights and hotels - transfers truly necessary "for concluding or performing a contract to which the individual is a party." Mind how the headcount is measured too: "cumulatively since January 1 of the current year," not the twelve months counting back from today

### 11. When choosing a server, ask first whether you can tolerate downtime, then whether anyone will run it, and only then compare prices
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Cloud hosts from the major cloud vendors run hundreds to thousands of yuan a month. A VPS from a small provider starts at tens of yuan a month - a VPS is a small virtual server. Dedicated-server colocation starts at several thousand yuan a year, and you buy the hardware yourself
- TL;DR: The difference between the three tiers isn't price - it's what's in your hands on the day something breaks. The big vendors have registration channels, public qualifications, incident announcements, compensation clauses in the contract, and people you can reach. A small provider costs half as much, but when it oversells, vanishes or gets cut off upstream, you don't even know whom to find. For a static site holding no user data, a tens-of-yuan VPS is enough.
- Benefit: The difference between the three tiers isn't price - it's what's in your hands on the day something breaks. The major cloud vendors have registration channels, their qualifications are public, outages come with announcements, the contract has compensation clauses, and when something goes wrong there are people to reach. A small provider can cost half as much, but when it oversells, vanishes or gets cut off upstream, you have nobody to appeal to - so before talking price, check in the industry ministry's system whether it holds a value-added telecom business license. Dedicated-server colocation only pays off when both conditions hold: bandwidth or hardware cost clearly cheaper, and you have someone of your own to run it. How to choose: for a static site holding no user data, the cheapest VPS is enough. With user registration and transactions, pick a cloud host that scales elastically and takes snapshot backups, with backups kept in at least two places
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience, no direct literature; the statutory requirements on provider qualifications and registration are in entry 4 of this chapter, and the 6-month log retention and graded-protection obligations are in entry 16 of chapter 11
- Notes: This entry is judgment, not evidence. The only thing in it that can actually be verified is the provider's license. Save money on configuration and bandwidth if you must save - not on qualifications
