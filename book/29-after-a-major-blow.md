# 29. After a major blow

The death of someone close. Being told a serious diagnosis. Losing a job. Divorce. These share one trait in the research: what breaks isn't only your mood - it's your body. The highest-risk stretch concentrates in the first few weeks to the first year. This chapter doesn't teach how to "move on." It covers only what must be watched during this stretch, which decisions must be postponed, and when to register at a clinic. Most entries count the probability of death. The four that discuss money and benefits count money: don't pay for grief counseling right away (entry 9), call 12356 and register at a psychological clinic (entry 11), push irreversible big decisions back (entry 12), and don't use death as a way to repay debt (entry 13). The two kinds of numbers are not converted against each other. For someone with no relatives and no friends to turn to, how "find someone to watch you" lands on the ground is in entry 6.

The paperwork, the body and the money to be claimed right after a death are in chapter 25. Guardianship and wills while an elder is alive are in chapter 17. What unemployment pays is in chapter 7. Exercise and light for low moods are in chapters 3 and 22. For suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first - entry 25 of chapter 1; the timescale of the thoughts is entry 32 of chapter 1; the aftermath after being saved is entry 33 of chapter 1. None of that is repeated here.

### 1. In the first days after losing someone close, don't stay alone; if you have heart disease, hypertension or diabetes, keep taking your medication as usual - and call 120 if your chest feels pressed
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Don't stay alone these days, and keep taking your medication.
- TL;DR: On the first day after a loved one's death, the probability of a heart attack is 21.1 times normal, then falls day by day. People aged 60 to 89 have about double the rate of heart attack or stroke in the 30 days after a partner's death, falling back after a month. If you already have cardiovascular disease, don't stop your medication these days. Chest pressing with pain, inability to breathe, weakness in the limbs on one side - call 120 immediately.
- Benefit: 1,985 acute heart-attack patients, each compared against themselves across different time windows before and after onset. Among them, 270 (13.6%) had lost someone important within 6 months before onset, 19 of them within 1 day before the heart attack. Within 24 hours of an important person's death, the acute heart-attack rate rose 21.1-fold (95% CI 13.1-34.1 - the true multiple roughly between 13 and 34). It declined day by day afterward. In actual people: among those with a 5% ten-year heart-attack risk, 1 extra case appears per 1,394 newly bereaved; among those with a 20% risk, 1 extra per 320. A UK study compared 30,447 bereaved people aged 60 to 89 against 83,588 well-matched controls. Heart attack or stroke within 30 days of the partner's death: 50 people (0.16%) on the bereaved side. 67 people (0.08%) on the control side. IRR 2.20 (1.52-3.15) - about 2.2 times the event probability. Heart attack alone: 2.14 (1.20-3.81), about 2.1x. Stroke: 2.40 (1.22-4.71), about 2.4x. It weakened after 30 days. Within 90 days two more disease classes were examined. One was non-heart-attack acute coronary syndrome (acute trouble in the heart's blood supply, short of a heart attack), IRR 2.20 (1.12-4.29). The other was pulmonary embolism (a clot blocking a lung vessel), IRR 2.37 (1.18-4.75)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>; Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Notes: What can be done is concrete. Hand the pill box to someone else - don't rely on your own memory. No long drives these days. Don't lean on alcohol to get through it; alcohol's ledger is in chapter 2. How to recognize a heart attack or stroke and how to call the ambulance is in chapter 13. Both studies look back over population data and can't tell how many of the people were going to have an event anyway. But both point the same direction over the same time window. If there's no one to hand the pill box to and you're bound to be alone these days, see entry 6 of this chapter.

### 2. In the week you're told a serious diagnosis, don't pick up the report alone - and postpone every decision beyond treatment
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Find someone to go with you for the report. Push every big decision beyond treatment back a few days.
- TL;DR: In the first week after a cancer diagnosis, the probability of suicide is 12.6 times that of undiagnosed people, and the probability of dying from cardiovascular disease is 5.6 times. By the first year it falls to 3.1 times, more pronounced the harder the cancer is to treat. The risk peaks in the days right after learning, then drops quickly. So bring someone the day you pick up the report, and don't stay alone back home. Whether to quit your job, whether to sell the apartment - leave it for the next clinic visit.
- Benefit: Sweden's national registers followed over 6.07 million people (6,073,240), from 1991 to 2006. Compared with people not diagnosed with cancer, the relative suicide risk in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6-17.8) - about 12.6 times, the true multiple roughly between 8.6 and 17.8. That week's incidence was 2.50 per 1,000 person-years - 1,000 people followed for a year, 2.5 cases. By the first year it fell to 3.1 (2.7-3.5, 0.60/1000 person-years) - about 3.1 times. Cardiovascular death in the first week: 5.6 (5.2-5.9, 116.80/1000 person-years) - about 5.6 times. First 4 weeks: 3.3 (3.1-3.4, 65.81/1000 person-years). The elevated risk fell back quickly within the first year after diagnosis. The harder the cancer is to treat and the lower the survival probability, the more pronounced the rise. Comparing the same patients against themselves across time windows gave the same conclusion
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Notes: This is cancer-patient data. For other major diagnoses there is no source of equal weight. The people harmed are actually few: the first-week suicide rate was 2.50 per 1,000 person-years - 2.5 cases per 1,000 person-years. It's written not to frighten, but to show that someone needs to be beside you in "the days right after learning." Long-term management, reimbursement and follow-up after diagnosis are in chapters 16 and 24. If you can't find anyone to go with you, see entry 6 of this chapter (swap "that person" for the neighbor and community list).

### 3. After losing a job, first fix your routine, your medical insurance and your job-hunting rhythm - don't stay home all day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. First register as unemployed and claim unemployment-insurance benefits, then schedule job hunting into a fixed routine. The hard part is getting up on time when nobody is pushing you.
- TL;DR: Unemployed people die at about sixty percent higher probability than employed people in the years that follow. The effect is more pronounced for men and for people not yet old, and highest in the first ten years after job loss. Part of it is within your own control: in the studies that also counted smoking and drinking, the computed increase shrank by about a quarter. First register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance - during the claiming period, your employee medical insurance is paid by the unemployment-insurance fund (see chapter 7).
- Benefit: Pooling 42 studies, 235 mortality-risk estimates, over 20 million people. After equalizing for age and other conditions, the unemployed's all-cause death risk averaged HR 1.63 - about sixty percent above the employed. Higher for men than women. The effect is marked for people in early and mid-career, smaller in late career. Risk is highest in the first 10 years of follow-up, then falls. In the subset of studies that also counted health behaviors like smoking and drinking, the average HR was 24% lower - the computed increase shrank by about a quarter
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Notes: This is research looking back over population data; it can't separate the direction of cause. People already in poor health also lose jobs more easily. But "after removing the lifestyle part of the effect, the effect is 24% lower" gives a direction to act on. What unemployment pays, where to claim it, and how to keep social insurance going are in chapter 7. What compensation a dismissal owes you, and why not to sign a "voluntary resignation," are in chapter 19.

### 4. If someone close died by suicide, accident or violence, don't count on toughing it out - actively seek professional help
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Calling 12356 is free. To see a doctor, a psychological clinic costs an ordinary outpatient registration. The hard part is opening your mouth to ask someone.
- TL;DR: People whose spouse died by suicide are about eighty percent (men) and seventy percent (women) more likely than the general population to be diagnosed with a mental disorder within five years. Compared with people whose spouse died of other causes, still seventy percent to double higher. This kind of bereaved person more often feels shunned by others and feels shame. That is exactly the reason to actively seek people out - don't wait to get better on your own.
- Benefit: Denmark's national registers followed 6.7 million adults from 1980 to 2014, over 136 million person-years in total (everyone's years of follow-up added up). For people widowed by a spouse's suicide, the risk of a new mental-disorder diagnosis within 5 years, compared with the general population: IRR 1.8 for men (95% CI 1.6-2.0) - about eighty percent higher. IRR 1.7 for women (1.6-1.8) - about seventy percent higher. Compared with people widowed by other causes of death: IRR 1.7 for men (1.5-1.9), 2.0 for women (1.9-2.2) - seventy percent to double higher. Within this group, suicidal behavior, mortality and the share needing municipal assistance were also higher. Physical illnesses like cirrhosis and sleep disorders carried elevated risk too. Another 57 studies were itemized without pooling the numbers. Bereavement by suicide is associated with these four things. First, elevated suicide risk in partners. Second, elevated risk of parents being admitted to psychiatric care after a child's suicide. Third, elevated suicide risk in mothers after an adult child's suicide. Fourth, elevated depression risk in children after a parent's suicide. Compared with people bereaved by other violent deaths, those bereaved by suicide more often report feeling shunned and ashamed
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>; Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>; Kristensen P et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76> (the review cited in the notes)
- Notes: Sudden, violent death means accidents, homicide, disasters. After this kind of death, the proportions of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and stuck grief are all higher than after death by illness. Recovery is also slower. What to do when suicidal thoughts appear in yourself is in entry 25 of chapter 1.

### 5. In the first six months of widowhood, hand three meals, sleep, chronic-disease medication and follow-up checks to one specific person to watch
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Find one specific person - a child, a sibling or a friend - and have them visit or call regularly. The hard part is opening your mouth and entrusting this to someone.
- TL;DR: Within half a year of losing a spouse, the probability of dying in the same period runs about forty percent above the non-widowed. After half a year it falls to about fifteen percent higher. For men it's about twenty-three percent higher; for women the margin is small enough to be coincidence. This gap isn't something "thinking positive" can carry you through. The thing to do is put the pills, the meals and the follow-ups onto one specific person - don't count on remembering them yourself.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 studies that registered people and followed them forward, 2,263,888 people. Widowed under 6 months: death RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.26-1.57) - about forty percent higher same-period death probability than the non-widowed. After 6 months: 1.14 (1.10-1.18) - down to about fifteen percent higher. Men: RR 1.23 (1.18-1.28), about twenty-three percent higher. Women: 1.04 (1.00-1.08, not statistically significant - the margin may just be coincidence). Under 65 versus 65+ showed no clear difference. Another analysis pooled 123 papers, 1,377 risk estimates, over 500 million people. After equalizing for differences in conditions: HR 1.23 (1.19-1.28) - about twenty-three percent higher overall. Men above women again: men 1.27 (1.19-1.35), twenty-seven percent higher; women 1.15 (1.08-1.22), fifteen percent higher
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>; Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: This is research looking back over population data; mixed-in factors can't be removed. Spouses' lifestyles and illnesses are similar to begin with. But the pattern "much higher in the first half year than after" is the same in two independent pooled analyses - worth treating as a care window. Men carry higher risk and ask for help less. If you have a widowed elder at home, sort out the accounts, guardianship and will together per chapter 17. If you can't find such a person (living alone, lost your only child, children also gone), hand this job to the community and your phone - see entry 6 of this chapter.

### 6. With no relatives and no friends, replace "the person watching you" with three things: a neighbor who can enter your home, the community's visit-care list, and emergency contacts on your phone
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is taking the initiative to say to a neighbor and the residents' committee, "I live alone, and something just happened at home."
- TL;DR: With no relatives and no friends, "find someone" is an empty sentence. That sentence actually does two jobs: if something happens, someone finds you; someone watches that you take your medication and eat. The second can only be done by yourself - write the pills and three meals into a checklist, set alarms, don't rely on memory. The first relies on three things: leave a spare key with a neighbor, ask the residents' committee about the visit-care list, set up emergency contacts on your phone and don't power it off.
- Benefit: 44,573 outpatients, all at risk of or with a history of atherothrombosis (the kind of disease where plaques grow in vessels and clots easily block them). Doctors in multiple countries enrolled them in REACH, an international program, from December 2003 to December 2004, followed for 4 years. Among them, 8,594 (19%) lived alone. Four-year all-cause death: 14.1% for those living alone, 11.1% for those living with someone. Cardiovascular death: 8.6% vs 6.8%. Both log-rank P<0.01 - the difference is unlikely to be chance. The gap differed by age band (interaction P=0.03). Ages 45 to 65: death living alone vs with others, 7.7% vs 5.7%. After equalizing other conditions, HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01-1.51) - about twenty-four percent higher. Ages 66 to 80: 13.2% vs 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01-1.26) - about ten percent higher. Over 80: 24.6% vs 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79-1.06) - no rise at all. A 2022 opinion from the Ministry of Civil Affairs and nine other departments requires visit-and-care services. The targets are elderly people living alone, empty-nesters, left-behind, disabled, severely disabled, and special family-planning families. The service form: "regular door-to-door visits, phone and video calls, remote monitoring." Townships and subdistricts run the screening with help from village and residents' committees, capturing basic information and "willingness to receive visit-and-care services," folding it into grass-roots grid management - meaning each patch is assigned to a specific person to watch. By end of 2025, "ensure a 100% monthly visit rate for elderly people in special difficulty," and "for disabled elderly in special difficulty, at least one visit of care per month." The document also advocates households installing smart call systems, smart electricity and water meters, health-monitoring products and elder-care monitoring devices, so that "once an abnormality is detected, an early warning can be issued promptly, with alert messages sent to emergency contacts in sync." Service staff who find an emergency "should immediately assist in calling the emergency number"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>; Ministry of Civil Affairs and nine other departments (2022). Guiding opinion on launching visit-and-care services for elderly people in special difficulty (Minfa [2022] No. 73). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Notes: How the three things work concretely. One, give a spare key or a temporary door-lock code to a neighbor or the property management, and say one sentence - "I'm alone these days." No friendship needed; just someone knowing you're in there. Two, go to the community residents' committee, explain that you live alone, and ask to be put on the visit-care list. Elderly people living alone, empty-nesters and those who lost their only child are explicitly named service targets; while you're there, ask whether the locality installs smart call buttons or smart water meters for free. Three, set up emergency contacts and a medical emergency card on your phone, and don't mute or power it off these days. Two reasons for grade B. First, the living-alone/mortality link comes from research looking back over population data, and the reverse explanation - "people already in poor health or poor conditions are more likely to live alone" - can't be removed; the original paper itself says the result awaits confirmation. Second, the visit-care document covers only the elderly. Under 60 has no corresponding dedicated system. What you can count on is one clause in it, requiring grid workers and social workers to "promptly spot psychological-crisis risks such as family upheaval, job loss and school dropout" (see entry 11 of this chapter - for someone to talk to, call 12356). Why "someone in the house" is worth money: see entry 1 of chapter 13 (chest compressions). Of cardiac arrests outside hospitals, 79.2% happen at home. With someone beside them doing compressions, 16.1% leave the hospital alive; with nobody, only 3.9%. Nobody in the house means landing in the lower bracket. Two more handy things. Arranging a funeral itself means visiting several windows - the funeral home, the community health service, the police, the housing fund (see chapter 25). Scheduling it as one fixed outing a day gives you ready-made outings for these days. The hotline can be called repeatedly - it's not one-time-only (see entry 11 of this chapter). "Living alone" is itself also a long-term risk factor: odds ratio 1.32 for death, about thirty percent higher - see entry 10 of chapter 22 (regular in-person contact as a health expense). But this entry governs only the days right now; that long-term ledger is not counted here.

### 7. After a parent's death, tell the child the truth, let them attend the farewell, and don't send them away to "change the environment"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is that you're grieving yourself and still have to say it clearly to the child.
- TL;DR: Children who lose a father or mother before 18 die at about fifty percent higher probability than others through early adulthood. When the parent died of a non-natural cause like an accident or suicide, about eighty percent higher; of illness, about thirty percent higher. This is not something a child forgets in a couple of days - the effect extends for years. What can be done: tell the truth, let them attend the farewell, don't interrupt school, don't send them to relatives for a change of scene.
- Benefit: The entire populations born in Denmark 1968-2008 and Sweden 1973-2006 are counted, plus a drawn 89.3% of Finland's 1987-2007 births - over 7.3 million people in total. Of them, 189,094 lost a parent before 18. These children's all-cause mortality was 50% above the non-bereaved (mortality rate ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43-1.58 - about 1.5x). When the parent died of a non-natural cause like accident or suicide: 1.84 (1.71-2.00), about eighty percent higher. Of natural causes like illness: 1.33 (1.24-1.41), about thirty percent higher. The elevation was highest when the child's cause of death matched the parent's
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Notes: When telling the child, don't invent "they went on a long trip," and put the guardianship and care arrangements in writing. Grade B isn't because the number is soft. This is a whole-population tracking study across three countries - the number itself earns A. Grade B because between that number and the concrete practices - "how to tell the child, whether they should attend the farewell" - there is no direct trial evidence. The authors also state that data on post-bereavement parent-child relationships and children's lifestyles are missing. Part of the elevation is inborn constitution, part is the long-term mark bereavement leaves. Appointing a guardian and arranging property is in chapter 17. The child's support and schooling are in chapter 18.

### 8. If grief is still in the same place after six months and life can't go on, register at a psychiatric or clinical-psychology department
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration fee plus treatment fees. This treatment runs in courses - in the trial, an average of 16 sessions over 19 weeks. The hard part is walking in to register yourself.
- TL;DR: About one in ten bereaved adults gets stuck: more than six months of persistent yearning that leaves them unable to eat, work or care for children, numb, feeling life is meaningless. This isn't a lack of strength - it has diagnostic criteria and a dedicated treatment. Targeted treatment works for 51%, ordinary psychotherapy for 28%, and it works faster.
- Benefit: Pooling 14 studies, covering adults whose loved one did not die violently and who were not themselves in psychiatric care. Prolonged grief disorder prevalence: 9.8% (95% CI 6.8-14.0) - about 10 of every 100 bereaved adults. The share rises with age. Another study tested how well the diagnostic criteria work, interviewing 291 bereaved people three times - 0-6, 6-12 and 12-24 months after the loss. The core feature of the diagnosis is intense yearning for the deceased. Plus at least five of these nine. The first five: emotional numbness, shock, feeling life is meaningless, distrust of others, pain over the loss. The last four: difficulty accepting, unclear sense of who one should be, avoidance of reality, inability to carry on with life. These features must appear daily, or be severe enough to prevent functioning. And they must still exist at least 6 months after the death, affecting daily life. A separate trial randomized 95 people into two groups for 16 sessions over an average 19 weeks. Complicated-grief-targeted treatment: 51% response. Ordinary interpersonal psychotherapy: 28% (P=0.02 - the difference is unlikely to be chance). The former also worked faster. Number needed to treat: 4.3 - on average, treating 4.3 people yields one more recovery
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>; Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>; Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Notes: Don't count months on your own. The real test is "affecting eating, sleeping, working, caring for children" - if that matches, go register. The department is called psychiatry, psychology or clinical psychology; public psychiatric hospitals all have it. This diagnosis is called complicated grief in some materials and prolonged grief disorder in others. Standards differ on how long it must persist - some write 6 months, some 12. The 9.8% proportion comes from 14 studies with quite different methods, and the authors themselves caution it may not transfer to other populations.

### 9. Don't pay for grief counseling right away - first check whether your grief is actually stuck (the symptoms in entry 8)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - it saves a counseling fee instead.
- TL;DR: Arranging psychological counseling universally for all bereaved people shows tiny effects right at the end of treatment, and no visible benefit when rechecked later. The reason: people who get no intervention mostly recover on their own anyway. But for people who are already clearly failing to adapt, the same treatment works as well as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. So the order is: first check against entry 8 (stuck grief goes to the psychiatric department), and only spend this money if it matches.
- Benefit: Pooling 61 controlled studies. Universal psychological intervention for the bereaved shows small benefits right at treatment's end, and no visible benefit when rechecked later. Interventions that enrolled only bereaved people "already clearly failing to adapt" worked as well as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. Studies that skipped pre-screening fared poorly, because the no-intervention group mostly recovered on its own with time. Another pooling of 14 randomized trials: treatment for people already having problems clearly beat the control both after treatment and at later follow-ups, with the effect still growing over time. Universal preventive intervention for people not yet having problems did nothing. A further study followed 205 people from before widowhood through 6 and 18 months after. It separated five trajectories: ordinary grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience - meaning holding up. Resilience was the most common; ordinary grief relatively uncommon
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>; Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>; Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>; Stroebe M et al. (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9> (the review cited in the notes)
- Notes: This entry says "not everyone needs it," not "nobody should do it." The high-risk bereaved of entry 4 (a loved one dead by suicide, accident or violence) and the already-stuck people of entry 8 are exactly the people evidence supports treating. A Lancet review's conclusion is the same sentence: grief is not an illness, and most people don't need professional intervention. Spend the effort on the high-risk, and on those who already show complicated grief, post-bereavement depression and stress disorders.

### 10. After separation or divorce, don't let medical insurance lapse and don't lean on alcohol
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Keep the insurance going, stop the drinking. The hard part is that leaning on alcohol comes easily in this stretch.
- TL;DR: In an analysis pooling 11 countries and 6.5 million people, separated or divorced people die early at clearly higher probability than the still-married. The rise is larger for men and for younger people. The original paper doesn't treat this as causation - divorced people may differ in health and income to begin with. Of the four causes the authors list, two are within your own control: don't let medical insurance and checkups lapse, don't lean on cigarettes and alcohol.
- Benefit: Pooling 32 studies that registered people and followed them forward, covering over 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, over 755,000 divorces, 11 countries. Compared with the still-married, separated or divorced adults show clearly elevated risk of early death. Men and younger people rise more markedly than women and older people. The authors discuss four possible pathways. One, social selection - people whose health or conditions already differ more easily end up divorced. Two, interrupted resources - income and security cut off. Three, changed health behaviors - smoking, drinking and routines worsening. Four, chronic psychological stress - long-term tension
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Notes: The original paper gives only "clearly elevated" and group comparisons, with no citable multiple, so this entry carries no specific number. The benefit is graded mid on "direction known, no threshold-applicable reduction." Property, debts and bride price in divorce are in chapters 8 and 10. The child's care arrangements are in chapter 18.

### 11. For someone to talk with, call 12356; minors and teenagers call 12355; to see a doctor, register at a psychological clinic
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Both hotlines are free. A psychological clinic registers as an ordinary outpatient visit.
- TL;DR: If you need someone to talk to, call 12356 - the national psychological-assistance hotline, free. Minors and teenagers have 12355, the youth comprehensive service platform. To see a doctor, register at the psychological clinic of a psychiatric, psychology or clinical-psychology department. When family upheaval, job loss or school dropout happens, community grid workers and social workers are supposed to spot it proactively.
- Benefit: The 2026 plan from the National Health Commission and 24 other departments requires improving the 12356 psychological-assistance hotline's working system: stable operation, higher answer rates, a push to build provincial 12356 information platforms, stronger linkage mechanisms with service hotlines like 110, and building up the 12355 youth comprehensive service platform in coordination with 12356. The goals for 2030 are three. First, over 80% of administrative villages and urban communities have a psychological-counseling room or social-work room providing psychological services. Second, public psychiatric hospitals at every level and at least one hospital in every county (district) provide psychological-clinic services. Third, every province builds a provincial 12356 psychological-assistance platform. Psychological-crisis intervention and assistance teams consist of psychiatrists, psychotherapy technicians, psychological counselors, social-work professionals and volunteers. The plan also requires three kinds of people to keep watch in ordinary times and spot things early: grid workers (community staff responsible for their patch), social-work professionals and volunteers - to promptly spot psychological-crisis risks such as family upheaval, job loss and school dropout (nationwide, issued March 2026)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: National Health Commission and 24 other departments (2026). Notice on issuing the implementation plan for improving the social psychological service system and crisis intervention mechanism (Guoweiyizhengfa [2026] No. 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Notes: Grade B because village and community counseling rooms and county-level psychological clinics are goals to be built by 2030 - not something every place has now. 12356 and 12355 can be called right now. 12356's launch date and daily hours are in entry 25 of chapter 1. Psychological counseling and psychotherapy are not the same thing. Diagnosing and prescribing must be done by psychiatrists, and treatment must happen at qualified medical institutions. The market's by-the-hour "healing" and "spiritual counseling" are outside this system.

### 12. In the first three months after the blow, push every irreversible big decision back
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - you're just postponing decisions, possibly missing some opportunities as a result. The hard part is that at the time it feels like opportunities won't wait.
- TL;DR: In this stretch your judgment of every kind of "opportunity" is at its worst. People claiming they can contact the dead, promising to win your money back, urging you to quit your job and start over in another city, pulling you into partnerships - they all come to you. Set yourself one hard rule: anything over a month's income, or anything whose reversal costs dearly, gets pushed back three months. Tell it first to someone with no stake in the matter - the only ask is not deciding on the day.
- Benefit: Avoiding irreversible property decisions made in the stretch right after a blow, when judgment is at its worst. These include selling the apartment, quitting the job, investing the bereavement payment or compensation in one shot, remarrying hastily, guaranteeing someone's loan. The amounts of such decisions usually run from tens of thousands of yuan to an apartment - which is why this entry's benefit grades mid. Official documents likewise list "family upheaval, job loss, school dropout" among the psychological-crisis risks communities should proactively screen for. In other words, this stretch of one's state is itself treated as a state where an outsider should lend a hand
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience, no direct literature; the "family upheaval, job loss, school dropout" wording as psychological-crisis risk appears in National Health Commission and 24 other departments (2026). Implementation plan for improving the social psychological service system and crisis intervention mechanism (Guoweiyizhengfa [2026] No. 8). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Notes: If there's no such stake-free person around, tell it once to 12356 (see entry 11 of this chapter), or write it down yourself and read it again three days later. "Three months" is a line the author drew himself - no literature supports that specific length, which is why this entry grades C. Common scams targeting the bereaved and the elderly are in chapter 17 (retirement investment, house-for-pension) and chapter 6 (fortune-telling). The traps of guarantees and IOUs are in chapter 8. A few sums of money should be claimed proactively, not "pushed back": the housing-fund balance, social-insurance benefits, and the three work-death payments - see chapters 25 and 19.

### 13. Don't use death as a way to repay debt: life insurance within two years doesn't pay, work injury doesn't recognize it, and the debt still comes out of the estate first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - just get this ledger straight.
- TL;DR: For insurance that pays on death, suicide within two years doesn't pay - it refunds only whatever cash value the policy has. Work-injury insurance doesn't recognize it either; the three work-death payments are zero. Debts don't vanish with the person - what you owe comes out of what you leave first, and the family gets what remains. What you leave them is a hole, plus the health cost of entry 4 of this chapter (bereavement by suicide or violent death).
- Benefit: Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs contracts that pay out on the insured's death. Within two years of the contract's formation or reinstatement, if the insured commits suicide, the insurer bears no liability to pay the benefit. If the insured was a person without civil capacity at the time of suicide - someone the law holds not responsible for their own acts - that is excepted. In this situation the insurer shall refund the policy's cash value as agreed in the contract - meaning only whatever the policy can refund, not the insured amount. Life-insurance sums usually run to hundreds of thousands of yuan, which is the basis for this entry's benefit grading big. Article 16, item 3 of the Work-Related Injury Insurance Regulation: self-harm or suicide may not be recognized as, or treated as, a work-related injury. The three work-death benefits of Article 39 fall away with it. One, the funeral subsidy - 6 months of the local previous-year average monthly wage. Two, the dependent-relative pension. Three, the one-time work-death payment - 20 times the previous year's national per-capita disposable income of urban residents. Article 1159 of the Civil Code: in dividing an estate, the taxes and debts the decedent was legally obliged to pay shall be settled - the estate pays taxes and debts first, and what remains gets divided. Article 1161: heirs settle debts up to the actual value of the estate they receive - repayment comes only from what was inherited, nothing from their own pockets, and those who renounce the inheritance bear no settlement liability
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (amended 2015). Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 44. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811>; State Council (revised 2010). Work-Related Injury Insurance Regulation (State Council Order No. 586), Articles 16, 39. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, Articles 1159, 1161. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: This entry counts only the money ledger. The other entries of this chapter and chapter 1 are about not reaching this step. Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs only the first two years; it's written out to state the provision completely, not to provide a timetable. The family's health cost is in entry 4 of this chapter (people whose spouse died by suicide carry seventy to eighty percent higher risk of a new mental-disorder diagnosis within 5 years). What to do when the thoughts surface is in entries 25 and 32 of chapter 1; the aftermath after being saved is entry 33 of chapter 1. The standards for the three work-death payments are in entry 16 of chapter 19.
