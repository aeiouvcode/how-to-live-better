# 28. Don't wreck your body for your appearance

This chapter covers the ways people "operate on their bodies to change their appearance": starving, pills, injections, surgery. These things share one trait. The decision usually comes from comparing yourself with others. The price falls entirely on you - and a large part of it can't be undone.

This chapter does not judge what anyone wants to make of themselves; that is each person's own business. It answers one question: for the same goal, how much does the risk differ between the route with "a prescription, licensed providers, follow-up checks" and the route of "online shops, beauty salons, self-adjusted doses." The ledger here mainly counts what actually happened to bodies - blindness, hospitalization, death. The two medical-cosmetology entries also involve criminal liability for illegal medical practice.

### 1. Don't control weight with extreme dieting, fasting or purging - if you're going to lose, lose from the exercise side
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. It even saves the money spent on weight-loss products.
- TL;DR: People with anorexia nervosa die at about 5.9 times the rate of their peers. Follow 1,000 of them for a year and 5.1 die. One in five of the deaths is suicide. And the entry point is dieting: tracking students at 44 secondary schools for three years, girls on severe diets developed eating disorders at 18 times the rate of non-dieters. To control weight, exercise more - don't eat down to hunger.
- Benefit: Pooling 36 studies. The anorexia nervosa cohort accumulated 166,642 person-years of follow-up - everyone's years of follow-up added together. Their standardized mortality ratio was 5.86, meaning a death probability about 5.9 times that of peers. Deaths ran at 5.1 per 1,000 person-years - 1,000 people followed for a year, 5.1 dead. About 1/5 of the deaths were suicide. Bulimia's standardized mortality ratio was 1.93, and other eating disorders 1.92 - about 1.9 times peers each. Another study ran in 44 secondary schools in Victoria, Australia, following 14-15-year-olds for 3 years across 6 waves - the same students surveyed six times in three years. New eating disorders in girls ran at 21.8 per 1,000 person-years - 1,000 girls followed for a year, 21.8 new cases. Girls on severe diets had 18 times the onset risk of non-dieters; moderate dieters, 5 times. Then strip out the effects of "had dieted before" and "already had psychiatric symptoms" - that is, adjust for prior dieting and psychiatric symptoms. After that, BMI (the fatness measure calculated as weight over height squared), exercise volume and sex no longer predicted new eating disorders. The authors' conclusion: "in adolescents, controlling weight through exercise rather than food restriction appears to carry a lower risk of eating disorders"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Arcelus J et al. (2011). Mortality rates in patients with anorexia nervosa and other eating disorders. A meta-analysis of 36 studies. Arch Gen Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.74>; Patton GC et al. (1999). Onset of adolescent eating disorders: population based cohort study over 3 years. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.318.7186.765>
- Notes: "Severe dieting" here means chronically pressing intake far below what the body needs - not skipping a meal or dropping late-night snacks. If purging, self-blame after binges, or compulsive repeated weighing have already appeared, see a psychiatric department or the clinical psychology department of a general hospital. This is an illness with standard treatments, not a willpower problem. The BMI-mortality relationship is in entry 33 of chapter 2 - that entry is about long-term weight range, not weight-loss methods.

### 2. Before any injection, thread-lift or surgery, check two things: does the institution's license say "medical cosmetology," and is the operator an attending physician
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Checking these two credentials takes minutes. The institution should hang its Medical Institution Practice License in the lobby. The doctor's qualification can be verified in the health authority's practitioner registry.
- TL;DR: Anything involving a needle, a scalpel or a medical device - anything that breaks the skin or enters the body - counts as medical treatment. It can only be done at an institution whose license lists "medical cosmetology," and the operator must be a registered licensed physician. Done in a beauty salon, a studio or a hotel room, it's illegal medical practice: serious cases get up to 3 years; injuring someone gets 3 to 10; killing someone gets 10 or more.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Measures for the administration of medical cosmetology services defines medical cosmetology. The original text: "repair and remodeling of a person's appearance and the forms of various body parts using surgery, drugs, medical devices and other traumatic or invasive medical techniques." In plain words: break the skin, insert a needle, enter the body - and it counts as medical treatment. Articles 8 and 24 require obtaining a Medical Institution Practice License first, with the medical-cosmetology practice subject approved by the registration authority, before operating. In other words, if the license doesn't list "medical cosmetology," the work can't be done. Article 11 requires the attending physician (the doctor doing the work) to hold physician qualifications and be registered. Cosmetic surgery additionally requires over 6 years of relevant clinical experience; cosmetic dentistry over 5; cosmetic traditional Chinese medicine and cosmetic dermatology over 3 each. Article 20 requires written disclosure of indications, contraindications (when it's suitable, when it must not be done), medical risks and precautions before the work, with signed consent. Article 336 of the Criminal Law covers illegal medical practice - people without physician qualifications giving injections, doing surgery or treating patients. Serious circumstances: up to 3 years' imprisonment, detention or control, plus or solely a fine. Serious harm to a patient's health: 3 to 10 years plus a fine. Causing a patient's death: 10 or more years plus a fine
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Health (2002). Measures for the administration of medical cosmetology services (Ministry of Health Order No. 19, Articles 2, 8, 11, 16, 20, 24). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62198.htm>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Article 336). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Notes: Article 16 of the same measures also provides that medical-cosmetology procedures can only be performed in medical-cosmetology institutions, or in hospitals and clinics with an established medical-cosmetology department. So door-to-door injections and private work in studios are non-compliant on location alone. Article 20 states that minors and others who cannot decide for themselves must have guardian consent for medical cosmetology - a parent's signature. Facials, massage and manicures are lifestyle beauty services, outside these rules. But the moment a needle or scalpel is involved, it counts as medical cosmetology.

### 3. For facial fillers, avoid the nasal dorsum, glabella and forehead - and for sure don't use an unlicensed injector to save money
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. It's just injecting a different site, or not doing it.
- TL;DR: Between 2015 and 2018, 48 new cases of blindness after filler injections were reported. The nose was the most common site, at 56.3%. Next came the glabella at 27.1%, the forehead at 18.8%, the nasolabial fold at 14.6%. Over eighty percent involved hyaluronic acid. Of these people, only 20.8% fully recovered vision and 16.7% partially recovered; the rest did not. Nearly a fifth also developed brain complications. No rescue method has been proven reliably effective, so the only defense is avoiding it beforehand.
- Benefit: Pooling every case the world's journals had reported. From January 2015 to September 2018, 48 new cases of partial or complete vision loss after filler injection were added. The most common site was the nose, at 56.3%. Then the glabella at 27.1%, the forehead at 18.8%, and the nasolabial fold (the laugh-line groove) at 14.6%. The filler was hyaluronic acid in 81.3% of cases. 43.8% had skin changes. 18.8% had central-nervous-system complications - trouble on the brain side. 10 cases (20.8%) fully recovered vision, 8 (16.7%) partially recovered, and the rest did not. The authors concluded that management approaches vary wildly and no treatment has been proven consistently effective
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Beleznay K et al. (2019). Update on Avoiding and Treating Blindness From Fillers: A Recent Review of the World Literature. Aesthet Surg J. <https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjz053>
- Notes: These numbers were counted case by case from journal reports. They contain only how many people were harmed, not how many injections happened. So they can't tell you "the probability of blindness per injection" - only which sites go wrong most, and that the aftermath is basically irreversible. That's also why the evidence is graded B. What legitimate institutions do to cut risk is routine: map the vessels under the injection site first, use blunt cannulas not sharp needles, inject small amounts while withdrawing, and keep hyaluronidase on hand. Every one of these requires a trained doctor doing the work - which is also the reason not to use unlicensed channels. Before any procedure, run the check from entry 2 of this chapter (the institution's license and the attending physician).

### 4. Don't buy weight-loss pills, slimming coffee, diet candies or enzyme plums that promise "fast results"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. It even saves the money spent on these products. The hard part is accepting that "lose several jin in a week" isn't real.
- TL;DR: The illegal additive most often found in these products is sibutramine. It used to be a legitimate weight-loss drug; China pulled it in 2010. One trial enrolled nearly ten thousand people who took it for over three years on average. Non-fatal heart attacks ran about thirty percent higher in the drug group, non-fatal strokes about thirty-six percent higher. The event rate was 11.4% versus 10.0%. And over three-plus years it bought only 1.7 kg more weight loss. Spot checks catching the additive happen after the fact - you've already swallowed it.
- Benefit: The SCOUT trial enrolled 9,804 people. All were over 55, overweight or obese, with cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes. They were randomized into drug and no-drug groups, treated for 3.4 years on average. The primary endpoint was a preset list of bad events to count: non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, resuscitation after cardiac arrest, and cardiovascular death. Their incidence was 11.4% vs 10.0%. HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.03-1.31, P=0.02). That is, the drug group's event probability was about sixteen percent higher, with the true value roughly between 3% and 31% higher. Non-fatal myocardial infarction 4.1% vs 3.2%, HR 1.28 (1.04-1.57) - about thirty percent higher. Non-fatal stroke 2.6% vs 1.9%, HR 1.36 (1.04-1.77) - about thirty-six percent higher. Cardiovascular death and all-cause death did not increase; all-cause means all causes added together. After randomization, the sibutramine group lost 1.7 kg more on average. The former state food and drug administration judged from its safety assessment that "the risks of the weight-loss treatment outweighed the benefits," and in October 2010 ordered sibutramine preparations and raw material stopped from production, sale and use. Both sibutramine and phenolphthalein are banned as food additives, health foods included
- Evidence: A
- Sources: James WPT et al. (2010). Effect of sibutramine on cardiovascular outcomes in overweight and obese subjects. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1003114>; Tianjin Municipal Market Supervision Commission. Beware of illegally added "sibutramine hydrochloride" and "phenolphthalein" in some foods. <https://scjg.tj.gov.cn/tjsscjdglwyh_52651/hdpt/cjwtyxfts/202007/t20200720_2972549.html>
- Notes: SCOUT enrolled people who already had heart disease or diabetes - a high-risk group. The trial did not prove healthy young people would be harmed the same way. But precisely because you can't tell who is high-risk, the regulator pulled it outright. The other frequently smuggled ingredient is phenolphthalein, originally a laxative; long-term heavy use scrambles electrolytes and can trigger arrhythmia. How to judge: a legitimate weight-loss drug is prescription-only, with an approval number verifiable at pharmacies and hospitals. Something sold as "coffee," "candy," "plums" or "meal replacement" that promises several jin a week should by definition have no drug effect - if it does have an effect, something was added. The original document 国食药监办〔2010〕432 is on the national drug administration's site, but it kept returning a 412 error when opened locally, so this cites a provincial regulator's repost page instead. The phrases "risks outweigh benefits" and "production, sale and use stopped in October 2010" can be checked word for word there.

### 5. Don't use anabolic steroids ("muscle-growth shots," "oral boosters") to build muscle
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves the drug money. The hard part is accepting that muscle grows slowly.
- TL;DR: Denmark tracked 545 people who tested positive in anti-doping tests against 5,450 same-age controls. Users had 3 times the death risk, and their median hospital visits ran 0.81 versus 0.36 per year. Acne, gynecomastia and erectile dysfunction each struck more than one in ten users. These drugs are prescription-only in China - any channel that will sell them to you is already breaking the law.
- Benefit: Denmark went back through its records and matched each user with same-age controls. The users were 545 men who tested positive for androgenic steroids in gym anti-doping tests between 2006-01-03 and 2018-03-01, matched with 5,450 controls. The check was repeated with another 644 people who refused testing and 6,440 controls. Results: users' mortality was 3 times the controls', HR 3.0 (95% CI 1.3-7.0) - the true multiple roughly somewhere between 1.3 and 7. The median number of hospitalizations or visits per year was 0.81 vs 0.36 (P<0.0001). The median is the value half the people exceed and half fall below; this P value means the difference is almost certainly not chance. Acne, gynecomastia and erectile dysfunction each affected over 10% of users - at least one in every ten users - all significantly above controls (P<0.0001). The verification batch came out the same. Article 7 of the Anti-Doping Regulation: the state strictly controls the banned substances in the doping catalogue, and no organization or individual may illegally produce, sell or import/export them. The same regulation puts anabolic agents and peptide hormones under prescription-drug management - buyable only with a doctor's prescription; "anabolic agents" is what the regulation calls this class of drugs
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Horwitz H et al. (2019). Health consequences of androgenic anabolic steroid use. J Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12850>; State Council (2004). Anti-Doping Regulation (State Council Order No. 398, Articles 7, 9). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63129.htm>
- Notes: Caveat: this is a study that followed a group of people and watched outcomes, not a randomized trial. The users may be bigger risk-takers in other ways too, so the 3x figure can't be booked entirely to the drugs. The true multiple could be as low as 1.3 or as high as 7.0 - a wide range. But "acne, gynecomastia and erectile dysfunction each struck over ten percent" are visible side effects, less subject to this bias. The regulation calls these drugs "anabolic agents" - those four words mean them. Ordinary protein powder and creatine are outside this entry. The trouble is products "promising especially fast muscle gain" may have smuggled ingredients. The judgment method is the same as the weight-loss entry (entry 4, don't buy fast-weight-loss pills): a legitimate drug is prescription-only with a checkable approval number. Something sold as "muscle powder" or a "supplement" that promises results in weeks - if it really works, something was added.

### 6. If you're going to take weight-loss medication, get the prescription at a hospital - don't buy from online shops that ship without one
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: One registration fee plus the round trip. Tens of yuan, half a day.
- TL;DR: Prescription drugs by regulation must be sold against a prescription, and the pharmacy must keep that prescription for five years. Selling prescription drugs online likewise requires a genuine, reliable prescription under a real name. So a shop that ships on order without asking for a consult or a prescription means one of two things: what it sells isn't what it claims, or the shop itself is violating the rules. Either way, nobody answers for your dosage or your contraindications when something goes wrong. That tens-of-yuan registration fee buys exactly this.
- Benefit: The Measures for the supervision and administration of drug distribution and use quality require drug retailers to follow the prescription/over-the-counter classification system - drugs are split into two classes: one buyable only with a doctor's prescription, one buyable yourself. Pharmacies must sell prescription drugs against prescriptions as required, keeping each prescription no less than five years. When no pharmacist or pharmaceutical technician is on duty, a sign must say so, and prescription drugs may not be sold without their review. Prescription drugs may not be sold open-shelf - not placed on shelves for customers to grab. Article 9 of the Measures for the supervision of online drug sales requires that online sales of prescription drugs to individuals ensure the prescription source is genuine and reliable, under a real-name system. Article 10 requires risk-warning information prominently displayed under every drug's listing page. Before the prescription passes review, the instructions may not be shown and no purchase service may be offered - meaning until the prescription clears, the page shouldn't even give you a buy button. Article 8 provides that drugs under special state control may not be sold online at all: vaccines, blood products, narcotics, psychotropics, toxic drugs for medical use, radioactive drugs, and drug-category precursor chemicals. These kinds simply cannot be sold online
- Evidence: B
- Sources: State Administration for Market Regulation (2023). Measures for the supervision and administration of drug distribution and use quality (SAMR Order No. 84). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10846/202311/content_6917322.html>; State Administration for Market Regulation (2022). Measures for the supervision of online drug sales (SAMR Order No. 58, Articles 8-10). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Notes: The statutes can be checked word for word, but "how much less goes wrong taking it by prescription versus self-purchasing" has never been quantified in research, so this entry grades B as "hard to quantify." This entry governs more than weight-loss drugs. The steroids and sex hormones of entries 5 and 7 in this chapter run under the same rules. When weight loss genuinely reaches the point of needing medication, the doctor first looks at your BMI, what other conditions you have, and whether any situation rules the drugs out - then decides whether to prescribe and which one. That judgment can't be bought.

### 7. Sex-hormone drugs only under a doctor's prescription with regular follow-ups - no online purchases, no self-adjusted doses
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Regular registrations plus blood-test follow-ups. A few hundred yuan a year, several half-days.
- TL;DR: This entry doesn't argue whether to use them, only how. One study followed a transgender population for about four years: trans women on estrogen had markedly more venous blood clots than same-age controls - 4.1 extra cases per thousand people over two years, 16.7 extra over eight. This kind of risk needs long-term follow-up watching it, and the premise is someone checking you regularly. On the buy-online-and-self-adjust route, that someone is exactly what's missing.
- Benefit: The researchers tracked a cohort from the electronic records of the Kaiser Permanente health system: 2,842 trans women and 2,118 trans men among them. Followed 4.0 and 3.6 years on average. Each person was matched by birth year, race, care site and enrollment time to roughly 10 times as many cisgender controls - 48,686 cisgender men and 48,775 cisgender women in total. Trans women had more venous thromboembolism (blood clots blocking vessels) than controls. Against cisgender men: at 2 years, 4.1 per 1,000 people (95% CI 1.6-6.7). At 8 years, 16.7 (6.4-27.5). That is, per 1,000 people, 4.1 extra cases over two years and 16.7 over eight. Against cisgender women, those two figures were 3.4 (1.1-5.6) and 13.7 (4.1-22.7). Ischemic stroke (the vessel-blockage kind) and myocardial infarction were broadly similar across groups. But among trans women who started hormone therapy during the follow-up period, the differences in venous thrombosis and ischemic stroke were more pronounced. The evidence for trans men was insufficient for conclusions. The authors' conclusion: "these results may suggest the need for long-term vigilance for vascular side effects of estrogen in transgender hormone therapy"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Getahun D et al. (2018). Cross-sex Hormones and Acute Cardiovascular Events in Transgender Persons: A Cohort Study. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785>; State Administration for Market Regulation (2022). Measures for the supervision of online drug sales (SAMR Order No. 58, Article 9). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Notes: The researchers themselves wrote down the study's weakness: how many people were buying their own medication outside the hospital system, they couldn't determine - so the line "using or not" was never cleanly drawn. That itself argues for putting hormone use inside formal follow-up. The study looked only at whether vessels had events; it doesn't answer whether hormone therapy should be used or what it gains - that's a different body of evidence. Every situation using sex hormones works the same, including contraceptive pills, menopausal hormone therapy and testosterone supplementation. Anyone buying online, adjusting doses off forum posts, never checking clotting and liver/kidney markers - the risk sits in the "nobody watching" segment, not in why the drug is used. The legitimate route in China is the endocrinology department or the relevant specialty at a proper medical institution: assessment, prescription, follow-up.

### 8. If "feeling ugly" has reached the point of repeated mirror-checking and repeated urges to change, get a body-image assessment before talking surgery
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: One registration at a psychiatric or clinical-psychology department, tens of yuan. The hard part is making this trip before booking the surgery.
- TL;DR: Pooling 33 studies: 15.04% of people presenting for plastic surgery meet criteria for body dysmorphic disorder - magnifying a flaw others can barely see until it affects life. Among dermatology cosmetic patients, 12.65%. Both groups are over seventy percent female. Surgery changes the face; it doesn't break the "check again, unsatisfied again" loop, so one operation easily follows another. Spend one registration fee to see whether you fall in this group before deciding to go under.
- Benefit: 33 studies gathered by the rules and pooled. Among plastic-surgery patients, the prevalence of body dysmorphic disorder (magnifying a flaw others barely notice until it affects life) was 15.04%, with individual studies ranging 2.21%-56.67%. That is, about 15 of every 100 people seeking plastic surgery qualify - study by study, anywhere from 2 to 57. This cohort averaged 34.54±12.41 years old (the second number is the spread), 74.38% female. Dermatology patients: 12.65%, studies ranging 4.52%-35.16%, average age 27.79±9.03, 76.09% female. The authors recommend plastic surgeons and dermatologists proactively assess, identify the high-risk people, and arrange joint management across departments
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Ribeiro RVE (2017). Prevalence of Body Dysmorphic Disorder in Plastic Surgery and Dermatology Patients: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Aesthetic Plast Surg. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0>
- Notes: The measured proportions run from 2.21% all the way to 56.67% - an enormous spread. The cause is different questionnaires and different surveyed populations. So what you can read out is "this proportion is not low and is worth screening once," not that 15.04% is the exact number - the B grade is also for this reason. This entry doesn't say people seeking cosmetic work have psychological problems; the vast majority do not. It's about the small slice where it is "repeated, persistent, already affecting work and social life" - surgery can't solve theirs. The telltale signs: spending long stretches of each day checking or concealing a body part, avoiding socializing or photos because of it, and finishing one procedure only to fixate on the next.
