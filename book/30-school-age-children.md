# 30. Children once they're in school

Chapter 20 covers a child's first year. Chapter 18 counts what raising a child costs in money and time. The stretch in between - ages 3 to 18 - was empty in this book. This chapter fills it. The screen-by-age entry starts from age 0 because the official tiers are 0-3, 3-6, and primary/secondary school - splitting them apart would make it less usable. The chapter answers one question: when "don't let it hurt their studies" collides with "the body," which things cannot wait. They cannot wait because they have time windows that, once passed, can't be made back. Whether academics should be that intense, this chapter doesn't discuss - it only gives the windows and the numbers.

The first eight entries and the last two count death and real physical damage. Entry 9 (don't buy "cure myopia" products) counts money. Entry 10 (the written rules on sleep, homework, PE and rankings) and entry 11 (suspending schooling while keeping enrollment) count time. Drowning, traffic, helmets and the HPV vaccine are in chapter 1. Infants are in chapter 20. Children being scammed and in-game top-ups are in chapter 5. The red lines minors themselves must not cross are in chapter 9. Recognizing emergencies and calling the ambulance is in chapter 13. Children after a parent's death are in chapter 29. None of that is repeated here.

### 1. If a child reports sudden severe pain that keeps getting worse, go to the hospital immediately - don't make them tough it out until school ends
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Take half a day off and bring the child to the emergency room.
- TL;DR: Some diseases are counted in hours. A boy with sudden severe pain in one side of the scrotum has testicular torsion - the vessel attached to the testicle has twisted and the blood supply is cut. Treated within 6 hours, the testicle survives 97.2% of the time. Wait until 19-24 hours, and only 42.5% remains. This kind of pain can't wait for the school bell, and can't wait for the exam to finish.
- Benefit: 30 case-series reports totaling 2,116 testicular-torsion patients were gathered by the rules and pooled. Of them, 1,283 were tallied in 6-hour bands. Counting from the start of pain: testicle survival at 0-6 hours, 97.2%. 7-12 hours, 79.3%. 13-18 hours, 61.3%. 19-24 hours, 42.5%. 25-48 hours, 24.4%. Over 48 hours, 7.4%. Merged into broad bands: within 12 hours, 90.4%; 13-24 hours, 54.0%; over 24 hours, 18.1%. The authors' conclusion is that the salvageable window can run longer than the usually quoted 6-8 hours. So even if the pain has already lasted a long time, still go get it treated - don't skip it because "it's too late anyway"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: Tell a boy ahead of time: if it suddenly hurts badly down there, say so right away - nothing to be embarrassed about. Testicular torsion strikes mostly adolescent boys, and many tough it out because they can't bring themselves to say it. Also counted in hours: acute appendicitis (it perforates if delayed), diabetic ketoacidosis, meningitis. For these, this chapter has not itemized "how much worse per hour of delay." Their common signal: sudden onset, steadily worsening, unlike any pain before. How to recognize emergencies and call the ambulance is in chapter 13.

### 2. Don't postpone needed treatment to "wait until the exams are over" - some windows follow bone age, not the exam calendar
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. This entry decides early versus late, not whether to spend. Leave and follow-up visits take time; the treatment's own cost is separate. The hard part is that the brace demands at least 18 hours a day of wear, and the child has to endure it continuously.
- TL;DR: Scoliosis is the spine curving sideways. A brace is a hard-shell vest worn to hold the spine. Worn while the bones are still growing, the share that curves far enough to need surgery drops clearly: the braced group succeeded 75% of the time, the observed-only group 42%. This window follows when the bones finish growing, not the high-school or college entrance exams. Wait for the exams, and what waits for you is often only the surgical route.
- Benefit: A trial run across multiple hospitals enrolled 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis eligible for bracing. 116 were randomized; 126 chose for themselves whether to wear a brace. Success and failure were defined in advance: progression past 50 degrees counted as failure; finishing bone growth short of that counted as success. Counted by original group assignment, regardless of actual wear. Randomized-to-brace success: 75%. Randomized-to-observation: 42% (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85-9.16) - about 4 times the odds of success. Pooling the randomized and self-selected groups: 72% vs 48% (after equalizing the two groups' baseline differences, OR 1.93, 1.08-3.46 - still about 1.9x). The more hours worn per day, the higher the success rate (P<0.001 - this relationship is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because the brace clearly worked
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is the example here because it has a randomized trial, a clear time window, and is one of the key items in the student health check (see entry 7). The same class includes strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and schedulable surgeries like those for congenital heart disease. What they share: the later it's done, the worse the effect or the bigger the cost. Ask the specialist about the specific window. Missing class is not the problem: during compulsory education a student can suspend schooling for up to 1 year with enrollment kept - see entry 11.

### 3. If your child is bullied, report it to the school that same day and demand written handling; if it involves hitting, extorting money or spreading rumors, call the police directly
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Find the homeroom teacher, and the school's student-bullying governance body. For the police, call 110. The hard part is withstanding advice like "don't make a big thing of it."
- TL;DR: Bullied children are about 2.2 times as likely to develop suicidal thoughts as the non-bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have made a suicide attempt. A school that receives a bullying report must investigate immediately, and once bullying is established, the offending student must be disciplined. If it's serious enough to violate public-security rules or be a suspected crime, the school may not cover it up - it must report to the police. So report the same day, demand the determination procedure, leave a written record - don't settle for "having the teacher talk to him."
- Benefit: Pooling 34 studies and 284,375 children and adolescents. Peer victimization is associated with suicidal ideation, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10-2.37) - about 2.2 times the odds of the non-bullied. Another 9 studies and 70,102 people: victimization associated with suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95-3.34) - about 2.6 times. The result holds across sex, age and study quality. Cyberbullying's association with suicidal ideation is stronger than offline bullying's. Article 21 of the Provisions on school protection of minors lists five kinds of behavior. One: beating, kicking, slapping, scratching and biting, shoving, dragging. Two: verbal abuse, mockery, insulting nicknames. Three: snatching, extorting or deliberately destroying property. Four: malicious exclusion and isolation. Five: fabricating facts online to defame or spreading rumors. The same article provides that when the side with the advantage in age, size or numbers does these things deliberately or maliciously, causing physical injury, property loss or mental harm, it can be determined as bullying. Article 22 requires staff who notice a student's abnormal mood or bodily injury to promptly look into it and report. Article 23 requires the school to investigate immediately on receiving a report, submit it to the bullying-governance body for determination and handling, and notify the parents to take part. Once bullying is established, educational discipline or a disciplinary sanction must follow. Serious bullying that violates public-security rules or is suspected of crime must not be concealed - it must be reported promptly to the police and the education authority (nationwide, effective September 1, 2021)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>; Ministry of Education (2021). Provisions on school protection of minors (MOE Order No. 50, Articles 21, 22, 23). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Notes: When unexplained injuries appear on a child, they suddenly refuse school, or their things keep going missing, start by asking along the signals in Article 22. This pooled analysis only tracked records without a randomized comparison, so it can't prove bullying alone causes suicide - but the direction and size of the risk are clear. The Plan for strengthening comprehensive governance of bullying among primary and secondary students additionally requires strictly distinguishing bullying from roughhousing between students. In other words, the school has a duty to make the determination - it can't close the matter with "kids just playing around."

### 4. Get the child outdoors for 2 hours every day - the only myopia-prevention method currently backed by a randomized trial
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. But it takes time daily, mostly squeezed out of homework and online-class time. The hard part is doing it every day, not a few times.
- TL;DR: A randomized trial across 12 primary schools in Guangzhou: first-graders got one extra 40-minute outdoor-activity class each school day. Three years later, 30.4% of children at those schools were myopic, versus 39.5% at the no-extra-class schools - 9.1 percentage points fewer. The health commission's quantity: at least 2 hours outdoors in daylight every day, or 14 hours a week in total. Overcast days count. Standing outside during recess counts.
- Benefit: First-graders at 12 Guangzhou primary schools, randomized by school (intervention 952 children, control 951, average age 6.6). The intervention: one added 40-minute outdoor-activity class per school day, plus encouraging parents to take the children out more after school. Three-year new myopia: intervention group 30.4% - 259 cases among 853 eligible children. Control group 39.5% - 287 among 726. Difference: -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001) - the true gap roughly between 4 and 14 points fewer. Three-year progression of refraction (spherical equivalent change): -1.42 D vs -1.59 D, a 0.17 D difference (P=0.04). The intervention group deepened slightly less. D is the unit of lens power. Axial length growth (front-to-back length of the eyeball) showed no visible difference (P=0.07). The Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents requires at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor activity daily for children and adolescents, or 14 hours weekly in total. It notes that outdoor activity protects even on overcast days, and requires avoiding the high-heat, strong-sun afternoon hours
- Evidence: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents (Guoweibanfuyouhan [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: The trial ran only 3 years, and the children all started at age 6. The 9.1-point difference between the groups means it keeps some children from becoming myopic - not "guaranteed no myopia." The same ten points give a few more practices. For reading and writing: "one chi, one fist, one cun" (eye a chi from the page, body a fist from the desk, fingers a cun from the pen tip). After 20 minutes of close-up work, look into the distance for at least 20 seconds. Non-study screen use for primary and secondary students: no more than 15 minutes at a time, no more than 1 hour a day in total. "Being outdoors" and "having PE class" are not the same thing - standing outside during recess counts.

### 5. No screens from 0 to 3, as few as possible from 3 to 6, and under 1 hour a day of non-study use for schoolchildren
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=yes benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is taking the screen out of the child's hands - and the parents looking at their own less in front of the child.
- TL;DR: The national health commission's tiers are hard: no screens from 0 to 3, avoid contact as much as possible from 3 to 6, and for schoolchildren no more than 15 minutes at a time and 1 hour a day of non-study use. The reason: long stretches of close-up viewing burn through the "hyperopic reserve" early, and myopia arrives early. The research evidence linking screens to myopia is weaker than this rule - what actually has a randomized trial behind it is the 2 daily hours outdoors.
- Benefit: Article 4 of the Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents, verbatim: "infants aged 0-3 do not use video-screen electronic products such as phones, tablets and computers; for children aged 3-6, contact with and use of video-screen electronic products such as phones, tablets and computers should be avoided as much as possible; for primary and secondary students, non-study use of electronic screens should not exceed 15 minutes per session and 1 hour per day in total." The same article states that "prolonged close-up use of electronic video-screen products easily consumes children's hyperopic reserve, and is an important reason for the early onset and high incidence of myopia in children and adolescents." The 2018 implementation plan from the Ministry of Education and seven other departments gives parents the same requirement, adding "the younger the age, the shorter the continuous use of electronic products should be." The research side is much weaker: a systematic review took in 33 studies, 11 entering the pooled calculation, with subjects from 3 months to 33 years old. For phones and tablets alone, the myopia odds ratio was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Combined with computers, 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors' conclusion goes only as far as "possibly associated," noting that none of the 33 studies had a reliable measure of screen time
- Evidence: B
- Sources: General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents (Guoweibanfuyouhan [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>; Ministry of Education and seven other departments (2018). Implementation plan for comprehensive prevention and control of myopia in children and adolescents (Jiaotiyi [2018] No. 3). <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html>; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7>; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: Grade B because the official side gives tiered rules, not numbers measured in a trial. On the research side, the odds ratio's lower bound sits right on 1.00, and the authors dare only say "possibly associated." So use this entry as an easy-to-enforce house rule, not as "no screens means no myopia." What genuinely has a randomized trial behind it is entry 4 (2 hours outdoors). Preschoolers also need their eyes checked: the same document states that at ages 1-3, 4-6 and after 7, children should have regular refraction screening to see how much hyperopic reserve remains - the method is in entry 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Screens' relationship with children's language skills has also been meta-analyzed: 42 studies, 18,905 people - more screen time, weaker language skills (r=-0.14, 95% CI -0.18 to -0.10). But co-viewing and educational content run the other way (r=0.16 and 0.13). This is correlation, not causation - it only says substituting a screen for talking with your child doesn't pay.

### 6. Treat myopia as damage that stays: the deeper the prescription, the more retinal detachment, macular disease and glaucoma later
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. This entry itself asks nothing - the doing is in entries 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, screen tiers by age).
- TL;DR: Myopia doesn't heal itself, and the prescription doesn't drop back. It's also not just "wear glasses." Compared with the non-myopic, the likelihood of myopic macular degeneration runs over ten times higher for mild myopia and several hundred times higher above 600 degrees. Retinal detachment: about 3 times higher for mild, about 13 times above 600 degrees. So the earlier a child becomes myopic and the faster it climbs, the bigger the risk they carry into the future.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies up to June 2019, in three prescription bands: mild (-0.5 to -3.00 D), moderate (-3.00 to -6.00 D), high (≤-6.00 D, i.e. above 600 degrees). Compared with non-myopes, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48) and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). Retinal detachment: 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). Posterior subcapsular cataract: 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). Open-angle glaucoma: 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91) for mild, 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52) for moderate and high combined. Odds ratios for visual impairment after age 60, by band: 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten core pieces of knowledge says the same: above 600 degrees is high myopia, and in this group the incidence of blinding eye diseases - cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic maculopathy, retinal detachment, myopic optic neuropathy - is markedly higher than in other groups. And "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents (Guoweibanfuyouhan [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: Everything pooled in is observational research, and the odds ratios are unusually large. The credible range for high myopia against macular degeneration runs from 230 to 3104 - the underlying studies differ wildly, so treat the numbers as orders of magnitude, not exact values. Most of these complications show up only in middle and old age, so this isn't "what happens tomorrow" - it's a bill being paid over decades. Myopia itself is not a disability; the vast majority see normally with correction. The use of this entry is swapping "myopic? just get glasses, done" for "worth the effort to delay it and control it." How to follow up after diagnosis is in entry 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Don't buy products claiming to cure it - entry 9 (curing myopia).

### 7. Read the annual student health-check report yourself, and take any abnormal item to a hospital that same year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. During compulsory education, the checkup fee comes out of the school's public funds - parents pay nothing extra.
- TL;DR: Primary and secondary schools organize one student health check every year. The report form is required to list overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal curvature abnormality, poor vision and dental caries as key guidance items. These happen to be the ones most easily postponed with "we'll see later." Don't glance at height and weight and put the form away. If any of these items is abnormal, take the child to the specialty that same year - don't wait for the next checkup.
- Benefit: The Measures for the administration of health checks for primary and secondary students (2021 edition) requires schools to organize one health check per enrolled student per year. The surgical exam covers head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, lymph nodes. The eye exam covers eye appearance, distance vision and refraction (the degree of myopia or hyperopia). The individual report form must include the findings, an overall assessment and health guidance. Among them, overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal curvature abnormality, poor vision and dental caries must be guidance priorities. The education authority should build a file on students found with health problems and follow up - record them and check back later. During compulsory education, the health-check fee is paid from the school's public funds - parents pay nothing extra (nationwide, issued September 2021)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: National Health Commission and Ministry of Education (2021). Measures for the administration of health checks for primary and secondary students (2021 edition) (Guoweiyifa [2021] No. 29). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: Grade B because this is a document regulating what schools do, not an effectiveness study. It guarantees "checked every year, these items checked, abnormalities followed up" - it doesn't guarantee someone acts on what's found. The checkup is only a first screen, not a diagnosis. Poor vision needs a cycloplegic refraction at an eye clinic (entry 12). A spinal curvature abnormality needs orthopedics or spine surgery (entry 2 - don't postpone treatment for exams). Both steps are trips the parents must make with the child themselves.

### 8. Children aged 12 to 18 get one depression screening - and don't take the school's psychological survey as a diagnosis
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: 0 to tens of yuan - fill in a screening questionnaire at a community center or hospital outpatient. The 12355 and 12356 hotlines cost nothing.
- TL;DR: The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depression, judging the benefit moderate and worth doing. For 11 and under, the evidence is insufficient. Suicide is the second-leading cause of death among 10- to 19-year-olds. The psychological survey the school runs each school year is a census, not a diagnosis. If the child says they're suffering, or eating, sleeping, interest and grades visibly change within a short time, register directly at a psychiatric or clinical-psychology department.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (grade B recommendation). Grade B means moderate certainty and a moderate net benefit - worth doing. Screening children 11 and under for major depression: insufficient evidence (I statement - the evidence can't support a recommendation). Screening children and adolescents specifically for suicide risk: insufficient evidence for both benefit and harm (I statement). The document notes suicide is the second-leading cause of death among 10- to 19-year-olds. It also notes major depression in children and adolescents is closely tied to many later problems - recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and elevated risk of suicidal ideation, suicide attempts and suicide death. The Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work among primary and secondary students requires a three-tier service system: homeroom teachers, full- and part-time mental-health teachers, and psychiatrists. Every student receives a psychological survey each school year, generally no more than once. The document also requires advancing referral-to-care and return-to-school mechanisms for mental illness, and giving play to the 12355 youth service desk and the 12356 psychological-assistance hotline (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2025). Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work among primary and secondary students (Jiaojiting [2025] No. 2). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: Grade B because the source gives a recommendation-strength rating, not an effect convertible to numbers. It's also a US recommendation - China has no corresponding official screening recommendation. A positive screen means only that a further assessment is needed, not a confirmed diagnosis. What to do when suicidal thoughts appear is in entry 25 of chapter 1. How the family copes is in chapter 29.

### 9. Don't buy products or services that claim to "cure myopia" or "lower the prescription"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free - it saves that money instead.
- TL;DR: The market regulation administration's own words: "under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." The phrases "rehabilitation," "recovery," "lowers the prescription," "cures myopia," "myopia nemesis" and "prescription repair" are listed outright as illegal marketing expressions to be strictly punished. Seeing this kind of wording lets you rule the product out directly - no need to comparison-shop. Eye-care devices count as medical devices, and e-commerce platforms are responsible for checking whether sellers are qualified to sell them.
- Benefit: The special rectification action notice from the market regulation administration's general office, November 2021 to March 2022, states plainly: "under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." The notice requires strict punishment under law of marketing children's myopia-control products with misleading expressions like "rehabilitation," "recovery," "lowers the prescription," "cures myopia," "myopia nemesis" and "prescription repair." It requires heavier enforcement against marketing that falsely uses the name of traditional Chinese medicine or borrows its theories and techniques to deceive consumers. It requires cracking down on two kinds of false and illegal advertising: before-and-after treatment-effect comparisons using patients' names or images, and guaranteed promises about treatment safety or efficacy. It also requires e-commerce platforms to strictly fulfill their duty to review sellers' qualifications for medical devices like eye-care devices and related special goods - the platform must verify the seller is qualified to sell such things (nationwide)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: General Office of the State Administration for Market Regulation (2021). Notice on launching the special rectification action against illegal commercial marketing of myopia-control products for children and adolescents. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Notes: Grade B because the basis is the regulator's enforcement wording, not controlled trials of the products. But the sentence "myopia cannot be cured" is stated identically by the health and regulation sides: the Ten core pieces of knowledge writes "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed." The two things genuinely backed by evidence are in entry 4 (outdoors) and entry 12 (proper refraction, fitting and follow-up). Blue-light-blocking glasses are in chapter 6.

### 10. Sleep, homework, PE and rankings all have written rules - if the school doesn't meet them, you can raise it
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Knowing the provisions is enough; when needed, raise it with the school or the local education authority. The hard part is opening your mouth to raise it.
- TL;DR: These are all written in black and white: primary students sleep 10 hours a day, middle school 9 hours, high school 8 hours. Primary school classes generally start no earlier than 8:20 AM, middle school no earlier than 8:00. A 2025 addition: no less than 2 hours of combined physical activity daily, and students may not be ranked by exam scores. If a child chronically under-sleeps, check against these provisions first - don't first blame the child for dawdling.
- Benefit: The Notice on further strengthening sleep management for primary and secondary students provides: primary students should get 10 hours of sleep daily, middle schoolers 9, high schoolers 8. Primary school morning classes generally start no earlier than 8:20, middle school no earlier than 8:00. Schools may not require students to arrive early for unified educational activities. Bedtime generally no later than 21:20 for primary, 22:00 for middle school, 23:00 for high school. Off-campus tutoring must end no later than 20:30, and livestreamed online tutoring no later than 21:00. From 22:00 daily to 8:00 the next day, no game services may be provided to minors (nationwide, issued March 2021). The Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work among primary and secondary students adds several provisions. Fully implement no less than 2 hours of combined physical activity daily for primary and secondary students. Encourage "15-minute recesses." Students may not be ranked by exam scores. Mechanically repetitive and punitive homework is strictly forbidden. Encourage setting one "no-homework day" per week (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: General Office of the Ministry of Education (2021). Notice on further strengthening sleep management for primary and secondary students (Jiaojitinghan [2021] No. 11). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/02/content_5597443.htm>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2025). Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work among primary and secondary students (Jiaojiting [2025] No. 2). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: Both documents come from the supervising departments. How far each place and each school executes them varies wildly - hence grade B. Their use is letting you know which things the school should be doing anyway, so you have a basis in hand when you go talk. Adults' sleep ledger is in chapters 2 and 3.

### 11. If the child can't hold up anymore, they can suspend schooling - the school must keep their enrollment, up to 1 year
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. The parents or other legal guardians apply to the school. The hard part is first accepting the child stopping for a while.
- TL;DR: Suspending schooling is a formal institution. The parents or other legal guardians apply; the school confirms and reports to the higher enrollment authority for approval. Each application covers no more than 1 year; if the suspension must continue past that, apply again. During the suspension, the school must keep the student's enrollment. So "suspending means ruined" does not hold institutionally. The detailed rules are set by each province - ask the school's academic office or the local education bureau.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Measures for the administration of enrollment status for primary and secondary students sets out how suspension works. The suspension is applied for by the parents or other legal guardians. The school confirms and reports to the higher enrollment authority for approval. On resuming, the school promptly handles the relevant procedures. Each suspension application covers no more than 1 year; if it genuinely must continue past that, apply anew. During the suspension the school keeps the student's enrollment - the student status and place are held. The detailed rules for suspension and resumption are made by provincial education authorities (nationwide, issued January 2025). The Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work requires advancing referral-to-care and post-recovery return-to-school mechanisms for mental illness, and guiding schools to make response plans for sudden on-campus psychological-crisis events
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Ministry of Education (2025). Measures for the administration of enrollment status for primary and secondary students (Jiaoji [2025] No. 1, Article 16). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002620.htm>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2025). Ten measures for further strengthening mental-health work among primary and secondary students (Jiaojiting [2025] No. 2). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: Before going to apply, ask three things first: what medical certificate is needed, how the enrollment is recorded in the system during the suspension, and whether resumption returns to the original grade or drops one. Grade B because the conditions, the materials and how resumption connects are all set by provincial education departments - the national level only builds the frame. The Provisions on school protection of minors also requires compulsory-education schools to register students who drop out, suspend or take long leave - so the school has walked this road before; it's not opening a special exception for you alone.

### 12. If poor vision is found, go to the hospital for a cycloplegic refraction, then recheck on the schedule the doctor gives
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Tens to a few hundred yuan for the exam and the glasses. After dilation the child will be light-sensitive and blurry up close, needing half a day to a day or two to recover.
- TL;DR: Cycloplegic refraction means first dripping in an eye drop that relaxes the muscle the eye uses to focus, then measuring the prescription. It's done at the hospital and is the most accurate way to diagnose myopia. The health commission states in writing that the drops do no harm to healthy eyes and the eye returns to normal after they wear off. Don't take one auto-refraction at a glasses shop as a diagnosis, and don't refuse glasses for the child out of "fear the prescription will climb."
- Benefit: The Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia explains what cycloplegic refraction is. First a cycloplegic agent, then refraction. A cycloplegic agent is an eye drop that relaxes the muscle the eye uses to focus. Cycloplegic refraction is a medical refraction done at the hospital - the "gold standard" for diagnosing myopia, meaning the most accurate method. The dilating drug does no damage to healthy eyes; light sensitivity and blurred near vision can appear briefly after use, and normal function returns after it wears off. Once myopia is diagnosed, intervene promptly and scientifically, with correction such as wearing glasses. Properly fitted glasses effectively correct blurred vision and slow myopia's progression. For those seeing normally with correction: preschool and primary children recheck every 3 to 6 months, middle and high school students every 6 to 12 months, with glasses replaced as the situation decides. Children should receive regular refraction screening (prescription checks) at ages 1-3, 4-6 and after 7, to monitor the hyperopic reserve (nationwide, issued July 2023)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Ten core pieces of knowledge for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents (Guoweibanfuyouhan [2023] No. 278). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: Grade B because this is the practice given by an official outreach document, not primary research with concrete numbers. High myopia leads to things like retinal detachment, so the rechecks are about more than replacing glasses. The "poor vision" found in the student health check is only a first screen - it still needs a complete eye exam at the hospital (see entry 7).

### 13. Once the permanent molars come in, get pit-and-fissure sealant done
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Tens to a couple hundred yuan per tooth. No anesthetic, no drilling, a few minutes each time. Many places have free programs for children of the right age.
- TL;DR: Pit-and-fissure sealant coats and seals the grooves on the chewing surface of the molars with resin, so food residue can't lodge in. At 24 months after, the probability of cavities is markedly lower than unsealed. If 16% of tooth surfaces decay in unsealed children, sealed runs about 5.2%. If 40% unsealed, sealed runs about 6.25%. The authors' summary: at 24 months, 11 to 51 percentage points fewer cavities than unsealed - still effective within 48 months.
- Benefit: 38 trials, 7,924 children aged 5 to 16, gathered by Cochrane's rules and pooled. Resin-based sealant against no sealant. First permanent molars of children aged 5-10, at 24 months after: caries OR 0.12 (95% CI 0.08-0.19) - about one eighth the odds of decay without sealant. This item comes from 7 trials, 1,548 randomized and 1,322 finally examined, moderate-quality evidence. If the control group decays on 16% of tooth surfaces within 24 months, sealed runs 5.2% (3.13%-7.37%). At 40% control: 6.25% (3.84%-9.63%). At 70% control: 19% (12.3%-27.2%). At 48 to 54 months, OR 0.21 (0.16-0.28) - still about a fifth of unsealed. The authors conclude resin sealant produces 11% to 51% less caries than no sealant at 24 months. Across the 4 trials that recorded adverse reactions, none appeared. Which of the other sealing materials (glass ionomer and the like) is better: insufficient evidence
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Notes: The first permanent molar (the big tooth that never gets replaced) usually emerges around age 6, the second around 12. Sealing should happen when the tooth has just emerged and hasn't yet decayed - once it has, the tooth can only be filled. Many regions list sealant as a free program for children of the right age - ask the local CDC or community health service center. This chapter found no nationwide document checkable word for word, so the coverage and age bands are not written here. Dental caries is also a key guidance item in the student health check - see entry 7.
