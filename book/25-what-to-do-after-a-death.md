# 25. What to do after someone dies

Lens: money. This chapter covers only two things: how the procedures work, and how the money is collected. What to do first when someone has just died. Which fees have a list you can check against. Which accounts hold money that can be withdrawn. Which matters close relatives have the right to handle. The first five entries are ordered by when things happen: the scene first, then the death certificate, then the remains and cremation, then what to do if you dispute the cause of death, and finally cancelling the household registration. The entries after that are ordered by value. Arrangements the elderly should make while alive - designated guardianship, a will, accounts - are in chapter 17. The three work-death benefits are in chapter 19.

The funeral part follows the new "Regulation on funeral and interment administration," effective March 30, 2026. How much the pension-insurance funeral subsidy and survivor's pension pay is set separately by the human-resources and social-security authorities. This round did not obtain the original text in a form that can be checked word for word, so the body gives no specific amounts.

### 1. When someone dies at home or outside, first tell a natural death from an unnatural one: accidents, unclear causes, and people found only after living alone a long time - call the police at once, and don't touch the body or the scene
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call.
- TL;DR: For deaths by accident, deaths of unknown cause, and people discovered only after living alone for a long time, the death certificate can only be issued by the police. If the scene has been disturbed, the facts can't be told clearly and the certificate is hard to issue. Without that piece of paper, everything after - cremation, cancelling the registration, insurance claims - jams. If you can't tell which kind it is, call 110 first. Once the police have examined the scene, they issue the certificate all the same.
- Benefit: Paragraph 2 of Article 8 of the household-registration regulation: "where a citizen dies in an accident or of an unclear cause, the head of household or the person who discovers it shall immediately report to the local police station or the township or town people's committee." Meaning: after an accident, or when you don't know how the person died, the head of household and the discoverer must report to the police station or township government at once. Article 23 of the funeral regulation: "where a natural person dies a natural death, the medical and health institution shall promptly issue a death certificate. For the deceased in an unnatural-death case (incident) handled by the public-security organ by law without treatment by a medical and health institution, the public-security organ shall issue the death certificate." Meaning: for an ordinary death from illness, the medical institution that treated them issues the death certificate. For a death with no hospital treatment that falls to the police to handle, the police issue it. The death certificate for an unnatural death can only be issued by the police. A disturbed scene both muddies the facts and blocks the certificate. Everything after - cremation, registration cancellation, insurance claims - jams on this one piece of paper
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (1958). Regulation on household registration of the People's Republic of China (Article 8). <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html> (published on the Guangdong Provincial People's Government portal); State Council (2026). Regulation on funeral and interment administration (State Council Order No. 824, effective March 30, 2026, Article 23). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>
- Notes: Long-term bedridden illness ending in death at home counts as a natural death. Get the certificate from the institution that treated them in life, or from the community health service center or township hospital covering where they lived. Whoever treated them issues the certificate - see entry 2 (the death certificate). If you can't tell which kind it is, treat it as unnatural and call 110 first. Once the police have investigated, they can still issue the certificate. The other way around - tidying up the scene and then calling - and nobody can help you. Don't move the body yourself. Don't wash it or change its clothes. Don't put away medicine bottles or notes.

### 2. The death certificate is the key to everything after: whoever treated them issues it; for a natural death at home, the community health service, signed within one day
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: If they died in a hospital, the institution that treated them issues the death certificate. If they died naturally at home, the community health service or township hospital covering where they lived issues it. Where the cause is clear, it's signed within one day. Cancelling the registration, cremation, withdrawing the housing fund, claiming social-insurance benefits, insurance claims, handling inheritance - every one of them needs it. Make several copies the moment you have it.
- Benefit: The "Specification for the administration of death information registration (trial)" says: for deaths inside a medical and health institution, it is "issued by the medical and health institution responsible for the treatment." So for a death in a hospital or health center, the institution that treated them issues it. For a natural death at home or elsewhere, it is "issued by the community health service institution of the jurisdiction or the township (subdistrict) health center." So the community health service center or township or subdistrict health center covering where they lived issues it. Where the public-security or judicial authorities determine an unnatural death, they handle it under the current provisions and procedures. A 2026 notice from five departments says the death certificate's "paper version and electronic certificate have equal legal effect" - the electronic one works the same as the paper. For a natural death with a clear cause, it should be signed within one day of the death. If the certificate is lost, you may apply to the original issuing institution for one replacement. If the basic information is wrong, you may apply once for reissuance with supporting material
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the National Health and Family Planning Commission (2014). Specification for the administration of death information registration (trial) (Guoweiban Guihua Fa [2014] No. 68, Article 9). <https://mzj.sz.gov.cn/szmz/pc/zwgk/jcxxgk/zcfg/byfw/content/post_2952215.html> (forwarded by the Shenzhen Civil Affairs Bureau); National Health Commission, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of Civil Affairs, National Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine, National Disease Control and Prevention Administration (2026). Notice on strengthening the registration of resident medical death-certificate information and the management of electronic certificates (Guowei Guihua Fa [2026] No. 5). <https://wjw.fujian.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gjwj/202602/t20260228_7102810.htm> (forwarded by the Fujian Provincial Health Commission)
- Notes: Cancelling the registration, cremation, withdrawing the housing fund, claiming social-insurance benefits, insurance claims, handling inheritance - every one of them needs it. Make several copies the moment you have it. Where an electronic certificate can be issued, apply for that too. There's only one chance for a replacement - don't lose the original. If the name or ID number is wrong, fix it on the spot. Discover it only when handling inheritance, and every window has to be run again.

### 3. Transport, storage and cremation of the remains go only through the funeral home; the hospital morgue doesn't run funerals, and storage generally doesn't exceed 3 days
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: Transport, storage, preservation, cosmetic preparation and cremation can only be done by the funeral home. The hospital morgue doesn't conduct funerals - it can only hold the remains temporarily. Funeral-home storage generally doesn't exceed 3 days; keeping them longer requires an extension procedure. Cremation requires the death certificate and the cremation confirmation. The cremation certificate issued afterward is needed for inheritance and claims.
- Benefit: Article 25 of the funeral regulation: "transport, storage, preservation, cosmetic preparation and cremation of remains are provided exclusively by funeral homes." Nobody else can do these things. Article 24: "the morgues of medical and health institutions may not be outsourced and may not conduct funeral services; they may provide convenient services for the temporary placement of remains." The hospital morgue can only hold them temporarily - it cannot take on the funeral. Article 27: "funeral homes generally store remains for no more than 3 days; where extended storage is needed, the bereaved family or the party handing over the remains shall complete the extension procedure at the funeral home." To keep them a few more days, go to the funeral home and file the extension. Article 28: "the funeral home shall cremate the remains on the strength of the death certificate and the cremation confirmation, and shall issue a cremation certificate." Both documents are required. After cremation you receive a cremation certificate. Article 29: "in areas where cremation is practiced, the remains shall be cremated at a funeral home on the spot or nearby." That is, find a funeral home close to where the person died
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Regulation on funeral and interment administration (State Council Order No. 824, effective March 30, 2026, Articles 8, 9, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>
- Notes: The "one-stop" operators who approach you after a death in hospital are mostly not funeral-home people. Transport itself is a basic item with a set price - see entry 6 (the basic-items list). Keep the cremation certificate safe: inheritance, insurance and relocating the ashes all need it. Whether your locality practices cremation or burial is designated by the provincial government. The funeral customs of ethnic minorities are protected by Article 9 of the regulation. Burying where cremation is required, or interring or building a grave outside a cemetery or eco-burial area, brings an order from the civil-affairs department to correct within a time limit - to change it back.

### 4. If you dispute the cause of death, request an autopsy within 48 hours - and seal the medical records at the same time
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is remembering to raise it at that moment.
- TL;DR: If you have doubts about the cause of death, request the autopsy within 48 hours of the death. Where the hospital has freezer storage, it can stretch to 7 days. The autopsy also needs a close relative's signed consent. Not signing, or dragging past the deadline, places the responsibility for an unclear cause of death on the side that delayed. While requesting the autopsy, also demand that the medical records be sealed. Once the body is cremated, no autopsy is ever possible again.
- Benefit: The autopsy time limit is written in Article 26 of the Regulation on the prevention and handling of medical disputes. The original text: "where the patient has died and the two sides dispute the cause of death, an autopsy shall be performed within 48 hours of the patient's death; where conditions for frozen storage of the body exist, it may be extended to 7 days." In other words, to find the cause of death, the autopsy happens within 48 hours of the death. With freezing facilities at the hospital, at most it stretches to 7 days. The same article also says: "the autopsy shall require the consent and signature of the deceased's close relatives; a refusal to sign is treated as the close relatives not consenting to the autopsy." If the family won't sign, it counts as refusal. It also says: "where a party does not consent to or delays the autopsy beyond the prescribed time, affecting the determination of the cause of death, that party bears the responsibility." Whoever refuses, whoever drags past the time - the responsibility for an unclear cause of death lands on them. Article 24: sealing and unsealing medical record materials "shall be done in the presence of both the patient side and the medical institution." Sealing the records and opening them again - you and the hospital must both be there
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2018). Regulation on the prevention and handling of medical disputes (State Council Order No. 701, Articles 24, 26). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm>
- Notes: Past this time limit, saying "I felt something was wrong back then" puts the responsibility on the side that delayed. Once the body is cremated, no autopsy is possible again. So settle this before contacting the funeral home. How to seal the medical records is in chapter 24.

### 5. Cancelling the household registration has a statutory deadline: in cities before the burial, in rural areas within one month
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. Bring the death certificate and the household register, one trip to the police station.
- TL;DR: In cities the registration is cancelled before the burial; in rural areas within one month. Bring the death certificate and household register to the police station. Leave it undone, and inheritance transfers, benefit claims and insurance cancellations all jam on the registration status.
- Benefit: Paragraph 1 of Article 8 of the household-registration regulation: "when a citizen dies - in cities before the burial, in rural areas within one month - the head of household, a relative, a supporter or a neighbor shall report the death to the household-registration authority for registration, and the household registration shall be cancelled. Where a citizen dies at a place of temporary residence, the household-registration authority of the temporary residence shall notify the household-registration authority of the permanent residence to cancel the registration." Meaning: in the city, do it before the burial; in the countryside, within a month. The person who goes can be the head of household, a relative, a supporter or a neighbor. The place is the household-registration authority - the police station - to report the death and cancel the registration. If the person died while staying temporarily elsewhere, the police station of the temporary residence notifies the police station of the registered domicile to cancel it
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (1958). Regulation on household registration of the People's Republic of China (Article 8). <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html> (published on the Guangdong Provincial People's Government portal)
- Notes: Leave the registration hanging, and inheritance transfers, benefit claims and insurance cancellations all jam - those windows all look at the registration status. If the pension keeps being paid out, that's claiming under false pretenses, and sooner or later it gets clawed back. While you're at the police station, ask the follow-ups: how the social-insurance and medical-insurance benefits are stopped, and how the funeral subsidy is claimed. Those payments are in entry 9 (housing-fund balance, social-insurance benefits).

### 6. Funeral services split into basic and non-basic items; the basic items have a list, and their prices are set by law
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes with the list before you set out.
- TL;DR: Transport, storage, the farewell ceremony, cremation, ash storage and eco-burial are all on the national basic-items list. Ash-niche interment provided by government-run institutions is on it too. The prices of these items are set by regulation. Outside the list, no new chargeable items may be invented. Before you set out, ask which items count as basic and what the local rates are - then talk about the rest.
- Benefit: Transport of remains, storage of remains, the farewell ceremony, cremation, ash storage and eco-burial are all on the national basic-items list. Ash-niche interment provided by government-run funeral institutions is on the list too. The fee standards for these items are set by law - the price is fixed by regulation, not up to the funeral home. The non-basic items list is set by civil-affairs departments at the prefecture-city level and above
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Regulation on funeral and interment administration (State Council Order No. 824, revised and adopted at the 72nd executive meeting of the State Council on November 14, 2025, "effective March 30, 2026"). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> Article 5: "funeral services are divided into basic items and non-basic items, and the fee policy is set by the State Council's development-and-reform and finance departments together with the civil-affairs and other departments." "The state establishes a list of basic funeral-service items, including transport of remains, storage of remains, the farewell ceremony, cremation, ash storage, eco-burial, and ash-niche interment provided by government-run funeral institutions"; Article 50: "funeral services are administered by list, dynamically adjusted, and it is forbidden to establish items or collect fees outside the list."
- Notes: Provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities directly under the central government may add items on top of the national list. Ask first which items count as basic and what the local rates are - then talk about the rest. Wanting to give your relative a decent send-off is not a fault, and this entry doesn't urge frugality. It solves one thing: telling apart which items are on the list with prices fixed by regulation, and which are optional. Once that line is clear, where the money goes is still your call - but the part someone overcharges you in your grief becomes something you can challenge

### 7. Forced bundling, surcharges on top of listed prices, splitting one item into several charges - note it down and complain
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is being willing to push back at that moment.
- TL;DR: These are all price violations the regulation names: not displaying a price list, charging above the listed price, forcing the sale of funeral goods in a bundle, pressuring you into someone else's paid service. Splitting one item into several charges also counts as a disguised price increase. On the spot, ask for the price list first, and keep the receipts and chat records. Complaining afterward still works. For price matters go to market regulation; for funeral services, to civil affairs.
- Benefit: The regulation lists five kinds of price violations. Four of them: not marking prices clearly, or charging above the listed price - no price list hung up, or charging more than the listed price. Forcing sales through bundling or attaching unreasonable conditions - won't do this unless you buy that. Forcing or covertly forcing acceptance of a third party's paid service - shoving another outfit's paid service at you. Covertly raising the fee standard - charging more under a different name. It also separately bans "covertly raising fee standards by splitting items, expanding the scope of charges and similar forms" - splitting one service into several separately charged items, or expanding the scope of what's charged
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Regulation on funeral and interment administration (State Council Order No. 824, revised and adopted at the 72nd executive meeting of the State Council on November 14, 2025, "effective March 30, 2026"). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> Article 42: "funeral service institutions may not, in violation of relevant provisions, establish fee items on their own, or covertly raise fee standards by splitting items, expanding the scope of charges and similar forms." The price violations listed in Article 43 include "(1) not marking prices clearly as required, or charging above the listed price or collecting unlisted fees," "(3) forcing or covertly forcing the sale of funeral goods or the provision of funeral-related services for a fee through bundling, attaching unreasonable conditions or unreasonable restrictions," "(4) forcing or covertly forcing the bereaved family to accept a third party's paid services, or charging repeatedly through related service outlets"; Article 51 requires the civil-affairs departments and the government-service convenience hotline to build an information-sharing supervision mechanism, under which "problems with funeral services reported by bereaved families shall be promptly handled together with the relevant departments, with the outcome made known to the family"
- Notes: Investigating price violations belongs to the market-regulation department. Funeral services themselves belong to the civil-affairs department. On the spot, ask for a price list first. Keep the receipts and chat records. Complaining afterward still works.

### 8. Funeral agents, goods purchasers, planners and hosts - these intermediaries must file with the county-level civil-affairs department
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Ask the one question: are you filed.
- TL;DR: Intermediaries - funeral agents, goods purchasing, planning and hosting, information consulting - must file with the county-level civil-affairs department. Unfiled ones aren't in the civil-affairs funeral-service information system. Whether you can look it up yourself varies by place; the surest way is to call the county-level civil-affairs department. Those who overcharge through deception or trade in the deceased's information are dealt with by law.
- Benefit: An unfiled intermediary isn't in the funeral-service information system. That system is run by the civil-affairs departments. Those who charge in violation by deceptive means, induce extravagant funerals, force services on families, or trade in the deceased's information are dealt with by law. They're also flagged in the system and disclosed to the public - recorded where anyone can see
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Regulation on funeral and interment administration (State Council Order No. 824, revised and adopted at the 72nd executive meeting of the State Council on November 14, 2025, "effective March 30, 2026"). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> Article 41: "apart from funeral service institutions, organizations and individuals engaged in funeral-related service activities such as funeral-service agency, goods purchasing, planning and hosting, and information consulting shall file with the civil-affairs department of the county-level people's government." "Those found charging in violation by deceptive or similar means, inducing extravagant funerals, forcing services, or trading in the deceased's information in ways that harm the bereaved family's rights shall be dealt with by law, flagged in the funeral-service information system, and disclosed to the public."
- Notes: "Trading in the deceased's information" is the most common trap in this trade. A flood of sales calls right after the paperwork is done usually means the information was resold. Online memorial platforms are regulated the same way: Article 45 of the regulation forbids them from inducing spending and from unlawfully handling the deceased's information.

### 9. The money scattered in different places has to be collected one account at a time: housing-fund balance, social-insurance benefits, work-injury benefits
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: Free, but it takes several windows. Have the death certificate and the proof of kinship ready before you go.
- TL;DR: The money left in their housing-provident-fund account can be withdrawn by the heirs and legatees. It isn't sitting in a bank account, and it doesn't automatically enter the estate - it's the easiest one to miss. For a work-related death there are three further payments: the funeral subsidy, the dependent relatives' pension, and the lump-sum work-death allowance. For the pension-insurance personal-account balance and survivor benefits, ask at the social-insurance office where they were enrolled.
- Benefit: The balance of the housing provident fund account - the money left in their account - can be withdrawn by the heirs and legatees. A legatee is the person named in the will to receive something. For a work-related death, there are three further payments: the funeral subsidy, the dependent relatives' pension, and the lump-sum work-death allowance
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Regulation on the administration of housing provident funds (as amended by State Council Order No. 844, effective September 20, 2026), final paragraph of Article 24. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>: "where an employee dies or is declared dead, the employee's heirs and legatees may withdraw the balance stored in the employee's housing provident fund account; where there are neither heirs nor legatees, the balance stored in the employee's housing provident fund account is incorporated into the fund's appreciation income."; the three benefits under Article 39 of the Regulation on work-related injury insurance, see chapter 19. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>; TODO (unverified: the inheritance rules for the basic pension-insurance personal-account balance, and the specific standards for the funeral subsidy and survivor's pension - the relevant human-resources ministry documents were not obtained this round)
- Notes: The housing fund is the one most often forgotten, because it isn't in a bank account and doesn't automatically enter the estate. For the money left in the pension-insurance personal account and the survivor benefits paid to the family, ask at the social-insurance window where they were enrolled. The required materials differ by place. The formal requirements for wills and inheritance are in chapter 17.

### 10. For the deceased's accounts and personal information, close relatives may request access, copies, correction and deletion
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Only if the platform won't cooperate do you need to complain or sue.
- TL;DR: After someone dies, close relatives may, for their own lawful and legitimate interests, ask a platform for access to, copies of, correction of and deletion of the deceased's personal information. If the platform refuses, it must give its reasons. You can also sue in court. There is one exception: if they made other arrangements while alive - named an account heir, left a clear wish - their arrangement governs.
- Benefit: The law states plainly: when a natural person dies, their close relatives may, for their own lawful and legitimate interests, exercise the rights of access, copying, correction and deletion over the deceased's relevant personal information. In other words, you can ask the platform to produce their information, give you a copy, fix errors, and delete it
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Personal Information Protection Law. Website of the National People's Congress. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html> Article 49: "where a natural person dies, their close relatives may, for their own lawful and legitimate interests, exercise the rights of access, copying, correction and deletion provided in this chapter over the deceased's relevant personal information; except where the deceased made other arrangements while alive."; Article 50: "personal information handlers shall establish convenient mechanisms for accepting and handling individuals' requests to exercise their rights. A refusal of an individual's request to exercise their rights shall state the reasons" and "the individual may file a lawsuit with a people's court by law"
- Notes: The statute carries the line "except where the deceased made other arrangements while alive." Meaning: if they named an account heir or left a clear wish in life, what they left governs. So while the person is still clear-minded, it's worth writing down where the accounts and passwords should go - see chapter 17. Closing an account and deleting personal information are two different things - see chapter 14.
