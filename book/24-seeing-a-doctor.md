# 24. Seeing a doctor: how to spend less and take fewer wrong turns

Lens: money and time. This chapter covers how the act of getting care itself works. Which hospital to go to first, how to get referred upward, where the money can be saved. It also covers how emergency queuing works, and - after a serious injury is treated - which assessments to do and which documents to get. What benefits you can claim is in chapter 7. Long-term management of chronic disease is in chapter 16. What to do first at the scene of an emergency is in chapter 13.

This chapter mainly follows the "Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system," issued by the General Office of the State Council in April 2026. Tiered diagnosis means choosing which level of hospital to visit first by how serious the condition is. The document ties "going to primary care" and "saving money" directly together. But rollout differs by region, and what the body text writes is the national-level rule. For your own coordinating region, follow the local medical-insurance and health authorities. A coordinating region is where your medical insurance is enrolled - usually a city.

### 1. For common conditions, start at the community clinic and get referred upward step by step - the hospitalization deductible carries over
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. It does take a trip to the community clinic first. The hard part is resisting the urge to book a specialist directly.
- TL;DR: Go to the community clinic first, then get referred up to a big hospital for admission, and the stretch of money you pay out of pocket is counted only once. Rush straight to a tertiary hospital yourself for admission, then transfer back to the community for rehab, and that stretch may have to be paid twice. Transferred down from a big hospital to continue at the community level, it's not recounted for the same illness in the same period either.
- Benefit: For insured patients referred upward step by step through primary care, the hospitalization deductible at the higher-level hospital can be counted continuously. The deductible is the stretch you pay yourself at each admission before insurance starts reimbursing. Counted continuously means the two hospitals together count it once - you don't pay it over again. For inpatients transferred down from a higher-level hospital to primary care, no new deductible is set within the same disease period
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2026). Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "for insured patients referred step by step through primary medical and health institutions within the coordinating region, the hospitalization deductible at the higher-level hospital may be counted continuously; for inpatients transferred down from a higher-level hospital to a primary medical and health institution, no separate hospitalization deductible shall be set within the same disease period."
- Notes: The deductible is the stretch you pay yourself at each admission before insurance starts reimbursing. Rush straight to a tertiary hospital for admission, then transfer back to primary care for rehab, and that stretch may be paid twice. Going through referral counts it once. Referral rules are set separately by each province, city and medical consortium. Ask at the community clinic before you go.

### 2. Within the same coordinating region (where your insurance is enrolled), the lower the hospital level, the higher the reimbursement share - by about 10 percentage points
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the pull of the big hospital.
- TL;DR: For the same hospitalization bill, the lower the hospital's level, the more gets reimbursed. Localities must spread the tiers apart, roughly ten percentage points per level. Something that could have been handled at the community level, taken to a big hospital, costs you about a tenth more of the same bill out of pocket. New medical-insurance funds also tilt toward primary care.
- Benefit: The document requires localities to spread hospitalization reimbursement levels across institutions of different grades, in principle by about 10 percentage points per level. In other words, for the same hospitalization bill, each level up costs you about a tenth more out of pocket. Newly added annual medical-insurance funds also tilt toward primary care
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2026). Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "appropriately spread, in line with local conditions, insured persons' hospitalization reimbursement levels across medical and health institutions of different grades, in principle widening reimbursement shares level by level by about 10 percentage points within a coordinating region." "Where conditions permit, outpatient reimbursement levels for insured persons across medical and health institutions of different grades may be widened as appropriate in line with the tiered-diagnosis orientation."
- Notes: For the same hospitalization bill, the out-of-pocket difference between primary care and a tertiary hospital can run to several thousand yuan. This entry doesn't say every condition belongs at the community clinic. It says: what can be finished at the primary level, don't carry upward.

### 3. To reach a big hospital, go through primary-care referral or the hospital's referral center - not a ticket scalper
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. It adds one referral procedure.
- TL;DR: Big hospitals must reserve a share of appointment slots and beds specifically for people referred from primary care. Hospitals must also set up referral centers, or designate a fixed department to handle referrals, with full coverage by 2027. Not being able to grab a slot doesn't mean there's no path. A scalper's slot isn't just expensive - it can also land you in the wrong department.
- Benefit: The lead hospital of a compact medical consortium must reserve a certain share of appointment slots and beds for primary care, and promptly receive patients referred from primary care. A compact medical consortium is a group of hospitals and community health institutions bound together to collaborate. In other words, the path up from the community has its own slots and beds - you don't crowd into the same pool as everyone grabbing slots in the lobby. Medical institutions must set up referral centers or designate a fixed department, with full coverage by 2027
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2026). Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "lead hospitals shall reserve a certain share of appointment slots and beds for primary medical and health institutions, and promptly receive patients referred through primary care." "Medical institutions shall strengthen unified management of referral services, setting up referral centers or designating fixed departments to handle patient referrals, achieving full coverage by 2027."
- Notes: The reserved slots are the channel for referred patients. Not grabbing a slot doesn't mean there's no path. The scalper's path isn't just expensive - it can land you in the wrong department. If you can't find the referral center, call the hospital's service line and ask.

### 4. Before traveling across provinces for care, ask locally first - necessity should in principle be assessed by an associate chief physician or above
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One extra question before you set out.
- TL;DR: Whether you need to go elsewhere for care should in principle be assessed by a doctor at the associate-chief-physician level or above at a secondary or tertiary hospital. How much temporary out-of-town care reimburses differs from same-level hospitals at home - the specifics are in your local rules. Asking once before leaving beats arriving out of town and discovering it won't be covered.
- Benefit: The necessity of seeking care across coordinating regions or across provinces should in principle be assessed by personnel at the associate-chief-physician level or above at secondary or tertiary hospitals. Crossing a coordinating region means leaving where your medical insurance is enrolled. In other words, that sentence has to come from a doctor of sufficient rank. For people seeking temporary care across provinces, the reimbursement level keeps a reasonable difference from same-level institutions at home. In other words, the same condition treated out of town pays back less than at home
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2026). Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "in principle, personnel at the associate-chief-physician level or above at secondary and tertiary hospitals shall assess the necessity for patients to seek care across coordinating regions or across provinces." "Further strengthen the administration of direct settlement for out-of-town care; for people seeking temporary care across provinces, keep the reimbursement level reasonably different from that of same-level medical institutions in the insured place."
- Notes: "Temporary care away from home" and "long-term residence elsewhere" are two different registrations, with different reimbursement levels. How to register and which categories of illness settle directly is in chapter 16. Don't travel out first and think about reimbursement afterward.

### 5. Stop using the tertiary hospital as a prescription-refill point - stable chronic disease belongs back at the primary level
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is breaking the "only big hospitals can be trusted" habit.
- TL;DR: Tertiary hospitals will mainly handle acute, critical and difficult cases. Follow-up visits for common conditions and general outpatient care for stable chronic disease will be gradually cut back. The same expert teams' general clinics are being extended into the community. Keep using the big hospital as a refill point, and getting a slot will get harder and harder.
- Benefit: Tertiary hospitals must focus on acute, critical and difficult complex diseases, and gradually reduce general outpatient services such as follow-up visits for common conditions and chronic diseases with clear diagnoses and stable conditions. In other words, those general clinic slots will be drawn in bit by bit as the situation allows. Higher-level hospitals will extend their expert teams' general clinics down to the primary level. In other words, the same expert teams' general clinics will open in the community
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2026). Measures on accelerating the construction of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>: "tertiary hospitals shall focus on acute, critical and difficult complex diseases, strengthen referral, consultation and inpatient services, and gradually reduce general outpatient services such as follow-up visits for common conditions and chronic diseases with clear diagnoses and stable conditions." "Within a compact medical consortium, higher-level hospitals shall open clinics for common and chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease at primary medical and health institutions, providing diagnosis, treatment and health management, and extend their expert teams' general clinics to primary medical and health institutions."
- Notes: This is policy direction, not a suggestion. General clinics at big hospitals will only get harder to book. For stable chronic disease, the primary level can issue 12 weeks of medication at once - see chapter 16.

### 6. After every visit, keep your own copy of the medical record, test reports and imaging
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hospital may charge a copying cost fee by regulation, and the rate must be public. The hard part is remembering to make the copy after every visit.
- TL;DR: Reading and copying your own medical record is a right the law gives you. Outpatient records, admission notes, lab reports, imaging, surgical and anesthesia records, pathology material, and itemized bills can all be copied. The hospital has a dedicated window for it and may only charge a cost fee. Changing hospitals, getting a second read from another doctor, claiming on commercial insurance, or a real dispute - all of them stand on this stack of papers.
- Benefit: The regulation explicitly lists reviewing and copying the medical record as a patient's right. The scope covers the full record: outpatient records, admission notes, lab reports, medical imaging, surgical and anesthesia records, pathology material, medical expenses and more. Changing hospitals, getting a second read from another doctor, claiming on commercial insurance, or a real dispute - all rely on these
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2018). Regulation on the prevention and handling of medical disputes (State Council Order No. 701), Article 16: "the patient has the right to review and copy their outpatient records, admission notes, temperature charts, physician order sheets, laboratory reports (test reports), medical imaging examination materials, special-examination consent forms, surgical consent forms, surgical and anesthesia records, pathology material, nursing records, medical expenses, and all other materials belonging to the medical record as prescribed by the health authority under the State Council." "Where a patient requests copies of medical record materials, the medical institution shall provide copying services and affix a certification mark to the copied materials... it may charge a cost fee, and the rate shall be made public." "Where the patient has died, their close relatives may review and copy the medical record materials in accordance with this Regulation." Article 15: "no organization or individual may tamper with, forge, conceal, destroy or seize medical record materials." <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm>; National Health and Family Planning Commission, National Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine (2013). Provisions on the administration of medical records of medical institutions (2013 edition): medical institutions "shall designate a department or dedicated (or part-time) personnel to accept applications for copying medical record materials," with copying done in the applicant's presence and affixed with a certification mark. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2600084.htm>
- Notes: Copying has a dedicated window - you don't have to beg the doctor. For the list of what to copy, read Article 16 of the Regulation on the prevention and handling of medical disputes directly: first the outpatient records, admission notes, temperature charts, physician order sheets, lab reports and imaging; then the surgical and anesthesia records, pathology material, nursing records and itemized medical expenses. Once you have them, file them in one folder in date order. The most important pieces inside are the discharge summary, the operative record, the pathology report and the key imaging. Keep the history of chronic-disease readings in a single notebook - see chapter 16.

### 7. If you doubt the care given, demand on the spot that the records be sealed - both sides present, a list drawn up, one copy each
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Put the sealing request to the hospital, and wait for both sides to be present and finish the procedure. The hard part is saying the sentence out loud in front of the doctor.
- TL;DR: If you have doubts about the treatment, you can demand that the medical record be sealed. Sealing requires both patient and hospital present, with a list drawn up, signed and stamped, one copy for each side. If the record isn't finished yet, seal the finished part first. If you suspect the problem came from an infusion, a transfusion, an injection or medication, the bag of fluid, the vial and the infusion set get sealed along with it.
- Benefit: The regulation provides that "where a medical dispute requires sealing or unsealing medical record materials, it shall be done in the presence of both the patient and the medical institution." What is sealed may be the original or a copy, kept by the medical institution. If the record is unfinished and must be sealed, the completed part is sealed first, with the rest sealed when completed later. The medical institution shall draw up a list of the sealed records, signed or stamped by both sides, one copy for each. Where an infusion, transfusion, injection or medication is suspected of causing harm, both sides shall jointly seal and unseal the physical items at the scene. Where testing is needed, it shall be commissioned jointly by both sides from a qualified institution. If the two sides cannot commission jointly, the health authority of the county-level government where the institution sits designates one. Where a transfusion is suspected of causing harm and blood must be sealed, the medical institution shall notify the blood station to send personnel to the scene. There is also a hard constraint: "no organization or individual may tamper with, forge, conceal, destroy or seize medical record materials." After sealing, the institution may unseal on its own if the dispute has been resolved, or if 3 years have passed without a further demand for resolution (nationwide, effective October 1, 2018)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2018). Regulation on the prevention and handling of medical disputes (State Council Order No. 701, Articles 15, 24, 25). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm>
- Notes: Sealing and copying are two different things. Copying is what you do routinely - see entry 6 (keep your own copy of records and imaging). Sealing fixes the evidence in place when a dispute arises, guarding against after-the-fact additions and edits. If you suspect the problem came from an infusion, transfusion or medication, seal the bag of fluid, the vial and the infusion set along with the records - don't let them be taken away and thrown out. Medical institutions must publish, in a prominent place, the routes, procedures and contact details for resolving medical disputes, and set up a unified complaints department. If something goes wrong, go to that department first - don't argue it out with the doctor on duty.

### 8. For acute and severe illness or injury, go straight to the emergency triage desk - don't queue at the registration window
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=time -->
- Cost: Free. A few extra steps at the entrance to find the triage desk. And state the most life-threatening symptom in one sentence.
- TL;DR: The emergency room doesn't run first-come-first-served - it queues in four grades by severity. Grading happens at the triage desk by the emergency entrance, not at the registration window. Skip triage and you have no grade in the system; however badly you're hurt, you inch along with the queue. On arrival, state the most dangerous thing first: heavy bleeding, can't breathe, confused consciousness, something lodged in the body, crushing chest pain.
- Benefit: The National Health Commission's 2024 emergency-medicine quality-control indicators require emergency triage to "divide patients into four grades by the severity of their condition." "Grade I is the critical patient, who must receive treatment immediately." "Grade II is the severe patient, where assessment and treatment often proceed simultaneously." "Grade III is the acute patient, who must be diagnosed and treated within a short time." "Grade IV is the non-acute or sub-acute patient, seen in order in the general treatment area." Pre-examination triage means a designated person assesses the severity of your condition when you enter emergency and sets the order in which people are seen. Beijing's implementation standard gives the response requirements per grade. Grade 1: "assess and treat immediately; arrange for the patient to enter the resuscitation or rescue room." Grade 2: "monitor vital signs immediately, treat within 10 minutes; arrange for the patient to enter the rescue room." Grade 3: "seen ahead of grade 4 patients, waiting in the general treatment area; if the wait exceeds 30 minutes, reassess." Grade 4: "seen in order; unless the condition changes, the wait is long; if the wait exceeds 4 hours, reassessment is possible." Triage must "uphold the principle of priority for acute and critical conditions," and a triage area should sit prominently at the emergency entrance (the four-grade standard is national; the response times are the Beijing standard)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the National Health Commission (2024). Notice on issuing medical quality-control indicators for 6 specialties including emergency medicine (2024 edition) (Guoweiban Yizheng Letter [2024] No. 150), Annex 1, indicator 4 "execution rate of emergency graded triage." <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6951260.htm>; Beijing Municipal Health Commission (2019). Beijing work plan for strengthening emergency pre-examination triage and grading, with annex "Beijing hospital emergency pre-examination triage grading standard (trial)": "uphold the principle of priority for acute and critical conditions. By the severity of patients' conditions, following the principle of severe before mild and rapidly changing before relatively stable, reasonably arrange the order in which patients are seen." "A triage area shall be set up in a prominent position at the emergency department entrance." <http://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/ylws/201912/t20191216_1242338.html>
- Notes: The four grades are uniform nationwide. How quickly each grade gets handled is set by each province and city; the Beijing document is a version that can be checked word for word. Grading looks at indicators like heart rate, blood pressure and blood oxygen, plus symptoms - not at how much pain you feel you're in. The hard indicators for grade 1 include these. One: heart rate above 180 or below 40 beats per minute. Two: systolic pressure below 70 mmHg - systolic is the higher of the two blood-pressure numbers. Three: blood oxygen below 80%. Four: shock, confirmed myocardial infarction, acute disturbance of consciousness. If you think triage graded you too low, add a clear statement of the most dangerous symptom. If the condition changes while waiting, go back to the triage desk at once for a fresh assessment. When things are truly critical, calling 120 is faster than going yourself - the pre-hospital doctors pre-grade by the same standard and notify the hospital ahead to prepare (see chapter 13).

### 9. No money, no ID, can't say who you are - the emergency room must still treat you first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: The state runs an emergency disease relief fund that pays the emergency-care costs of two kinds of people: those whose identity can't be established, and those whose identity is clear but who can't pay. Medical institutions may not refuse, dodge or delay treatment for any reason. Emergency centers may not refuse or delay dispatching an ambulance over cost either. So don't tough it out at home because you're short of cash, and don't hold back from calling an ambulance for fear of the bill. The fund only covers the emergency stretch; the hospitalization after that still runs through medical insurance and medical assistance.
- Benefit: The emergency disease relief system covers "patients within China who suffer acute, severe or critical illness or injury, need emergency care, and whose identity is unclear or who cannot pay the corresponding costs." The fund pays two kinds of costs: "emergency-care costs incurred by patients whose identity cannot be established," and "emergency-care costs owed by patients whose identity is clear but who cannot pay." The document also states plainly that "medical institutions of all levels and kinds and their staff must treat acute, severe and critical patients promptly and effectively, and may not refuse, dodge or delay treatment for any reason." The pre-hospital stretch has rules too: the national pre-hospital emergency call number is "120." Emergency centers (stations) "may not refuse, dodge or delay pre-hospital emergency medical services for reasons of dispatch," and likewise "may not refuse or delay pre-hospital emergency medical services over cost." Transport follows the principle of "nearest, most urgent, meeting professional needs, and accommodating the patient's wishes" (nationwide, since 2013)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2013). Guiding opinion on establishing the emergency disease relief system (Guobanfa [2013] No. 15). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2013-03/01/content_6069.htm>; National Health and Family Planning Commission (2014). Measures for the administration of pre-hospital emergency medical care (Commission Order No. 3, Articles 13, 22, 23, 25). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2580977.htm>
- Notes: The fund only backs the "emergency-care costs" stretch. And it's treat first, with the medical institution applying to the fund afterward - not you collecting cash at a window. If you have medical insurance, it goes through insurance first as usual. The application process and caps differ by province. In the transport principle, "nearest, most urgent" ranks ahead of "patient's wishes," so the ambulance may not take you to the hospital you name - in critical cases it's the nearest one that can handle it. If the out-of-pocket part afterward is still too heavy, use medical assistance - see entry 10 of chapter 7 (medical assistance).

### 10. Wait until treatment is finished before getting a disability assessment - done too early, the grade comes out lower
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=money -->
- Cost: The assessment fee runs from a few hundred to two thousand yuan, usually advanced by the side applying. You must wait for treatment to conclude, or for the clinical effect to stabilize - usually three to six months after the injury, sometimes longer. The hard part is resisting the urge to settle it quickly before you've fully healed.
- TL;DR: How much compensation you get depends mostly on the grade assessed, and the grade depends on how much function remains after treatment. The standard states plainly that assessment happens after treatment concludes or the clinical treatment effect stabilizes. Get assessed while the bone hasn't healed and the plate hasn't come out, and you're graded on how you are that day - pressing your own grade down by hand. An ordinary fracture that heals without affecting function doesn't grade at all.
- Benefit: The "Grading of disability degree in human injury" prescribes that the timing of assessment "shall be after the primary injury and its definitively related complications have concluded treatment, or the clinical treatment effect has stabilized." The standard "divides the degree of disability from human injury into 10 grades, from grade 1 (100% disability rate) to grade 10 (10% disability rate), with the disability rate differing by 10% per grade." The judgment rests on "the structural destruction of human tissues and organs, the functional impairment, and the degree of dependence on medical care and nursing, with appropriate consideration of the effects of disability on social interaction and psychology." The grade-10 clauses touching on fractures include these. One: "compression fracture of one vertebral body (compression reaching 1/3) or comminuted fracture." Two: "two or more pelvic fractures, or comminuted fracture, healing with deformity." Three: "loss of 25% or more of function in any major limb joint (excluding the ankle)." Disabilities at the same site and of the same nature are not counted twice. Where two or more sites are disabled, the grade of each shall be stated separately (nationwide, effective January 1, 2017)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of State Security, Ministry of Justice (2016). Announcement on issuing the "Grading of disability degree in human injury" (issued April 18, 2016, effective January 1, 2017), standard clauses 4.2, 4.4, 4.5, 5.10.6, 6.2. <https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/zwxxgk/fdzdgknr/fdzdgknrtzwj/201908/P020210316701681025917.pdf>
- Notes: The three grading systems don't interchange - don't mix them up. Being assaulted, traffic accidents and other personal injuries go through the judicial-authentication "Grading of disability degree in human injury," which feeds the disability compensation calculation (see chapter 9). Work injury goes through the work-capacity assessment, a different set of ten disability grades governing work-injury benefits (see chapter 19). To receive policy benefits for people with disabilities, you separately apply for a disability certificate (see entry 11, the disability certificate). Before the assessment, have the medical record, operative records and follow-up imaging ready (see entry 6: keep your own copy of records and imaging); missing imaging is the most common reason a grade gets pressed down. When this injury mixes with a pre-existing condition, the assessment opinion must also state how large a share of the disability outcome this injury accounts for. That line directly moves the compensation amount.

### 11. If treatment leaves you with a lasting functional impairment, apply for a disability certificate at the county-level disabled persons' federation where your household is registered
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: The certificate itself carries no fee. The evaluation fee and photo fee are in principle yours to bear, with waivers available for hardship. Bring your ID card, household register and 3 two-inch white-background photos to the county-level disabled persons' federation where your household is registered. After a passing evaluation, there's a 5-working-day public notice in the village (community), and the federation finishes its review within 10 working days. The hard part is the trips and the waiting.
- TL;DR: The disability certificate is the threshold for disability benefits, not a proof of injury. It has seven categories - vision, hearing, speech, physical, intellectual, mental, and multiple - each graded one through four. The process is application, acceptance, evaluation at a designated institution, public notice, review and approval, issuance; it's valid ten years and renewed free. If the evaluation doesn't pass, it doesn't pass - this isn't something connections can pull off.
- Benefit: The disability certificate "is the lawful credential recognizing a person with a disability and their category and grade of disability, and an important basis for persons with disabilities to enjoy state and local government preferential policies by law." The evaluation standard is the national standard "Classification and grading of disabilities of persons with disabilities" (GB/T 26341-2010). Issuance follows voluntary application and territorial administration. All seven categories - vision, hearing, speech, physical, intellectual, mental and multiple - may apply, with grades one through four. The county-level federation handles application acceptance and issuance, based on the evaluation conclusion of a hospital or professional institution designated at the provincial level. Where the evaluation conclusion meets the standard, a 5-working-day public notice runs in the village (community), and the county-level federation "completes its review within 10 working days." "No fee is charged for issuing a disability certificate." Evaluation and photo costs "are in principle borne by the applicant; where conditions permit, local finance may subsidize them, and for applicants in special hardship the relevant departments shall be coordinated to reduce or waive them." The certificate is valid ten years and renewed free at expiry. Where the category or grade of disability changes, re-evaluation may be requested. Objecting to an evaluation conclusion, you may apply for re-evaluation at the prefecture-level federation within 10 working days. Objecting still, the provincial disability evaluation expert committee makes the final conclusion (nationwide, effective January 1, 2018)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: China Disabled Persons' Federation, National Health and Family Planning Commission (2017). Measures for the administration of disability certificates of the People's Republic of China (issued May 27, 2017, effective January 1, 2018; Articles 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 17, 19, 22, 25). <https://www.bdpf.org.cn/cms68/web1459/subject/n1/n1459/n1508/n1509/n1512/c68465/content.html>
- Notes: Count the ledger before deciding whether to apply. The certificate itself pays nothing directly. What it connects to is the two subsidies for persons with disabilities: a living subsidy for persons with disabilities in subsistence-allowance households, and a care subsidy for those at grades one and two needing long-term care - see entry 8 of chapter 7 (the two subsidies). The certificate also connects to local rehabilitation, assistive-device, tax, employment and transport preferences. These standards differ enormously by province - check the local federation's website first, then decide whether the trip is worth it. The certificate, the judicial-authentication disability grade, and the work-injury work-capacity assessment are three separate systems, none replacing another (see entry 10: wait until treatment is finished before the disability assessment). The certificate is for the holder only and may not be lent. Someone whose condition no longer meets the standard has the certificate cancelled, and may not reapply for one year after cancellation. Multiple disabilities are graded by the most severe category.

### 12. To thank the doctor who saved you, use a thank-you letter, a commendation banner and the satisfaction survey - not a red envelope: the code bans valuables, not gratitude
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. Writing a thank-you letter, or filling in the satisfaction platform once, takes a dozen minutes.
- TL;DR: Money slipped in beforehand to buy "priority" buys nothing: appointment slots, beds, scarce drugs and consumables, and the scheduling of tests and surgery are all expressly forbidden from being traded for favors. Wanting to thank the person who saved your life afterward - thank them, but change the form: taking your cigarettes, liquor or red envelope puts the other person in a rule-breaking position. Three forms of thanks actually land on them: fill in the satisfaction survey carefully, write a thank-you letter naming them or send a commendation banner, and come back for follow-up as ordered.
- Benefit: Item 7 of the "Nine codes of clean practice for medical institution staff" (Guowei Yifa [2021] No. 41): "maintain the order of diagnosis and treatment, and do not undermine fairness in access to care. Uphold the principle of equality and jointly build a fair care environment. It is strictly forbidden to use medical resources such as appointment slots, beds and scarce drugs and consumables, or treatment arrangements such as tests and surgery, to receive favors or enrich oneself at public expense." Item 8: "jointly build harmonious relations, and do not accept 'red envelopes' from the patient side. Keep medical ethics and strict self-discipline." The same item states plainly: "it is strictly forbidden to solicit or accept money or valuables from patients or their relatives and friends, including gifts, cash, shopping cards, negotiable securities, equity and other financial products." Meals and entertainment paid by the patient side are the same: "it is strictly forbidden to attend banquets or travel, fitness or entertainment activities arranged, organized or paid for by them." The document also requires medical institutions to carry primary responsibility, run inspections and "resolutely investigate and punish violations." The satisfaction survey is written into the performance assessment of tertiary public hospitals. The basis is the "Opinion of the General Office of the State Council on strengthening performance assessment of tertiary public hospitals" (Guobanfa [2019] No. 4). The document says: "the performance assessment indicator system for tertiary public hospitals consists of indicators in 4 areas: medical quality, operational efficiency, sustainable development and satisfaction evaluation." The system has a unified national platform: "the state establishes a public-hospital satisfaction management system..." Hospitals must also handle complaints. The basis is Article 2 of the "Measures for the administration of complaints at medical institutions" (National Health Commission Order No. 3, 2019). Complaint administration covers patients "raising opinions, suggestions or complaint requests" about medical service conduct, medical management, medical quality and safety. The hospital is responsible for investigating, handling and responding. "Medical institutions at the secondary level and above shall set up a doctor-patient relations office or designate a department (hereinafter the complaints department) to take unified charge of complaint administration." The complaints department's duties include "uniformly accepting complaints, investigating and verifying complaint matters, proposing handling opinions, and replying to patients promptly." Medical institutions "shall encourage staff to proactively collect patients' opinions and suggestions on medical services, medical quality and safety" (nationwide)
