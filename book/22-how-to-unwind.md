# 22. How to unwind: entertainment venues and stress relief

This chapter comes in two halves. The first half covers entertainment venues: which spending is a trap, and where the fire exits are. Its lens is money and personal freedom. The second half covers how to relieve stress, with a lens of energy and all-cause mortality. The numbers from the two halves are not converted against each other. Chapter 3 already covered what to do first when your mood is low; what follows here is more specific.

### 1. Before settling into a KTV, bar or escape room, glance at where the fire exits are; if they're locked or blocked, go somewhere else
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Look up for 10 seconds on the way in.
- TL;DR: By regulation, while an entertainment venue is open, evacuation routes and fire exits must be kept clear. No blocking them shut, no locking them, no placing things in the way, and no covering the indicator signs. Internet cafes likewise may not seal doors or windows. A venue that fails this is breaking the law, and you can report it. A private room in a basement, a single exit, a corridor piled with clutter - go somewhere else.
- Benefit: This is the operator's statutory duty. Failing it is illegal, and you can report it. The regulation's original text: "during business hours, an entertainment venue shall keep evacuation routes and fire exits clear; it shall not block or lock evacuation routes and fire exits, and shall not place barriers such as grilles that impede evacuation in evacuation routes and fire exits." The regulation also requires that "clear indicator signs be placed at evacuation routes and fire exits, and that indicator signs not be covered or obscured." Internet cafes are the same - required to "not block or lock doors, windows, evacuation routes and fire exits during business hours," with "open-flame lighting and smoking prohibited"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2006). Regulation on the administration of entertainment venues (State Council Order No. 458, Articles 20, 21). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>; State Council (2002). Regulation on the administration of internet access service establishments (State Council Order No. 363, Article 21). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>
- Notes: If any one of these holds, go elsewhere: the room is in a basement, there is only one exit, the corridor is piled with clutter, the exit sign lights are dark. When this kind of place catches fire, the smoke is heavy, the corridors narrow, the crowds dense - and most people have been drinking. The time you have to get out is counted in seconds. By regulation, the report hotline should be written on the warning signs inside the venue

### 2. Ask for the price list before ordering; entertainment venues must display prices openly - never accept "play first, settle later"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=mid lens=money -->
- Cost: Free. One ask for the list. The hard part is asking about prices in front of people - it feels embarrassing.
- TL;DR: By regulation, an entertainment venue must price its services and goods openly, and must proactively show you the price list. The so-called "sky-high liquor bill" almost always happens where nobody saw a price list and the bill was settled on a verbal quote after the fact. Ask for the list when you enter the room and photograph it. After ordering, ask for a confirmation list. If the bill is disputed, call 12315 on the spot.
- Benefit: The regulation's original text: "an entertainment venue providing entertainment services and selling goods shall mark prices clearly and show consumers the price list; it shall not force or deceive consumers into accepting services or buying goods." Violations of price laws and regulations are penalized by the relevant departments. The so-called "sky-high liquor bill" disputes almost all happen in this setting: no price list shown, the bill settled afterward on a verbal quote
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2006). Regulation on the administration of entertainment venues (State Council Order No. 458, Articles 29, 53). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>
- Notes: How to do it: ask for a price list when you enter the room - paper or electronic, either works - and photograph it. After ordering, ask for a confirmed list. Anything the server recommends, a "special mix" or a "fruit plate," ask the price before ordering. If the bill is disputed, call 12315 on the spot. Don't pay first and seek justice afterward - once you've paid, proving your case is much harder

### 3. If someone passes "something" around the venue, leave at once; hosting and providing are not "doing a friend a favor"
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Stand up and walk out. The hard part is leaving the table in front of everyone.
- TL;DR: Entertainment venues may not sell drugs, provide drugs, or let people use drugs inside - violations bring 3 to 6 months of suspended business for rectification, and serious cases lose the license. The personal side is heavier: using drugs yourself brings a public-security penalty. Letting someone "have a quick use" in a room you booked or at your place is the crime of harboring others for drug use. If someone pulls out an unknown powder, pill or vape cartridge and invites you to try, leave on the spot.
- Benefit: The regulation expressly bans entertainment venues from "trafficking or providing drugs, or organizing, coercing, instigating, inducing, deceiving or harboring others in ingesting or injecting drugs." Staff likewise may not use drugs or create conditions for customers to do any of the above. Violations bring confiscation of illegal gains and property by the public-security authorities, plus 3 to 6 months of suspended business for rectification. Serious cases lose the entertainment-business license. The regulation also requires venues to hang anti-drug, anti-gambling and anti-prostitution warning signs, with the report hotline noted, in prominent spots in the lobby and private rooms
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2006). Regulation on the administration of entertainment venues (State Council Order No. 458, Articles 14, 30, 42). <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>
- Notes: The moment someone brings out an unknown powder, pill or vape cartridge and invites you to "give it a try," leave on the spot - don't stay to watch the fun. The risk on the personal side is heavier than on the venue. Using drugs yourself is itself a public-security violation. "Letting a friend have a quick use here" in a room you booked or at your own place constitutes harboring others for drug use - a criminal offense

### 4. Don't take candy or snacks from strangers, don't drink anything that left your sight, don't take a vape cartridge someone hands you
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Whatever gets handed to you, decline it. If your cup left your sight, get a fresh one.
- TL;DR: Today's drugs don't look like drugs. A "head-high" e-cigarette laced with synthetic cannabinoids can be tinted and flavored to look just like an ordinary vape, passed around for people to try in bars, KTVs and hotels. Tricking someone into using brings 10 to 15 days of detention and a 1,000-to-5,000-yuan fine. The person tricked into using also faces 10 to 15 days of detention. A drink that left your sight gets replaced.
- Benefit: Today's drugs don't look like drugs. Synthetic cannabinoids have been controlled as an entire class since July 1, 2021 - the whole category is treated as drugs. People mix them into vape oil to make "head-high" e-cigarettes. In cases reported by the Supreme People's Procuratorate, investigators describe this vape oil as something that "can be made into all colors and flavors by adding food coloring," "basically identical in appearance to an ordinary e-cigarette." Sellers pitch them to gathered friends in bars, KTVs and hotels, inducing others to use. In the report, nearly forty percent of users in the cases handled locally that year were minors. Etomidate was added to the Class II psychotropic substances catalog on October 1, 2023. Tricking someone into using is its own tier of offense: inducing, instigating, deceiving or coercing another into ingesting or injecting drugs brings 10 to 15 days of detention plus a 1,000-to-5,000-yuan fine. Constituting a crime, it carries up to 3 years; serious cases 3 to 7 years; coercion 3 to 10 years; and targeting minors is punished more heavily. The consequences land on the person deceived too: ingesting or injecting drugs brings 10 to 15 days of detention plus a fine of up to 3,000 yuan; lighter cases up to 5 days of detention or a fine of up to 1,000 yuan. The person may also be ordered to stay out of entertainment venues for 6 months to a year. Addiction brings an order for 3 years of community-based rehabilitation. Refusing community rehabilitation, or using again during it, brings 2 years of compulsory isolated rehabilitation, extendable by up to 1 more year (nationwide; the revised public-security administration punishment law applies from January 2026)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Procuratorate (2021). Wujiang District, Suzhou, Jiangsu: prosecutors reveal the new drugs wrapped in trendy packaging. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>; National Medical Products Administration, Ministry of Public Security, National Health Commission (2023). Announcement on adjusting the catalogs of narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances (No. 120 of 2023). <https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/qtgzwj/20230911/3b612c94e35b4b63abba314c972a2593.html> (reposted by the Shanghai Medical Products Administration; the original nmpa.gov.cn page returned a 412 error from this machine); Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public security administration punishment law (2025 revision, Articles 84, 85). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law (Article 353). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Anti-drug law (Articles 33, 38, 47). <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2520706.html> (reposted by the Guangdong Provincial People's Government)
- Notes: This entry works beyond entertainment venues. School gates, friends' gatherings, long-distance buses - same logic. Whether what's offered is candy, a drink or a vape cartridge, the answer is the same. Being drugged is often followed by robbery, sexual assault and extortion - there is no public official statistic for this part; it's a judgment from experience. Replacing a drink that left your sight is far less trouble than pursuing the matter afterward. What to do when someone brings out unknown powders, pills or cartridges at a venue is in entry 3 of this chapter. Carrying things for others is in entry 29 of chapter 8.

### 5. At an internet cafe, use your own ID card - don't lend yours and don't use someone else's
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=freedom -->
- Cost: Free.
- TL;DR: By regulation, an internet cafe must check and register each customer's ID card, record their usage information, and keep the records at least 60 days. The public-security and culture authorities can pull them at any time. During this period the cafe may not alter or delete them. Whose ID you surf on - that's whose name everything done on that machine in that time lands under.
- Benefit: The regulation's original text: "an internet access service establishment shall check and register the ID cards or other valid documents of consumers using its internet access, and record the relevant usage information. The registered content and record backups shall be kept for no less than 60 days." When the culture authorities or public-security organs query them by law, the cafe must provide them. Within the retention period they may not be modified or deleted. In other words, whose ID you surf on - that's whose name everything done on that machine in that time lands under
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2002). Regulation on the administration of internet access service establishments (State Council Order No. 363, Article 23). <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>
- Notes: This is the same red line as "don't lend your ID card" in chapter 9, just in a different setting. Internet cafes may not admit minors, and a no-minors sign must hang prominently at the entrance. Song-and-dance entertainment venues likewise don't admit minors. Helping a minor "flash a card to get into an internet cafe" gets the cafe fined - but the record stays on your document

### 6. For murder-mystery games and escape rooms, skip the ones in residential buildings or below basement level one
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Ask which floor the address is on before booking.
- TL;DR: A 2022 notice from five ministries states plainly that script-entertainment venues may not operate inside residential buildings, nor below basement level one. These spaces are dark, chopped up by partitions, full of flammable props - and the doors often only open once you solve the puzzle. In a fire, they're far harder to escape than an ordinary shop. Check the address and floor on the review page before booking; if it doesn't comply, pick another one.
- Benefit: The 2022 special notice from five ministries is quite specific: "script-entertainment business premises shall not be located inside residential buildings, or below basement level one of a building (basement level one not included)." The notice also requires venues to implement the work-safety and fire-protection laws, "routinely knowing, checking and correcting their own fire risks, and improving their ability to organize evacuation and escape in emergencies and to fight initial fires." The same notice also requires age-appropriateness notices for the scripts. Where the content is unsuitable for minors, minors may not be admitted. Outside statutory holidays, weekends and winter and summer vacations, script-entertainment activities may not be offered to minors at all
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, Ministry of Emergency Management, State Administration for Market Regulation (2022). Notice on strengthening the administration of script-entertainment business premises. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-06/27/content_5698021.htm>
- Notes: What defines these places: dark, chopped up by partitions, flammable props, and doors that often open only after you "solve the puzzle." In a fire, they're harder to escape than an ordinary shop. Before booking, the review page shows you the floor - and whether it's a residential building. If it doesn't comply, go straight to another one
### 7. When your mood is bad, go walk or run - the antidepressant effect size scales with intensity
<!-- tags: money=0 time=mid will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. About half an hour each time. The hard part is that a bad mood is exactly when you least want to move - you have to push yourself.
- TL;DR: Pool 218 randomized trials covering over 14,000 people with major depression. The biggest improvement came from brisk walking or jogging. Next came yoga, strength training, mixed aerobics and tai chi/qigong, all fairly close. The higher the prescribed intensity, the better the effect; strength training and yoga were the easiest to stick with. The authors themselves rate the evidence quality as low; the usable direction is that moving beats not moving - pick the kind you can keep up.
- Benefit: This pools 218 randomized controlled trials into one calculation, covering 495 groups and 14,170 participants meeting the clinical criteria for major depression. The method lets the different exercises be compared against each other (a Bayesian network meta-analysis). The comparison was against usual care and placebo pills (active controls). Effect size is given as Hedges' g - the further negative the number, the more symptoms improved. Brisk walking or jogging −0.62 (95% credible interval −0.80 to −0.45, meaning the true value roughly falls within this span). Yoga −0.55 (−0.73 to −0.36). Strength training −0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29). Mixed aerobics −0.43 (−0.61 to −0.24). Tai chi or qigong −0.42 (−0.65 to −0.21). The original text says "the effects of exercise were proportional to the intensity prescribed" - the higher the intensity, the better the effect. Strength training and yoga had the best acceptability, meaning they were the easiest to stick with
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Noetel M, Sanders T, Gallardo-Gómez D, et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ, 384, e075847. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>
- Notes: Disputed. The authors themselves note that of the 218 studies, only 1 was run cleanly enough to resist being skewed by its design. Under the standard scoring method, brisk walking and jogging rate only "low" confidence, the rest "very low." Exercise can't be blinded from participants either - people know they're exercising and so tend to feel better, and that expectation can't be stripped out. The direction of the conclusion is still usable: moving beats not moving, don't set the intensity too low, pick what you can stick with. This entry expands chapter 3's "when your mood is low, get moving first" - it does not replace proper treatment after a diagnosis

### 8. When anxiety starts interfering with life, a mindfulness-based stress reduction course works no worse than a common anti-anxiety drug
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: The course costs some money. It runs 8 weeks, one class a week, plus daily practice at home. The hard part is practicing every day for 8 straight weeks.
- TL;DR: 276 adults with diagnosed anxiety disorders were randomized into two groups. One took an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course; the other took escitalopram. At the end, symptom improvement was nearly identical. The difference was side effects: nearly eighty percent of the drug group had at least one adverse reaction, and 10 dropped out because of it. In the course group it was about fifteen percent, with no dropouts. What was tested is the standardized 8-week course - not just any meditation app.
- Benefit: The trial was run at 3 urban academic medical centers in the United States, designed to see whether the course would be worse than the drug (a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial). 276 adults with diagnosed anxiety disorders were randomized 1:1 - split in half. One group took the 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course. The other took escitalopram (flexible dose, 10 to 20 mg). 208 completed the course. At the end, measured by clinician-rated anxiety severity (the Clinical Global Impression of Severity scale), the mindfulness group fell 1.35 and the drug group fell 1.43. The difference between groups was −0.07 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.23 - the true gap roughly falls within this span; P=0.65, meaning a gap this size is indistinguishable from no gap). The lower end of that span did not cross the pre-set "counts as worse" line (the non-inferiority margin of −0.495). The side-effect difference was large: 78.6% of the drug group had at least one trial-related adverse reaction, and 10 people dropped out because of it. In the mindfulness group, 15.4% - and nobody dropped out over it
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023). Mindfulness-based stress reduction vs escitalopram for the treatment of adults with anxiety disorders: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13-21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>
- Notes: The trial used the standardized 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course (MBSR), not just any meditation app - the two are not interchangeable. This entry targets people who already meet the diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder; if you should see a doctor, see one. Its practical use: if concern about drug side effects has kept you from treating it at all, there is another option with comparable strength of evidence

### 9. To calm down in the moment, use 5 minutes of "cyclic sighing": two-part inhale, long exhale
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Five minutes a day.
- TL;DR: In a controlled trial of 5 minutes a day for a month, three breathing exercises each improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation of the same length. The best was "cyclic sighing," with its extended exhale. The method: breathe in through the nose, top up with a second small sip of air to fill the lungs, then slowly let it all out through the mouth. There is only this one trial, and the results are self-reported - but the cost is zero.
- Benefit: This is an at-home, randomized controlled trial comparing three 5-minute daily breathing exercises against mindfulness meditation of the same length, for 1 month. The three breathing methods: cyclic sighing (emphasizing the extended exhale), box breathing (equal-length inhale, hold, exhale), and cyclic hyperventilation with retention (long inhale, short exhale). Pooling each person's repeated measurements (a mixed-effects model), the breathing exercises all beat mindfulness meditation - especially the exhale-focused cyclic sighing. It was better both at mood improvement (P<0.05, meaning a difference this size doesn't look like chance) and at lowering breathing rate (P<0.05)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>
- Notes: The method: breathe in through the nose, briefly top up with a second small sip of air to fill the lungs, then let the air out slowly and long through the mouth; repeat for 5 minutes. There is only this one trial, done at home, with outcomes reported by the participants themselves in questionnaires, and the sample is small. The effect is "slightly better than meditation," not "problem solved." But the cost is zero, it takes 5 minutes, and you can do it anywhere - good value

### 10. Treat "seeing people regularly" as a health expense - don't wait for a bad mood to reach out
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Some money, plus the time the meetups take. The hard part is reaching out to people when your mood is fine.
- TL;DR: 70 studies, 3.4 million people, followed an average of 7 years: socially isolated people were about thirty percent more likely to die in the same period than people who weren't isolated. Feeling lonely, about a quarter more. Living alone, about thirty percent more. All three are in the same league, and the effect is stronger in people under 65. These are all observational data, and the reverse reading - "people whose health was already poor are more likely to live alone" - can't be stripped out.
- Benefit: This pools 70 studies that recorded people's situations first and then followed them forward (a meta-analysis), 3.4 million participants in all, followed an average of 7 years. Social isolation, loneliness and living alone were all associated with higher mortality. After stripping out confounders like age and physical condition, the weighted average odds ratios were: social isolation 1.29, loneliness 1.26, living alone 1.32. In other words, the probability of dying in the same period was about thirty percent higher, about a quarter higher, and about thirty percent higher respectively. The three effect sizes are similar, and larger in people under 65 than in the elderly
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227-237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>
- Notes: Disputed. These are associations observed by following groups of people, not randomized controlled trials, and cause and effect can't be separated. People whose health is already poor are more likely to live alone and socialize less. The authors treat social relationships as a health factor in the same league as smoking and obesity, but no randomized trial proves that "more socializing lowers mortality." This doesn't conflict with chapter 3's "cut the relationships that drain you": that one says cut the relationships that cost you, this one says keep the few that restore you

### 11. If you live near green space, use it - walking, strolling, just sitting all count
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=small lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Done in passing.
- TL;DR: 9 studies, 7 countries, 8.32 million people: for every 0.1 increase in the vegetation index within 500 meters of home, the probability of dying in the same period was about 4% lower. What was measured is the greenness around the home, not how often you visit a park. Neighborhoods with good greenery also tend to house wealthier people and better air. So don't read this as "going to the park extends your life" - the usable part is counting nearby green space when choosing where to live.
- Benefit: This pools 9 studies that followed groups of people for years (a meta-analysis of cohort studies), covering 7 countries and 8.32 million people. It looked at surrounding greenness within 500 meters of home, measured by the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI, a number that converts ground vegetation into a score). For each 0.1 increase in the index, the pooled hazard ratio for all-cause mortality was 0.96 (95% CI 0.94-0.97). That is, the probability of dying in the same period was about 4% lower, with the true value roughly falling between 3% and 6% lower. Seven of the 9 studies found lower mortality with more greenery, and the difference doesn't look like chance; the other 2 found no association
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Rojas-Rueda D, Nieuwenhuijsen MJ, Gascon M, Perez-Leon D, Mudu P (2019). Green spaces and mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. The Lancet Planetary Health, 3(11), e469-e477. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196(19)30215-3>
- Notes: Disputed. The individual studies' results differ widely (I²=95% - the bigger this number, the more the studies disagree with each other). And it measures the greenness around where you live, not "how often you go to the park." People who live in greener places also tend to be wealthier, breathe better air and exercise more - which of these is doing the work can't be separated. Don't read it as "going to the park extends your life"; the usable part is counting green space when choosing where to live, at zero cost. The normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) converts ground vegetation into a number: bare ground and concrete sit near 0, dense vegetation near 1. So "0.1" is roughly the difference between a bare block and one with rows of street trees
