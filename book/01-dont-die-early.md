# 1. Don't Die Early

This chapter covers three things. First, accidental and injury deaths - car crashes, fires, poisoning. Second, the handful of vaccines and screenings with the hardest evidence. Third, which signals from your body mean you should get checked. The numbers in the Benefit field come from randomized trials, studies that follow large groups of people for years, or official statistics. The Cost field is the author's rough estimate at market prices, only for comparing entries with each other - don't quote it.

### 1. Wear a seat belt, in the front and the back seat
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Two extra seconds each time you get in the car.
- TL;DR: Buckling up in the front seat cuts your chance of being killed in a crash by about half; in the front of a light truck, by about 60%. Of the people who died inside vehicles in the US in 2022, half of those whose belt status is known were unbuckled.
- Benefit: The US traffic-safety agency (NHTSA) estimates that seat belts cut the risk of fatal injury for front-seat car occupants by 45%, and for front-seat light-truck occupants by 60%. Of the people who died inside passenger vehicles (cars meant to carry people) in the US in 2022, 50% of those whose belt status is known were unbuckled. The World Health Organization (WHO) puts it this way: seat belts reduce the risk of death for vehicle occupants by up to 50%. WHO estimates that 248,099 people died in road traffic accidents in China in 2021, or 17.4 deaths per 100,000 people
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; WHO GHO RS_196/RS_198 (China, 2021). <https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/RS_196?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20%27CHN%27>
- Notes: Buckle up in the back seat too. In the same report, 60% of those killed in the second row were unbuckled. The 45% and 60% figures are estimates the US traffic-safety agency (NHTSA) computed from its crash database (citing Kahane 2015), not a randomized trial. WHO's China death toll is an estimate, several times higher than the number registered by the traffic police; the two count different scopes, and only WHO's figure is used here.

### 2. Wear a properly fastened helmet on motorcycles and e-bikes
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A helmet costs 100 to 300 yuan. Five seconds to put on each time.
- TL;DR: Wearing a helmet with the chin strap fastened lowers a motorcycle rider's chance of dying in a crash by about 40%, and of head injury by about 70%. The strap must be snug - a helmet dangling loosely on your head doesn't count as worn.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled multiple studies. Motorcycle riders wearing helmets had a 42% lower risk of death; the original value is OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.68. Head injuries were down 69% (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.38)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Liu BC et al. (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3>
- Notes: Fasten the chin strap; a helmet hanging on the handlebar doesn't count. The data above comes from motorcycles. E-bikes are an extrapolation - there is no direct study, but what happens to a head on impact is the same thing.

### 3. Install smoke alarms; if you heat with coal or gas indoors in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A smoke alarm costs 30 to 100 yuan. A carbon monoxide alarm costs 50 to 150 yuan. Once installed, change the battery once a year.
- TL;DR: A working smoke alarm cuts the chance of dying in a home fire by about 60%. Four in ten people are asleep when a fire starts, and your nose alone won't wake you. If you burn coal or use gas heating indoors in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm. In 2018, 11.5 thousand people in China died of carbon monoxide poisoning, and in the three winter months about 70% of those deaths happened at home.
- Benefit: A study in North Carolina looked back at households that did and didn't suffer fatal residential fires. Having one working smoke alarm cut the risk of death by about 60%; the original value is OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.83. In the US from 2018 to 2020, of fatal fires in occupied homes, 24% had no smoke alarm and 41% started while people were asleep. China reported 11,523 carbon monoxide poisoning deaths in 2018; of these deaths, 72.59% in December, 67.42% in January and 66.48% in February occurred at home
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Marshall SW et al. (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018-2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf> ; You J et al. (2020). Number of Deaths due to Carbon Monoxide Poisoning by Month and by Place of Death - China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2020.008>
- Notes: The reason for a B: the smoke-alarm side rests on a single study, done by comparing households after the fact rather than randomizing. The CO alarm itself has no mortality-reduction study; the benefit here is inferred from "poisoning deaths concentrate in winter and at home".

### 4. Replace gas hoses and stoves when they expire, never reroute gas pipes yourself, and feel free to refuse the gas company's door-to-door sales
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A compliant gas hose costs tens of yuan. Stoves get replaced every few years. When replacing one, glance at whether it is past its service life.
- TL;DR: A compliant hose costs tens of yuan. Replace it when it expires - that is a user obligation written into the Regulations on the Administration of Urban Gas. Rerouting pipes or removing the gas meter yourself is explicitly prohibited by the same regulations. They also say the gas company may not require you to buy its designated products or services, so a doorstep pitch that says "you must replace this" can simply be refused.
- Benefit: The regulations make "timely replacement of gas-burning appliances and connecting pipes that the state has ordered phased out or whose service life has expired" a user obligation. In other words, when your stove or hose reaches its age limit, or the state retires that model, you replace it. The regulations also list "installing, modifying or removing indoor gas facilities and gas metering devices without authorization" as prohibited behavior - DIY pipe work and meter removal are both covered. The regulations also make clear that a gas supplier may not require users to buy its designated products or accept its provided services
- Evidence: A
- Sources: State Council (2010). Regulations on the Administration of Urban Gas (State Council Order No. 583), Article 27: "Gas users shall abide by safe gas-use rules, use qualified gas-burning appliances and cylinders, and promptly replace gas-burning appliances, connecting pipes and the like that the state has ordered phased out or whose service life has expired"; Article 28's prohibited acts include "(1) operating public gas valves without authorization", "(2) using gas pipelines as load-bearing supports or grounding leads" and "(4) installing, modifying or removing indoor gas facilities and gas metering devices without authorization"; Article 20: a gas supplier may not "(6) require gas users to purchase its designated products or accept services it provides"; Article 29: users may complain to the gas administration and other departments about charges and service, and "the relevant department shall handle the complaint within 15 working days of receiving it". <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1758214.htm>
- Notes: Some people come to the door under the name of a "safety inspection" and, once the check is done, pitch self-closing valves and alarms. Article 20(6) closes that path outright: you can refuse, and you can complain. ### 5. Don't pick, buy or eat wild mushrooms - no "folk test" works
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is giving up a seasonal delicacy.
- TL;DR: In 2025 alone there were 828 mushroom-poisoning incidents nationwide, with 2,165 people poisoned and 13 dead. That same year, 34 more mushroom species were newly recorded as poisonous to humans. Silver-needle testing, "bugs ate it so it's safe", "only the bright-colored ones are poisonous" - none of these claims hold up.
- Benefit: China CDC investigated 828 mushroom-poisoning incidents in 2025, involving 2,165 people with 13 deaths. The case fatality rate was 0.6%, meaning 0.6% of the poisoned died. From 2019 to 2024 there were 276 to 676 incidents per year, with case fatality rates between 0.87% and 2.86%. In 2025 alone, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, 34 of them recorded causing poisoning in China for the first time
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mushroom Poisoning Outbreaks - China, 2025. China CDC Weekly (2026): "In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents across 27 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs), affecting 2,165 individuals and causing 13 deaths - a case fatality rate of 0.6%, the lowest of the past six years. In total, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, including 34 newly recorded in poisoning incidents in China." "From 2019 to 2024, the annual number of incidents ranged from 276 to 676, and the case fatality rate ranged from 0.87% to 2.86%." <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2026.120> ; the same journal's 2024 annual report <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2025.106>
- Notes: If you eat the wrong one, induce vomiting immediately, then take the leftover mushrooms or photos of them to the hospital. Mushroom poisoning comes in several types, and identifying which mushroom it was directly decides how the doctor treats it. New species are recorded as poisonous every year, so the premise "locals can tell them apart" doesn't hold. Silver-needle testing, boiling with garlic, "bugs ate it so it's safe", "bright colors mean poison" - none of these claims hold up.

### 6. Don't take e-bikes into hallways or elevators, and don't charge them at home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is walking the extra tens of meters to the shared charging spot downstairs, every single time.
- TL;DR: Taking an e-bike into a hallway or elevator, or charging it at home, is explicitly banned by the Ministry of Emergency Management's Fire Safety Management Rules for High-Rise Residential Buildings. Refuse to correct it and an individual is fined 500 to 1,000 yuan, a business 2,000 to 10,000 yuan. The fine is the small matter; what matters is that this kind of fire burns right in your only escape route.
- Benefit: The ministry's rules state in black and white: "It is prohibited to park electric bicycles or charge electric bicycles in the public lobbies, evacuation walkways, stairwells or emergency exits of high-rise residential buildings." In other words, the lobby, walkways, stairwell and exits take neither parking nor charging. Refuse to correct it and a non-business organization or individual is fined 500 to 1,000 yuan; a business is fined 2,000 to 10,000 yuan
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Emergency Management (2021). Fire Safety Management Rules for High-Rise Residential Buildings (MEM Order No. 5, Articles 37 and 47). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/01/content_5628815.htm>
- Notes: Don't buy modified batteries, don't use no-name chargers, don't leave it charging overnight unattended. Once a lithium battery catches, the whole thing goes up within tens of seconds while giving off highly toxic smoke. A bike parked in the hallway is your only escape route set on fire. ### 7. Measure your blood pressure; if it's high, take medication and bring it down to target
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A home blood-pressure monitor costs 100 to 200 yuan. One minute per reading. Most blood-pressure drugs cost a few to tens of yuan a month. The hard part is keeping taking them when you feel no symptoms.
- TL;DR: Every 10 mmHg drop in blood pressure cuts the chance of a major cardiovascular event by about 20%, stroke by 27%, heart failure by 28%, and the chance of dying by 13%. Among Chinese aged 35 to 75, 44.7% have hypertension, and only 7.2% have it actually controlled.
- Benefit: Pooling 123 trials with over 610,000 people: every 10 mmHg drop in systolic pressure brings about 20% fewer major cardiovascular events; the original value is RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.83. Systolic is the higher of the two blood-pressure numbers. Stroke about 27% lower (RR 0.73). Heart failure about 28% lower (RR 0.72). All-cause mortality down 13% (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84 to 0.91). China screened 1.7 million people aged 35 to 75: 44.7% had hypertension. Of these people, 44.7% knew they had it, 30.1% were being treated, and only 7.2% had it controlled
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ettehad D et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Lu J et al. (2017). Prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension in China (China PEACE Million Persons Project). Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32478-9>
- Notes: Go get your blood pressure measured once. Fewer than 1 in 10 Chinese hypertension patients have it controlled, so this single measurement is one of the best-value moves in the whole book. What counts as target (130 or 140) is still under discussion, but "know you have hypertension and bring it down" is not in dispute.

### 8. After 35, if you're overweight at all, get a fasting blood glucose test; even if it's normal, retest every three years
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A fasting glucose test costs ten-odd yuan; a glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) test, tens of yuan. One blood draw at the hospital.
- TL;DR: A blood draw costing tens of yuan can catch prediabetes while you still have zero symptoms. The US official screening advice: people aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese should get tested. If it comes back normal, testing again every three years is reasonable.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force is the official US body for screening recommendations. Its current recommendation: screen adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, even with no symptoms at all. Overweight means a body mass index of 25 or above, obesity 30 or above. The recommendation's grade is B. It also states: "for adults with normal blood glucose, screening every 3 years is a reasonable approach"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2021). Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes>
- Notes: Finding prediabetes doesn't mean going on medication. A fair share of people can turn it back with lifestyle change alone. Among Chinese adults, those who know they have diabetes have always been a minority; many only find out when complications arrive

### 9. Don't speed, don't drink and drive
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Driving a bit slower adds a few minutes per trip. The hard part is drinking less at dinners.
- TL;DR: Every 1% increase in average speed brings about a 4% increase in the chance of a fatal crash. There is no "a little is fine" line for alcohol. Risk starts climbing while blood alcohol is still very low.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization (WHO): every 1% increase in average speed brings a 4% increase in the risk of a fatal crash. Drunk-driving risk starts rising from very low blood alcohol concentrations
- Evidence: B
- Sources: WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Notes: The 1% and 4% speed-death figures are model-based estimates, not a randomized trial. For drunk driving, WHO gives only the direction, no number; this chapter has not verified the primary literature on "how much alcohol raises risk by how much".

### 10. Use a child safety seat for kids under 4 - don't hold them in your arms
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A seat costs 300 to 2,000 yuan and lasts several years. One extra minute per trip to strap the child in.
- TL;DR: Infants under one in a safety seat are about 70% less likely to suffer fatal injury in a crash; children aged 1 to 4, about half as likely. Holding them in your arms has no such effect - you can't hold on during an emergency brake.
- Benefit: The US traffic-safety agency (NHTSA) estimates that child safety seats cut the risk of fatal injury by 71% for infants under 1 in cars, and by 54% for toddlers aged 1 to 4. The World Health Organization (WHO) puts infant deaths down 71%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Notes: Fix the seat exactly per its manual - installed wrong is the same as not installed. Under age 1 it mounts rear-facing, with the child facing the back of the car. The numbers above are US official estimates from crash data.

### 11. If you have small children, fit window and balcony restrictors - insect screens are not protection
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A pair of window restrictors or child safety locks costs tens of yuan. Ten minutes to install.
- TL;DR: A pair of restrictors costs tens of yuan. New York City gave high-risk households free window guards plus doorstep education, and reported child falls in one borough dropped by half over three years. New York later simply legislated that landlords must install guards in apartments with children. An insect screen pops out with one push - it is not protection.
- Benefit: Starting in 1972, New York City ran a program called "Children Can't Fly", handing out free window guards in high-risk districts with doorstep education. Reported falls in the Bronx dropped 50% from 1973 to 1975. The city then amended its health code to require landlords to install window guards in apartments where children aged 10 and under live
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Spiegel CN, Lindaman FC (1977). Children can't fly: a program to prevent childhood morbidity and mortality from window falls. American Journal of Public Health: "Significant reduction in falls resulted, particularly in the Bronx, where reported falls declined 50 percent from 1973 to 1975"; the 1976 New York City health-code amendment was "to require that landlords provide window guards in apartments where children ten years old and younger reside". <https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.67.12.1143> ; (2021). Unintentional Window Falls in Children and Adolescents. Academic Pediatrics: 38,840 emergency visits in the National Electronic Injury Surveillance System from January 2007 to August 2017, "The majority of falls occurred in children under the age of 6 and were related to falls from a second story or below". <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acap.2020.07.008>
- Notes: Keep beds, sofas, boxes and other climbable things away from windows and balconies. Insect screens only stop mosquitoes - they fall out with a push; don't treat them as guards. And this isn't only for high floors: in the US data, most falls happened from the second story or below.

### 12. Keep children in sight near water; wear life jackets for boating and wild swimming
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A life jacket costs 50 to 200 yuan. Plus the attention it takes to watch the child.
- TL;DR: Wearing a life jacket when you end up in the water roughly halves the chance of drowning. Drowning is still the number-one cause of death for Chinese children aged 1 to 14. The drowning death rate in rural areas is about double the urban rate.
- Benefit: A study using US Coast Guard data matched similar people into pairs for comparison. Among people who fell into the water from recreational boats, those wearing life jackets had about half the risk of drowning; the original value is adjusted RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.74. China's drowning death rate for under-20s fell from 6.60 per 100,000 in 2013 to 3.28 per 100,000 in 2021. Rural is about 2 times urban. Drowning remains the leading cause of death for children aged 1 to 14. Among injury deaths of ages 0 to 19 in 2021, drowning accounted for 31.1% and road traffic for 27.9%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Cummings P et al. (2011). Association between wearing a personal floatation device and death by drowning among recreational boaters. Injury Prevention. <https://doi.org/10.1136/ip.2010.028688> ; Li Z et al. (2023). Unintentional Drowning Mortality Among Individuals Under Age 20 - China, 2013-2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2023.198> ; Zhou J et al. (2024). Injury Mortality of Children and Adolescents Aged 0-19 Years - China, 2010-2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2024.057>
- Notes: The A grade only covers the life-jacket half. "Never out of sight" is everyone's consensus, but on its own it is grade C - there is no randomized trial and there never will be. China's drowning deaths concentrate in rural areas, in summer, and among males aged 15 to 19.

### 13. After 60, train balance and leg strength, and adapt the bathroom and stairs at home
<!-- tags: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Practice 2 to 3 times a week, 30 minutes of tai chi or balance training each time. Bathroom anti-slip mats, grab bars and night lights cost tens to hundreds of yuan. The hard part is keeping it up long-term.
- TL;DR: Practicing balance two or three times a week, tai chi for example, cuts the number of falls among the elderly by about 23% and the number of people who fall by 15%. Anti-slip work and grab bars at home cut it by about another 20%. Falls are the leading injury cause of death for Chinese over 65, and more than half happen right at home.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 108 trials with 23,407 people. Exercise lowered the number of falls among older people by 23%; the original value is rate ratio RaR 0.77, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.83. The number of people who fell dropped 15% (RR 0.85). Having someone come assess and modify the home for safety cut the fall rate by about 20% (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68 to 0.97). Practicing tai chi lowered fall risk by about 29% (RR 0.71). In China, falls are the leading injury cause of death for people over 65. Of the elderly falls monitored in 2018, 55.97% happened at home
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sherrington C et al. (2019). Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012424.pub2> ; Gillespie LD et al. (2012). Interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007146.pub3> ; Lu Z et al. (2021). Characteristics of Falls Among Older People - China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2021.013>
- Notes: This entry applies to people over 60 living in their own homes, not in care facilities. The studies above counted falls, not deaths. The road from a fall to a hip fracture to death is a short one for the elderly.

### 14. Get a hepatitis B panel; if you have no antibodies, get vaccinated
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: The test costs tens of yuan. The adult hepatitis B vaccine is 3 shots, 100 to 300 yuan in total, completed within half a year.
- TL;DR: The test costs tens of yuan. In the Qidong trial followed for 30 years, people vaccinated against hepatitis B at birth were 84% less likely to develop primary liver cancer later, and 72% less likely to carry the hepatitis B virus. Adults whose test shows no antibodies get three catch-up shots within half a year, for one to two hundred yuan.
- Benefit: The Qidong trial randomized by cluster, not by individual, and was followed for 30 years. Neonatal hepatitis B vaccination brought primary liver cancer down 84%; the confidence range is 95% CI 23% to 97%. The hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positive rate fell 72% (95% CI 68% to 75%) - that positive rate is the share of people found to carry the virus. China's population-wide HBsAg positive rate fell 52% from 1992 to 2014, and 97% among under-5s
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Qu C et al. (2014). Efficacy of neonatal HBV vaccination on liver cancer and other liver diseases over 30-year follow-up of the Qidong hepatitis B intervention study. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001774> ; Cui F et al. (2017). Prevention of Chronic Hepatitis B after 3 Decades of Escalating Vaccination Policy, China. Emerging Infectious Diseases. <https://doi.org/10.3201/eid2305.161477>
- Notes: This entry is for adults born before 1992 who were never vaccinated or whose antibodies have faded. The 84% liver-cancer drop comes from people vaccinated at birth. In the same trial, those first vaccinated as teenagers got only 21% efficacy against becoming carriers. Adults get vaccinated mainly to avoid new infection - you can't borrow that 84%. Vaccination does nothing for people already infected; they need regular follow-up in a hepatology clinic. What counts as already infected: the HBsAg line on the lab report is positive.

### 15. Punctured by a nail or splinter, or wound got soil in it - get it treated the same day, and ask about tetanus while you're there
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration plus wound care costs tens of yuan. If the doctor says a shot is needed, the vaccine or immunoglobulin costs tens to hundreds of yuan.
- TL;DR: Once tetanus develops, about 1 in 10 dies. Deep wounds and soil-contaminated wounds carry the highest risk. Same-day treatment costs tens of yuan at most - don't wait for the wound to swell.
- Benefit: Of tetanus cases in the United States, about 1 in 10 is fatal. Deep wounds and soil-contaminated wounds carry higher risk
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Tetanus: "Tetanus bacteria can get into someone's body through broken skin, usually through injuries." "Tetanus can lead to death (1 in 10 cases in the United States are fatal)." "People who didn't complete the primary series or who aren't up to date with their 10-year tetanus booster shots are also at increased risk." "Vaccination also helps prevent tetanus in people with wounds, depending on their tetanus vaccination history." <https://www.cdc.gov/tetanus/about/index.html> ; National Health Commission General Office (2024). Notice on issuing the Diagnosis and Treatment Protocol for Non-neonatal Tetanus (2024 edition) (Guoweiban Yijihan [2024] No. 381). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/content_6982262.htm>
- Notes: At the hospital, let the doctor judge - don't decide for yourself, and don't skip asking because it feels like a hassle. Not every wound needs a shot: the doctor looks at the nature of the wound, then at when your last vaccination was, and decides. "Vaccinated as a kid means protected for life" is wrong - boosters count in 10-year spans. ### 16. Women should get the HPV vaccine, the earlier the better
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: The domestic bivalent vaccine is about 300-odd yuan per shot; the imported nine-valent about 1,300 yuan per shot. Two to three shots in total. Each shot is a half-day trip.
- TL;DR: In data on 1.67 million Swedish women, those vaccinated against HPV before 17 were about one-eighth as likely to develop invasive cervical cancer as the unvaccinated. Those vaccinated at 17 to 30 were about half as likely. So the earlier, the more it's worth.
- Benefit: Sweden followed 1.67 million women: the vaccinated had a lower incidence of invasive cervical cancer than the unvaccinated. Vaccinated before 17: about 88% lower (IRR 0.12, 95% CI 0.00 to 0.34 - the original value with its confidence range in parentheses). Vaccinated at 17 to 30: about 53% lower (IRR 0.47, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.75)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lei J et al. (2020). HPV Vaccination and the Risk of Invasive Cervical Cancer. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1917338>
- Notes: Vaccination at 9 to 14 brings the biggest benefit. At 17 to 30 there is still a drop of more than half. After 30 the benefit shrinks steadily. Vaccinated women still need cervical cancer screening - the vaccine can't replace it; see entry 18 (cervical cancer screening for women over 30).

### 17. Women should start breast cancer screening at 40: a mammogram every two years
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A mammogram costs two to three hundred yuan each time. Many regions have free screening programs. Half a day every two years.
- TL;DR: Women aged 40 to 74 should have a mammogram every two years - that is the clear recommendation in the US official screening advice. For over-75s, and for adding ultrasound and MRI for dense breasts, the official conclusion is that the evidence is still insufficient. When a checkup package charges extra for those, you can skip them.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force is the official US body for screening recommendations. Its current recommendation: "recommends biennial screening mammography for women aged 40 to 74 years". Mammography is what people call a mammogram. The recommendation's grade is B. For women 75 and older, and for supplemental ultrasound or MRI for dense breasts, it ruled the "current evidence is insufficient" in both cases
- Evidence: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2024). Breast Cancer: Screening. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening>
- Notes: Starting ages differ by country; some Chinese guidelines and local programs start at 45. People whose parents, children or siblings have had breast cancer, or who carry a BRCA mutation, are high-risk and need a plan set individually with a doctor - this entry does not apply to them

### 18. Women over 30 should get cervical cancer screening, HPV testing first
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: An HPV test costs 100 to 300 yuan. If the result is negative, once every 5 years is enough. Sampling takes a few minutes.
- TL;DR: In the rural India trial, women who had just one HPV test in their lives were about half as likely to die of cervical cancer and about 50% less likely to be found with advanced cervical cancer. One to two hundred yuan a time, and a negative result covers 5 years.
- Benefit: The rural India trial randomized by cluster, not by individual. The subjects were women aged 30 to 59. A single round of HPV screening cut the risk of dying of cervical cancer by about 48%; the original value is HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.83. The risk of being found with advanced cervical cancer fell about 53% (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.69)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sankaranarayanan R et al. (2009). HPV screening for cervical cancer in rural India. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0808516>
- Notes: Women vaccinated against HPV still need screening, because the vaccine doesn't cover all virus types. What makes this study rare: it is a randomized screening trial that counted deaths directly, with only one round of screening.

### 19. Start colorectal cancer screening at 45 to 50: a fecal immunochemical test for occult blood, or a colonoscopy
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: The stool test costs tens of yuan, done every 1 to 2 years. A colonoscopy costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan; if it's negative, once every 10 years is enough. Set aside a day for bowel prep before a colonoscopy.
- TL;DR: A fecal occult blood test every year or two, costing tens of yuan, lowers the chance of dying of colorectal cancer by about 16%. People who attended at least one round were 25% lower. The group invited to a one-off colonoscopy had their ten-year colorectal cancer rate drop from 1.20% to 0.98%.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled multiple studies. People screened with fecal occult blood tests had about 16% lower risk of dying of colorectal cancer; the original value is RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.90. Those who attended at least one round were about 25% lower (RR 0.75). Then the NordICC randomized trial: people invited to a colonoscopy had their 10-year colorectal cancer incidence drop from 1.20% to 0.98%; the original value is RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.93
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hewitson P et al. (2007). Screening for colorectal cancer using the faecal occult blood test, Hemoccult. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001216.pub2> ; Bretthauer M et al. (2022). - Notes: Disputed. NordICC counted by "who was invited to a colonoscopy". Colorectal cancer deaths were 0.28% versus 0.31% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.16). That difference is still within the error range. In other words, colonoscopy's mortality benefit was overestimated in the past. The stool test actually has harder evidence on mortality. Starting ages in national guidelines run from 45 to 50. People with family members who had this cancer should start earlier.

### 20. People with cardiovascular disease and the elderly should get a flu shot every year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: 50 to 150 yuan a shot, once every autumn.
- TL;DR: In the trial that gave flu shots after heart attacks, the share who died within a year fell from 4.9% to 2.9%. People with cardiovascular disease and the elderly should get a shot every autumn, for 50 to 150 yuan.
- Benefit: One trial, run after myocardial infarction, randomized 2,571 people; neither doctors nor patients knew which shot was which. Those given the flu vaccine had 12-month all-cause mortality of 2.9%. Those given placebo, 4.9%. The original value is HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.89. A placebo is a dummy shot with no active ingredient. The HR for cardiovascular death was 0.59. Another study pooled multiple studies: vaccinated people had major cardiovascular events at 3.6% versus 5.4% for controls; the original value is RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.83. Cardiovascular death RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.42 to 1.30 - this difference is still within the error range, not statistically significant)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Fröbert O et al. (2021). Influenza Vaccination After Myocardial Infarction: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004876.pub4> (the Cochrane summary mentioned in the notes)
- Notes: Disputed. The A grade only covers people with cardiovascular disease. For otherwise healthy elderly people, the Cochrane review could only reach one conclusion: flu illness in a season fell from 6% to 2.4%. ### 21. Get the shingles vaccine after 50
<!-- tags: money=high time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: The recombinant shingles vaccine takes two shots, about 3,000 to 4,000 yuan in total, entirely out of pocket.
- TL;DR: In the phase-3 trial, this vaccine blocked 97% of shingles, roughly the same across age groups. The downside is 3,000 to 4,000 yuan for two shots, all self-paid. What that money buys back is a 97% drop in incidence.
- Benefit: A phase-3 trial across 18 countries with 15.4 thousand people randomized them into two groups, vaccine or control shot, followed for an average of 3.2 years. Result: 6 shingles cases in the vaccine group versus 210 in the placebo group. Converted, that is 0.3 versus 9.1 per thousand person-years - per thousand person-years means the number of cases observed in 1,000 people over one year. The paper writes "overall vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster of 97.2% (95% CI 93.7-99.0, P<0.001)". Efficacy across age groups ran from 96.6% to 97.9%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lal H, Cunningham AL, Godeaux O, et al. (2015). Efficacy of an adjuvanted herpes zoster subunit vaccine in older adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(22), 2087-2096. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1501184>
- Notes: This entry ranks low on value for one reason: the price. Shingles itself rarely kills; the real trouble is postherpetic neuralgia, which can hurt for months to years. After the shot, injection-site pain and fever are not uncommon. The most severe tier (grade-3 symptoms) was 17.0% versus 3.2%

### 22. Get the pneumococcal vaccine after 65
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A few hundred yuan. Free for the elderly in some regions.
- TL;DR: In a trial of 84.5 thousand elderly people, the 13-valent pneumococcal vaccine blocked about 46% of pneumonia from the strains it covers, and 75% of the most dangerous invasive infections. But it showed no effect on "pneumonia of all causes" - don't expect it to cover every pneumonia.
- Benefit: The Netherlands randomized 84.5 thousand people over 65 into two groups. The vaccine was the 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine. Its efficacy against vaccine-type pneumonia was 45.6%; the confidence range is 95.2% CI 21.8-62.5. Vaccine-type means the strains this vaccine covers. Efficacy against invasive pneumococcal disease was 75.0% (95% CI 41.4-90.8) - the most dangerous class of infection. Against pneumonia of all causes combined, it had no effect
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Bonten MJ, Huijts SM, Bolkenbaas M, et al. (2015). Polysaccharide conjugate vaccine against pneumococcal pneumonia in adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(12), 1114-1125. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1408544>
- Notes: It only prevents the strains the vaccine covers - one shot doesn't make you pneumonia-proof. The shot commonly given to Chinese elderly is the 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine, not the same as the 13-valent conjugate vaccine in this trial; the efficacy numbers above don't carry over directly

### 23. Test for Helicobacter pylori; if positive, eradicate it
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A carbon-13 or carbon-14 breath test costs 100 to 200 yuan. Eradication means four drugs together, 200 to 500 yuan for the two-week course. The hard part is taking the drugs on schedule for the full two weeks.
- TL;DR: In the Linqu, Shandong trial followed for 22 years, people whose H. pylori was eradicated were about half as likely to develop stomach cancer later, and about 40% less likely to die of it. The test costs one to two hundred yuan; eradication takes two weeks of drugs.
- Benefit: The randomized trial in Linqu, Shandong was followed for 22 years. People who received H. pylori eradication therapy had about half the risk of stomach cancer; the original value is OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.71. The risk of dying of stomach cancer was about 38% lower (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.99)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Li WQ et al. (2019). Effects of Helicobacter pylori treatment and vitamin and garlic supplementation on gastric cancer incidence and mortality: follow-up of a randomized intervention trial. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l5016>
- Notes: Reinfection is possible after eradication, so the whole household should test and treat together. The trial's population lived in a high-incidence area for stomach cancer. People in low-incidence areas will get a much smaller real benefit.

### 24. Heavy smokers should get a low-dose chest CT every year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: 200 to 400 yuan a time, done in 10 minutes. Also count the follow-ups after a false alarm, and the days of dread in between.
- TL;DR: Eligible heavy smokers getting a low-dose CT every year were 20% less likely to die of lung cancer than those only getting a chest X-ray, and 6.7% less likely to die overall. The price is the follow-up exams after suspicious findings, and the dread that comes with them.
- Benefit: The US NLST randomized trial enrolled 53,454 people, all aged 55 to 74 with at least 30 pack-years of smoking and no more than 15 years quit. A pack-year is the unit for smoking: one pack a day for one year. The result: people given low-dose CT had a 20.0% relative drop in lung-cancer death versus those given only chest X-rays; the confidence range is 95% CI 6.8 to 26.7. All-cause mortality fell 6.7% relatively, 95% CI 1.2 to 13.6
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Lung Screening Trial Research Team (2011). Reduced Lung-Cancer Mortality with Low-Dose Computed Tomographic Screening. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873>
- Notes: This entry only applies to high-risk smokers. In the same trial, 24.2% of those given low-dose CT had a suspicious finding, and 96.4% of those were false alarms - false positives. For non-smokers, this exam is buying anxiety with money.

### 25. When depressed or having suicidal thoughts, call 12356; don't stockpile sleeping pills or pesticides at home
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call. Plus locking up medications and keeping pesticides out of the home.
- TL;DR: The evidence for taking lethal means out of reach keeps getting stronger: after painkillers were restricted, suicides involving them dropped 43%; after barriers went up at frequent jumping spots, 86%. So don't stockpile sleeping pills or pesticides at home. When things feel unbearable, call 12356 first - it works nationwide, open at least 18 hours a day.
- Benefit: A review that systematically surveyed the last 10 years of research: the evidence for restricting access to lethal means keeps strengthening. After painkiller restrictions, suicides involving them fell 43%. After barriers were added at frequent jumping locations, 86% (79% to 91%, the confidence range in parentheses). Cognitive programs in schools brought suicide attempts down about 55% (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.85). Medication and psychotherapy for depression are an important part of suicide prevention. Since May 1, 2025, dialing 12356 anywhere in China reaches the psychological assistance hotline, open no less than 18 hours a day
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zalsman G et al. (2016). Suicide prevention strategies revisited: 10-year systematic review. Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(16)30030-X> ; National Health Commission (2024). Notice on adopting "12356" as the national unified psychological assistance hotline number (Guowei Yizhenghan [2024] No. 259). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202412/content_6994470.htm>
- Notes: Why a B: the means-restriction evidence is mostly ecological studies, comparing numbers before and after a policy; the hotline itself has no mortality-reduction evidence. Suicidal urges often last only minutes to hours - moving lethal things one step further away is itself an intervention. For data on that time scale, and long-term outcomes after an attempt, see entry 32 of this chapter (tell one person near you the moment suicidal thoughts surface). For what remains after being saved, see entry 33 of this chapter.

### 26. Keep a fire extinguisher, fire blanket, escape breathing masks and a first-aid kit; check them once a year
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: An extinguisher, a fire blanket, one breathing mask per person, plus a first-aid kit - a few hundred yuan for the set. Buy once, then spend ten minutes a year checking it.
- TL;DR: The extinguisher, fire blanket, escape masks and first-aid kit are all on the Ministry of Emergency Management's 11-item family emergency list. A few hundred yuan, bought once. In the studies where households received home safety guidance, injury rates were about a quarter lower.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Emergency Management's National Basic Household Emergency Supplies List has 11 items. "Fire extinguisher and fire blanket" are for "fighting incipient fires", and the list notes "a fire blanket can be draped over the body for escape". The "breathing mask" is a "fire-filtering self-rescue respirator, used for escaping fires". "External-use medication" means "hemostatic powder, band-aids, gauze bandages and the like, for treating wounds". Cochrane pooled 98 studies with 2,605,044 people. Home safety interventions brought the injury incidence rate to IRR 0.89; the confidence range is 95% CI 0.78 to 1.01. Among them, interventions delivered door to door had injury incidence about 25% lower (IRR 0.75, 95% CI 0.62 to 0.91)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Ministry of Emergency Management (2020). National Basic Household Emergency Supplies Reserve Recommendation List. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/kp/shaq/202011/t20201129_372149.shtml> ; Kendrick D et al. (2012). - Notes: Disputed. In the same Cochrane summary, the interventions that did not hand out equipment had injury rates about 20% lower; the original value is IRR 0.78, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.92. The groups that did get equipment did no better. What lowers injuries is mainly in-home education and modification, so the equipment's benefit is marked "mid", not "big". When buying an extinguisher, look for the CCC mark; on a stored-pressure type the needle should sit in the green zone. Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are in entry 3 of this chapter. ### 27. If you see blood in your urine with the naked eye - even painless, even if it's clear the next day - get checked once
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=small lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A urinalysis costs tens of yuan, a urinary ultrasound one to two hundred. If the doctor thinks it's needed, add a cystoscopy at around a thousand yuan.
- TL;DR: One episode of visible blood in the urine in someone over 60 ends in a bladder cancer diagnosis about 2.8% of the time; at 40 to 59, about 1.2%. That number is not high, but it is high enough that you must get checked once. Go even if the bleeding stops the next day.
- Benefit: A UK study using community GP (primary care) electronic records compared 4,915 bladder cancer cases with 21,718 controls. Of people with visible hematuria, the share finally diagnosed with bladder cancer: over 60, 2.8%, 95% CI 2.5 to 3.1; aged 40 to 59, 1.2%, 95% CI 0.6 to 2.3. The technical name for that share is positive predictive value. Blood only visible under a microscope matters too: odds ratio 20, 95% CI 12 to 33; for over-60s the share is 1.6%, 95% CI 1.2 to 2.1
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Price SJ, Shephard EA, Stapley SA, Barraclough K, Hamilton WT (2014). Non-visible versus visible haematuria and bladder cancer risk: a study of electronic records in primary care. British Journal of General Practice, 64(626), e584-e589. <https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp14X681409>
- Notes: "Painless" is the key point. Blood from stones or infection usually comes with colicky waist or abdominal pain, or frequent, urgent, painful urination. Tumor bleeding often comes with no feeling at all, stops by itself for a few days and returns - many people let it go that way. Being on anticoagulants is no reason to skip the check. Blood after intense exercise, or around a woman's period, can be rechecked once before deciding. The benefit is marked "small" because there is only a detection rate, no effect size for "how much earlier detection lowers death".

### 28. When erectile function has problems, check the cardiovascular system first - don't treat it as just "a bedroom thing"
<!-- tags: money=low time=mid will=no benefit=mid lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration costs tens of yuan. Blood pressure plus a blood draw for glucose and lipids, one to two hundred. One hospital trip, then a few days waiting for results.
- TL;DR: Men with erectile dysfunction develop cardiovascular disease about 40% more often than others, and heart attacks about 60% more. The younger the man, the bigger the gap. This problem often shows up before chest pain. So check blood pressure, glucose and lipids first - don't self-medicate to suppress it.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled 14 follow-along studies with 92,757 men, followed an average of 6.1 years. Compared with men without erectile dysfunction, those with it had later risks as follows. All cardiovascular events about 44% higher (RR 1.44, 95% CI 1.27 to 1.63). Heart attack about 62% higher (RR 1.62, 1.34 to 1.96). Cerebrovascular events about 39% higher (RR 1.39, 1.23 to 1.57). All-cause death about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 1.12 to 1.39). Cardiovascular death did not separate (RR 1.19, 0.97 to 1.46). The younger the age, the bigger the multiple. People whose baseline cardiovascular risk was medium had bigger multiples than both high- and low-risk people. Studies that judged by questionnaire came out at 1.61 (1.38 to 1.86); those asking a single question, 1.27 (1.18 to 1.37)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Vlachopoulos CV, Terentes-Printzios DG, Ioakeimidis NK, Aznaouridis KA, Stefanadis CI (2013). Prediction of cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality with erectile dysfunction: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes, 6(1), 99-109. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.112.966903> ; DailyMed (the US National Library of Medicine's drug-label library). VIAGRA (sildenafil citrate) tablet, CONTRAINDICATIONS. <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d905dc8d-917f-4ea3-a4ee-a1ecf6967d4e>
- Notes: All 14 studies follow groups of people and watch outcomes - they are not randomized trials. So "40% more" is an association, not a cause. One or two occasional episodes don't count; three months or more of persistence is what counts as a problem. What to check is the same batch as entries 7 and 8 of this chapter: blood pressure, fasting glucose, plus lipids, smoking and waist circumference. Don't buy "male enhancement" supplements online. The legitimate drugs of that class are prescription-only; the label lists nitrates as a contraindication - people on nitroglycerin-type drugs must not take them, because together they crash blood pressure. For how to improve, see entry 29 of this chapter (losing weight and quitting smoking can improve erectile function). The benefit is "mid" not "big": 44% is the risk gap between two groups, not "how much death one checkup prevents". Nor is it "small": signals like entry 27 of this chapter (visible blood in urine) only have a detection rate, while the hypertension, diabetes and lipid problems found here all have ready treatments.

### 29. Lose weight, quit smoking, control blood pressure and glucose - erectile function improves along with them
<!-- tags: money=0 time=high will=yes benefit=small lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, and may even save money. The hard part is long-term persistence: watch what you eat, move more, quit smoking.
- TL;DR: Getting cardiovascular risk factors under control improves erectile function itself. Pooling randomized trials, questionnaire scores rose 2 to 3 points on average. The questionnaire is scored out of 25, with 22 or above counting as normal.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled 6 randomized controlled trials with 740 men. The interventions were lifestyle change, or medication to control cardiovascular risk factors, with at least 6 weeks of follow-up. Result: erectile function questionnaire (IIEF-5) scores rose by an average of 2.66 points (95% CI 1.86 to 3.47). Removing the 2 statin trials (143 people), the remaining 4 lifestyle-only trials (597 people) still rose 2.40 points (1.19 to 3.61). The IIEF-5 is a 5-question questionnaire scored 5 to 25. The original paper splits scores into five bands: 22 to 25 counts as no dysfunction, 5 to 7 as severe
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Gupta BP, Murad MH, Clifton MM, Prokop L, Nehra A, Kopecky SL (2011). The effect of lifestyle modification and cardiovascular risk factor reduction on erectile dysfunction: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Archives of Internal Medicine, 171(20), 1797-1803. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2011.440> ; Rosen RC, Cappelleri JC, Smith MD, Lipsky J, Peña BM (1999). Development and evaluation of an abridged, 5-item version of the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF-5) as a diagnostic tool for erectile dysfunction. International Journal of Impotence Research, 11(6), 319-326. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ijir.3900472>
- Notes: The 6 trials intervened differently - some weight loss plus exercise, some blood-pressure and lipid drugs. Only 740 people in total. So the direction is clear, but how much any one person improves varies. The benefit is marked "small" because the endpoint is only a questionnaire score, with no numbers for "how much less disease later". The same actions have hard endpoints elsewhere: quitting smoking, see entry 1 of chapter 2 (quit smoking, the earlier the better); losing weight, see entry 33 of chapter 2 (keep BMI at 20-25); blood pressure, see entry 7 of this chapter (measure blood pressure, medicate to target). Taking medication itself is not off-limits, as long as a doctor prescribes it. Online "supplements" are often secretly spiked with these ingredients at unknown doses; how to tell them apart is in entry 4 of chapter 28 (don't buy weight-loss drugs that promise fast results).

### 30. Use condoms for the whole of every sexual encounter; never share needles
<!-- tags: money=low time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: A yuan or two each. The hard part isn't money - it's using one every time, from the very start.
- TL;DR: Worn every time, all the way through, condoms cut the chance of HIV transmission between heterosexual partners by about 70% or more. "Most of the time" works barely better than not at all. Syphilis, gonorrhea and the like are blocked along with it.
- Benefit: A study pooled 25 studies with 10,676 heterosexual couples. In every couple one partner had HIV and the other didn't - technically serodiscordant. Compared with people who never used condoms, those who used them every time had about 71% lower HIV transmission risk; the original value is RR 0.29, 95% CI 0.20 to 0.43. Compared with intermittent users, about 77% lower (RR 0.23, 95% CI 0.13 to 0.40). The authors' conclusion: condoms are imperfect, but "consistent use can reduce HIV transmission by more than 70%". The same layer of protection covers syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia and other sexually transmitted infections
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Giannou FK, Tsiara CG, Nikolopoulos GK, et al. (2016). Condom effectiveness in reducing heterosexual HIV transmission: a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on HIV serodiscordant couples. Expert Review of Pharmacoeconomics & Outcomes Research, 16(4), 489-499. <https://doi.org/10.1586/14737167.2016.1102635> ; Fonner VA et al. (2016). Effectiveness and safety of oral HIV preexposure prophylaxis for all populations. AIDS. <https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000001145> (pre-exposure prophylaxis, PrEP)
- Notes: The gap between "irregular use" and "not using" is tiny - this entry's entire effect lives in the four words "every time, all the way". Shared needles are a separate, independent high-efficiency transmission route with nothing to do with sex: injected drugs, tattoos and piercings all count. High-risk people also have pre-exposure prophylaxis, called PrEP - taking a drug beforehand to block infection. Pooling 18 studies: in every trial where more than 70% of people took the drug on schedule, those on PrEP had about 70% lower HIV infection risk than those on placebo; the original value is risk ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.45. Trials with low adherence saw no protective effect. PrEP is a prescription drug - ask at an infectious-disease clinic or the CDC; don't buy it online yourself. What to do after an exposure has already happened: see chapter 13

### 31. After any high-risk behavior, get an HIV test once - free at the CDC, results confidential
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to the CDC or a designated medical facility, one tube of blood. The hard part is bringing yourself to go.
- TL;DR: HIV testing at the CDC costs nothing, and under the National HIV Testing Work Management Measures, staff may not disclose your name, address or test results. A positive result is not a death sentence: people who keep the virus suppressed to undetectable with medication do not pass it to sexual partners. That study followed over seven hundred couples through more than seventy thousand condomless sex acts - partner transmission was zero.
- Benefit: Article 23 of the Regulations on AIDS Prevention and Treatment states: health departments of local people's governments at county level and above designate medical and health institutions, and these institutions "shall provide free counseling and initial screening tests to people who voluntarily accept HIV counseling and testing". Testing early is not about peace of mind: current antiretroviral therapy can suppress the virus to undetectable levels. The World Health Organization's position is "people living with HIV who are on antiretroviral therapy and have an undetectable viral load do not transmit HIV to their sexual partners". The PARTNER study followed 782 gay couples, each serodiscordant, for a combined 1,593 couple-years - the follow-up years of all couples added together. During that time there were 76,088 condomless sex acts and 15 new infections. Phylogenetic analysis comparing virus genes showed all 15 came from outside the couple - partner transmission was zero. The National HIV Testing Work Management Measures stipulate that staff of HIV testing laboratories may not disclose the names, addresses, test results or related information of AIDS patients or infected people
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Health (2006). National HIV Testing Work Management Measures. <https://www.gov.cn/zwgk/2006-06/27/content_320458.htm> ; State Council (2006). Regulations on AIDS Prevention and Treatment (Articles 3, 23 and 38). <https://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/zcwj2022/flfg/202304/t20230408_2992986.html> (reposted by the Beijing Municipal Health Commission) ; Rodger AJ, Cambiano V, Bruun T, et al. (2019). Risk of HIV transmission through condomless sex in serodifferent gay couples with the HIV-positive partner taking suppressive antiretroviral therapy (PARTNER): final results of a multicentre, prospective, observational study. The Lancet, 393(10189), 2428-2438. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(19)30418-0> ; Guangdong Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention. HIV testing window period explainer. <https://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/jkjy/kpydjwjxz/content/post_3441820.html> (the window-period numbers)
- Notes: The window period decides when a test counts. Per the Guangdong CDC: nucleic acid testing can detect infection at about 1 week, fourth-generation antigen-antibody combination tests at about 2 weeks, third-generation antibody tests at about 3 weeks. The advice is to test at least 2 weeks later and retest every 2 to 4 weeks. Three full months after the last high-risk behavior, more than 99.99% of infections can be ruled out. A positive self-test kit is not a diagnosis - it must be confirmed at the CDC or a proper facility. ### 32. The moment suicidal thoughts surface, tell one person near you - hand over those few dozen minutes
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=some benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is speaking up at the time, and admitting you can't be alone for those few hours.
- TL;DR: Of 82 suicide attempters, 47.6% said no more than 10 minutes passed from the first thought to the act. What you have to survive is not an incomprehensible lifetime - it's a few dozen minutes. In the same group, 76.8% had contact with someone during that stretch and just didn't speak. ### 33. Don't treat "being saved" as a safety net: after pesticide or gas, the ER can keep your life - not your lungs or your brain
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Just knowing this in advance.
- TL;DR: Paraquat has no antidote; gastric lavage should not be done, and hemoperfusion and dialysis can't change the course. It is actively pumped into the lungs, causing pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis, while injuring kidneys and liver. The case fatality rate across 257 cases in 10 Bangladeshi hospitals was 43.2%. Carbon monoxide is the other end: six weeks later, 46.1% of the normal-pressure oxygen group had cognitive impairment, versus 25.0% of the hyperbaric group. There is no clean ending down this road - the more common product is the sequelae that remain.
- Benefit: A systematic review of paraquat poisoning: the case fatality rate is extremely high because the poison itself is highly toxic and effective treatment is lacking. It is actively transported into lung tissue against the concentration gradient - technically, active uptake against a gradient - causing pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis, plus kidney and liver injury. The review states: gastric lavage should not be performed. Hemodialysis and hemoperfusion are unlikely to change the clinical course. Evidence for steroids and immunosuppressants is very weak. It also writes: "case fatality rates were high across all centers, despite large differences in treatment". Common presentations were lower-limb swelling from acute kidney failure 71.2%, vomiting 65.8%, abdominal pain 58.0%. China revoked the registration and production license for paraquat aqueous solution on July 1, 2014, and stopped production. From July 1, 2016, the aqueous solution was withdrawn from domestic sale and use. From September 26, 2020, soluble gel formulations were banned from domestic sale and use. The document states that "suicide incidents involving paraquat ingestion kept occurring" as the direct reason for tighter regulation

### 35. Don't trade "one kidney less is nothing" for money: the remaining one works for two, and 86% of kidney sellers say their health worsened afterwards
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Just seeing the bill clearly in advance.
- TL;DR: Look at the best case first: legitimate donors who passed layer after layer of screening and get follow-up after surgery. In US data on 96 thousand people, 15 years after donation, 31 per ten thousand reached uremia - versus 4 per ten thousand among equally healthy, equally eligible people who didn't donate. Then look at the selling side: 305 Indians who sold a kidney, interviewed on average 6 years later - 86% said their health had worsened, and three-quarters were still in debt. Legitimate donors get screening and rechecks; sellers get none of that.
- Benefit: The US compared 96,217 living kidney donors with 20,024 similarly healthy non-donors, the latter from NHANES III, the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. The estimated risk of reaching uremia within 15 years of donation: donors 30.8 per ten thousand, 95% CI 24.3-38.5; healthy non-donors 3.9 per ten thousand, 95% CI 0.8-8.9, P<0.001. The technical name for uremia is end-stage renal disease (ESRD) - at that point you survive on dialysis or a transplant. Estimated lifetime risk of ESRD: donors 90 per ten thousand, the unscreened general population 326 per ten thousand, healthy non-donors 14 per ten thousand. Norway ran its own comparison: 1,901 kidney donors with a median follow-up of 15.1 years, against 32,621 donation-eligible controls followed a median of 24.9 years. Donors had all-cause mortality about 30% higher (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11-1.52), cardiovascular death about 40% higher (HR 1.40, 1.03-1.91), and ESRD about ten times higher (HR 11.38, 4.37-29.6). The total ESRD incidence among these donors was 302 per million. Separately, of 3,698 US donors, 11 developed ESRD - 180 cases per million person-years observed, versus 268 in the general population over the same period. Of these donors, 255 returned for rechecks on average 12.2±9.2 years after donation: 85.5% had a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of at least 60 ml/min/1.73 m², 60 being the clinical lower limit of normal; 32.1% had hypertension; 12.7% had proteinuria, protein leaking into the urine. After nephrectomy, average household income fell by a third (P<0.001), the number living below the poverty line rose, and three-quarters were still in debt at survey time. 86% reported worse health than before the nephrectomy; 79% would not advise others to sell a kidney

- Sources: Muzaale AD, Massie AB, Wang MC, Montgomery RA, McBride MA, Wainright JL, Segev DL (2014). Risk of end-stage renal disease following live kidney donation. - Notes: Disputed: Ibrahim's study compared donors with ordinary people and concluded that donors' survival and their risk of reaching uremia (ESRD) were both similar to the general population's. ### 36. Don't pick up, take apart or sell metal parts of unknown origin - stay away from anything with the trefoil warning sign
<!-- tags: money=0 time=low will=no benefit=big lens=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. See an unfamiliar metal cylinder, lead canister, or a part stripped from equipment - walk around it: don't pick it up, don't open it, don't pocket it.
- TL;DR: Radiation has no smell, color or temperature; you can't feel it, so "it looks fine" proves nothing. Most radioactive sources are just a piece of ordinary metal - no glow, no heat. The odds an ordinary person runs into one are extremely low: about 178 thousand sources are in use nationwide, with an accident rate steady at under 1 per ten thousand. But the one time you do is irreversible: in Brazil in 1987, someone scrapped an abandoned clinic's radiotherapy machine for metal - about 112 thousand people had to be monitored, and 4 died.
- Benefit: As of 2025, China has about 178 thousand radioactive sources in use and 355 thousand ray-generating devices (sets). The annual rate of radiation accidents involving sources stays below 1 per ten thousand. The grading works like this: loss, theft or loss of control of a Class I or II source constitutes a major radiation accident; Class III, a relatively major accident; loss or theft of a Class IV or V source, or exposure of a person beyond the annual dose limit, a general accident. An official case: in June 2020, Ningxia Iron & Steel Group's ironworks ran gamma-ray flaw detection during a blast-furnace pipeline retrofit. A Class II iridium-192 source exposed three workers. Chen suffered grade III acute radiation skin injury to the left hand; Liu, grade IV to the left thumb and index finger and grade III to the middle, ring and little fingers; Fan, grade I-II to the left hand. All three received doses far beyond the national dose limits, and the two responsible organizations had their radiation safety licenses revoked and were fined 70,000 yuan. An international case: in Goiânia, Brazil, 1987, a teletherapy machine from an abandoned clinic was dismantled, removing a 50.8 TBq cesium-137 source and rupturing its sealed capsule. About 112,000 people were monitored; 249 had internal or external contamination. Of those 249, 129 were moderately to severely contaminated and 50 needed close medical supervision. In the end, 4 people died (national / international)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ministry of Ecology and Environment (2025). 2024 China Ecological Environment Status Bulletin. <https://www.mee.gov.cn/ywgz/sthjjcgl/hjzljcypj/202506/t20250605_1120773.shtml> ; National Nuclear Safety Administration (2026). China's nuclear and radiation safety regulation achievements in 2025. <https://nnsa.mee.gov.cn/tpxw/202601/t20260107_1140062.html> ; Ministry of Ecology and Environment (2021). Circular on the handling of a radiation accident caused by violations in a gamma-ray flaw-detection operation. <https://www.mee.gov.cn/xxgk2018/xxgk/xxgk06/202101/t20210121_818216.html> ; State Council (2019). Regulations on the Safety and Protection of Radioisotopes and Ray Devices (2019 revision, Articles 34, 42 and 68). <https://www.mee.gov.cn/ywgz/fgbz/xzfg/201909/t20190918_734315.shtml> ; Ministry of Environmental Protection (2009). Notice on establishing the graded handling and reporting system for radiation accidents involving radioisotopes and ray devices (accident grading standards). <https://www.mee.gov.cn/gkml/zj/wj/200910/t20091022_172414.htm> ; International Atomic Energy Agency (1988). The Radiological Accident in Goiânia. IAEA, Vienna. <https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub815_web.pdf>
- Notes: If you really run into one: back away, note the location, don't move it, call 110. The regulations require reporting to the ecology-and-environment, public-security and health authorities. Distance and time are the only effective protection - a pocket or plastic bag blocks nothing. X-ray machines and CT scanners produce rays only while powered; once off, nothing radioactive remains. The real danger is the sealed source inside the equipment. Hospital radiotherapy machines, gamma flaw detectors on pipelines and boilers, well-logging tools and level gauges all contain sources. Opening the casing exposes the source - and their appearance is indistinguishable from ordinary metal parts. So the rule isn't "learn to recognize a source" - it's "never dismantle, sell or take home any equipment part of unknown origin". People in scrap recycling, dismantling, hardware and construction run far higher risk than average; on a worksite, walk around any cordoned area with the trefoil sign. This entry is about orphaned sources, not nuclear plant leaks - the odds of an ordinary person meeting the latter are lower still. The beneficiaries are mainly you and the family standing next to you.
